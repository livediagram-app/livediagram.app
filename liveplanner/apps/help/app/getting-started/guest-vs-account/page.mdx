A core principle of liveplanner is that **liveplanner always works without signing in**. A first-time visitor can open a [new plan](/new), plan something real, share it, and collaborate, all as a guest. Signing in is never a wall in front of the editor; it simply unlocks a few things that need an account to make sense. This article explains the difference so you can choose what suits you.

## What you get as a guest

As a guest you have the full editing experience. Your work is saved to liveplanner automatically, owned by a guest identity that liveworkspace gives this browser, so it is there when you come back on the same device. The same guest is you in every liveworkspace app.

- **Everything in the editor:** boards, sheets, forms and dashboards, every template and every theme.
- **Persistence** for your plans, on this device ([sign in](/help/account-and-data/signing-in/) to sync across devices).
- **Your library** in the [Explorer](/help/explorer/explorer-page/): folders, favourites and the plans shared with you. See [Guest Identity](/help/account-and-data/guest-identity/).
- **Sharing and collaboration:** open share links as an Editor, Participant or Viewer, and mint your own share links so others can join.
- **Real-time presence** on any shared session.

The one trade-off: your guest identity lives in your current browser. Clearing your browser data, or switching to another device, means that guest library does not follow you.

## What an account adds

Creating a free account (at `/get-started`, with your liveworkspace account, confirmed with a code we email you; see [Signing In](/help/account-and-data/signing-in/)) adds the things that only make sense once you have an identity:

- **Cross-device sync.** Your plans travel with your account, so they appear on every device you sign in to.
- **A persistent identity.** Your display name comes from your profile rather than being set per session.
- **[Teams](/help/collaboration/teams/).** Join or create a team to work in a shared space with teammates.
- **[Email notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/)** when someone comments on your plans or fills in one of your forms, on deployments that send email.
- **[API tokens](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/)** for your own scripts and for AI tools connected over MCP.

Accounts are free like everything else in liveplanner, there is no paid tier.

## The Sign-In Reminder

While you work as a guest, a card at the bottom of the screen reminds you that an account is there: **Sign in to access Teams & AI**, with a line saying what that brings: sharing with your team, building with AI, and your plans synced across devices. It has two buttons and a close:

- **Sign in** takes you to the sign-in page, and brings you back where you were afterwards.
- **Learn more** opens **Why sign in?**, a short list of what an account adds: keeping your plans safe from a cache clear, opening them on any device, working as a team, connecting AI tools with API tokens, showing your real name to collaborators, and managing all your share links in one place. It has its own **Sign in** button, and **Maybe later** to close it.
- **×** dismisses the card.

**When it appears.** In the [Explorer](/help/explorer/explorer-page/) it shows straight away. In the editor it waits until you have been in a plan for about five minutes, so it never greets you the moment you open one, and it stays out of [Zen mode](/help/tools/zen-mode/) and embedded plans.

**Dismissing it** hides it everywhere in this browser, in the Explorer and the editor alike, and it doesn't come back. It is only a reminder: nothing stops working if you ignore it.

It never appears once you are signed in.

## Moving from guest to account

You do not lose anything by signing up. When you create your account, or sign in to one, in the same browser, everything you built as a guest **moves into your account** automatically: your plans and folders, favourites and your Shared With Me list, in every liveworkspace app at once. The work you have already done carries over rather than being left behind.

The two paths also coexist forever: a signed-in user can hand a [share link](/help/getting-started/sharing-your-project/) to a guest, who edits the same plan with no account at all.
