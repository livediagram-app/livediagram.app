Comments let you talk about the work without changing it. Leave a question or a piece of feedback where it belongs, on the card or the cell it is about, and resolve it once it has been dealt with. They are the lightweight way to review work, agree on changes, and keep a record of why something is the way it is.

There are two places a comment can live: on a **card**, and on a **cell** of a Sheet. Boards, Dashboards and Forms carry no comments of their own: the conversation lives on the cards they show.

## Commenting on a card

A card holds **comment threads** at the end of the **Overview** tab of its card panel, under **Comments**: as many separate conversations as it needs, each with its own title and replies.

<Step n={1}>
  Click a card to open its panel. Scroll to **Comments** at the end of the **Overview** tab.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **New Thread**, type your comment and press **Start Thread** (or **Ctrl+Enter**,
  **Cmd+Enter** on a Mac).
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  The thread appears with your name, colour and how long ago it was posted. Others reply in the box
  at its foot and press **Reply**. On the board, the card shows how many comments its open threads
  hold.
</Step>

You can edit and delete your own comments; people who can edit the plan can delete anyone's. See [Comment Threads](/help/plan/cards/#comment-threads) for renaming threads, putting one thread on several cards, and everything else.

## Commenting on a Sheet cell

On a Sheet, a comment is about a single cell. Right-click the cell and choose **Insert Comment** (or press Ctrl+Alt+M, ⌘⌥M on a Mac), type your note and press **Comment**. The cell shows a small amber triangle in its corner while the thread is open; rest the pointer on the cell to read the first comment, and press the triangle (or **Show Comments** in the cell menu) to open the whole thread. See [Comment on a Cell](/help/plan/sheets/#comment-on-a-cell).

While a tab has cell comments, a **Collaborate** button appears in the bottom-right cluster with a count of the threads still open. It lists every cell thread on the tab by its Sheet and cell, such as `Budget!B4`; switch to **Resolved** to see the ones that are settled, and press a row to go to its cell.

## Mentioning a teammate

Type **@** in a comment to tag someone on your team. A list of teammates appears as you type: use the arrow keys and **Enter** (or click) to pick one, and their handle, like `@priya-kaur`, goes into the comment. **Esc** closes the list and leaves what you typed.

A mention does two things for the person you tag:

- The thread appears on their [Inbox](/help/explorer/activity/), even if they never commented.
- They get an email quoting your comment, unless they have turned off **Someone Mentions Me in a Comment** in their [email notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/). For a card, the email's button opens that card.

You can mention the members of the team whose library holds the plan, as long as you are a member of that team too, including people who have been invited but not joined yet. On a personal plan there is nobody to mention; move it into a team library first. You need to be signed in to mention anyone.

## Reacting to a comment

Agree, cheer or say "I've seen this" with one press instead of a reply. Hover a comment (on a touch screen it's always there) and press **Add Reaction**, the smile with a plus: six emoji open under its text, 👍 👎 😄 🎉 ❤️ 👀. Pick one and it shows as a chip with a count. You can leave several different emoji on one comment, each once.

- Your reactions are highlighted. Press a chip to add yours, or press it again to take yours back. Nobody can take away anyone else's.
- Hover a chip, focus it, or hold your finger on it to see who reacted ("You, Ana and Sam reacted").
- Reactions work on card threads and on Sheet cell comments. A comment in a resolved thread keeps its chips but takes no new ones until the thread is reopened.
- Editors and Participants react; a Viewer sees the reactions and who left them, but can't add one.

## Resolving comments

Once a comment has been actioned, resolve its thread to clear it from the working view while keeping the record. On a card, **Resolve** sits beside **Reply** at the foot of the thread; with a reply typed it reads **Comment and Resolve**, posting your reply and resolving the thread in one press, and the thread moves under **Resolved** at the bottom of the list. On a Sheet cell, press **Resolve** at the top of the thread. A resolved thread is drawn quieter, with who resolved it, and has no reply box; on a card its comments leave the card's count, and on a cell the amber triangle goes. **Reopen** brings the thread back. Use comments to:

- **Request changes** during a review without editing someone else's work directly.
- **Ask a question** on the exact card or cell it is about.
- **Capture a decision** so the reasoning is not lost once the plan changes.

## Comments and collaboration

New comments appear for everyone in the plan as they are posted. A collaborator can answer a question where it was asked, and a reviewer can leave feedback for the owner to pick up later. Open threads you are part of are gathered on your [Inbox](/help/explorer/activity/), across every plan.

Because liveplanner works without signing in, a guest you hand a share link to can read and join the conversation too. Commenting needs a **Participant** or **Editor** share link: someone on a **Viewer** link can read the threads but not add to them, react, resolve them or delete anything.

A few things to know:

- **Comments aren't part of undo.** Pressing undo never takes back a comment, yours or anyone else's.
- **Owners can be emailed.** When someone comments on a plan you own, you can get an email, controlled by **Someone Comments on My Plan** in your [email notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/).
- **Comments stay with their card.** A card keeps its thread when it moves between columns or boards. A copy of a Sheet starts without its cell comments.

<Tip>
  Keep each comment to one point. A thread that asks a single, specific question is far easier to
  resolve than a long one covering five unrelated things.
</Tip>
