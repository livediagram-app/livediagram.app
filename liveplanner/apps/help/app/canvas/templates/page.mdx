An empty tab is freeing, but sometimes you would rather start with structure already in place. Templates give you a ready-made project as a starting point: boards with their columns and card types, Sheets with their formulas, dashboards that read every card, and believable example cards, so you can see how it runs before you add your own work.

## Starting from a Template

When you [create a new project](/new), it is made there and then, saved where new projects go (see [Default Folders](/help/explorer/folders/default-folders/)), and its empty tab opens on **Start Planning**: the **Recommended** templates first, then every template in groups beside them, with a search. Click a template and the tab fills with it, adding any further tabs the template brings; from there it is an ordinary project, to move, edit, add to and delete from as any other.

Every new tab, and every tab you empty, offers the same start. Close it with its **×** to begin from an empty tab; while the tab is still empty, **Show Templates** in the strip under the palette brings it back. Pressing the down arrow in the search box takes you to the first card it found.

## What's in the Catalogue

The templates are grouped by the job you are doing, in five categories listed in alphabetical order. A template lives in one category, and a few also appear in another where you would naturally look for them (an Invoice is under both **Finance** and **Spreadsheets**), so they are listed under each:

**Search templates...** finds a template by its name, its description, its category, or the everyday words people use for it: try "sprint", "retro", "invoice", "mortgage" or "crm". Every word you type has to match.

The blanks lead the **Popular** shelf and sit apart from the categories, since starting from nothing is not a kind of work: **Blank Plan** (an empty tab that opens on **Start Planning**, to pick the board or Sheet that fits the work), **Blank Spreadsheet** (a tab holding one empty Sheet, ready to type into), **Blank Form** (an empty [form](/help/canvas/plan-mode/forms/) for you to choose a board or a sheet for) and **Blank Tab** (an empty tab, the one an agent makes by name). Search for them, or pick them from **Blank Slate** on the liveplanner home page. After the blanks, Popular shows a few of the templates most people start from.

## What a Template Sets Up

Most templates set up a way of working across several tabs, with example cards to show how it runs: **Kanban Board**, **Project Planner**, **Bug Tracker**, **Team Retro**, **Weekly Planner**, **Content Calendar**, **Hiring Pipeline**, **OKRs**, **Product Launch** and **Feedback Board**. The spreadsheet templates fill one tab with a [Sheet](/help/canvas/plan-mode/sheets/) ready to use: **Budget Planner**, **Timesheet**, **Contact List**, **Task Tracker**, **Invoice**, **Expense Report**, **Loan Calculator**, **Sales Pipeline**, **Inventory**, **Guest List**, **Grade Book** and **Habit Tracker**. The form templates start a [form](/help/canvas/plan-mode/forms/) with its questions written.

Each one arrives as real boards, cards and sheets rather than a picture: move the cards, rename the columns, change the formulas. Nothing in a template is locked or special.

## Changing the Theme

A new project starts on the Default theme, and a template picked on a start takes the tab's current theme. Once you are on the canvas, click the **Theme & canvas** button in the bottom-right corner to open the **Tab Style** dialog and try a different [theme](/help/canvas/themes/), including any of your [custom themes](/help/canvas/themes/custom-themes/): it recolours the whole template at once.

## Making It Your Own

A template is just a head start, not a constraint. Everything in it is fully editable: rename the boards and columns, change the card types, restyle the whole thing with a different theme, or strip out the tabs you do not need. Because the project works the same as any other, you can also add [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/), invite others, and share it once you have shaped it into what you want.

<Tip>
  Templates pair naturally with themes: pick a template that fits your work, then try a few themes
  from the Tab Style dialog to find the look you want. Double-click a theme to apply it and close
  the dialog in one go.
</Tip>
