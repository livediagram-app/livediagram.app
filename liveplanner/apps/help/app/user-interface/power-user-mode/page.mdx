Once you know where everything is, the words that taught you start to take up room. **Power user mode** is one switch for that moment. Turn it on from [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/), under **Editor → Power User**.

## What it sets

Switching it on applies these settings once:

- Every tour under **Tours** (the Plan tours and the Forms tour): off, so none of them offers itself.
- **Minimal Chrome**: on (see below).

Panel opacity and UI scale are left as they are.

While the mode is on, its row in Settings shows **Minimal Chrome** beneath it, and a **Set By Power User Mode** list of the settings above with their current values. **Change** takes you to each one.

It is a starting point, not a lock. Change any of these afterwards and the mode stays on. Switch the mode off and every setting you left alone goes back to what it was before; the ones you changed keep your change.

## Quick light and dark

While power user mode is on, the appearance button in the bottom bar switches straight between light and dark: one click flips it. Right-click it (or focus it and press **Shift+F10**) to follow your device again. Outside the mode it steps through Light, Dark and System as usual.

Power user mode also draws the Explorer's Details view in dense rows, at half height.

## Minimal chrome

Minimal chrome is a setting that only appears while power user mode is on. It hides the words and hints that teach the interface:

- the text beside Search, Settings and the appearance button in the bottom bar;
- the "Tabs" label;
- panel titles and their **?** buttons (the Explorer's **⋯** menu gains a **Help** row instead);
- onboarding notices, such as the sign-in notice in the Explorer panel;
- in the full-page Explorer, the sidebar's section titles (they become thin dividers) and the words on plan badges (icons only).

The **Editing** / **Participating** / **Viewing** [role pill](/help/user-interface/title-bar/#the-role-pill) leaves the title bar and becomes a pencil, a ballot or an eye at the start of the bottom bar. Click it, as you would the pill, to preview your plan as a Participant, then read-only.

Every control stays. Hover one for a second, or focus it with the keyboard, and its name appears. Screen readers hear the same names as before.

<Tip>
  liveplanner offers power user mode once, after you have edited on 20 separate days or used 50
  keyboard shortcuts on this device. Say no thanks and it will not ask again; the switch is always
  in Settings. It isn't offered while In-Editor Notifications are off.
</Tip>
