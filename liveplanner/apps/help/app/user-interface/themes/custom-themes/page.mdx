The built-in catalogue covers a lot of ground, but sometimes you want your own palette: brand colours, or a house style for your team's boards. Custom themes let you author exactly that, save it, and reuse it. A saved custom theme behaves just like a built-in one: pick it on a tab and the tab takes its colours. Custom themes work for everyone who can edit the tab, signed in or not.

## Building a Theme

Open the **Tab Style** dialog from the **Style** button in the bottom-right corner of the editor, and press **Colours** under **Fine-tune**. In the **Colours** panel, page to the end of the theme list and choose the **New Theme** card to open the theme builder in place of the theme grid. (To start from a built-in theme instead, hover its card and press **Copy**: the builder opens with that theme's colours filled in.) The builder is fast by default and deep on demand:

<Step n={1}>
  Give the theme a name, then set the **Base colours**: **Background** for the area behind your
  work, **Fill** for surfaces such as a board and its cards, and **Stroke** for borders and accents.
  From just these three, liveplanner derives sensible defaults for everything else, so a usable
  theme exists after three clicks.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  **Text** sits beside them in the same row. On a new theme it follows the **Fill** colour, staying
  readable against it, until you set it yourself. A copied or edited theme keeps the text colour it
  already has.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  The **Pattern** section (open to start with) picks the backdrop pattern, static or animated, and
  sets its opacity. There is no pattern colour to pick: it tints itself from your background.
</Step>

A live preview across the top shows your in-progress theme, with the real pattern behind it, so you see the result before saving. Every colour box can also copy its colour and paste it into another, which saves retyping a brand colour. **Save theme** stores the theme and applies it to the active tab; **Cancel** (or **Back**) leaves without saving.

## Saving and Reusing

Custom themes belong to you, not to a project, so they are listed at the end of the theme list in the Tab Style dialog on every tab and every project you open. As a guest they are kept with your browser's guest identity, and if you sign up after creating themes as a guest, they carry over to your account.

Applying a custom theme saves its colours on the tab, so collaborators see the same look; only you see the theme itself in your theme list.

## Managing Your Themes

The [Explorer](/help/explorer/explorer-page/) has a **Themes** entry under **Library** in its sidebar where you can manage them all in one place (see [Themes Library](/help/explorer/themes-library/)). Each is shown as a preview card you can **Edit**, **Duplicate**, or **Delete**, beside a **New Theme** card. In the dialog's theme list, hovering one of your theme's cards offers Edit and Delete too.

## Editing and Deleting

Editing a theme opens the same builder again, and **Save changes** updates it and applies it to the active tab. Deleting one asks you to confirm first, and is safe for projects that still reference it: they fall back to the default look and keep rendering rather than breaking. If you delete a theme that the project you have open is using, the tabs using it revert to the default theme straight away so the change is visible.

<Tip>
  To tweak a built-in theme, press **Copy** on its card and edit from there. The shipped catalogue
  stays read-only, so your edits never touch the originals.
</Tip>
