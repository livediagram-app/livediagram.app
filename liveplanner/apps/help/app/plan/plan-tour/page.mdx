The Plan tour shows you around your own board, or your own spreadsheet. It works on the real editor rather than screenshots: it spotlights each part of your board or sheet with a highlight ring and a short explanation. It takes about a minute, and you can leave at any point.

## Starting the Tour

The tour needs something to show, so it's there when you're on a tab you can edit **with a board or a sheet open**: a Board tab or a Sheet tab. On a new tab, pick a board or a spreadsheet from **Start Planning** first; a Dashboard has its own tour (below).

Press Start Boards Tour or Start Sheets Tour in the bottom bar, just left of **Search**. On a phone the button shows only its play icon. The tour never starts on its own, and the button stays there after you've taken it, so you can press it again whenever you want another look. It isn't there on a locked tab, in an embed, or for a Participant or a Viewer.

When your tab has both a board and a sheet, the button names the one you haven't taken yet (boards first), and runs on the board or sheet you can see. Turning on **Power User Mode** in Settings marks them both as taken.

## What It Covers

### Boards

1. **Your Board**: a card on its own, about your board's columns, the stages work moves through. See [Boards](/help/plan/boards/).
2. **Add Cards**: adds an example card, **Your First Card**, to your first column, where **Add card** would put one.
3. **Move Work Along**: moves the example card to the next column, as you would by dragging it (or with **Shift** and an arrow key).
4. **The Card Panel**: opens the example card's panel: everything about one piece of work, saved as you type. See [Cards](/help/plan/cards/).
5. **The Board Header**: the widgets that count and filter the board, and a column's cog.
6. **Card Types**: the colours and fields of each kind of card. See [Card Types](/help/plan/card-types/).

### Spreadsheets

1. **Your Sheet**: a card on its own, about your sheet, a spreadsheet in your plan. See [Sheets](/help/plan/sheets/).
2. **Type Into Cells**: cell **A1**, selecting a cell and typing, with **Enter** and **Tab** to move on.
3. **Formulas**: the formula bar, which shows the selected cell's value or formula, and pointing at cells as you write one. See [Sheet Functions](/help/plan/sheet-functions/).
4. **The Toolbar**: its category switcher and the tools each category shows.
5. **Sheet Settings**: the cog with the sheet's title, CSV, gridlines, sizes, freeze and named ranges.

Each step has **Next**, **Back** and **Skip tour**. The last card, **You're ready to plan**, links to [Planning Basics](/help/plan/planning-basics/) (or [Sheets](/help/plan/sheets/) after the Spreadsheets tour) and ends the tour. The steps are the same on a phone.

### Dashboards

On a [dashboard](/help/plan/dashboards/) you can edit, the bottom bar's button reads Start Dashboards Tour: its rows of slots, filling a slot with **Add to Slot**, a slot's cog, and **Dashboard Settings**. Press it whenever you like; Dashboard Settings › **Show Me** runs the same tour.

## Your Work Is Left Alone

- The tour only points things out. The one thing it adds is the Boards tour's example card, which goes again when the tour ends, however it ends (even after a reload). It selects no cells and types nothing, so your board or sheet is as you left it.
- Nothing goes into your undo history.
- It shows the board or sheet filling the tab you're on.
- The one thing it opens is the example card's panel, which it closes again.
- If you switch tab, or the board or sheet is deleted mid-tour, it ends as skipped.

## Replaying the Tour

The quickest way is the bottom bar: press Start Boards Tour, Start Sheets Tour or Start Dashboards Tour again. Settings can start it too:

<Step n={1}>Open **Settings** from the editor and go to **Tours**.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Switch **Boards Tour** or **Sheets Tour** back on, or **Plan Tours** for all of them.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Close Settings. If you have a board or sheet open, the tour starts straight away; otherwise
  nothing happens, and the button is in the bottom bar once you do.
</Step>
