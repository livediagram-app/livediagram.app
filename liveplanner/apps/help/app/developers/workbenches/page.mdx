A **workbench** is a developer tool that shows a liveplanner project live beside a coding agent. You work in the editor right there (move cards, fill in a sheet, check a dashboard), everything reaches liveplanner at once, and when you type a message to your agent, the tool attaches what you had selected so the agent reads exactly that.

The editor in a workbench is signed in as you, for one project at a time. It has the project's tabs, its boards, sheets, dashboards and forms, and the controls around them; account settings, sharing and your project library stay in liveplanner itself, one click away through **Open in liveplanner**.

Building a tool of your own? [Building a Workbench](/help/developers/workbenches/building-a-workbench/) has the full integration contract.

## Pair a Workbench Once

A workbench opens your projects through your [CLI](/help/developers/cli/) sign-in, and only after you have allowed it. The first time, the tool asks you to approve it, or you can pair it yourself:

```bash
liveplanner workbench pair --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --name "Acme Editor"
```

The command prints a link and, in a terminal, opens it in your browser: a page headed **Allow this workbench?**, asking **Allow Acme Editor at `https://127.0.0.1:5175` to open your projects with the token liveplanner CLI?** Check the address: it is what liveplanner enforces, while the name is only a label. Choose **Allow**, and the command finishes by itself. **Don't allow** pairs nothing.

Pairing belongs to one CLI sign-in and one address. If someone obtained your token, it would still open nothing in a tool you never allowed.

## Open a Project

The workbench does this for you when you open a project in it. By hand:

```bash
liveplanner workbench open "Sprint 14" --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --json
```

It prints a link that works once, within a minute, and only inside the workbench at that address. Opened anywhere else, the page shows **This view opens only inside** the workbench's address and does nothing.

## Open Your Whole Workspace

A workbench can also keep your Explorer beside your code: Home, your folders, your teams' projects, the Inbox, Recent and the Trash, with every project opening in the same view and a **Back** to where you were. **New Project** makes the project right there. Your account stays out of it: no Settings, no API tokens, no team administration.

The whole workspace is a second approval, even for a tool you already paired:

```bash
liveplanner workbench pair --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --name "Acme Editor" --workspace
liveplanner folder ls
liveplanner workbench open --workspace --folder Minigames --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --json
```

The page asks **Allow Acme Editor at `https://127.0.0.1:5175` to open your whole workspace, and to create, move, share and trash projects and folders in it, with the token liveplanner CLI?** A token made read-only gives a workspace that browses and reads. `liveplanner folder create <name> [--in <folder>]` makes a folder for a project and prints its id.

## Talk to Your Agent About a Selection

Select something on the tab, such as a board or a sheet, then type your message. The workbench adds a short block to it, naming the project, the tab and what you selected, for example:

```text
[liveplanner] "Sprint 14" › tab "Board" (project 4cd7082b-…, tab 8bf8, rev 41)
selected: plan-board 0933
read: liveplanner tab view 4cd7082b-… --tab 8bf8 --view show --ref 0933
```

With nothing selected, it names the whole tab. The block is plain text in your message, nothing hidden. Your agent reads the tab with the CLI, and its changes appear in the editor as you watch; a change to the tab itself comes with an **Undo**.

## Stop or Unpair

A workbench session lasts a working day and renews itself while the tool is open. To stop a tool for good, go to **Settings**, then **API Tokens** under **Account**: each token lists its **Paired workbenches**, each with what it may open (**One project at a time** or **Whole workspace**), and **Unpair** turns their open projects read-only straight away. Revoking the token does the same for every tool it paired.
