# Team shared documents

The payoff [Teams](teams.md) deferred: every team gets a shared library — a folder tree plus documents — that all **joined** members can see and manage. It renders as a "Team documents" section on the team detail page in the Explorer. The concept is the personal folder tree ([Folders](folders.md)), just shared.

## Data model

The baseline migration, `0001_livedocuments_baseline.sql`, adds a nullable `team_id` to both tables:

- **`folders.team_id`** — non-null = a team folder. Personal reads/writes always filter `team_id IS NULL`, so the two trees can't bleed into each other. `owner_id` keeps the creator for audit, but authorisation is by team membership, not ownership.
- **`documents.team_id`** — non-null = the document lives in that team's library. Placement mirrors [Folders](folders.md): `folder_id NULL` = the root of the team's library (listed beside its root folders, not a row, can't be deleted), otherwise a folder of the same team.

A document is in exactly one place: personal tree or one team's library. Moving it into a team removes it from the owner's personal lists (`GET /api/documents` filters `team_id IS NULL`). A team document is managed by **every joined member**, not just its creator, so any joined member may move it: re-folder it within the team, move it to another team they belong to, or move it out into **their own** personal library. Moving it out transfers **ownership to the mover** (folders are owner-scoped, so the document follows them into the mover's tree); the owner moving it out keeps ownership. **Any joined member may also rename, duplicate, and delete a team document** (it belongs to the whole team): the api authorises the owner OR a joined member to `DELETE` it, but never a share-link visitor. A delete moves it to the **team Trash** ([Trash](trash.md)), which every joined member sees in Settings › Trash and may restore from or purge, with the same authority. Deleting the **team itself** re-homes its documents to the root of their owners' My Documents (members' work is never destroyed) and drops the team's folders.

## Access

Joined team membership grants **edit** on every document in the team's library, enforced at the same choke points share codes use:

- `canReadDocument` / `canEditDocument` accept the document's `teamId`: joined-member-of-team → share code. **Owning a team document grants nothing on its own**: its owner is a joined member while they are in the team, and once they leave or are removed the row must not stay open to them. The owner-only routes (`ownsDocument`: moving it out) likewise require the owner to still be a joined member. A member who leaves or is removed has their team documents and team folders handed to the remaining joined member account deletion would pick (the earliest admin, else the earliest member), so nothing the team keeps is owned by somebody who has gone. Their open realtime sessions on the team's documents end too: for each team document the api asks its room to close the sessions carrying that member's per-document person tag (close code 4005, "access changed"), so the editor reloads and meets the gates without them. The room still holds no account id; the person tag is a one-way hash of the document and the account and never reaches a client. Bounded to the team's 500 most recently saved documents, ten rooms at a time, off the response path. **Trust boundary:** for a team document the owner + membership legs are checked against the **verified Clerk user id** (`ctx.clerkUserId`), never the unsigned `X-Owner-Id` header — team owner/member ids are Clerk ids shared among teammates, so trusting the header would let a removed member forge access. Personal documents keep the hybrid `X-Owner-Id` guest path. All REST surfaces that gate on them (tabs, comments, images) inherit the rule.
- The WebSocket upgrade resolves `role = 'edit'` for a joined member of the document's team. The `o=` param was once trusted unverified (browsers can't set a Bearer on the upgrade), carrying the same forgeability the REST header fix above closed; the signed **room ticket** that was named here as the proper follow-up has since shipped — `POST /api/documents/<id>/room-ticket` mints a one-time ticket the client redeems on the upgrade (see [API app](../015-api/api.md)), and team members are admitted by ticket only. Saving still goes through the secured REST path: the room keeps ordering and collaboration state of its own ([API app → Realtime model](../015-api/api.md#realtime-model)).
- **The OWNER-ONLY surfaces obey the same trust boundary, via `ownsDocument`** (`apps/api/src/routes/context.ts`). The read/edit gates above were fixed for team documents; the owner-only guards beside them were not, and they front a strictly more dangerous set: `GET /api/documents/<id>/share` (which returns the **share password in the clear**), minting an edit-role share link, setting/clearing that password, `DELETE /api/documents/<id>` (and, until its removal on 2026-10-03, wiping a tab's change log). Each compared `resolveOwner() === ownerId` — the hybrid identity — so on a team document anyone holding the owner's Clerk id could reach all of them. `ownsDocument` is now the single answer to "is this caller the owner": **team document → verified account id only; personal document → the hybrid path** (safe there, where the owner id is an unguessable UUID). Four call sites route through it — `requireOwnedDocument` (which fronted the share family until every joined member could share, below), `DELETE /api/documents/:id` (via `managesDocument`), `POST /api/documents/:id/tabs/:tabId/link`, and the `isOwner` scope decision in `PUT /api/documents/:id/folder`. Any new owner-only gate uses it rather than comparing ids by hand; a bare `resolveOwner() === ownerId` is the bug, not the pattern.
- The ids that make this reachable are handed out on purpose: `GET /api/teams/<id>` lists `members[].userId`. That is the roster the Explorer and the assigned-actions picker need, so the fix is to stop the ids being _credentials_, not to hide them — here, and unconditionally at the header ([Public API and API tokens §4.1](../015-api/public-api-and-tokens.md#41-a-clerk-account-id-in-x-owner-id-is-refused-unconditionally)).
- `invited` (un-accepted) members get nothing, consistent with [Teams](teams.md).
- **Sharing belongs to every joined member.** The share-link family (`GET`/`POST`/`DELETE /api/documents/<id>/share`, revoking, rescoping and extending one link, and `PUT .../share-password`) is guarded by `requireManagedDocument`: the owner, or a joined member of the document's team on the verified account id (`managesDocument`, the same authority as delete and restore). A share-link visitor never passes, an Editor link included. The Share Link Created timeline event and the first-share email name the member who shared. In the editor, a team document opened without a share code shows **Share** (header button, the canvas menu, the Share Document command) to every joined member, never Make a Copy (`mayShareDocument` in `editor-page-helpers.ts`); a teammate who arrives on a share link is a visitor of that link and still gets Make a Copy.
- Share links and the share password otherwise work on team documents unchanged — they're orthogonal grants.

## API

- `GET /api/teams/:id/library` — `{ folders, documents }` for the team. Joined members only.
- `POST /api/folders` body gains `teamId`: creates a team folder (caller must be a joined member; `parentId` must be a folder of the same team). `PUT` / `DELETE /api/folders/:id` authorise team folders by joined membership instead of ownership. Deleting a team folder moves its documents and subfolders up to its parent, the team's root when it sat at the top ([Folders: Deleting a folder](folders.md#deleting-a-folder)).
- `POST /api/documents` creates a document straight into a team library when its body carries `teamId` (and optionally a `folderId` of that team), in the one write that creates it ([Folders → Placement on create](folders.md#placement-on-create)). The caller must be a joined member, by verified account id only; anything else is a named rejection (`team_forbidden`, `folder_not_found`, `folder_scope_mismatch`), never a document left at the root of the caller's My Documents.
- `PUT /api/documents/:id/folder` body gains `teamId`. Authorisation is by membership (a team document belongs to every joined member):
  - **Into a team / between teams** (`{teamId: T, folderId}`): the caller must be a joined member of T. A personal document can only be filed into a team by its owner; a team document can be moved on by any joined member of its current team. When a team document moves to another team whose joined members don't include its owner, **ownership transfers to the mover**, the same rule as moving it out to personal: otherwise someone outside the team would keep owner access (owner access is checked before membership) and be the only one able to share it. `folderId` must be T's folder or null.
  - **Within the current team**: any joined member may re-folder it.
  - **Out of a team** (`{teamId: null, folderId}`): any joined member may move it into their own personal library; `folderId` must be the **caller's** personal folder (or null for the root of their My Documents), and ownership transfers to the caller. When the owner moves it out, ownership is unchanged.
  - A purely personal move (no team on either side) stays owner-only. Folder/scope mismatches 404.

## Explorer UI

- **Team detail page** gains a "Team documents" card between the header and the member list: breadcrumb navigation through the team's folder tree, the team's root documents listed beside its root folders, a brand-filled **"New document"** action (in the "Create" menu alongside "New folder"; hands off to `/new?team=<id>&folder=<currentFolderId?>`, which creates the document straight into the team folder being browsed, in one write, never via the personal root), "New folder" / "New subfolder", folder rename / move / delete, and per-document actions available to **every joined member**: open, Rename, Duplicate (the copy is created beside the original, in the same team folder, by one create), "Change Folder" (within the team), and **Delete** (with a confirm). There is no "Remove from team" action. Breadcrumb crumbs keep the folder's own casing; only the root "Team documents" crumb uses the uppercase section-label style.
- **Move to folder picker**: one ownership-aware modal for every document (personal or team), built on the **shared placement browser** ([Folders](folders.md) + [Offline Mode](../006-document/offline-mode.md)): a space overview first (My Documents + each team), or, for a document already in a folder, that folder's level ([Save locations](../006-document/save-locations.md), "It opens where its selection is"), then that space's folder drill-down with a "here" card at every level and an inline New Folder tile. Picking a destination routes by scope: a personal folder files the document personally (moving a team document out transfers it to the mover, per the rules above), a team root/folder files it into that team. **Every document-move surface offers every space** — the /explorer page, the team page's own library, and the editor's floating Explorer panel (the panel's Current Document row includes team documents) — so a team document is never trapped: My Documents and the other teams are one Back away on the space overview. Only **folder** moves stay scoped (a folder can't change scope: personal folders re-parent within the personal tree, team folders within their team). The team page supports a `&folder=<id>` deep-link param (read at mount) so search results and links can open a specific team folder. Whatever the team page changes (a folder made, renamed, moved or deleted, a document re-filed, renamed, copied or deleted, a folder made from its picker in My Documents or another team) reaches the Explorer's own lists at once: the sidebar, the search panel and every other move picker show it without a reload.
- **Sidebar**: **My Documents** and each team are root-level folder rows in the Spaces group ([Explorer structure](explorer-structure.md)), rendered as the same collapsible folder tree (a team expands to reveal its folders; My Documents lists its root folders). The editor's Explorer team rows and its Current Document card give a team-library document a per-row **Delete** to any joined member (the api authorises owner-or-member delete). Fuller team-row management (rename, move, the full menu) stays on the /explorer + team pages. Because team rows aren't in the personal list the panel can't optimistically prune, the panel hides a just-deleted team row locally until the team-library sweep re-fetches.
- The personal space is named **My Documents**. (It was "My Work", then "Personal Space"; the help article moved from `explorer/my-work` to `explorer/personal-space`, which keeps its path.)
- **Editor header badge**: a document in a team library shows a brand-tinted **"Team"** title badge instead of "Private" (every joined member can open it, so "Private" would be a lie). Share links still win: a shared team document reads "Shared" as normal.
- **Recent documents** spans the personal library, every joined team's shared documents (swept lazily, one library fetch per team, via `useTeamLibrariesSweep`), AND documents shared with you ("Shared with you"), interleaved by recency and capped. Team rows show a brand-tinted **"Team"** visibility badge and a team menu (available to every joined member): Rename, Duplicate, "Change Folder" (the unified picker), "Open Team" (deep-links to the folder), and Delete. There is no "Remove from team". Shared-with-you rows show the **"Shared"** badge, the sharer as owner, open via the share link, and a "Dismiss" action. Recent and Shared-with-you show a desktop-only **Owner** column: "You" for personal rows, the team name for team rows, the sharer's display name for shared rows.

## Telemetry

`Team/Added/Document` (moved into a team), `Team/Removed/Document` (moved out), `Team/Moved/Document` (re-foldered within a team). Team folder CRUD reuses the `Folder` category with type `Team` (`Folder/Created/Team` …).

## Out of scope

- Per-member or per-folder permissions inside a team (every joined member can manage everything).
- Realtime presence indicators on the team library list.
- Team-level share links.

(Note: moving a team document out into a member's personal library does transfer ownership to that member — see the data model above. Ownership is never transferred _to_ a team as a collective entity; the document always has a single owner row.)
