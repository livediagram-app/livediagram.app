Every tab in livedocuments is a row of **pages**, made for building finished work: a poster, a one-pager, a set of social posts, an article or a short deck. Instead of an endless canvas you work on pages, sheets laid out in a row, each one a picture you build up and then export or present.

## Three Kinds of Page

- **Infographic**: a sheet to lay out freely, starting from a layout if you like. Everything on this page is about infographic pages unless it says otherwise.
- **Article**: a page to write on, like a document, its writing flowing onto new pages as it grows. See [Writing Articles](/help/pages/articles/).
- **Slide**: one slide of a deck, always landscape in a slide size (16:9, or the classic 4:3). It starts from its own **slide layouts** (Title Slide, Section Header, Title and Bullets, Two Columns, Image and Text, Statement, Thank You and more), has no portrait to turn to, and joins the slide deck in one press. See [Slide Pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/).

A tab's first page asks **What Is This Page For?** while it is the only page and still empty, and your answer is the kind of the whole tab: **every page of a tab is the same kind**. A slide deck holds only slides, an infographic only infographic pages, and an article tab only articles. For another kind, add a new tab.

## Starting From a Template

A new, empty tab opens on **Start Making Pages**: every Pages template, with **Recommended** first, then **Blank Pages**, then the templates grouped by what the document is for (**Reports**, **Plans and Proposals** and more), and a search. No page is drawn behind it until you choose. **Recommended** leads with **Blank Infographic**, **Blank Article** and **Blank Slide Deck**, each one empty page of that kind with nothing to choose, and the **Project Brief** template, one article page written as a short project brief, is under **Plans and Proposals** ([Templates](/help/canvas/templates/)). The **×** leaves one empty page, ready to ask what it is for. Deleting the last page empties the tab and brings Start Making Pages back.

## Content That Does Not Fit a Page

Open a tab whose content does not fit on its first page (an imported tab, say), and a page is made **around it, where it is**: nothing moves, shrinks or is scaled. The page is A4 when the content fits on it, and otherwise **Fit to Content**, a page exactly the size of the content plus a margin all round. A message says so, and **Undo** takes the page away. Content you later leave off the pages (in the space around them) stays where you put it.

**Split Into Pages** breaks a Fit to Content page into separate pages, from its page panel. Things joined by arrows, or sitting close together, stay together; each group gets a page of its own, A4 when it fits or Fit to Content when it does not, turned portrait or landscape to suit its shape, in reading order (top to bottom, then left to right). Each group moves onto its page at its own size, never scaled. At most 20 pages are made in one go; anything left over shares the last of them.

## Pages

- **Add a page** with the **+** after the last page. It adds another page like the last one, of the tab's kind (its tooltip says which, **Add Another Slide**, say). A new infographic page takes the last one's size and orientation, and every new page **carries over the background** of the page before it (its colour, gradient or picture, and its pattern), so a series keeps its look. It is the new page's own from then on. With no background to carry over, a new infographic page starts on a deep gradient in your theme's colours (the Theme Dusk gradient), and the slide of a new slide deck on the light grey Steel gradient; a new article page starts on plain paper. A tab holds up to 100 pages.
- **Each page's label** sits above its top-left corner: its name, or "Page 2", then its size ("A4 · Portrait", "Square", "Story (9:16)"), then "Article" or "Slide" on those kinds of page. An infographic page, the plain page, leaves its kind off, so its title bar keeps the room for its buttons. **Press the label** to fit that page to the screen.
- **Drag a label sideways to reorder the pages.** A blue bar in the gap shows where the page will land; let go and it moves there with everything on it. **Escape** cancels.
- **Content belongs to its page.** Anything whose centre is on a page moves with it when the pages around it change, and whatever hangs off a page's edge is cut off at the edge, on screen and in exports, as if the pages were the only paper.
- **Arrows stay on one page.** An arrow's end pins to an element only when that element is on the same page as the arrow's other end; elements on another page show no connection points, so the end stays free where you drop it.
- **The page navigator** in the bottom-right corner, beside Undo and Redo (once there are two or more pages), shows "2 of 5" between **Previous page** and **Next page**, each fitting that page to the screen.
- **The arrow keys** do the same with nothing selected: **Right** or **Down** for the next page, **Left** or **Up** for the previous one.
- **The view always lands on a page.** Pan and zoom freely, and when you let go the view glides onto the page you are on, never stuck between pages or lost off the end of the row. A short swipe or flick turns to the next or previous page, like a slide deck. Zoom out past a whole page and it springs back to fit; zoom in as far as you like and it stays zoomed in, just kept on the page.
- **Back where you left off.** Come back to a tab of pages, from another tab or after a reload, and it opens on the page you were last on rather than the first. Each person's place is their own, kept in this browser.
- **Snapping:** move or resize an element and it snaps to its page's edges, centre lines and margins, with the same guides as lining up with other elements.
- **The title bar** above each page holds its buttons, ending in the padlock and the cog: see [Beside the Cog](#beside-the-cog).
- In [Zen mode](/help/tools/zen-mode/) and while presenting, the pages show on their own, without labels, cogs or buttons.

## Contents

**Contents** lists every page in the tab, each with a picture of the page as it is, its number and its name. The page you are looking at is highlighted.

On a computer, once a tab has more than one page, Contents sits as a sidebar against the left edge of the canvas, the way a slide editor shows its slides, and keeps highlighting the page you are on as you move around. Drag it by its title to pin it to the right edge instead. Press **Minimise** at its top to tuck it into a **Contents** button in the bottom-right corner, beside the theme button; press that button for the same list, and **Dock as Sidebar** at its top to bring the sidebar back. livedocuments remembers which you chose, on every document and every device you sign in on (and which side, on this device). You can also choose in **Settings › Editor › Pages**. On a phone, Contents is always the button.

- **Go to a page**: press its row, or move with the arrow keys and press Enter. The canvas glides there. On a computer the list stays open so you can hop around, and the page lands beside the sidebar rather than under it; on a phone it closes so you can see the page.
- **Fit**: **Fit** in the bottom-right corner fits the page you are nearest, beside the sidebar.
- **Find a page**: with more than six pages, type in **Find a Page** to narrow the list by a page's name, kind or number, or by any words on it.
- **Page actions**: in the sidebar, rest the pointer on a page and its tools appear down its left, under its number: the grip to drag it, **Delete**, and **⋯** for its menu. In the popover the **⋯** sits under the number and **Delete** is in the menu. The menu (a right-click on the row, or a long press on a phone, opens it too) holds **Page Settings**, the page's place in the slide deck (**Add to Slides**, **Hide in Slides** or **Show in Slides**), **Duplicate**, **Move Up**, **Move Down**, **Lock** or **Unlock**, and **Delete** last. A page hidden in Slides shows a crossed eye beside its name.
- **Add a page**: at the foot, **Add Page** adds another page like the last one, of the tab's kind (its tooltip says which, **Add Another Slide**, say). The new page lands after the last one and the list marks it.

Anyone can open Contents to find their way round. Only Editors see the page actions and the add buttons.

## Beside the Cog

An infographic or slide page's title bar, above its top-right corner, holds the buttons you reach for most, so you rarely need its panel:

- **Layout** opens the page's layouts in a popover: press a category, hover a layout to preview it on the page, press it to place it. It is there on every page, empty or not, and placing a layout replaces what is on the page at once (one **Undo** brings it back). A slide page offers the [slide layouts](/help/pages/slide-pages/#slide-layouts). See [Infographic Layouts](/help/pages/infographic-layouts/).
- **Tidy** (the broom, just left of the padlock and the cog, on infographic and slide pages while there is something to straighten) straightens the page in one press: it nudges anything poking past the margins back inside, lines up edges that nearly line up, evens out gaps that are nearly even, and settles the rest on a fine grid. It keeps your layout, never moves anything far, never makes things overlap, and leaves locked and turned elements where they are. Hover it to see the tidied page first; a page that is already tidy says so and nothing changes.
- **Background** opens the page's backgrounds and patterns in a popover, with the same hover previews as the panel (see [Backgrounds and Patterns](#backgrounds-and-patterns)). It stays open for another pick.
- The **padlock** locks the page ([Locking a Page](#locking-a-page)), and the **cog** opens the page panel.

While the page is wide enough on screen, each button shows its name beside its icon; zoom out, or use a phone, and they drop to their icons (hover one for its name). On a phone, Layout and Background open as a sheet from the bottom of the screen. A locked page shows no Layout, Tidy Up or Background button. Only Editors see these buttons: Participants and Viewers see the pages without them.

An article page keeps its background in its panel.

## The Page Panel

Press the cog above a page's top-right corner to open its panel, which opens beside the page so the page stays in view (on a phone it slides up from the bottom of the screen). At the top is the page's **name** (leave it empty to go back to "Page 2"). Below it, what the page offers:

- An **infographic** page: its size and orientation. Its layouts and background are on the **Layout** and **Background** buttons beside the cog.
- A **slide** page: the **Page** and **Background** tabs. Its layouts are on the **Layout** button.
- An **article** page: the **Page**, **Background**, **Style** and **Text** tabs (see [Writing Articles](/help/pages/articles/)).

Along the bottom are the page's actions.

### Size and Orientation

The page's settings start with the **Size** tiles, each drawn to scale:

| Size   | For                                                         |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| A4     | Print, the default                                          |
| Letter | US Letter, for print in North America                       |
| A3     | Posters                                                     |
| Square | Square social posts                                         |
| Post   | Portrait posts for Instagram and LinkedIn (4:5)             |
| Story  | Stories (9:16); turned landscape it reads Widescreen (16:9) |
| Fit    | Fit to Content: sized around the content it was made for    |

**Fit** shows only on a page already in it, a page made around content that did not fit (see [Content That Does Not Fit a Page](#content-that-does-not-fit-a-page)); choose another size and it is gone for good. Slide sizes belong to slide pages: a deck is made of [slide pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/), which offer only **Slide** (16:9) and **Classic** (4:3), and no orientation. (An infographic page made before this, still on the Slide size, keeps that tile until you move it off.)

Below them, **Portrait** and **Landscape** turn the page (a square page, a slide size and Fit to Content have no orientation). Changing a page's size or orientation keeps everything on it: content that still fits stays as it is, and content that no longer fits is scaled down as one to fit inside the margins, its text with it (the words in a shape shrink with the shape, so a button's label still fits its button). Nothing is cut off.

### Backgrounds and Patterns

Every kind of page has the same backgrounds, sorted into **Solid**, **Gradient** and **Image** at the top of the backgrounds; they open on the kind the page has now. An infographic page keeps them on the **Background** button beside its cog; a slide page has that button and its panel's **Background** tab; an article page has the **Background** tab.

- **Solid**: **Paper** (the plain page) leading your tab theme's colours, then a row of light colours and a row of dark ones (the same colours every colour picker in livedocuments offers), the colours already used in the document, and **Add a custom colour** (four coloured dots) for a **custom colour** of your own.
- **Gradient**: a **Theme** group of fourteen gradients drawn from your tab's theme colours, light ones first, then bold and deep ones (change the tab's theme and these follow it), then a **Gradients** group of thirteen presets, from Sunrise and Peach to Night, Forest and Ember, plus the rainbow swatch for a **custom gradient**: pick its **From** and **To** colours from the same colours, turn its **Angle**, or **Swap** the two.
- **Image**: a photo covering the whole page. Choose one from your images, or **Upload an Image** of your own. Over the picture, an **Overlay** keeps your content readable: **None** leaves it bare, **Light** or **Dark** washes it, **Tint** washes it in your theme's colour, and **Fade** fades it to white towards the bottom; **Strength** sets how much, and a new picture starts with a 60% wash. **Position** chooses which part of a tall picture stays in view (**Top**, **Centre** or **Bottom**). To change it, choose or upload again under **Replace Image**; **Remove** puts the page back on plain paper.
- **Pattern**, over any of them: **None**, **Dots**, **Grid** or **Lines**, drawn faintly so they never fight the content.

**Hover a swatch to preview it** on the page; press it to keep it. A dark background keeps the page readable: elements without colours of their own are drawn in light ink on it, and text, arrows and icons you coloured yourself are lightened if they would no longer stand out (or darkened again on a light page). On a bright or mid-tone colour, such as teal or yellow, icons, progress bars and outlines are lightened or darkened the same way, keeping their hue, and charts adjust their colours as they are drawn.

### Page Actions

Along the bottom of the panel: **Split Into Pages** (on a Fit to Content page only, see above), **Duplicate page** (a copy right after it, with everything on it), **Move page left**, **Move page right** and **Delete page** (the page and everything on it; the pages after it close the gap, and the view glides to the page before it so you are never left looking at empty canvas). On an article page the same four act on the whole article. Each one is a single step that **Undo** reverses.

## Locking a Page

Press the padlock beside a page's cog to **lock** it; it reads **Locked** while it is. A locked page, of any kind, stays as it is:

- What is on it can't be selected, moved, changed or deleted, and nothing new can be drawn, dropped or pasted onto it (a short notice says why).
- Its name, size, orientation, background and layout can't change, and it can't be deleted. You can still move it along the row or duplicate it (the copy is unlocked).
- Locking a page of an article makes the article's writing read-only.

Press the padlock again to unlock it. The lock is part of the document, so everyone you share it with sees the page locked too.

## Good to Know

- Everything else works as always: sharing, live collaboration, comments, undo and redo. Collaborators see your pages and their changes live.
- People who can only view a tab see the pages but not their cogs, buttons or the **+**.
