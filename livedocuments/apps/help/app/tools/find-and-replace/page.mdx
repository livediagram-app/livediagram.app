**Find** looks for a word or phrase on every page of the tab you are on: the writing of its articles and the words on its elements. Every match is highlighted where it sits, a count says how many there are, and the page turns to the one you are on. **Replace** swaps the match in hand, or every match at once, for other words.

## Opening Find

- Press **⌘F** (**Ctrl+F** on Windows and Linux) while you are working on the document: on the canvas, with something selected, or writing in an article. Words you have selected in the writing become the search.
- Press **⌘⇧H** (**Ctrl+Shift+H**) to open it with the Replace row showing.
- On a touch screen, open the [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/) and run **Find in Tab**, or **Find and Replace**.

Anywhere the editor does not have the keyboard (typing a comment, in a dialog, while a deck presents, or in an embed), ⌘F is your browser's own find, as usual.

The **Find bar** opens at the top right of the canvas, the caret in its field. On a phone it runs across the top of the canvas instead.

## Finding

Type in the **Find** field and the matches light up a moment after you stop typing:

- **In the writing**, each match is tinted yellow, and the match in hand is a stronger orange.
- **On an element**, a dashed ring goes round each element holding a match, and a solid ring round the one holding the match in hand.

The count sits at the end of the field: **3 of 12**, or **No Results**. Press **Enter** or **Next Match** for the next one, and **Shift+Enter** or **Previous Match** for the one before; both wrap round at the ends. A match on another page brings that whole page into view (on a flashcard, it turns to that card), and the keyboard stays in the field so you can keep stepping.

Two options sit beside the field:

- **Match Case** (**Aa**, or **Alt+C**): only matches with the same capital letters.
- **Whole Word** (**ab**, or **Alt+W**): only whole words, so "plan" no longer finds "planning".

The search follows the tab as it changes: if you or a collaborator edit it, the matches update and the one in hand stays where it was.

## What Find Searches

- Every page of the tab, in page order: on each page, the article writing that starts on it, then the words on its elements, top to bottom.
- An article's paragraphs, headings, list items, code and table cells.
- An element's label, its title and subtitle, its code, the cells of a table and the items of a checklist.

It never searches notes, comments, alt text, link addresses or chart data, and it searches only the tab you are on. A search is plain text, never a pattern: every character means itself. Up to 5,000 matches are counted and highlighted; past that the count reads **5,000+**.

## Replacing

Press the chevron at the left of the bar (**Show Replace**) for the Replace row, type the new words in **Replace With**, then:

- **Replace** swaps the match in hand and moves on to the next one. Press **Enter** in the Replace With field to do the same.
- **Replace All** swaps every match at once (**⌘Enter** in the Replace With field). A message says how many were replaced.

Replacing keeps the formatting: the new words take the formatting of the first letter they replace, so bold stays bold and a link stays a link. An empty Replace With deletes the matches. A **Replace All** is one step, so one **Undo** brings every match back. Collaborators see the change land at once.

## Who Can Find and Replace

- **Editors** find and replace anywhere.
- **Participants** find everywhere, but replace only in text they can write: an article's writing and the words of elements (labels, table cells, a card's title and subtitle, code). Anything else they find, such as a checklist's items, is shown in grey and passed over, and the bar says how many can't be replaced.
- **Viewers** can find but not replace: the bar has no chevron and no Replace row.

Nobody replaces text on a locked tab, page, layer or element: those matches are found and shown, but skipped, and **Replace** says "This text is locked".

## Closing Find

Press **Escape** or **Close Find**. The highlights go and the keyboard goes back to the canvas. The bar remembers your search and options while the document is open, so the next ⌘F picks up where you left off. Switching tabs keeps the bar open and searches the new tab.
