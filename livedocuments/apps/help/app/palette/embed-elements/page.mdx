An **Embed** puts someone else's page on your canvas: a video that plays where it sits, a Figma file, a Google Doc, or any website at all.

| Service         | Links that work                                                                                      |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **YouTube**     | watch, share (`youtu.be`), Shorts, live and embed links                                              |
| **Vimeo**       | a `vimeo.com` video link                                                                             |
| **Loom**        | a share or embed link                                                                                |
| **Figma**       | any file, prototype or board link                                                                    |
| **Google Docs** | a Doc, Sheet or Slides link you have shared                                                          |
| **Website**     | any `http` or `https` address, for anything not in the list above                                    |
| **Live Embed**  | not a link: a file from our apps, chosen from your files ([below](#live-embed-a-file-from-our-apps)) |

Pick **Website** when the page you want isn't one of the named services. It
frames the address exactly as you type it. Be aware that plenty of sites refuse
to be shown inside another page (news sites and banks especially): when one
does, the embed comes up blank. A running website embed always carries an
**open in a new tab** control for exactly that case, and a
Link Card is often the better tool from the start. See [Website embeds](/help/palette/embed-elements/website/) for the
detail.

## Adding one

Embeds live in the palette's **Media** category, each as its own card in the **Embeds** group: open Media and pick the service you want.

<Step n={1}>Open **Media** and pick a service from the **Embeds** group.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Double-click the card and paste the link. It tells you straight away if it can't embed that
  address.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>Press play, or **Load embed**, when you want it running.</Step>

**The tile you picked is only a hint.** It names the empty card ("Add a Figma link") and the dialog, and the link is what actually decides: paste a Vimeo URL into a card you made from the Figma tile and you get Vimeo, because refusing a link that plainly works would be pedantry. There is no separate URL field to keep in step either: double-clicking the card is how you change its address. A link the card can't use leaves it reading "Can't embed that link", with the address beneath.

The link dialog is deliberately narrow: it takes only a **web address**, never a tab, because a video pointed at a tab has nothing to play. It checks the address as you type and again when you save. To show another of your files instead, use a [Live Embed](#live-embed-a-file-from-our-apps).

**Embed cards keep a 16:9 shape** as you resize them, for the same reason an icon is locked square: a letterboxed or stretched video frame is never what anyone wanted. And like images and stickies, an embed **keeps its own look across [themes](/help/canvas/themes/)**: somebody else's page is not yours to recolour.

## Nothing loads until you ask

An embed does not start on its own. A YouTube card shows the video's poster frame and waits for you to press play; every other service shows a card with its name and a **Load embed** button.

<Figure caption="Before anything loads: YouTube shows its poster and a play button, every other service a named card with Load embed.">
  <EmbedCardsIdle />
</Figure>

That is deliberate, and not only for tidiness. A canvas with eight videos would otherwise open eight players every time anyone looked at it, and simply opening a document would report you to those services. YouTube embeds play through **youtube-nocookie.com**, which sets nothing until playback actually starts.

Loading is per person: pressing play starts the embed on your screen, not on everyone else's. Changing the link stops anything that was playing.

## Keeping the canvas usable

An embedded page would normally swallow every click inside its rectangle, which would leave a hole in your canvas that you could not drag or select. So the player **ignores the pointer by default**, even while it is playing: you can always drag an embed around.

When you want to use the player itself, hover it: two small buttons appear in its top-left corner while it runs.

- The pointer button (**Use the player controls**) hands the pointer over so you can seek, change the volume or go fullscreen. Press it again (**Lock the player (drag the video)**) to get dragging back.
- The square (**Stop video**) closes the player and returns to the card. This is also how you pause, since pausing means clicking the player.

<Figure caption="While an embed runs, hover it for the two controls top-left; the link badge top-right opens the original.">
  <EmbedPlayerControls />
</Figure>

<Note>
  The link badge in the corner still opens the original page in a new tab. Playing something here
  and opening it at the source are different intentions, and both are worth having.
</Note>

## Live Embed: a file from our apps

The **Live Embed** card, last in the **Embeds** group, shows a canvas, plan, page or design from livediagram, liveplanner, livedocuments or livedesign, live and read-only. It follows every change the file's people make, as they make it.

<Step n={1}>Add **Live Embed** from the **Embeds** group, then double-click the card.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  In **Choose a File**, pick one of your **Connected** files or a **Recent** one (**Find a file**
  searches them), or paste one of those apps' share links under **Paste a Share Link**.
</Step>

Double-click it again to choose another file. Only an Editor of this document can choose one, and not while the card is locked.

- **Everyone sees it with their own access.** Someone who can open the file sees it live. Anyone else sees a blurred picture of it, "You don't have access to" the file, and **Open in** its app. Choosing a file never shares it with anyone.
- **Hover it** for **Use the file**, which hands it the pointer so you can scroll and click inside it (**Done using the file** gives dragging back), and **Open in** its app, which opens the file in a new tab.
- **It runs only on screen.** A live embed starts once it is in view, and at most three run on a page at once; the rest show their last picture until one leaves the screen.
- **Exports draw its picture**, as do thumbnails, so an image or PDF of the page shows the file as it last looked.
