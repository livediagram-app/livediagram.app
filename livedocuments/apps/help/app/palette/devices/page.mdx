The **Devices** row, in the palette's **Components** category, holds device frames for screenshots and UI mockups. Each is a realistic outline of a screen you can build a design inside.

## Finding them

Rest on **Components** on the palette strip, or click it, then rest on or click its **Devices** row: the frames open in a second panel beside it, the desktop frames, then the mobile ones. Tap a frame to add it, or drag it to where you want it. Search finds each frame by name too ("phone", "browser"), in the palette strip's [Search](/help/palette/toolbar-layout/#search) and the editor's [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/).

## What the row contains

- **Browser** for a desktop web page, with window dots, back, forward and reload, and an address bar.
- **Monitor** for a full-screen desktop layout, on a stand.
- **Laptop** for a portable-screen view.
- **Phone** for a mobile app or page.
- **Tablet** for a larger touch layout.
- **Foldable** for a book-style phone, opened out to its inner screen with the fold down the middle.
- **Watch** for a compact smartwatch screen.

Frames pick up the tab theme's colours when you drop them, like any other shape.

## How to use a device frame

Drop a frame onto the canvas, then build your mockup inside or on top of it using shapes, text, images, and icons. The frame gives your design a true-to-life boundary so the proportions read correctly to anyone reviewing the work. Sketch a flow across several phone frames to show a journey, or pair a monitor with a phone to show a responsive layout side by side.

A device frame is an ordinary element, not a container: dragging it does not carry what you have drawn on it.

<Tip>
  Draw a [stack](/help/palette/stacks/) around your finished mockup and its device, so the screen
  and its contents move together when you rearrange the canvas. The stack is in the Components
  category.
</Tip>
