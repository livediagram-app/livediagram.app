A deck is built in **Contents**, the list of your pages down the left of the canvas (or behind the **Contents** button in the bottom-right corner when you have tucked it away). Every [slide page](/help/pages/slide-pages/) is in the deck already. Everything else you do to a slide is on its page's **⋯** menu in Contents: rest the pointer on a row and press **⋯**, or right-click the row.

Building a deck needs edit access: on a view-only or participant link the rows have no menu. Presenting doesn't: the **Present** button in the top bar is there for everyone on the document.

## Adding a page

An infographic or article page is not in the deck until you add it. Open its **⋯** menu and press **Add to Slides**: the page joins the show in its place in the page order, and from then on it is a slide like any other.

## Hiding a slide from the run

On a page that is in the deck, the menu offers **Hide in Slides**, and on a hidden one **Show in Slides**.

Hiding is a different idea from deleting. It is for the slide you might want next week, the backup detail for a question you may not get, the section you cut for time: the page stays, and so does its slide, it just doesn't run. Contents sets a hidden slide's picture aside under a **Hidden** veil, so you can always see it is there.

Hidden slides are skipped everywhere at once: the count on the **Present** button, the `7 / 23` in the presenter's display, the jump list, and what advancing lands on all leave them out, so nothing can disagree about how long the deck is.

## Presenting from a slide

**Present From Here**, on a slide's menu, starts the show at that slide, so you can rehearse the second half without clicking through the first.

## Ordering the deck

**The deck runs in Contents' order.** Drag a row up or down the list to move its page, and its slide moves with it. The list does not reshuffle while you drag: a caret shows where the row will land, and the move happens when you let go. Each tab presents on its own: **Present** runs the tab you are on, so a talk lives on one tab and another tab can hold another talk.

## Speaker notes

Each slide page's title bar has **Notes**. Press it to write what you mean to _say_ over this slide, as note cards, one point a card. Press **Add First Card** and type; `⌘↵` (`Ctrl+Enter`) starts the next card, and `Esc` or **Done** closes the one you are writing. Drag a card by its grip, or press **Move Up** or **Move Down** (`Alt+↑` / `Alt+↓`), to change the order; the bin removes one, with **Undo** for a few seconds. A slide holds up to 20 cards.

Notes belong to the slide, not to anything on it. Each card is plain text with its speaking time in the corner, since it is a script you read off rather than something anybody needs to bold, and during the presentation they stay hidden until you ask for them.

<Tip>
  Build the deck in the order you will speak, then walk it once with Present From Here on each slide
  you are unsure of. You are checking that every slide makes sense on its own, which is the one
  thing the list cannot tell you.
</Tip>
