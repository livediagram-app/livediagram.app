Select one or more shapes, arrows or text elements and a small **Quick Style** bar appears above the page, just left of the page's own buttons (Style, Layout, Background and the cog). It holds the handful of style choices people make most often, so restyling is a click or two rather than a trip through the right-click menu. The right-click menu is still the home of every setting; the bar only repeats the favourites.

## What's on it

Each button shows its current value (a colour chip, or the line or alignment in use) and opens its choices just below it. Pick one and it applies straight away; the choices stay open so you can try another. Press outside, press **Esc** or press the button again to close them.

- **Stroke**: seven colours for the outline or line, then **Ink**. The first is the theme's own default.
- **Fill**: seven colours for a shape's inside, then **Transparent**.
- **Text**, for text elements: seven colours for the words, then **Ink**. The first is the theme's own text colour.
- Every colour row ends with **More colours**, which opens the full colour picker: the row's theme colours, the same ten colours every picker offers, the colours already used in this document, and **Add a custom colour** (four coloured dots) for any colour of your own. (A shape's label colour is in the right-click menu's Colours section.)
- **Width**: thin, medium or thick.
- **Line**: solid, dashed or dotted for shapes. For arrows it is solid, dashed or **flowing**, a dashed line that marches along its direction, so an animated connector is a single click. A selection mixing shapes and arrows gets the shape set.
- **Align**: left, centre or right. It shows once a shape has a label to align.
- **Icon**, when a shape carries an icon: before, above or after its label.

The buttons name themselves while the page has room on screen, and drop to their icons when you zoom out, just as the page's own buttons do. When the selected elements don't agree on a value, its button shows a split chip or a faded glyph.

A section only shows when something in the selection can use it, so an arrow on its own shows stroke, width and style, a text element on its own shows text colour, and a shape with an icon gains icon alignment. Sticky notes, tables, images and other non-shape elements are not styled here; select only those and the bar stays away. The bar sits on the page holding most of the selection.

## Colours that follow the theme

The seven colours come from the tab's theme. A multi-colour theme offers its own six branch colours; any other theme offers red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet in its own tone, so a muted theme gets muted colours. Backgrounds always keep the label readable, and text colours are always dark enough (or, on a dark canvas, light enough) to read.

Switch the theme later and colours picked here move with it: a shape you made green stays green, in the new theme's green.

**Ink** is different: it is the same drawing colour on every theme, dark on a light canvas and light on a dark one.

## Your own colours

Right-click any of the six theme colours in a row (or focus it and press **Shift+F10** or the context-menu key) to open the custom colour picker. Press **Use** and the colour is saved into that swatch, replacing the theme's colour there, and a small dot in its corner marks it as yours. Screen readers and the tooltip name it too, for example "Custom orange, in place of Green".

Picking a colour only changes the swatch; click it afterwards to use it. **Clear override** in the same popover brings the theme's colour back, and anything you already painted keeps its colour. Your swatches belong to you and to the theme you made them in: they follow you to other documents and devices, and switching to another theme shows that theme's own colours (and any you changed there). The first swatch, the theme default, always stays the theme's, and so does Ink. A custom colour does not follow later theme changes. For colours a whole document should share, pick a [theme](/help/canvas/themes/).

## Styling many at once

Select several shapes and arrows, even of different kinds, and a choice applies to every one that supports it. A highlighted option means every selected element shares it; when they differ, nothing is highlighted. Locked elements are left alone. Each choice is one undo step.

## The next shape remembers

When you change a shape's colours, width, style or alignment, from this bar or from the right-click menu, the next shape **of the same kind** you draw arrives the same way. Make a circle green and the next circle is green; the next square is not. Arrows remember their own style, separately from shapes, and text elements remember their colour. Picking the theme default again forgets that choice.

This only applies to shapes and arrows you draw yourself, from the palette, a tool or a quick-connect plus. Pasted, duplicated and templated elements keep their own looks. The memory belongs to the document and stays in this browser.

## Back to the theme's look

To put an element back to the theme's look, pick the theme's own choice in each row: the first colour, **Medium** width and a **solid** line. The next shape of the same kind then arrives plain again.

## Turning it off

Open **Settings**, then **Panels**, **Quick Style**, and switch off **Enable Quick Style Bar**. Every style is still in the element's right-click menu, and the next shape still remembers its style. See [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/).

## On a phone

There is no room for the bar on a phone, so its choices join the page's **Appearance** sheet: select something, tap **Appearance** above the page, and under **Selected Element** there is a row for each choice that applies (Stroke, Fill, Width and so on), each showing its current value. Tap one to open its choices in a sheet of their own. The long-press menu has every option too.

## Good to know

- It hides while a right-click menu is open, and stands aside in zen mode, in split view, while presenting, for Viewers and Participants, and on a locked page or tab.
- Every button and option has a name for screen readers and a tooltip; each row of choices is a group you move through with the arrow keys.
