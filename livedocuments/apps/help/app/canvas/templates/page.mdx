A blank page is freeing, but sometimes you would rather start with structure already in place. Templates give you a ready-made document as a starting point, filled with believable example content, so you can begin from something shaped roughly like what you need and adapt it, instead of building every page from nothing.

## Starting from a Template

When you [create a new document](/new), it is made there and then, saved where new documents go (see [Default Folders](/help/explorer/folders/default-folders/)), and its empty tab opens on **Start Making Pages**: **Blank Pages** first, one empty page of each kind, then every template in the same categories as below, grouped by what the document is for, with a search. Click a template and the tab fills with its pages; from there it is an ordinary document, to move, edit, add to and delete from as on any tab.

Every new tab, and every tab you empty, offers the same start. It stays until you choose something: it has no **×**, and Escape or a click outside it does not close it. To begin from nothing, choose a blank from **Blank Pages**, the first group, and the tab gets one empty page of that kind. A document opened as a blank (**Start Blank**, or a blank's link) skips the start; while its tab is still empty, **Show Templates** in the strip under the palette brings it back.

The search at the top of the start, **Find a template**, finds a template by its name, its description, its category, or the everyday words people use for it: try "powerpoint", "cv", "minutes", "flyer", "school" or "rfc". Every word you type has to match, and pressing the down arrow in the search box takes you to the first card it found.

After each category's templates come up to three documents from the [Community](/help/collaboration/sharing/finding-community-documents/), marked **Community**: the ones people liked most in the past month (each with at least five likes in that time), with who made them. Choose one to make your own copy of it, which opens as a new document of yours. A search finds them by their title, after the templates. With no Community documents that popular yet, or with the Community turned off, the start shows its templates alone.

## What's in the Catalogue

The templates are grouped by what the document is for, in ten categories. A few are in two, where you might look for them in either:

| Category      | Templates                                                                                     |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Careers**   | Résumé, Cover Letter                                                                          |
| **Events**    | Event Poster, Group Card, Wedding Toast                                                       |
| **Home**      | Recipe Card, Floor Plan                                                                       |
| **Learning**  | Lesson Plan, Class Lesson, School Presentation, How It Works                                  |
| **Marketing** | Social Carousel, Versus, How It Works, Storyboard, Event Poster                               |
| **Pitches**   | Slide Deck, Launch Keynote, Product Pitch                                                     |
| **Plans**     | [Project Brief](/help/pages/articles/), Working Backwards, Business Proposal, Design Doc      |
| **Reports**   | Year in Review, Quarterly Review, Data Story, Status Report                                   |
| **Talks**     | [Conference Talk, Wedding Toast](/help/pages/flashcards/), Product Pitch, School Presentation |
| **Teams**     | Meeting Notes, Workshop Kickoff, Team Welcome                                                 |

**Launch Keynote**, **Quarterly Review**, **Team Welcome**, **Workshop Kickoff** and **Class Lesson** are decks built from the [slide layouts](/help/pages/slide-pages/), each opening and closing on an animated background. **Working Backwards** is Amazon's PR/FAQ, written as an article.

**Meeting Notes**, **Lesson Plan**, **Business Proposal**, **Design Doc**, **Status Report** and **Cover Letter** are [articles](/help/pages/articles/) written out as the real thing, so you can see what an article holds: tables for the attendees, the timings or the prices, a table of contents in the proposal and the design doc, a code block in the design doc, and headers and footers with the date, the document's name and "Page 2 of 4". Retype the words and keep the shape, or export the finished document to Word.

The blanks sit apart from the categories, since starting from nothing is not a kind of page. **Blank Illustration** is one empty page that asks what it is for; it is what **Start Blank** gives you, and it is not offered on the start, where **Blank Infographic** is the same page. The others start on one empty page of a kind you already know: **Blank Slide Deck**, **Blank Article**, **Blank Infographic** and **Blank Flashcards**. Find them in **Blank Pages** on **Start Making Pages**, search for them, or pick them from **Blank Slate** on the livedocuments home page. **Blank Tab**, an empty tab with one page that asks what it is for, is made by name, from an AI agent or a link, rather than offered on the start.

Each one arrives as real elements rather than a picture: a slide's headings are text you retype, a storyboard's shots are frames you can rearrange. Nothing in a template is locked or special.

## Changing the Theme

A new document starts on the Default theme, and a template picked on a start takes the tab's current theme. Once you are on the canvas, click the **Style** button on a page's title bar to open the **Tab Style** dialog and try a different [theme](/help/canvas/themes/): it recolours the whole template at once.

## Making It Your Own

A template is just a head start, not a constraint. Everything in it is fully editable: rename and recolour the elements, restyle the whole thing with a different theme, or strip out the parts you do not need. Because the document works the same as any other, you can also add [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/), invite others, and share it once you have shaped it into what you want.

<Tip>
  Templates pair naturally with themes: pick a layout that fits your content, then try a few themes
  from the Tab Style dialog to find the look you want. Double-click a theme to apply it and close
  the dialog in one go.
</Tip>
