# Marketing site

> **Meet-first** (Work Tracker #423, part 5, 2026-11-05): livehuddle is for meeting in Spaces
> ([Purpose](../001-project-vision/purpose.md)), and the site sells only that: avatars, rooms, presence, light content,
> the Timer and Stopwatch pieces, and the facilitator's tools. Nothing on it offers workshops, retros, polls, Q&A,
> quizzes, decks or diagrams; `lib/meet-first.test.ts` holds the beats, the FAQ, the comparisons and the AI page to that.

The marketing app (`apps/marketing`) is the public landing site served at `/` (everything the router doesn't hand to the live editor's routes, `/api/*`, or `/telemetry`, see [08-router-app](../016-platform/router-app.md)). Static Next.js export, no SSR. It is the top of the acquisition funnel: its only job is to explain what livehuddle is and send visitors to `/new`.

What every product's marketing site has in common lives once, in `@liveworkspace/marketing-site` ([its README](../../../../packages/marketing-site/README.md)): the page hero, sections, CTA link and band classes, the Build with AI page (which livehuddle does not use), the FAQ browser, the licences page, the shared stylesheets and the source guards. This app keeps its words, its art, its routes and its header and footer, and hands them over as props.

## Golden rule: claims map to shipped features

**Every feature claim on the marketing site must correspond to something a visitor can actually do today.** No aspirational copy, no roadmap dressed as present tense.

- If a feature is not built (teams/permissions, true concurrent/CRDT editing, see [02-prototype-scope](../005-project-roadmap/prototype-scope.md)), it does **not** appear on the landing page, even softened.
- When the editor gains or loses a feature, update the landing page in the same change. Drift between the page and the product is a bug.
- Counts (templates, themes, shapes) must match the source of truth ([09-canvas-and-palette](../008-canvas/canvas-and-palette.md), `packages/templates/src/templates.ts`, `apps/editor/lib/themes.ts`). As of this spec: **19 templates**: Blank Space, leading the landing gallery's Blank Slate ([Templates by mode](../007-editor/templates-by-mode.md)), and eighteen Spaces to walk round in Avatar mode, from the Virtual Office, Team Pod and All-Hands Hall to the Campus templates, the Expo Hall and the Beach Offsite ([Virtual office template](../012-collaboration/virtual-office-template.md)); the rest left on the meet-first refocus ([Not in livehuddle](../002-project-scope/not-in-livehuddle.md)). **25 themes** (12 default + 13 extra, the extras including the dark schemes and three multi-colour "rainbow" schemes; see [Multi-colour (rainbow) themes](../011-theme/multicolour-themes.md)). Both counts are pinned by `apps/editor/lib/templates.test.ts` + `themes.test.ts` so spec drift surfaces as a test failure.
- Be honest about trade-offs where it builds trust: realtime is **last-write-wins**, not CRDT, say so rather than implying conflict-free merging.

## Structure

Single landing page (`app/page.tsx`), in this order: a sticky header, the hero, the template gallery, the Community section ([Community](../025-community/community.md#featured-on-the-home-page): six shared documents, loaded after the page, the one landing section that reads the api), the promises, **three story beats**, the privacy callout, a closing CTA, and a footer. The beats tell the product as its core features (collaborate, diagrams, whiteboard) rather than as generic categories.

**The landing page tells a story; the detail lives on a page per category.** The page once ran one block per category, thirteen identical bands (about 12,500px) that read as a checklist; then five beats grouped those thirteen generic categories (The basics, Customisation, Reliability and so on) under ideas. Neither said what the app is for. So the body is now **one story beat per core feature**, and the feature categories are those same three: `LANDING_SECTIONS` in `lib/landing-content.tsx` is both the beats and the `/features/<id>` pages, rendered on the landing page by `components/StoryBeat.tsx`. A beat has the feature's **glyph and plain name** (`label`, in brand caps beside a brand disc holding the category's glyph from `components/category-icons.tsx`, one per id and none shared, pinned by `category-icons.test.tsx`), a **headline** above a pitch, **four highlights** (`highlights`: feature titles, each a link to its card on the category page via `../packages/marketing-site/src/lib/feature-anchor.ts`), a primary link into its category page, and **the hero scene that shows the feature** (`scene`, see "Feature scenes" below) playing beside it. The beats run wider than the page's other bands (`max-w-7xl`) and the text column has a **fixed width** (22rem, 26rem from `xl`), the scene taking the rest (about 760px at a 1440px screen, the size the hero draws its scenes), so its small text stays crisp, and it starts on a whole pixel, where a fractional split (5fr to 7fr) landed it between pixels and blurred it. The scene column has **no scroll-in slide**: the scene plays its own build as it arrives, and a transform held mid-entry kept the window on its own layer, painted off the pixel grid; the text column keeps its entrance. Beats alternate the scene's side and the tinted background. While a beat is **one column** (below `lg`, the scene hidden) its text is **centred** (eyebrow, headline, pitch and link), the highlights a centred block with their ticks still lined up on the left; from `lg` it sits left-aligned beside the scene. A category page's hero centres the same way. `landing-content.test.ts` pins the three ids in order, a different scene per beat, four highlights that each resolve to one of the beat's own cards, art on every card and no em dash in a beat's copy. The full `FeatureGrid` of cards renders on each **`/features/<id>`** page. See "Feature category pages" below.

Only the marketing pages show it (`SiteHeader`'s `account`); the help centre and the dashboard keep their bar as it is. The CTAs are `buttonClassName` anchors (`md` size). Top to bottom, centred: two **promise badges**, "Free and open source" and "No sign-up to start" (each true today; a phone shows the first only, so the row stays one line); the headline **"Meet your team in a Space"** (the D5 pitch, static, the page's largest paint, never animated); the subhead "Walk in as your own character, find the team round the table and say hello. Rooms to meet in, with a timer on the wall when you need one. Share one link and everyone’s in."; and two calls to action, the primary **Open a Space** (`/new`, slot `Home.Hero`, the header primary's `--hero-cta` fill) and the quiet **Browse Templates** (`/#templates`). Around and under them, **the band itself is the Space's floor** (`components/HeroSpaceStage.tsx`, its data in `components/hero-space-floor-cast.ts`): no editor window, no frame. The band's dot grid is the canvas, and on it **office furniture stands round the edges** while **the team walks about, stops in small groups and talks**. The furniture is the editor's own office art (desks, a sofa, a meeting table on a rug, a coffee machine, a water cooler, plants and a bookshelf: `createShape` from `@livehuddle/document` drawn by its own SVG renderer, `renderElementsToSvg`), so it is what a Space's furniture looks like; on a wide screen the furniture stands in two **wings** beside the copy (a coffee corner on the left, desks and a sofa on the right), on **the floor** under the calls to action (a pair of desks, the meeting table on its rug, plants, a water cooler and a bookshelf), and along **the back of the office** above the copy (a bookshelf, a jukebox and an arcade machine, a notice board, a ping-pong table, a fireplace, an office pod, a drinks fridge and a plant), and the band grows by the floor's height so the floor is never under the copy. **The copy stands on the floor**: when the screen makes the band taller ([the shared hero height](../../../../packages/marketing-site/src/css/hero-tall.css)), all the extra height goes above the copy (`--hero-tall-below: 0`), so the copy keeps one place on the stage at every height and a taller window shows more of the room above it, never an empty band (Work Tracker #505). **What happens**, on one 30 second loop: two teammates play ping-pong at the back and talk across the table ("Your serve!", "Best of three?"), two chat at the coffee machine, two sit on the sofa talking, two stand at the ends of the meeting table, and one walks the long way round the floor, under the calls to action, and waves to the table, which waves back in words; one comes up from their desk on the floor to the coffee corner and back. Each stop says something in a **speech bubble**, the editor's bubble look (a card edged in the speaker's colour, their name under the words), short, warm and about meeting: "Got a minute?", "Coffee?", "Stand-up in 5", never lorem and never about workshops, boards or retros. Within a group the lines take turns, so two bubbles in one group never show at once (a test holds them apart). **Every bubble stands on clear floor**: never over a piece of furniture (a rug lies under it, so it may), another character or a name chip, at any moment it shows, and nobody walking past covers someone else's name chip; the pair at the ends of a table (the meeting table, the ping-pong table, the phone's table) lean their bubbles off its ends, and the coffee corner's pair stand on its rug in front of the machines, so their lines show under them; between sm and lg a line said on the floor stays inside the middle a 640px window shows, so none runs off its edge (a test walks the whole loop on each stage, Work Tracker #587). Walking is the editor's walk cycle (its two stride frames, facing the way each leg goes); a stop shows the standing figure turned to whom they talk to, a wave is Avatar mode's raised hand, and the sofa's pair are seated (the sprite's seated pose). **Sizes**: the wide stage is 1600 by 1152 px (the band's tallest), drawn 1:1 and centred on the band's foot, so a narrower window crops its wings symmetrically and keeps the floor, and a shorter one crops the back of the office from the top, never scaling the characters down. Everything from 404 px under the stage's top shows at every height from `lg` (the band is never shorter than its content, 748 px), and the back of the office stands wholly between 144 and 324 px. **The back of the office is never cut**: it is a layer of its own (`HeroSpaceBackRow`, a clipped copy of that strip of the drawing with its two players and their bubbles), at its place on the stage when the band is tall enough and otherwise slid down to just under the band's top (6 px, so a bubble clears the header), and hidden whole on a window under 948 px tall, where it would meet the wings or the copy. So at every height it shows whole, heads and bubbles, or not at all; a test walks every window height from 900 to 1200 px, and every width band, and holds it clear of the copy and the wings. The stage itself draws only from 324 px down. The keep-clear column is 840 px wide, wider than the 792 px headline. Below `lg`, where the headline spans the window, the stage shows its floor alone (clipped under the calls to action) and the back of the office layer above the copy by the same rule, so no cropped character stands against the copy and a tall tablet sees the room above the copy rather than an empty band; **a phone and a narrow window** (below `sm`) get their own smaller stage, in the flow under the calls to action, with **fewer characters** (three) and only the table, its rug, a plant and the coffee machine, so nothing crowds the copy. **Light and dark**: the furniture is drawn once per appearance on the renderer's own light and dark canvas ink (two drawings swapped by the `dark` class, never a filter), and a page fetches only the drawing for its appearance (both are lazy `<img>`s, and a browser never fetches a lazy image it does not render); the characters keep their own colours; the bubbles and name chips take the page's dark surfaces. **No script runs it, and it costs less than the window did**: the stage is a server component, the motion is CSS keyframes on `transform` and `opacity` only, so the hero adds **no client JavaScript**; the characters and bubbles are HTML boxes moved by transform (each character a one-still window over a film strip of its stills, the strip stepped to the still it shows), which the browser composites without layout or repaint (measured: the landing page's main thread did 690 ms of work in six idle seconds under the window's SVG animations and 112 ms with the floor, with no layouts). **The art and the motion are generated, never hand-drawn**: `lib/hero-space-floor.test.tsx` draws the furniture through the renderer once per appearance and stage (`public/hero/floor-wide-light.svg`, `floor-wide-dark.svg`, `floor-phone-light.svg`, `floor-phone-dark.svg`), every still the characters show through the sprite into one sprite sheet (`public/hero/floor-cast.svg`, one `<symbol>` per still, the page's HTML holding a `<use>` per still rather than its pixels), and every character's moves into keyframes (`app/hero-scenes/space-floor.generated.css`, from the walk's waypoints and stops in the cast file), and fails when a committed file drifts from what they make today (`HERO_SPACE_WRITE=1` writes them afresh) or when the sheet and the stills the hero uses differ. The stage's stylesheets are imported by the stage itself, so only the landing page loads them. **Reduced motion** shows one settled frame of the loop: everyone at a stop, one bubble up in each talking group, nothing moving. **Off screen**, the stage is `content-visibility: auto`, so a browser that skips rendering it there does no work for it. The stage is decoration (`aria-hidden`, no pointer events); its story is told in words by the headline and subhead. The template gallery (see 4) sits directly under the hero and is where a visitor browses every template; the search and the dealt fan of templates that livediagram's hero carries are not here.

4. **Template gallery** (`components/TemplateGallery.tsx`): a deliberately different layout from the feature blocks: an **inset band** (a neutral slate band with white cards in light, the page's deepest tone under slate-900 cards in dark, shared with the privacy callout through `../packages/marketing-site/src/lib/band-classes.ts`) anchored `#templates` and headed **"Browse every template"**. It **only browses**, with no search box or mode filter: the editor's wizard (`/new`) is the one search; and **one card per template the editor ships**, laid out **as the editor's template step lays them out** ([Templates](../008-canvas/canvas-and-palette.md#templates)): **Blank Slate** first (the shelf id is still `popular`): every blank and nothing else, which is Blank Space, the one blank ([Templates by mode](../007-editor/templates-by-mode.md#the-blank)); the starters the editor's Popular adds after its blanks (`POPULAR_BY_MODE`) are not on it, each being on its category shelf already), then the catalogue's categories **alphabetically by name** (`TEMPLATE_CATEGORIES`, sorted by label; [Template categories](../008-canvas/canvas-and-palette.md#template-categories)), a template cross-listed in several categories shown on each, each category's cards **mixed** rather than in the order they were added (a seeded shuffle, `SHUFFLE_SEED` in `lib/template-gallery.ts`, dealt once so the order is the same on every build and visit; Blank Slate keeps its fixed order), the blanks only ever on Blank Slate ([Templates by mode](../007-editor/templates-by-mode.md)). **Each shelf is a carousel** (`components/TemplateCarousel.tsx`): four cards across on desktop, two on a tablet, one on a phone (a shelf with fewer cards keeps them that size from the left, as the editor's step does), with prev / next arrows on the heading row that page through the rest (on a phone the heading also carries a **count badge**, how many templates the row holds, since one card per page hides how far it goes; each press moves exactly one page, the visible width plus the gap, so it always lands on a card edge, including on iOS where a programmatic scroll doesn't re-snap; a scroll-snapping track, so a touch swipe works too; arrows disable at the ends and hide when every card fits). The band carries no CTA button of its own: every card is the call to action. **One shelf is open at a time, Blank Slate on load**; every other shelf is folded into a grid of **category cards** below it (`components/CategoryTiles.tsx`, headed "Explore More Categories" with a count of the categories and templates still folded) so the band stays short. Each card shows a fanned stack of its category's first three previews (the picker's art on one `preview-art-tile` plate, so dark re-lights it; the fan spreads on hover or focus; only the **front preview is in the page's HTML**, the other two being added when the browser is idle, `DeferredPreviewFan` over `@liveworkspace/template-previews`' fan: one idle callback for every card, a timer where there is none, the cards fading up in their places with no layout shift as the plate keeps its height, so the folded categories do not carry three SVGs each), the category's name and a template-count pill, with the pointer cursor of a control: a row of plain chips read as tags nobody noticed. Clicking a card opens that shelf **in the open one's place**, as the editor's step does, the shelf it replaces folding back into the cards (Blank Slate leading them); it rises into place, the row fading up and its cards following in a short stagger (`tg-reveal`; off under reduced motion; Blank Slate on page load sits still). A card pressed lower down would open its shelf out of sight above, so when the opened shelf does not show whole (its top under the 72 px header or above the screen, or its bottom below it) the page scrolls smoothly up to it, its heading 16 px below the header (`../packages/marketing-site/src/lib/shelf-scroll.ts`; at once under reduced motion); a shelf already on screen is left where it is. **Every card is a link that creates its document straight away** and opens it in the editor (`/new?template=<kind>`, [Dedicated route for new-document creation](../007-editor/new-document-route.md)), so the landing page is one click from a drawn scaffold. The card artwork is the editor picker's own preview (`@livehuddle/template-previews`), drawn on its light plate in light and re-lit onto the dark canvas colour in dark by the same `preview-art-tile` rule the editor's picker uses (the package ships it as `preview-art-tile.css`), so a template looks the same here as in the app. **Hovering (or keyboard-focusing) a card brings its preview to life** (`preview-motion.css` in `@livehuddle/template-previews`, opted into with `preview-motion-host` on the card and `preview-motion` on the tile; the editor's template picker opts in too, so the New Space wizard and the in-editor Quick Start play the same story on hover or focus, while its category collages stay still and its Reduce motion preference turns the stories off, as the OS setting does): every preview replays its own build, shape after shape in drawing order, and then **every template tells its own story**, showing what the template is for: each Space fills with the people and things it is for, and so on for all of them. The stories are built from a shared toolkit of moves (appear, arrive, shift, route, leave, grow, draw, travel, pulse, spin: see the header of `preview-motion.css`), so their timing reads as one family, and `template-preview.test.ts` fails if a template has no story. ## Feature category pages

**Arriving on a page is animated, never a cut** (`../packages/marketing-site/src/css/page-motion.css`). Moving between two marketing pages (home to a category page and back) is a cross-document **view transition**: the pages crossfade and the sticky header (`view-transition-name: site-header` on the shared `SiteHeader`) holds still. The opt-in, `@view-transition { navigation: auto }`, is **inlined in the root layout's `<head>`** rather than left in the bundled stylesheet: the browser only runs the transition if the new page has opted in by its first paint, and from the stylesheet it sometimes arrived later, so the transition was skipped and the old page logged "Transition was skipped" (about half of navigations in testing; none since). A link out of the marketing site (into the editor at `/new`, the help centre, the dashboard) **skips the transition** as the page swaps: only the marketing pages opt in, so the browser would otherwise start one, find the editor declined, and report "Transition was aborted because of invalid state" as an uncaught error on every hero click. The marketing page decides by the destination's app, the router's own split (`pageViewApp`), in a small inline `pageswap` listener beside the opt-in. On arrival the category hero eases up piece by piece (`.enter`, staggered by `--enter-delay`), and the feature cards and the landing beats rise in as they scroll into view (`.enter-on-scroll`, a scroll-driven animation where supported, a load-time one elsewhere; on the cards it sits on a wrapper, because the card's hover lift is a transform the animation would pin). All of it is CSS over content already in the static HTML, so crawlers and no-JS visitors get the same page, a browser without these features shows the page as before, and reduced motion turns it all off.

## Social sharing

Word of mouth is part of the acquisition funnel, so the page lets a happy visitor pass it on in one click. `packages/ui/src/ShareRail.tsx` renders a **vertical rail pinned to the right edge of the page**: a rotated "Share" label above the share targets (X, LinkedIn, Facebook, WhatsApp, email) and a **Copy link** action.

It began as a "Share" popover in the header and moved out to the edge to give the header room to breathe. Two consequences of where it ended up:

- **It lives in `@livehuddle/ui`, not in the marketing app**, because `SiteHeader` renders it. Surfaces that use the shared header carry the rail by default (the telemetry dashboard is just as worth passing on as the landing page); the help centre turns it off (`shareRail={false}`) because its pages run `max-w-7xl`, so the rail would cover the article sidebar ([Help app](../018-help/help-app.md)).
- **It renders as a _sibling_ of the header, not a descendant.** The header's `backdrop-filter` establishes a containing block that would trap the rail's `fixed` positioning against the header instead of the viewport.

Beyond that:

- **Wide desktop only (`xl`, 1280px).** Page content is `max-w-6xl` (1152px), so only past `xl` is there a real gutter for a fixed rail to sit in without overlapping the content. Below that it is hidden, and a page URL is inherently shareable anyway.
- **Share targets are plain anchor links** to each network's public share-intent URL (`twitter.com/intent/tweet`, `linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite`, `facebook.com/sharer`, `wa.me`, `mailto:`). No SDKs, no tracking pixels (keeps the "no tracking pixels" claim honest under [03](../002-project-scope/open-source-and-business-model.md)). They open in a new tab (`target="_blank"` + `rel="noopener noreferrer"`).
- **The Appearance rail sits under it.** A second, separate rail of the same card, a gap below, holds the [Appearance](../004-interface-design/appearance.md) toggle, its only place on the public sites: never in the header, and left out below `xl`.
- **Copy link** uses the Clipboard API to copy the canonical URL and flips to a tick for a beat, via the shared `useCopiedFlash`, so a rapid second copy restarts its own countdown rather than inheriting the first one's remainder.
- The shared URL is the canonical production site, `SITE_URL` from the package's own `site.ts` (the one origin constant every app uses), and the share text mirrors the product's one-line description, a constant in the component; update it if the positioning copy changes.
- The icons are stored as raw `<path>` `d` strings rather than JSX, so the module evaluates no top-level JSX at import time and the shared barrel stays importable from non-React test transforms.
- On the landing page itself the `'use client'` boundaries are the `TemplateGallery` (search state over the template cards) ; the hero, its Space included, is a server component like everything else, for static export. The rail is a client component too, but it now arrives via the shared header rather than the page.

**Feature-card illustrations.** Every feature card carries a small animated mock of the real editor surface it describes (`components/FeatureArt.tsx`), passed via the card's `art` slot. Like the hero, the motion is **pure CSS**, shared keyframe classes (`fa-*`) live in `app/globals.css`, so it works under static export with no JS and settles to a finished frame under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The mocks must stay faithful to how the feature actually looks (presence avatars, the explorer / share panels), and every Space in them is drawn as the editor draws it on the **Default theme**: its canvas, grid and the ink of unpainted shapes, arrows and labels, in the light half or the dark half with the appearance ([Appearance](../004-interface-design/appearance.md)). Only identity colours (stickies, cursors, highlights, selection), elements the example deliberately painted (the editor keeps a user's paint on any theme) and art whose subject is colour keep other colours; when a feature's UI changes, update its art. This is part of the golden rule above, an illustration is a claim too.

## SEO and metadata

The marketing site is the indexable surface for the product, so its `<head>` carries the search and social signals. The editor's routes and the API at `/api/*` are not optimised for organic search (the editor lives behind client-side bootstraps and has nothing useful to index).

- **One rule for every subpage, shared with the help centre**: subpage metadata comes from `pageMetadata()` in `@livehuddle/ui` (the help centre wraps the same function, [Help app](../018-help/help-app.md) "SEO"). The title passes through verbatim (no suffix added), `og:type` is `article`, and the card is the large brand card (`summary_large_image`, `/opengraph-image` + `/twitter-image` by absolute URL) unless the page ships its own route-level image (`ownOgImage`, the `/features/<id>` cards). An app's front page that is a hub or dashboard rather than an article (the Community gallery, the Telemetry dashboard) passes `type: 'website'`; both now carry the brand card like every other public page. A `/features/<id>` card carries the category's label as its kicker, its headline, and a subtitle from `cardSubtitle` (`../packages/ui/src/social-card.tsx`, beside the shared `renderSocialCard`): the first sentence of the description when it fits two lines (100 characters), otherwise cut at the last whole word, so the card never ends mid-word or runs into its footer. The origin, site name, repo URL, favicon set (`BRAND_ICONS`) and public viewport (`PUBLIC_VIEWPORT`) are shared constants in the same package, used by every app.
- `metadataBase`: `https://livehuddle.app`. Lets relative paths in metadata resolve into absolute URLs (the canonical, OG image, sitemap entries).
- `alternates.canonical`: every static page declares its own canonical (`/`, `/faq`, ...). Stops query-string variants and the `www` mirror from competing with the canonical form. The retired `/terms` and `/privacy` routes are the exception: they are noindex redirect stubs whose canonical points at the help-centre Policies articles they now forward to (see "Content pages" below).
- `<html lang="en-GB">` on the root layout. Matches the OG locale + JSON-LD `inLanguage` so every locale signal across the page agrees. The earlier broader `lang="en"` worked but reads inconsistent next to OG's specific `en_GB` declaration.
- `openGraph`: title, description, url, siteName, locale (`en_GB`), type (`website` for the landing page, `article` for the FAQ and the comparisons, whose `article:modified_time` is their last review). Mirrors the page-level title/description so social cards match the actual head. (The Terms and Privacy Policy moved into the help centre and carry their own "Last updated" lines there.) Card image is generated by `apps/marketing/app/opengraph-image.tsx` via `next/og`'s `ImageResponse`; Next.js's file convention pre-renders it to a static 1200x630 PNG at build time and ships it in `out/`, so the asset has no runtime dependency.
- `twitter`: card `summary_large_image`, title, description, and the card image. `apps/marketing/app/twitter-image.tsx` re-exports the OG handler so the Twitter card and the OG card stay byte-identical. Copy is identical to the OG / page-level title and description.
- **Meta descriptions** are 50 to 160 characters, so a search result shows each one whole. A feature category page uses its section's `metaDescription` (written for the result, not the on-page lede, which runs longer); comparison pages use their `description`. `lib/meta-descriptions.test.ts` holds every page to it, and the shared `comparisonProblems` every comparison (`lib/alternatives.test.ts`).
- `robots`: explicit `{ index: true, follow: true }` on every page. The hosted product wants indexing; ambiguity about defaults is the kind of thing a future migration silently breaks.
- `app/sitemap.ts`: lists the indexable marketing URLs, `/` + `/faq` + `/alternatives` + one entry per `/alternatives/<slug>` competitor page (`comparisonSitemap` over the same list the routes read, dated by the comparisons' last review, see [the comparison-pages spec](../../../../packages/comparisons/README.md)). `/community/` ([Community](../025-community/community.md), the public gallery of shared documents, priority 0.6, daily) is listed beside them. The two public operational surfaces, `/telemetry` (the anonymous-events dashboard) and `/status` (the service-status page), are liveworkspace's now, one for every app, so neither is listed: the router redirects both to liveworkspace.app, whose sitemap lists them ([liveworkspace/docs/specs/010-site/site.md](../../../../liveworkspace/docs/specs/010-site/site.md#status-status)). `/terms`, `/privacy` and `/security` are deliberately not listed: all three policies live in the help centre (the help app's own sitemap lists the canonical Policies articles), and each marketing route is now a noindex redirect there. `lastModified` is per-page truth, not blanket build-time: `/` and `/faq` use `new Date()` because they change with product releases. Without that split, every build claims content changed today, which is misleading to crawlers and dilutes the change-frequency signal for pages that actually move. Next.js generates `/sitemap.xml` at the root from this file.
- `app/robots.ts`: allows the marketing routes, points at the sitemap, and `Disallow`s the editor app routes and `/api/`. The router worker stitches all three apps onto the same hostname, so an unconditional allow would let crawlers waste budget on the editor's auth-walled HTML and the API's JSON responses. The editor app also declares `noindex, nofollow` at its root layout (see [07-editor-app.md](../007-editor/editor-app.md)) as the in-page complement; together the two signals tell crawlers to skip the editor app entirely.
- **`viewport` export** (Next.js metadata API): the root layout exports a `Viewport` object carrying `themeColor: '#10b981'` (brand-500, see [01-color-scheme](../004-interface-design/color-scheme.md)) and `colorScheme: 'light dark'`. The first tints mobile browser chrome (Android Chrome's URL bar, iOS PWA status bar) to the brand, the second says the pages paint both appearances; which one follows the reader's origin-wide [Appearance](../004-interface-design/appearance.md) (System by default, the rail's toggle to override), applied before first paint. Every page, illustrations included, has a dark appearance, and its editor mock-ups show the editor as it looks in dark. Both are head meta, not page content, so they don't conflict with the "claims map to shipped features" rule.
- **`app/manifest.ts`**: emits `/manifest.webmanifest` at build time with `name`, `short_name`, `start_url: '/'`, `display: 'standalone'`, the brand theme + background colours, and the existing `/icon.svg` as the icon (which `purpose: 'any'` covers since the cube's corners reach outside a maskable safe zone). Surfaces the Android Chrome "Add to Home Screen" prompt + the iOS Safari "Add to Home Screen" landing icon, with no behaviour change for normal page loads. Same `export const dynamic = 'force-static'` opt-in as robots.ts / sitemap.ts so it round-trips through `output: 'export'`. Because `livehuddle.app` is one origin (the router stitches the apps by path), this is the **origin's** manifest: the editor (editor app) references it (and `/apple-icon`) by absolute path from its own layout metadata, so installing the PWA from the editor gets the same icon + name rather than nothing (see [07-live-app](../007-editor/editor-app.md)).
- **JSON-LD structured data**: the root layout renders a single `<script type="application/ld+json">` carrying a `@graph` of two schema.org types: `WebSite` (so the brand-name search produces a rich result) and `SoftwareApplication` (so the product is categorised correctly in browse / category-style results). Both schemas restate facts already in the page (name, url, description) and the SoftwareApplication offer is `price: "0"` (truthful: the hosted product is free with no paid tier, see [03](../002-project-scope/open-source-and-business-model.md)). No claims that aren't already true on the surface. Each schema also carries an `image` field pointing at the same OG card (`/opengraph-image`) so Google can pair the brand card with a visual, an `inLanguage` field matching the OG locale (`en-GB`), and a `sameAs` array on the SoftwareApplication that links the canonical GitHub repo (already linked from the footer + the FAQ deployment answer) so the brand's open-source presence is connected to the same entity.
- **FAQ JSON-LD**: `/faq` emits an additional `<script type="application/ld+json">` with schema.org `FAQPage` + a `Question` / `Answer` pair per entry. Unlocks Google's "expandable FAQ" rich result for the page. The JSON-LD `acceptedAnswer.text` is the plain-text form of each answer; entries that render JSX (a link to the help-centre privacy policy in the data-storage question, for instance) carry a parallel `aText` field with the same sentence, sans markup. The page builds it from the same `FAQ_CATEGORIES` it renders, and `lib/faq-content.test.tsx` renders every JSX answer and fails if its visible text differs from `aText`, so the structured data can't drift from the visible content.
- **Breadcrumb JSON-LD**: every indexable subpage (`/faq`, each `/features/<id>`, `/alternatives` and each `/alternatives/<slug>`) emits a `BreadcrumbList` script. (The retired `/terms` + `/privacy` redirect stubs don't, since they render no breadcrumb trail.) Two items for two-level pages (`Home → <Page>`), three for a comparison (`Home → Comparison → <competitor> alternative`). The comparison hub adds an `ItemList` and each comparison a `FAQPage` ([the comparison-pages spec](../../../../packages/comparisons/README.md) "Metadata"). Unlocks Google's SERP breadcrumb display so the search result shows `livehuddle.app > FAQ` instead of the bare URL. Rendered via `components/BreadcrumbJsonLd.tsx` so the pages share one source of truth; the `BreadcrumbList` shape itself is the shared `breadcrumbJsonLd` builder in `@livehuddle/ui`, which the help centre's breadcrumbs use too. The landing page (`/`) doesn't get a breadcrumb (it IS home, the trail would be a single entry).

When a new public page lands, add it to both `app/sitemap.ts` and its own metadata block (with `alternates.canonical`). Updating one without the other is a regression.

## Content pages (FAQ)

`/faq` (`app/faq`) is the marketing site's one long-form content route. Its questions live in `lib/faq-content.tsx` as `FAQ_CATEGORIES`, nine categories (Getting started, Free and open source, Creating (templates, the one palette, themes, organising a Space, walking the room through something), Working together, Sharing and embedding, API and Integrations, Import and export, Privacy and your data, Help and support), each with a Lucide icon, a one-line blurb and its questions; every question and answer is about livehuddle alone: its sister apps (liveplanner, livedocuments, livedesign) and the kinds of work that live there are named only in an answer about a genuine integration, which `faq-scope.test.ts` allow-lists by question; the template and template-category counts are written out (the catalogue is too heavy for the FAQ's client bundle) and `faq-content.test.tsx` pins each to its catalogue, so they never drift. The page (`../packages/marketing-site/src/components/faq/FaqBrowser.tsx`, the same in every product, handed this app's categories by `components/faq/FaqBrowser.tsx`) opens on a tinted hero with a search box that narrows every category as you type (`filterFaq` in `../packages/marketing-site/src/lib/faq.ts`, beside the category shape: every word must appear in the question, its plain-text answer or its category title; matches open, empty categories drop out, no match points at the Help Centre). Wide screens add a sticky category index with per-category counts beside the questions. Each question is a native `<details>` card, so every answer is in the static HTML and opens without script.
