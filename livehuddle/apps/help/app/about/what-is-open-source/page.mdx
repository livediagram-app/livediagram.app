Most software you use is a black box: you get the finished app, but the source code that makes it work is kept private. **Open source** flips that around. The code is published in the open for anyone to read, run, modify, and share. livehuddle is open source, and that shapes the whole product, from what it costs to how your data is handled.

## What "open source" actually means

Software is open source when its source code, the human-readable instructions that make the program work, is published under a licence that grants everyone the right to use it freely. In practice that means three things:

- **You can read it.** Nothing about how the app works is hidden. The exact code that runs is the code you can inspect.
- **You can modify it.** Fork it, change it, adapt it to your needs.
- **You can share it.** Pass on the original or your modified version.

The licence is what makes those rights real rather than a promise. Different open-source licences attach different conditions; livehuddle uses one of the most permissive.

## livehuddle is MIT-licensed

livehuddle is released under the **MIT licence**, one of the simplest and most permissive open-source licences. It is permissive on purpose: anyone may read, fork, modify, or embed the code, for any purpose, commercial or not, with no copyleft strings attached. The whole codebase is publicly viewable.

That licence choice has concrete consequences for you.

### It is free, with no paid tier

Because the project is open and there is no commercial product to protect, livehuddle is **free for everyone**. There is no paid tier, no "Pro" upgrade, and no plan to introduce one. Every feature ships to every user. See [Why use livehuddle?](/help/about/why-livehuddle/).

### You can verify the claims, not just trust them

A public codebase means you do not have to take any privacy or security claim on faith. How a canvas is stored, what telemetry records, how share passwords work, all of it is in the open for you (or anyone) to audit. See [Open source and trust](/help/privacy-and-security/open-source-trust/).

### You are never locked in

Nothing in the code phones home to check a licence, and your canvases export to portable formats, so you are never trapped.

## Open source, hosted for convenience

Being open source does not mean you have to touch any code. livehuddle runs at livehuddle.app so you can just use it, while the code stays open for anyone who wants to read it.
