<ArticleLayout title="Why Use livehuddle?" description="Free and open source, no sign-in wall, and everyone in the room live from the moment they walk in." categoryTitle="About livehuddle" categorySlug="about">

There are plenty of ways to get a team together online. livehuddle is built around four ideas that make it a comfortable place for a team to meet: it is **free**, it is **open source**, it never puts a **sign-in wall** in front of the Space, and it is **multiplayer from the start**. Here is what each of those actually means for you.

## Free, with no paid tier

livehuddle is **free for everyone**. There is no paid tier, no "Pro" upgrade, no trial that expires, and no plan to introduce any of those. Every feature in the product is available to every user. livehuddle.app costs nothing to use.

## Open source

The whole codebase is **MIT-licensed** and publicly viewable. That has real consequences:

- **You can check the claims.** How a Space is stored and what telemetry records are in the open for anyone to audit.
- **No lock-in.** Because the code is open and your Spaces export to portable formats, you are never trapped.

## No sign-in wall

The [Space always works without signing in](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/). A first-time visitor can open a [new Space](/new), walk round it, share it, and only later be asked to create an account, never blocked by a modal before they have even started. An [account](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/) adds cross-device sync and [teams](/help/collaboration/teams/), but it is never a gate in front of the editor.

## Multiplayer from the start

Being together is the point, not a bolt-on. Hand someone a [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/) and they walk into the same Space, where everyone sees each other's characters, cursors and speech bubbles as they happen. When someone changes the Space, an element another person currently has selected is gently locked for everyone else, so edits never collide.
