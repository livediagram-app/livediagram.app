livehuddle is for teams who think visually and need to do that thinking **together**. A single-player diagramming tool is not the point. The product shines when several people are on the same space at once, building one shared diagram in [real time](/help/collaboration/sharing/). That said, it works just as well for one person sketching something out, and the [space always works without signing in](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/).

## The teams it serves

A few groups feel right at home:

- **Engineering teams** sketching architecture and system designs. Use [shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [arrows](/help/palette/arrows/), [icons](/help/palette/icons/) (search finds the cloud and infrastructure marks), and [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/) to keep related views together.
- **Product and design teams** mapping user journeys, information architecture, and brainstorms with [shapes](/help/palette/shapes/), [text and fonts](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/), and [themes](/help/canvas/themes/).
- **Cross-functional groups** in workshops, planning sessions, and retrospectives, where everyone needs to contribute at once and see each other's input live. [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) puts timers, dot votes and polls in front of whoever runs the session.

## Whether you sign in or not

livehuddle is friction-free on purpose. A first-time visitor can build a real diagram as a [guest](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/) and only later create an account to sync across devices and join [teams](/help/collaboration/teams/). A signed-in user can still hand a share link to a guest who edits without any account at all.
