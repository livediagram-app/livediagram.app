A Space full of people has no idea who is running the meeting. Two people start countdowns over each other, or reset one somebody else is running. Making one person the **facilitator** settles it: the tools that run the room answer to them, and everyone else gets on with the work.

Nobody is the facilitator until somebody takes the role, and a meeting that never uses it behaves exactly as it always has.

## Making someone the facilitator

Click any avatar on a tab (or the **+N** badge) to open **Collaborators**, then use the button on the person's row:

- **Make Facilitator** on somebody else's row. It is offered while nobody is facilitating, to the current facilitator (to hand it on), and always to the space's owner.
- **Take Facilitation** on your own row, when nobody is running the meeting.
- **Step Down** on your own row when you're the facilitator and you're done.
- **Take Over** on your own row, if you own the space and somebody else is facilitating. The owner can always take the meeting back.

Whoever is running it wears a **Facilitating** badge on their row in Collaborators. A visitor on a Participant or Viewer share link can't hold the role, because running a meeting is an editor's job: **Make Facilitator** on their row is greyed out, and its hover card says why. There is one facilitator for the whole space, not one per tab.

Everyone in the room gets a short notification when the role moves, like "Alex is now facilitating" or "Nobody is facilitating now", so nobody has to guess. If you've turned off **In-Editor Notifications** in [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/) › Notifications, you won't see those, and the badge still tells you who has it.

## What the facilitator runs

While somebody holds the role, these are theirs:

- Pressing **Bring Focus**.
- [Freeing an element](/help/collaboration/live-presence/#freeing-an-element-someone-left-selected) somebody has wandered off with selected: right-click it and choose their Release row in the **In use** menu. While nobody facilitates, any Editor can do this.

[Bring Focus](/help/palette/behaviour/bring-focus/), which any editor can normally press, becomes the facilitator's to press.

Everything else is unchanged for everyone: commenting, walking, sitting, talking, following somebody, and every part of editing the canvas. A tool only the facilitator can answer wouldn't be a tool.

A [Timer or Stopwatch](/help/collaboration/session-tools/timer/) placed in a room is not the facilitator's alone: anyone with an Editor or Participant pass can run one, so whoever is talking can keep time.

## If they leave or refresh

Refreshing changes nothing: the role comes straight back to them, usually before the page has finished loading. Same for a dropped connection or a closed laptop lid.

If they leave for good, the role is released about a minute and a half later and everyone is told, so the tools open up again rather than staying locked behind somebody who has gone. The space's owner can also take it back at any moment, and when everyone leaves the space the role is simply over: the next session starts with nobody facilitating.

The facilitator role is about who is driving, not a permission: it keeps two well-meaning people from calling the room's focus at once rather than defending the space from anyone. A [space in This Browser](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) has no live room, so it has no facilitator.

<Tip>
  Building the space and running the session are different jobs. Make the space, then hand the
  facilitator role to whoever is running the meeting; as the owner you keep the ability to take it
  back.
</Tip>
