In a Space you talk by walking up to people. Out on the open floor you hear the characters near yours and nobody else: a voice fades as its person walks away, and sits to your left or right as they stand. Step into a room and it becomes a call of its own, with walls nobody hears through. Someone too far away to hear still shows that they are talking, so you can choose to walk over and join in.

Audio rides [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/): only a walking character has a place on the board, so only someone walking hears or is heard. People in a Space can also turn on a camera or share a screen; see [Cameras and Screen Sharing](/help/collaboration/cameras-and-screen-sharing/).

<Note>
  You can only talk in a Space that's saved online. A Space saved only in your browser (it shows
  **This Browser** at the top) can't have anyone else in it, so **Join Audio** is greyed there. Save
  the Space online to talk with people. Everything else in the Space works as usual.
</Note>

## Join Audio

<Step n={1}>
  Walk into the Space: a Space from the templates opens with you walking, arriving on its **Avatar
  Pad**; otherwise press **Walk** at the start of the palette strip. The call's controls sit at the
  left of the avatar palette, the strip across the top of the canvas while you walk.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **Join Audio**. Your browser asks for your microphone the first time (allow it to be heard).
  If your link lets you listen but not speak, the button says **Listen In** instead and asks for
  nothing.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  You now hear the people near you. Once you are in, the call group shows your microphone,
  **Camera**, **Share** (for Editors) and **Leave**. Press **Leave** to stop hearing and being
  heard: your microphone is released straight away.
</Step>

<Figure caption="The call group at the left of the avatar palette: Join Audio before you join, then your microphone, Camera, Share and Leave.">
  <CallStrip />
</Figure>

Audio only works on a Space other people can reach. A Space saved in this browser alone has nobody to talk to, so it shows no audio controls. Leaving Avatar mode, switching to another tab or closing the page also leaves the call.

## Who you hear

You hear people on the same tab, and only while you both walk. Somebody sitting in a chair is heard from where the chair is. Anywhere you stand, on the open floor or inside a room, you hear the people within earshot:

- **Closer is louder.** A voice is full volume when you are right next to its person, and fades smoothly to silence as they walk out of range. There is a little give at the edge, so somebody standing on the boundary does not flicker in and out.
- **Left is left.** A person standing to your left is heard more in your left ear, which helps when a few people talk at once.
- **Walking away leaves a conversation.** There is nothing to hang up: walk over to another group to join theirs.

## Rooms are private

A room in a Space (an Office Room, with its walls and doorway) keeps a meeting in it private:

- **Inside a room, you hear the people near you**, just as on the open floor: closer is louder, and someone across a big room is out of earshot. Gather round to talk.
- **Walls block sound.** Nobody outside hears anyone inside, however close they stand to the wall or the door, and nobody inside hears anyone outside. Two rooms side by side don't hear each other through their shared wall.
- **The doorway is the line.** You are in a room once your feet are past the line of its walls. Standing in the doorway with your feet on the corridor side, you are still in the corridor; one step in, you are in the meeting.
- **A room inside a room** is private from the one around it, so a phone booth drawn inside an open-plan room is a quiet corner of its own.

In the **Virtual Office** template, the meeting rooms, the phone booths and the kitchen are each walled off from the open floor between them. The **Coworking Space** and the **All-Hands Hall** are each one big room, where you hear your neighbours, not the whole room.

## Your microphone

Once you are in, the first button in the strip is your microphone. Press it to mute, and again to unmute. When muted, nothing is sent at all. Its look tells you at a glance whether anyone hears you, and hovering it names the state in words:

<Figure caption="The microphone's states, as the strip shows them.">
  <MicStates />
</Figure>

The button shows only its glyph: hover it to see its state, such as **Mic: Live**.

- **Live**: somebody hears you: anyone in earshot on your side of the walls. A soft ring round the button breathes with your voice.
- **Nobody Near**: your microphone is on, but nobody hears you. People further off, or on the other side of a wall, still see that you are talking.
- **Muted**: nobody hears you. Press to unmute.
- **Mic Blocked**: your browser refused the microphone. Allow it from the address bar, then join again. You still hear everyone.
- **Mic Off**: nobody else is on this tab, so your microphone has closed (your browser's recording light goes out). It opens again the moment somebody arrives. This happens after you have been alone on the tab for 15 seconds.

## Seeing who is talking

When someone talks, three small bars in their colour bounce above their name. You see them for everyone on your tab, not just the people you hear, even through a room's walls: the bars only say that somebody is talking, never what is said, so a closed meeting room reads like glass.

- **People you don't hear** have calm, faint bars, fading further with distance, so the nearest conversations stand out.
- **People you hear** have brighter bars, so you can see who is coming through.
- **Your own bars** show above your own head while you talk, so you can tell you are coming through.

Hover or tap someone's bars to see "Sam is talking, walk over". Press them and your character walks towards Sam, stopping a short way off so you do not bump into them. This works while you are walking; otherwise the bars only show.

<Figure caption="Sam is in earshot and heard. Priya is further off: her bars are faint, and pressing them walks you over.">
  <Earshot />
</Figure>

Only a rough level of each person's voice is sent to show the bars, never the sound itself: nobody out of earshot receives any audio. Nothing said in a call is recorded or stored anywhere.

## Speaking from a stage

A Space can hold a **Stage**: a raised platform with its name on a plate at the front. Whoever stands on it, and may speak from it, is heard by everyone on the tab, in any room or none, at full volume. Several people on a stage hear each other as one room does.

While someone speaks from a stage, everyone else in its room (or, for a stage on the open floor, everyone else on the tab) is the **audience**: the stage does not hear them, and they hear each other only by distance, so neighbours can whisper without the whole hall hearing. Walls still stand, so a meeting room elsewhere on the tab stays its own call. Step off the stage and you are back in your room's call straight away.

## Raise your hand

In a stage's audience, the stage does not hear you, but you can ask to speak.

- **Raise Hand** appears in the call strip while you are in a stage's audience. Press it and a small amber hand shows beside your character, where everyone can see it. Press **Lower Hand** to put it down.
- **The people on the stage see how many hands are up**, as a count in their call strip. They, and any Editor, can press the hand beside your character to **Let Speak**.
- **Once you are let speak**, the hand turns into a green microphone and your strip reads **Speaking**: everyone in the Space hears you, as if you were on the stage. Your camera stays with the people who would see you anyway.
- **Your turn ends** when you press **Lower Hand**, when the stage presses your microphone again (**Done**), or when you walk out of the room. Leaving audio or the Space puts your hand down too.

If you stand on a stage that only lets Editors speak, a short line above the call strip says so: you are heard as the audience is, and you can raise your hand like anyone else.

## Do Not Disturb

Need to concentrate? Open the **Avatar** panel and set your **Status** to **Do Not Disturb**. A red dot with a white bar shows above your character, so people know not to expect an answer.

- **No voice reaches you**, not even from a stage, and no one's camera or screen. Other people's talking bars disappear for you.
- **You can still be heard** if you speak, so turning to a neighbour does not need the status cleared. Your microphone keeps its own mute.
- While you are in a call, the strip shows **Do Not Disturb**; press it to turn it off. It lasts for this visit only, like every status.

## Who can speak

| What you can do                   | Editor | Participant  | Viewer |
| --------------------------------- | ------ | ------------ | ------ |
| Join and hear the people near you | Yes    | Yes          | Yes    |
| Speak from a stage                | Yes    | When allowed | No     |
| Speak, mute and unmute            | Yes    | Yes          | No     |
| Show your talking bars to others  | Yes    | Yes          | No     |
| See who is talking, and walk over | Yes    | Yes          | Yes    |
| Raise and lower a hand            | Yes    | Yes          | No     |
| Let a raised hand speak           | Yes    | From a stage | No     |
| Set Do Not Disturb                | Yes    | Yes          | Yes    |

A stage lets Editors speak from it, and Participants too when it is set to; anyone else standing on it is heard as the audience is. Guests speak too, wherever their [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/) is an Editor or Participant one: audio needs no sign-in. A Viewer link listens: its button says **Listen In**, and the strip shows **Listening** rather than a microphone.

## When calls are paused

Calls can be paused for a while across livehuddle. The button then reads **Calls Paused**, calmly rather than as an error, and everything else in the Space carries on. Press it to try again. If it says **Audio Unavailable**, audio could not start; press it to try again.

<Tip>
  Wear headphones if you can: hearing a voice on your left or right works best with one ear on each
  side.
</Tip>
