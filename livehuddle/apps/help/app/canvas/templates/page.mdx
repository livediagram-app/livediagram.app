A blank canvas is freeing, but sometimes you would rather start with structure already in place. Templates give you a ready-made canvas as a starting point, filled with believable example content, so you can begin from something shaped roughly like what you need and adapt it, instead of building every diagram from nothing.

## Starting from a Template

When you [create a new canvas](/new), the canvas is made there and then, saved where new canvases go (see [Default Folders](/help/explorer/folders/default-folders/)). The empty tab then opens on **Start a Workshop**: its **Recommended** templates first, then every template in groups beside them, with a search. Click a template and the tab fills with it; from there it is an ordinary canvas, to move, edit, add to and delete from as on any canvas.

Every new tab, and every tab you empty, offers the same start. Close it with its **×** to begin from a blank canvas; while the tab is still empty, **Show Templates** in the strip under the palette brings it back.

The search at the top of the start shows up to six matches, with **Show All** for the rest, and pressing the down arrow in the search box takes you to the first card it found.

**Find a template** finds a template by its name, its description, its category, or the everyday words people use for it: try "1:1", "retro", "erd", "vsm", "pestel" or "onboarding". Every word you type has to match.

**Blank Workshop** leads the **Popular** shelf, apart from the categories since starting from nothing is not a kind of diagram: an empty tab to run a retro, town hall or workshop with timers, votes and polls to hand (see [Workshop Mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/)). After it, Popular shows a few of the templates most people start from. **Blank Diagram** and **Blank Whiteboard** are empty canvases too, kept for links that name them; like every template they open as a Workshop tab.

Each template arrives as real elements rather than a picture: a Gantt chart's bars are shapes you drag, a Sketchnote's strokes are drawings you can move or erase. Nothing in a template is locked or special.

## Changing the Theme

A new canvas starts on the Default theme, and a template picked on a start takes the tab's current theme. Once you are on the canvas, click the **Theme & canvas** button in the bottom-right corner to open the **Tab Style** dialog and try a different [theme](/help/canvas/themes/): it recolours the whole template at once.

## Making It Your Own

A template is just a head start, not a constraint. Everything in it is fully editable: rename and recolour the elements, restyle the whole thing with a different theme, or strip out the parts you do not need. Because the canvas works the same as any other, you can also add [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/), invite others, and share it once you have shaped it into what you want.

<Tip>
  Templates pair naturally with themes: pick a layout that fits your content, then try a few themes
  from the Tab Style dialog to find the look you want. Double-click a theme to apply it and close
  the dialog in one go.
</Tip>
