Most themes paint every element one colour. A multicolour theme is different: it carries a palette of several colours and gives each element on your board the next one in turn, so a board of scattered notes and boxes gets a spread of hues rather than collapsing to one. They are perfect for brainstorms and any board where colour helps you tell one thing from another.

## What They Are

A multicolour theme holds an ordered palette of colours plus a neutral trunk colour. Elements cycle through the palette, so a board with more elements than colours simply repeats hues rather than running out. The backdrop (the canvas background and pattern) works exactly as it does for any other theme; only the element colours change.

livehuddle ships three multicolour themes in the [Tab Style dialog](/help/canvas/themes/changing-theme/): a saturated **Rainbow** spectrum, a soft **Pastel** version and a bright, summery **Tropical** palette. A tab saved on the retired Autumn or Jewel themes opens on Tropical or Rainbow, with any colours you picked yourself kept.

## How the Colours Are Given Out

When you pick a multicolour theme, each element on the tab takes the next colour of the palette in turn. Sticky notes and images keep their own colours, as they do under every theme, and a colour you set by hand is kept.

## Adding to a Multicolour Board

A brand-new element you drop on a multicolour tab takes the neutral trunk colour. To give it a hue, paint a neighbour's look onto it with the [Format Painter](/help/tips-and-tricks/format-painter/). (Re-picking the theme does not do it: by then the trunk colour counts as one you chose, so it is kept.) This is deliberate: livehuddle does not silently re-colour your whole canvas every time you add something, which would fight any hand-colouring you have done.

<Tip>

</Tip>
