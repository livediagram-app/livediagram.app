The **meeting elements** are the ones that do something when somebody presses them or walks onto them. Everywhere else in the palette you are placing a picture; here you are placing a seat, a doorway, a room, or a button that hands everyone a different tool.

This page is the map. Each element has its own short article, so you can link somebody straight to the one they need.

## Where to find them

The meeting elements are the palette's **Meet** band on the palette strip: **Travel**, **Seating**, **Rooms**, **Amenities** and **Selection Mode**, after Write.

</Figure>

The strip's [element search](/help/palette/toolbar-layout/) finds and places every one too.

## What the categories contain

In each category, every element is a row with its name and a one-line description of what it does, because a glyph cannot say where a Portal goes or what a pad does when you step on it.

- **Travel**: what takes somebody somewhere: the [Portal](/help/palette/tools/portals/), [Bring Focus](/help/palette/behaviour/bring-focus/), the [Avatar Pad](/help/palette/avatar-pads/), the [Directory](/help/palette/directory/), the [Elevator](/help/palette/elevator/) and the [Avatar Button](/help/palette/behaviour/mode-buttons/).
- **Seating**: what people sit at: the [Chair, Desk and Sofa](/help/palette/chairs/), and the [Virtual Office](/help/palette/virtual-office/)'s meeting table.
- **Rooms**: what a floor is laid out with: the [Fence](/help/palette/fences/), rooms, a [Stage](/help/palette/virtual-office/stage/) and rugs.
- **Amenities**: what makes a Space feel like a place: plants, a bookshelf, a water cooler and a coffee machine.
- **Meeting**: what a meeting room needs: a [Screen](/help/palette/screen/) that shows the room's screen share, a [Room Sign](/help/palette/room-sign/) by its door that says whether it's free and lets you knock, and a [Timer and Stopwatch](/help/collaboration/session-tools/timer/) the whole room runs together.

They are grouped by the question you are answering as you build a Space: how people get somewhere, where they sit, how the floor is laid out, and what makes it feel like a place. That is why the Portal sits beside Bring Focus, the Avatar Pad, the Avatar Button and the Link card: each takes somebody somewhere (another tab, one spot on the canvas, the way in, into Avatar mode, or the page the card points at). **Walk In**, the button the Spaces templates carry, is an Avatar Button.

## Changing one

Most elements here carry a **…** in their top corner, which opens that element's own menu beside it. It is the same menu a right-click gives you, in the place you are already looking. Bring Focus and the Chair have no **…**: their faces already say what they do, so right-click them for their settings instead.

## Working with them

Each behaves like any other element once placed: select, restyle, and move it freely. A click presses it and a drag moves it, so you can arrange a row of them without setting one off. They keep working while you are in a selection mode that makes the rest of the canvas read-only (Avatar), so you can walk onto or press them mid-walk.

On a Participant or Viewer link, Bring Focus is inactive. A Viewer's Avatar Button still switches their own tool; a Participant is always walking, so theirs does nothing.

<Tip>
  Put a Walk In button at a Space's entrance and the Space carries its own welcome: people walk in
  and sit down without opening a menu.
</Tip>
