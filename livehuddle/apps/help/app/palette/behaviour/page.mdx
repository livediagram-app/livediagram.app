The **meeting elements** are the ones that do something when somebody presses them or walks onto them. Everywhere else in the palette you are placing words or a picture; here you are placing a seat, a doorway, a room, or a button that sends people somewhere.

This page is the map. Each element has its own short article, so you can link somebody straight to the one they need.

## Where to find them

The meeting elements fill the palette strip after **Write**: **Rooms**, **Seats**, **Perks**, **Play**, **Garden** and **Meet**, then **Travel**, set apart by a thin line.

The strip's [element search](/help/palette/toolbar-layout/) finds and places every one too.

## What the categories contain

In each category, every element is a row with its name and a one-line description of what it does, because a glyph cannot say where a Portal goes or what a pad does when you step on it.

- **Rooms**: what a floor is laid out with: rooms, the Room Builder, corridors, doors and windows, then the [Fence](/help/palette/fences/), a [Stage](/help/palette/virtual-office/stage/), pods, rugs and pillars.
- **Seats**: what people sit at: the [Chair, Desk and Sofa](/help/palette/chairs/), and the [Virtual Office](/help/palette/virtual-office/)'s tables, bean bags and desks.
- **Perks**: what makes a Space feel like a place: plants, shelves and office kit, a kitchen, and a restroom.
- **Play**: somewhere to unwind: a bar, games, a gym corner and a lounge.
- **Garden**: the outdoors: plants, paths, benches, water and parking.
- **Meet**: what a meeting room and a team need: a [Screen](/help/palette/screen/) that shows the room's screen share, a [Room Sign](/help/palette/room-sign/) by its door that says whether it's free and lets you knock, the boards and clocks for its walls, and a [Timer and Stopwatch](/help/collaboration/session-tools/timer/) the whole room runs together.
- **Travel**: what takes somebody somewhere: the [Portal](/help/palette/tools/portals/), [Bring Focus](/help/palette/behaviour/bring-focus/), the [Avatar Pad](/help/palette/avatar-pads/), the [Directory](/help/palette/directory/), the Signpost, the [Elevator](/help/palette/elevator/), the [Stairs](/help/palette/stairs/) and the [Avatar Button](/help/palette/behaviour/mode-buttons/).

They are grouped by the question you are answering as you build a Space: how the floor is laid out, where people sit, what makes it feel like a place, and how people get somewhere. That is why the Portal sits beside Bring Focus, the Avatar Pad and the Avatar Button: each takes somebody somewhere (another tab, one spot on the canvas, the way in, or into Avatar mode). The Spaces templates arrive on an Avatar Pad; a **Walk In** button on a Space made before the pads is an Avatar Button.

## Changing one

A few elements here, the Portal, the Avatar Button, the Timer and the Stopwatch, carry a **…** in their top corner, which opens that element's own menu beside it. It is the same menu a right-click gives you, in the place you are already looking. Everything else, Bring Focus and the furniture included, has no **…**: right-click it for its settings instead.

## Working with them

Each behaves like any other element once placed: select, restyle, and move it freely. A click presses it and a drag moves it, so you can arrange a row of them without setting one off. They keep working while you walk, when the rest of the canvas is read-only, so you can walk onto or press them mid-walk.

On a Participant or Viewer link, Bring Focus is inactive: clicking it does nothing, and hovering its target says who can press it. Participants and Viewers are always walking, so an Avatar Button does nothing for them either.

<Tip>
  Put an Avatar Pad at a Space's entrance and the Space carries its own welcome: people arrive there
  and walk in to sit down without opening a menu.
</Tip>
