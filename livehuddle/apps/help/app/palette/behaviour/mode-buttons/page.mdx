A Selection Mode button is an element that hands out [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/). Put one on the canvas and anybody can press it to start walking, without ever having opened the mode picker. Avatar is the one mode a button hands out in livehuddle, so it is called the **Avatar Button** in the palette.

It is the board's own way into Avatar mode, beside **Avatar** in the Selection Mode menu, and a space with one opens walking, just below the first, once each visit. To make a board somewhere people walk round, put an Avatar button on it, and an [Avatar Pad](/help/palette/avatar-pads/) where you want them to arrive.

## Adding One

The palette's **Travel** category, one of the [meeting elements](/help/palette/behaviour/), holds the **Avatar Button**, beside the Portal, Bring Focus and the Avatar Pad. Search adds one too: type "avatar".

It drops ready to press, reading "Switch to Avatar". You can type your own label over the derived one ("Walk with me"), and clearing it brings the derived text back.

## What Pressing It Does

Pressing it changes the mode for **the person who pressed it** and nobody else. Everyone else carries on in whatever tool they were already using.

Pressing it again while you are walking hands you back your previous tool, so the button is never a one-way door: while you are in Avatar mode it reads "Leave Avatar" rather than offering it again.

A click on the round icon presses the button; a click anywhere else on the card selects it, and a drag moves it, so you can move it without setting it off.

Your character appears just below the button, rather than in the middle of the screen, so it shows up where you pressed. An Avatar Pad on the tab wins: everyone starts on the pad.

## Where It Works

The button keeps working while you walk, even though Avatar mode makes the rest of the canvas read-only, which is what makes it the way back out. It also works for somebody on a view-only link, since switching your own tool changes nothing for anyone else. That is what makes it useful on a walkthrough: the viewer has the way in right there on the canvas, rather than in a menu they were never told about.

In a read-only embed there is no tool to switch, so the button shows but does nothing.
