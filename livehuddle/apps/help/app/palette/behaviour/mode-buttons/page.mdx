A Selection Mode button is an element that hands out [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/). Put one on the canvas and an editor can press it to start walking; Participants and view-only visitors are always walking, so for them it is just a sign. Avatar is the one mode a button hands out in livehuddle, so it is called the **Avatar Button** in the palette.

It is the board's own way into Avatar mode, beside **Walk** at the start of the palette strip, and a space with one opens walking, just below the first, once each visit. The templates no longer carry one: every Space, and every blank tab you start, has an [Avatar Pad](/help/palette/avatar-pads/) where people arrive instead. Spaces made before keep their **Walk In** button, and it still works; add an Avatar button yourself when you want a visible door on the board.

## Adding One

The palette's **Travel** category, one of the [meeting elements](/help/palette/behaviour/), holds the **Avatar Button**, beside the Portal, Bring Focus and the Avatar Pad. Search adds one too: type "avatar".

It drops ready to press, reading "Switch to Avatar". You can type your own label over the derived one ("Walk with me"), and clearing it brings the derived text back.

## What Pressing It Does

Pressing it changes the mode for **the person who pressed it** and nobody else. Everyone else carries on in whatever tool they were already using.

Pressing it again while you are walking hands you back your previous tool, so the button is never a one-way door: while you are in Avatar mode it reads "Leave Avatar" rather than offering it again.

A click on the round icon presses the button; a click anywhere else on the card selects it, and a drag moves it, so you can move it without setting it off.

Your character appears just below the button, rather than in the middle of the screen, so it shows up where you pressed. An Avatar Pad on the tab wins: everyone starts on the pad.

## Where It Works

The button is for Editors: it keeps working while you walk, even though walking makes the rest of the canvas read-only, which makes it a way back out. Participants are always walking, so for them the button does nothing; hover it to see why.

In a read-only embed there is no walking, so the button shows but does nothing.
