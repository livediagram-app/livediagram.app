Your spaces are the whole point, so it helps to know exactly where they live and who can reach them. livehuddle stores spaces on Cloudflare infrastructure behind an authenticated API, never in a third-party analytics product, and you keep control of them whether you sign in or stay a guest. This page explains how it works; the [Privacy Policy](https://www.liveworkspace.app/privacy), which covers every liveworkspace app, is the formal statement for the hosted service.

## Where your spaces live

Spaces are saved through a single Cloudflare Worker API. The browser never talks to storage directly.

- **Durable storage** is one Cloudflare Durable Object per space, reached only through the API: the space's tabs, comments and the images it places are stored there. Where it is filed, its folders, share links and its Timeline events are kept by liveworkspace's index, the shared library behind every liveworkspace app.
- **Real-time state** (who is in the room, live cursors, in-flight edits) is handled by the same Durable Object, the space's room. It also keeps a working record of collaborative input (comments, reactions and the like) in its own storage, so that saves made at the same moment can be merged.
- **Images** you add are kept by liveworkspace's media service, and a cached picture of each space, used for its thumbnail and live image, in Cloudflare R2 object storage; both are served back through the same API. Location and camera details are stripped from a JPEG before it is stored.

**Or nowhere but your own machine.** A space created in [Offline Mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) lives only in this browser's IndexedDB. It is never stored by the API, so none of the storage above holds a copy of it, and it stays that way until you choose **Sync Space** to move it to the cloud.

livehuddle runs on Cloudflare. Your space content goes to another company only when you use a feature that needs one:

- **[Google Drive](/help/account-and-data/google-drive/)**, if you connect it, gets a copy of your spaces in your own Drive, written by liveworkspace's Drive service (it holds your Google grant, sealed, and never hands it to your browser).
- **Image search** sends what you type in the image picker's **Search** tab straight from your browser to Openverse, the open image search engine; no livehuddle server sees it.
- **Emails** about your work go through the email provider. They name the space, and a mention or an assigned action quotes a little of the comment or action (see [Email Notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/)).

## Calls, cameras and screens

What you say, show on camera or share from your screen in a Space is never stored. It travels from your browser through Cloudflare's realtime service to the people who can hear you, and nowhere else: livehuddle's server sets up each connection but never receives the sound or picture, and nothing is recorded or transcribed.

- **Only when you choose.** The microphone opens the first time you unmute (it starts muted), the camera when you turn it on, and a screen when you pick it, each after your browser asks. A muted microphone is released after 15 seconds with nobody else on your tab, and a screen share carries no sound.
- **A preview stays with you.** A camera or screen turned on with no call joined shows only to you, marked **Only You**; nothing is sent.
- **Who receives it.** The people in earshot, or in the same room, hear you and see your camera and screen. From a stage, the whole tab hears you, and sees you only if the stage shows cameras. Everyone on the tab can see that you are talking, never what you say.
- **Never peer to peer.** Your browser connects to Cloudflare, not to anyone else's browser, so other people never see your IP address.
- **Your character's moves, messages, status, raised hand and knocks** go live to the people in the Space and are never stored.

The [Privacy Policy](https://www.liveworkspace.app/privacy#livehuddle-calls) says the same, formally.

## What is stored, and who can read it

A space is owned by an identity. That identity is either a signed-in account or, for guests, a per-browser id kept in your browser's local storage. Only the owner, people holding a valid [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/), and the members of the [team](/help/collaboration/teams/) whose library the space is in can read a space. A space you [share to the Community](/help/collaboration/sharing/community/) is public until you remove it. Opening a space you cannot access returns a plain "not found", so its contents and name are never revealed.

## Staying in control

- **Guest by default.** You can build and share spaces without ever creating an account. See [working as a guest](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/).
- **Keep it in the browser.** [Offline Mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) keeps a space off the server entirely.
- **Share on your terms.** Add a [share password](/help/privacy-and-security/share-link-security/) to a space or an expiry to a share link, and revoke links at any time.
- **Delete what you own.** Deleting a space moves it to the [Trash](/help/account-and-data/trash/) for 30 days, then removes it with its tabs, comments and share links; you can delete it permanently from the Trash sooner. Signed-in users can [delete their whole account](/help/account-and-data/deleting-your-data/#deleting-your-account), which removes their personal spaces, folders, images, tokens and preferences straight away (spaces in a team library stay with the team).
- **Opt out of analytics.** The anonymous usage events have a switch in Settings. See [What We Collect](/help/privacy-and-security/what-we-collect/).
