A share link (the Share dialog calls each one a **pass**) is a capability: anyone holding the URL has its role, **Editor** or **Participant** (a new pass starts as Participant). A pass made before livehuddle stopped offering Viewer links still works as a **Viewer (Legacy)** pass. That is what makes sharing frictionless, but it also means a link left lying around keeps working. livehuddle gives you two controls to tighten that up, a password on the space and an expiry on each link, plus revoking a link outright. All three live in the **Share** dialog, and none of them ever stops you, the owner.

## Share password

You can put a single password on a space: switch on **Password Protection** in the Share dialog, type it, and choose **Save**. A space shared to the Community can't have a password while its post is public. Once it is set, anyone opening any share link for that space must enter it before they can view, and the password rides along on every later request so their edits stay gated too.

- **One password covers every link** on the space, [embeds](/help/collaboration/sharing/embeds/) included, not one password per link. While a password is set, the live image option is unavailable, because an image can't ask for a password.
- **You always bypass it.** The owner is identified directly, so you never get prompted on your own space. So are members of the [team](/help/collaboration/teams/) whose library holds it.
- **Nobody can read it back.** livehuddle stores only a scrambled (hashed) form of the password, so even you see just **Password Set**. Note it down when you choose it; use **Replace** or **Remove** any time.
- **Changing it takes effect at once.** Setting or replacing the password sends everyone currently in the space through a link back to the password prompt.
- **Guessing is limited.** Share-code and password attempts are rate-limited per network (30 a minute on livehuddle.app), wherever they are tried.

The goal is to stop people guessing or stumbling onto a share URL, not to provide cryptographic protection. See [Share Passwords](/help/collaboration/sharing/share-passwords/) for the full walkthrough.

## Link expiry

When you create a pass, the **Valid** control sets its lifetime, so access ends automatically without you having to remember to revoke it.

| Choice   | Lifetime                |
| -------- | ----------------------- |
| Forever  | no expiry (the default) |
| 1 week   | 7 days                  |
| 1 month  | 30 days                 |
| 6 months | 183 days                |

When a link expires it stops granting access everywhere at once: the share URL, its embed and live image, and the real-time room all behave as if the link were revoked. The expired link does not vanish, though. It moves to the **Expired** section of the Share dialog, where you can delete it or **Extend** it for the same lifetime again, counted from now. A link's lifetime can't be changed after it is created.

<Note>
  Existing live sessions are not force-disconnected at the exact expiry moment; a visitor loses
  access on their next load or save. To cut someone off instantly, revoke the link instead: revoking
  disconnects everyone using it straight away.
</Note>

## Link Previews

Paste a share link into Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, iMessage, WhatsApp, X or LinkedIn and its preview shows the space's name and a picture of its tab: the link's own tab for a [link to one tab](/help/collaboration/sharing/one-tab/), otherwise the first. The picture is the same one a [live image](/help/collaboration/sharing/live-image/) shows, and it updates after the space is saved.

A preview never gives away more than the link does. These show only the plain livehuddle card, with no name and no picture:

- A link to a space with a **share password**.
- A link that is revoked or expired, or to a space in the Trash.
- A space's own address, which only its owner can open, so pasting it in a public channel tells the channel nothing.

So if a space's name or first tab is sensitive, set a share password before you paste its link anywhere public.

<Tip>
  Use the two together for sensitive work: a password stops casual access, and an expiry bounds how
  long even the right people keep it. Keep to a **Participant** pass, the default, unless people
  need to build: Participants walk, talk and react without changing the space. And remember that
  anyone who can see an embed's page source can see its link.
</Tip>
