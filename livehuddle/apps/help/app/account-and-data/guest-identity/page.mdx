The space always works without signing in. You can land on [a new space](/new), build something real, and only later decide whether you want an account. To make that possible, livehuddle gives every guest a quiet, per-browser identity that owns whatever you put on the server.

## Your per-browser id

The first time you visit, your browser takes a quick trip to the liveworkspace accounts site and comes back with a guest token, which it keeps in `localStorage`. The token names your guest id and lasts 45 days, renewed when you visit in its last 15 days. Everything of yours on the server as a guest (spaces you share or sync, folders, images) is owned by that id, which is how the app knows which content to show you when you come back. The editor sends the token with each request; you never see or type it.

Your guest id is liveworkspace's, so the other liveworkspace apps you use in the same browser know you as the same guest.

New spaces you make as a guest start [offline](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/), saved only in this browser, so nothing you try out is sent to our servers until you choose to. To share one, open **Share** and press **Sync Space**: it uploads under your guest id and the Share options appear. A space you create inside a folder goes to the server straight away.

Two things follow from this:

- **It is per browser, not per person.** The id lives in one browser on one device. Open livehuddle in a different browser or on another device and you get a fresh guest id, so your earlier spaces will not appear there.
- **It is not cross-device.** Guest content does not sync between devices, because there is no account tying them together yet.

As a guest you still get the core of livehuddle: saving, share links and real-time collaboration all work, once a space is synced. A few things need an address or an account to attach to, so they ask you to sign in: [teams](/help/collaboration/teams/), [API tokens](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/), [email notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/) and the [Google Drive](/help/account-and-data/google-drive/) mirror.

## What this means for your data

Because your guest content is tied to a single browser, clearing that browser's storage, or using private browsing that discards it, deletes your spaces in This Browser and means the app can no longer recognise you as the owner of the ones on the server. Those are still there, but nothing in a fresh browser can claim them back. Treat your guest spaces as living in that browser.

Share links are the exception: anyone holding one, including you in another browser, can still open that space with the link's role. They just won't be its owner there.

If you want your spaces to follow you across browsers and devices, [signing in](/help/account-and-data/signing-in/) is the answer. When you sign in, whether to a new account or an existing one, the spaces of yours on the server move into it automatically, and we offer to move the ones in This Browser too, so you do not lose the work you have already done.

<Tip>
  Doing serious work as a guest? Create an account before clearing your browser data, and move your
  spaces in This Browser across when we offer, so nothing is left behind.
</Tip>
