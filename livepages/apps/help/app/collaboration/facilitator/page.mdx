A shared document has no idea who is running the session. Two people start countdowns over each other, or a vote ends while people are still placing dots. Making one person the **facilitator** settles it: the tools that run the room answer to them, and everyone else gets on with the work.

Nobody is the facilitator until somebody takes the role, and a session that never uses it behaves exactly as it always has.

## Making someone the facilitator

Click any avatar on a tab (or the **+N** badge) to open **Collaborators**, then use the button on the person's row:

- **Make Facilitator** on somebody else's row. It is offered while nobody is facilitating, to the current facilitator (to hand it on), and always to the document's owner.
- **Take Facilitation** on your own row, when nobody is running the session.
- **Step Down** on your own row when you're the facilitator and you're done.
- **Take Over** on your own row, if you own the document and somebody else is facilitating. The owner can always take the session back.

Whoever is running it wears a **Facilitating** badge on their row in Collaborators. A visitor on a Participant or Viewer share link can't hold the role, because running a session is an editor's job: **Make Facilitator** on their row is greyed out, and its hover card says why. There is one facilitator for the whole document, not one per tab.

Everyone in the room gets a short notification when the role moves, like "Alex is now facilitating" or "Nobody is facilitating now", so nobody has to guess. If you've turned off **In-Editor Notifications** in [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/) › Notifications, you won't see those, and the badge still tells you who has it.

## What the facilitator runs

While somebody holds the role, these are theirs:

- The **timer**: start, pause, extend, reset, clear.
- **Dot voting**: starting a round, revealing it, ending it, stepping through the results.
- **Session buttons** (the Timer, Stopwatch and Dot Vote buttons on a page): pressing one.
- [Freeing an element](/help/collaboration/live-presence/#freeing-an-element-someone-left-selected) somebody has wandered off with selected: right-click it and choose their Release row in the **In use** menu. While nobody facilitates, any Editor can do this.

Everything else is unchanged for everyone: casting your dots, commenting, pointing with the laser, following somebody, presenting a slide deck on your own screen, and every part of editing the document. A tool only the facilitator can answer wouldn't be a tool.

When somebody else is facilitating, the session buttons say who is running the session and can't be pressed, and the [Vote panel](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/vote-panel/) shows its readout without the buttons.

## If they leave or refresh

Refreshing changes nothing: the role comes straight back to them, usually before the page has finished loading. Same for a dropped connection or a closed laptop lid.

If they leave for good, the role is released about a minute and a half later and everyone is told, so the tools open up again rather than staying locked behind somebody who has gone. The document's owner can also take it back at any moment, and when everyone leaves the document the role is simply over: the next session starts with nobody facilitating.

The facilitator role is about who is driving, not a permission: it keeps two well-meaning people from starting timers at once rather than defending the document from anyone. A [document in This Browser](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) has no live room, so it has no facilitator.

<Tip>
  Building the document and running the session are different jobs. Make the document, then hand the
  facilitator role to whoever is running the meeting; as the owner you keep the ability to take it
  back.
</Tip>
