A tab of pages shows everything at once, which is exactly what you want for reference and exactly what you don't want when you are narrating it. **Slide Deck** turns your pages into a talk: a set of slides, each one a page, presented full screen one at a time.

Choose it from the tool picker at the start of the palette strip, in the **Present** band beside **Laser** and **Spotlight**. Like them it needs something to work with, so it is disabled on an empty canvas. Slide Deck is also **desktop only**: a phone screen has no room for the deck list and the canvas side by side, so the mode isn't offered there. A deck you built on a desktop browser is kept, ready for the next time you open the document on one.

Picking the tool does **not** start presenting. It opens the **Slide Deck** panel, where you build and check the deck; starting is a deliberate second act. The **Slides** button in the bottom-right corner opens the same panel.

The cog in the panel's header opens the same presenter settings as the cog you get while presenting (transitions, auto-advance, looping and the rest), so you can set them before you start rather than in front of the room. See [The Presenter's Controls](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenter-controls/#presenter-settings).

## What a slide is

**A slide is a page.** It shows whatever is on that page now, framed to exactly the page, so it follows your edits without you touching the deck.

- **Every [slide page](/help/pages/slide-pages/) is already in the deck**, from the moment you make it, in page order. Move a page and its slide moves with it.
- **Any other page joins a page at a time**: pick it from the list at the bottom of the panel and press **Add as slide**.
- **The deck spans tabs.** Slide 1 can be a page on one tab and slide 2 a page on another.

See [Exporting and Presenting Pages](/help/pages/exporting-pages/#turning-pages-into-slides) for how a page slide behaves.

Everything else about building, the slide menu, hiding a slide from the run, reordering, notes and time budgets, is in [Building a Deck](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/building-a-deck/).

## Presenting it

Press **Present** at the bottom of the panel and the deck opens full screen at slide 1 with every part of the editor hidden. Its badge counts the slides that will run, and it stays greyed out until the deck has at least one slide that isn't hidden. See [Presenting](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenting/) for how a slide is framed, how you move through the deck, and what you can and cannot touch while it runs, and [The Presenter's Controls](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenter-controls/) for the strip in the corner, the pacing clock and the settings behind the cog.

**Starting a presentation broadcasts nothing.** Nobody else is pulled into your deck and nobody's view moves. The way you show a deck to people is the way you already show them everything else in the meeting: **share your screen**.

**The deck itself is ordinary document data**, though: a teammate who opens the document sees your slides in their own Slide Deck panel, and can edit them. It saves and travels exactly like the document's name does.

Building and presenting a deck needs edit access. People on a view-only or participant [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/) don't get the Slide Deck tool, the Slides button or the Slide Deck command, so to show them a deck, present it yourself and share your screen.

<Tip>
  Build the deck as you would an agenda: one slide per point you intend to make, in the order you
  intend to make them.
</Tip>
