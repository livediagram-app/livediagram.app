A **Lane** is a wide band with a titled gutter down one edge, for cross-functional flows: a role, a team or a system per lane, with its steps laid inside.

The Pages palette doesn't show a lane, but the [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/) (`⌘K` or `Ctrl+K`) adds one: type its name and pick it under **Add to canvas**. See [Build Elements](/help/palette/build/). A new lane is 900 by 200, titled "Lane".

## Take the tour

The first time you select a lane, a small **Show Me** toolbar appears beside its toolbar. Press **Play** for a short walkthrough on your own lane: its title gutter, the seam you drag to size it, what it carries, and turning it into a column. Press the cross if you would rather not; either way it is not offered again. To be offered it again, open **Settings**, then **Tours**, open **Show Me** and switch **Lane** back on.

## Dragging a lane takes its contents

Drag a lane and everything **inside** it comes along. That is the difference between a lane and a plain rectangle: a swimlane is a container, so moving the band moves the work.

<Figure caption="Dragging a lane carries every element whose centre it holds; one straddling the edge with its centre outside stays put.">
  <LaneCarry />
</Figure>

The rules are the same ones a [frame](/help/palette/build/frames/) follows:

- An element belongs to a lane when its **centre** is inside the band. Something straddling the edge travels if its centre is in, and stays if its centre is out.
- If an element sits inside two containers, it belongs to the **smaller** one. A frame or lane nested inside the lane travels with it, carrying its own contents.
- Pinned arrows between elements follow them, and a loose arrow travels when both of its free ends are inside.

Resizing a lane is deliberately different: it resizes the band and leaves the contents where they are. A lane always draws behind the elements on it, so dropping one over a finished flow never hides it.

## The title and its gutter

The title is an ordinary element label, so you edit, format and align it like any other. The tinted gutter is its backdrop and **follows its alignment**:

- Align the title left or right and the gutter runs down that side.
- Centre it at the top or bottom and the gutter becomes a band across that edge, which turns the lane into a **column** with a header, for a row of columns rather than a stack of rows.

Drag the seam between the gutter and the body to make the gutter wider or narrower. It snaps to the seams of other lanes, so a stack of lanes keeps its headings lined up. To recolour the gutter, right-click the lane and pick a **Heading** colour in the **Colours** section; left unset, it is a light wash of the lane's own border colour.

For a lane with its gutter down a side, **Upright Title** under the alignment grid (right-click, then **Text**, then **Alignment**) turns the title a quarter so it reads from bottom to top in a slim strip, the way BPMN tools draw swimlanes.

## Stacking lanes

Lanes are placed like anything else and are not auto-stacked; snapping and the [alignment guides](/help/palette/alignment-guides/) make butting one under another straightforward. Flush lanes look right: a lane's outline is a single hairline, so two touching lanes do not double into a heavier line.

<Tip>
  Build the flow first and drop the lanes underneath it afterwards. Because a lane picks up whatever
  sits inside it, sizing a band around a finished row is often quicker than placing the band and
  then working inside it.
</Tip>
