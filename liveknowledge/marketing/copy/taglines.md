# Taglines

Short one-liners grouped by character budget, for directory listings, app store
fields, social bios, and the like. Pick the longest one that fits the field's
limit. Counts in brackets are exact character counts including spaces.

Every line is built from [`facts.md`](facts.md). Don't add a claim that isn't
there. No em dashes. **Knowledgebase** is one word, and the product is never
called a wiki.

## Ultra-short, <= 30 characters

For tight bios, badges, app subtitles.

- Knowledge your team trusts. [27]
- A knowledgebase agents read. [28]
- Verified pages, cited answers. [30]
- For people and for AI. [22]
- Write it down. Keep it right. [29]

## Short, <= 40 characters

Classic "tagline" slot on most directories.

- A team knowledgebase, for people and AI. [40]
- Pages your team and its agents trust. [37]
- Verified pages. Cited answers. [30]
- Free, open source, no sign-up wall. [35]

## Medium, <= 60 characters

Listing headlines, Open Graph titles, search-result titles.

- A team knowledgebase, for people and for AI. [44]
- The knowledgebase your team and its AI both trust. [50]
- Verified pages, cited answers, and agents that read them. [57]
- Team knowledge with owners, reviews and an MCP server. [54]

## Long, <= 80 characters

Hero subheads, Twitter/X header copy, longer headline fields.

- A team knowledgebase where every page has an owner and every answer a source. [77]
- Pages with owners and verified dates, read the same way by people and agents. [77]
- A free, open-source knowledgebase with cited answers on your own Claude key. [76]

## Extra-long, <= 120 characters

Meta descriptions' first clause, longer directory blurbs.

- A free, open-source team knowledgebase: verified pages, cited answers on your own Claude key, and an MCP server. [112]
- Write your team's knowledge as pages with owners and reviews, then let people and agents ask it, with sources. [110]

## The pitch line

Use it whole wherever one sentence is asked for:

> A team knowledgebase, for people and for AI.
