_Last updated: 23 October 2026._

livedesign is built to need as little of your data as possible, and the whole codebase is public so you can check that for yourself. This policy explains what the hosted service at **livedesign.app** does with your data. It sits alongside our [Terms of Service](/help/policies/terms/). If you self-host livedesign, you are responsible for your own deployment and this policy does not apply to it.

## What we store

- **Your designs.** The content you create is saved so it is there when you come back, and so the people you share it with can see it. Each design is stored on its own on Cloudflare, with its comments beside what they are about. A design you create in [Offline Mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) is the exception: it stays in your browser and never reaches our servers, unless you ask the AI assistant about it (see **AI features**), or add a web link to it (our server fetches the link's preview).
- **Images you upload.** Images placed in a design are kept in Cloudflare object storage and served back through the same API. Before a JPEG is stored, identifying metadata such as GPS location and camera details is stripped from it, so it never reaches anyone you share the design with.
- **A guest id.** If you use livedesign without an account, liveworkspace.app gives your browser a random guest id, the same in every liveworkspace app, to link your designs to your browser. It is not tied to your name or email. If you later sign in or create an account, what you made as a guest becomes your account's.
- **Your display name and colour.** The name and cursor colour collaborators see are stored on our server against your guest id or account.
- **Account details, if you sign in.** Sign-in is handled by liveworkspace accounts (liveworkspace.app), the one sign-in shared by our apps, which emails you a code to confirm your address. If you create an account we receive your email address and the name on your account. We use these to identify your account and to send you the account and onboarding emails described under **Emails we send** below.
- **API tokens, if you create them.** [API tokens](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/) are made on liveworkspace.app and work in every liveworkspace app. liveworkspace stores a one-way hash of each token (never the token itself), its name, whether it is read-only, and when it was last used. Tokens expire after six months and are deleted with your account. When a token is used with livedesign, livedesign keeps a note of that token (never the secret) and of anything paired through it, such as a workbench.
- **Connecting an AI tool, if you do.** If you connect an AI tool via our [MCP server](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/), we store the app's registration (its name and redirect address, kept for 30 days), short-lived authorization sessions and codes that expire within minutes, and, for an hour, a count of registrations from your IP address to stop abuse. The connection itself is an API token, covered above, and you can revoke it any time.
- **Your teams.** Teams are run on liveworkspace.app, which stores each team's name and its members' accounts, email addresses and roles, and sends team invitations. livedesign keeps no copy of your teams: liveworkspace decides who may open a team's work.
- **Your Explorer.** liveworkspace.app keeps one list of what you make in every liveworkspace app, so the [Explorer](/help/explorer/) can show all of it: each design's title, owner, team, folder, tags, favourite and Trash state, its share links, when you last opened it, and a small summary for its card. For the Inbox it also keeps, for each comment thread and action, who is in it, whether it is resolved or done, and the start of the latest comment (up to 500 characters).
- **Basic operational logs.** Our host, Cloudflare, keeps standard request logs that help us run the service securely.

## AI features

The AI features run only when you use them, and only on a deployment that has an AI provider configured.

- **The AI assistant.** When you ask the assistant something, your prompt, the tab's name, the elements on the current tab (their text, shapes and positions) and your last few messages in that conversation are sent through our API to **OpenAI**, which writes the answer. Nothing is sent until you ask, and the assistant is off until you turn it on in Settings.
- **Connected AI tools.** If you [connect an AI tool](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/) such as Claude, that tool reads and changes your designs through our API using the token you approved. What the tool does with what it reads is governed by that tool's own terms and privacy policy, not this one.

We do not use your designs to train AI models. The providers above process what is sent to them under their own API terms.

## What we do not do

We do not use tracking pixels, advertising, or third-party analytics SDKs. We do not sell your data, and we do not build advertising profiles. The repository is public, so what runs is what you can read.

## Anonymous product analytics

To understand which features actually help, we record anonymous, first-party product events, for example "a design was created" or "a square was added". Each event is just three fixed labels: a category, an action, and an optional type like "Square" or "Edit". They never include anything that identifies you or your work: no names, no design or element content, no account id, no IP address. Page views are counted the same way: which page was opened, with ids and anything after a `?` or `#` removed in your browser before it is sent. When one of our own buttons opens the New Design page, we count which button it was (from a fixed list, like the landing page's hero) and whether a design followed; the label is removed from the address straight away. Error health is counted the same way too: a failed request or a browser-side exception is recorded only as a generic status or kind, never a message or stack trace. How long key moments take (a design opening, a save, a page loading) is counted the same way: only the range it fell in, like "under one second", never the exact time. Events are deleted after 60 days.

To count sign-ups and sign-ins once each, our server also remembers which sign-in sessions it has already counted, for 90 days, and that an account has been seen before (deleted when you delete your account). These records are never published; only the totals are.

We collect this ourselves, through liveworkspace's own telemetry service (liveworkspace is the family of apps livedesign belongs to), which stores it in its own database beside the same anonymous events from our other apps, each labelled with the app it came from. We use no third-party analytics vendors. The data is never sold or shared, and the only place it goes beyond our own use is our public [telemetry dashboard](/telemetry) at liveworkspace.app, where you can see exactly the same anonymous totals we do, for livedesign or for all our apps together. You can turn off the events your browser sends at any time from Settings (see [What We Collect](/help/privacy-and-security/what-we-collect/)). A few anonymous totals are counted by our server and can't be turned off: sign-ups and sign-ins, first visits to a share link, multiplayer sessions, emails sent, and server errors.

## Cookies and local storage

If you use livedesign without an account, liveworkspace.app sets a cookie holding your guest id. We use local storage to keep your guest id, and your preferences (also saved to our database so they follow you). If you sign in, we set cookies to keep you signed in and to remember that you are; no sign-in script loads for visitors who don't sign in. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies.

## Emails we send

If you have an account, we use your email address to send you a small number of emails:

- **Account and service emails**: a confirmation when you delete your account. Team invitations and API token reminders come from liveworkspace.app.
- **Notifications about your work**: when someone opens a design you shared for the first time, comments on a design you own, assigns you an action, or mentions you in a comment. A mention email quotes the comment (up to about 280 characters) and an assigned-action email includes the action's name and the start of its description; the others name the design but not its contents.
- **A short onboarding series**: a welcome message, a few tips during your first two weeks, a nudge if you have not made a design after a few days or have been away for about four weeks, and the occasional milestone.

You can turn off every notification, tip and milestone email in **Settings**, under **Account**, then **Notifications**, or from the link at the bottom of each one (see [Email Notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/)). We do not send advertising or marketing beyond these, and we never share your address other than with our email provider for the sole purpose of delivering them. Apart from team invitations and the emails that follow from them, people who use livedesign without an account are never emailed.

## Service providers

We rely on a small number of providers to run the service:

- **Cloudflare**, for hosting, database storage and image storage.
- **Resend**, for delivering every email described above, and the sign-in codes liveworkspace.app emails you.
- **OpenAI**, for the AI assistant (only when you use it; see **AI features** above).

These providers process data on our behalf so that we can run the service.

One more service is involved only when you use it: **Openverse**, run by the WordPress Foundation, answers searches in the image picker's Search tab. Your browser sends your search words to Openverse directly, so Openverse sees them and your IP address under its own privacy policy; our servers never see the search. A picture you pick is downloaded from its original host and then stored like any image you upload.

Your browser also loads the editor's fonts from **Google Fonts**. A YouTube video's poster image, and a link card's icon and image, load from the provider when a design is viewed; other embeds (Vimeo, Loom, Figma, Google Docs, any website) load only when a viewer presses Load. Either way the provider sees the viewer's IP address. Link previews are fetched by our server from the linked site.

## Sharing and visibility

Anything you put in a design you share becomes visible to people who have the share link. Share links are kept on liveworkspace.app, with the rest of your Explorer. You can protect a link with a password or give it an expiry, and revoking a link stops further access through it. See [Share Link Security](/help/privacy-and-security/share-link-security/).

## Keeping and deleting your data

You can delete any design you own at any time. A deleted design waits in the [Trash](/help/account-and-data/trash/) for 30 days, so you can restore it, and is then removed for good; you can also delete it permanently from the Trash sooner. Its comments are removed with it. A design left empty and untouched for 30 days moves to the Trash on its own.

You can delete your account, and the data associated with it, yourself: open **Settings**, then **Account**, then **Delete Account**. This closes your liveworkspace account for every app at once: the designs and everything else you own in every liveworkspace app (the Trash included), your folders, tags, favourites, images and preferences, your account details, sessions and API tokens are deleted straight away, and you leave every team. Designs that belong to a team stay with the team. See [Deleting Your Data](/help/account-and-data/deleting-your-data/).

Other things are kept only as long as they are useful: anonymous product events for 60 days, changes made by connected AI tools for 30 days (so they can be undone), and an uploaded image that no design uses is removed once it is more than 30 days old. A guest's designs have no account to delete: remove them in the Explorer, or ask us.

## Security

We protect your data with authentication and access controls on every request, and the code is public so the controls can be checked. If you find a security vulnerability, please report it privately as described in [Reporting a Vulnerability](/help/policies/report-a-vulnerability/).
