livedesign is for teams who think visually and need to do that thinking **together**. A single-player diagramming tool is not the point. The product shines when several people are on the same canvas at once, building one shared diagram in [real time](/help/collaboration/sharing/). That said, it works just as well for one person sketching something out, and the [canvas always works without signing in](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/).

## The teams it serves

A few groups feel right at home:

- **Engineering teams** sketching architecture, sequence flows, and system designs. Use [shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [arrows](/help/palette/arrows/), [technology icons](/help/palette/technology/) for cloud and infrastructure marks, and [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/) to keep related views together.
- **Product and design teams** mapping user journeys, information architecture, and brainstorms with [shapes](/help/palette/shapes/), [text and fonts](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/), and [themes](/help/canvas/themes/).
- **Cross-functional groups** in workshops, planning sessions, and retrospectives, where everyone needs to contribute at once and see each other's input live. [Plan mode](/help/canvas/plan-mode/) turns a tab into a board of cards for sprints, triage and roadmaps, and [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) puts timers, dot votes and polls in front of whoever runs the session.
- **Anyone making something to hand out**: [Pages mode](/help/canvas/pages-mode/) lays a tab out as pages for infographics, posters and one-pagers you can export or present.

## Whether you sign in or not

livedesign is friction-free on purpose. A first-time visitor can build a real diagram as a [guest](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/) and only later create an account to sync across devices and join [teams](/help/collaboration/teams/). A signed-in user can still hand a share link to a guest who edits without any account at all.
