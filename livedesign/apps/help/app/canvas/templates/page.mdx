A blank canvas is freeing, but sometimes you would rather start with structure already in place. Templates give you a ready-made document as a starting point, filled with believable example content, so you can begin from something shaped roughly like what you need and adapt it, instead of building every diagram from nothing.

## Starting from a Template

When you [create a new document](/new), the **New Document** wizard asks what you are making: pick a mode (Diagram, Draw, Workshop, Pages or Plan) and the document is made there and then, saved where new documents go (see [Default Folders](/help/explorer/folders/default-folders/)). The empty tab then opens on that mode's start, **Start Diagramming**, **Start a Workshop**, **Start Making Pages** or **Start Planning**: its **Recommended** templates first, then every template of that mode in groups beside them, with a search. Click a template and the tab fills with it; from there it is an ordinary document, to move, edit, add to and delete from as on any canvas.

Every new tab, and every tab you empty, offers the same start. Close it with its **×** to begin from a blank canvas; while the tab is still empty, **Show Templates** in the strip under the palette brings it back. Picked the wrong mode? The mode switch at the top of the start's list changes it, Plan included, while the tab is empty. In Draw the start is **Start Drawing**, with the pen already in hand, and since Draw has a dock rather than the palette, **Show Templates** sits in a pill at the top of the canvas.

The search at the top of a start looks in every mode, not just the tab's own. Type "sailboat" on Start Planning and the Sailboat retrospective turns up under **In Other Modes**, in a Workshop block, its card saying **Opens in Workshop**: click it and the tab switches to Workshop with the template on it. Each mode shows up to six matches, with **Show All** for the rest, and pressing the down arrow in the search box takes you to the first card it found.

**Search templates...** finds a template by its name, its description, its category, or the everyday words people use for it: try "sprint", "1:1", "retro", "invoice", "mortgage", "crm", "erd" or "persona". Every word you type has to match.

Five blanks lead the **Popular** shelf, one for each way of working, and sit apart from the categories since starting from nothing is not a kind of diagram: **Blank Diagram** (an empty canvas), **Blank Whiteboard** (a board in [Draw mode](/help/canvas/draw-mode/) for free drawing without distractions), **Blank Session** (an empty tab in [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/), to run a retro, town hall or workshop with timers, votes and polls to hand), **Blank Illustration** (an empty page in [Pages mode](/help/canvas/pages-mode/), for an infographic, a logo or a wireframe) and **Blank Plan** (an empty tab in [Plan mode](/help/canvas/plan-mode/) that opens on **Start Planning**, to pick the board (or Sheet) that fits the work). A sixth blank, **Blank Spreadsheet**, makes a tab holding one empty Sheet, ready to type into, a seventh, **Blank Form**, makes an empty [form](/help/canvas/plan-mode/forms/) for you to choose a board or a sheet for, and more start Pages on one empty page of a kind you already know: **Blank Infographic**, **Blank Logo**, and the five blank [wireframes](/help/canvas/pages-mode/wireframe-pages/), **Blank Phone Wireframe**, **Blank Tablet Wireframe**, **Blank Website Wireframe**, **Blank Laptop Wireframe** and **Blank Watch Wireframe**. Search for them, find them first in their groups on **Start Making Pages**, or pick them from **Blank Slate** on the livedesign home page. After the blanks, Popular shows a few of the templates most people start from; choose a mode and Popular changes to that mode's favourites, its blank first.

## Finding Templates for a Mode

Every template opens in one mode: **Diagram**, **Draw**, **Workshop**, **Pages** or **Plan**, and each mode's start lists its own. To browse another mode's, switch the tab's mode with the switch at the top of the start's list while the tab is empty, or just search: a template from another mode shows which mode it opens in.

- **Draw** templates are drawn by hand, ready to draw over: **Sketchnote**, **Rich Picture**, **Comic Strip**, **Doodle Warm-Up**, **Paper Prototype**, **Journey Doodle**, **Pre-Mortem** and **Idea Garden**.
- **Workshop** templates are sessions to run with your team: **Blank Session**, **Retrospective**, **Start, Stop, Continue**, **Mad, Sad, Glad**, **4Ls Retrospective**, **Sailboat Retrospective**, **Town Hall Q&A**, **Lean Coffee**, **Crazy 8s**, the quizzes and games (**Pub Quiz**, **Icebreaker Questions**, **Two Truths and a Lie**, **Would You Rather** and **Find Someone Who Bingo**), the spaces to walk round together in Avatar mode (**Virtual Office**, **Coworking Space**, **Team Pod** and **All-Hands Hall**; see [Virtual Office](/help/palette/virtual-office/)), **Meeting Agenda**, **Workshop Board**, **Brainstorm Board**, the boards that come with a dot vote, **Affinity Map**, **Prioritization Matrix**, **Risk Matrix** and **Value Proposition Canvas**, and the sticky-note boards a group fills in together, such as **SWOT**, **Empathy Map** and **User Journey**. See [Workshop Mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/).
- **Pages** templates open on pages of their own: **Logo Design**, **Group Card**, **Event Poster**, **Year in Review**, **Résumé**, **Recipe Card**, **Data Story**, **How It Works**, **Versus** and **Social Carousel**.
- **Plan** templates set up a way of working across several tabs, with example cards to show how it runs: **Kanban Board**, **Project Planner**, **Bug Tracker**, **Team Retro**, **Weekly Planner**, **Content Calendar**, **Hiring Pipeline**, **OKRs**, **Product Launch** and **Feedback Board**; or a spreadsheet ready to use: **Budget Planner**, **Timesheet**, **Contact List**, **Task Tracker**, **Invoice**, **Expense Report**, **Loan Calculator**, **Sales Pipeline**, **Inventory**, **Guest List**, **Grade Book** and **Habit Tracker**.
- Every other template is a **Diagram** template.

Three Diagram templates come with a live spreadsheet on a second tab, worked from the same numbers the drawing shows: the **Threat Model** opens a **Threat Register** that scores and rates each threat, the **Metric Tree** a **Driver Model** where a what-if shows what it does to the north star, and the **Value Stream Map** a **Flow Data** sheet that works out lead time and flow efficiency. The second tab is an ordinary [Sheet](/help/canvas/plan-mode/sheets/) in Plan mode; the drawing and the sheet start out agreeing, and each is edited on its own after that.

Each one arrives as real elements rather than a picture: a Gantt chart's bars are shapes you drag, a wireframe's frames are [device frames](/help/palette/devices/) you can rearrange. Nothing in a template is locked or special.

## Changing the Theme

A new document starts on the Default theme, and a template picked on a start takes the tab's current theme. Once you are on the canvas, click the **Theme & canvas** button in the bottom-right corner to open the **Tab Style** dialog and try a different [theme](/help/canvas/themes/), including any of your [custom themes](/help/canvas/themes/custom-themes/): it recolours the whole template at once.

## Making It Your Own

A template is just a head start, not a constraint. Everything in it is fully editable: rename and recolour the elements, restyle the whole thing with a different theme, or strip out the parts you do not need. Because the document works the same as any other, you can also add [tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/), invite others, and share it once you have shaped it into what you want.

<Tip>
  Templates pair naturally with themes: pick a layout that fits your content, then try a few themes
  from the Tab Style dialog to find the look you want. Double-click a theme to apply it and close
  the dialog in one go.
</Tip>
