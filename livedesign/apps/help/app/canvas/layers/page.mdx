Every tab starts with a single layer, and for most pages that's all it ever has. Layers are stacking bands: everything on a higher layer paints above everything on a lower one. They are saved with the tab and sync live, so everyone sees the same stack.

You don't manage layers yourself. A tab is laid out by its pages, so livedesign has no Layers panel and no setting for one; the stack builds itself as you bring things forward and send them back. (A [photo page](/help/pages/photo-pages/) has layers of its own, in the photo editor's **Layers** tab.)

## How Layers Are Made

**Bring to Front** and **Send to Back** in the right-click menu's **Layer** category (and **Cmd+Shift+]** / **Cmd+Shift+[**, Ctrl on Windows and Linux) work through layers: the selection moves onto the top (or bottom) layer, and a fresh layer is created first when that layer holds anything else. A layer those buttons empty out is removed automatically.

The **Bring to Front** and **Send to Back** buttons on the selection's floating toolbar are different: they restack the selection within its own layer and never move it to another. See [Layer Order and Opacity](/help/canvas/layer-order/).

## Layers a Tab Already Has

A tab that came with layers (from a template, an import, or another app) keeps them exactly as saved: their order, their opacity, and any layer that was hidden or locked. A hidden layer stays off the page and out of every [export](/help/account-and-data/exporting-designs/) and shared thumbnail, and a layer's opacity carries through to exports. What sits on a locked layer can't be selected. New elements go on the top layer that is neither hidden nor locked; while there is none, adding is paused and a notice explains why.

## Who Can Change Layers

Changing the stack is editing, so it needs edit access to the tab. Viewers and Participants see the same stack but can't change it.

<Note>
  You never have to think about layers to use livedesign: a tab with one layer behaves exactly the
  same, and Bring to Front and Send to Back do the layering for you.
</Note>
