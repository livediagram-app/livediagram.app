In Pages mode, export and present work in **pages**, not the content of the whole tab.

## Exporting

Open **Export** as usual (see [Exporting a Tab](/help/tabs/export-tabs/)). Pages mode offers the formats that suit pages: **PDF**, **PNG**, **SVG** and **GIF**, plus **JSON** to keep a copy of the tab. Markdown is not offered, since a page is not an outline.

Pick a format and choose **All pages** or **One page** above the preview:

- **PDF** starts on **All pages**: one PDF with every page in order, each at its own size and orientation. Paper sizes come out at print size: an A4 page is a true A4 page. With **One page** you get a one-page PDF of the page you pick.
- **PNG** and **SVG** start on **One page**, the first by default, and you get that page's image. With **All pages** you get a **.zip** holding one image per page, numbered in page order (`01 Page 1 - A4 - Portrait - Infographic.png`, ...).

A line under the choice says what the download will be ("3 pages, one PDF.", "A .zip of 3 PNG files, one per page.").

The file is named after the document and tab, plus the page's label when you export one page. With All pages, the page list picks which page the preview shows.

Each page exports **exactly its sheet**: its size, its background and pattern, and what is on it, cut off at its edges just as the canvas shows it. The space around the pages is never included. The plain paper exports white, except on a [logo page](/help/canvas/pages-mode/logo-pages/), whose PNG and SVG leave it transparent. The preview shows the page you picked.

The **Isometric view** and **Background pattern** switches are not offered for pages: a page is its own background, and it is never tilted.

### Animated GIF

**GIF** makes one animated image that plays anywhere a picture does: a chat message, an email, a README or a social post. Everything that moves on a page moves in the GIF, exactly as it does on the canvas: an animated background mid-motion, and every element animation. A page's animations start from the beginning each time it comes round, as when a slide opens while presenting.

- **Pages**: **All pages** plays them in order and loops back to the first; **One page** loops that page alone.
- **Each page holds**: how long every page shows, from 1 to 20 seconds. It starts at the deck's **Auto-Advance** time when that is on, otherwise 3 seconds.
- **Size**: **Small**, **Medium** or **Large**, the page's longer side at 480, 960 or 1440 pixels.
- Between pages, the GIF uses the transition and speed set in the **Slide Deck** settings, so it moves from page to page as your presentation does, including from the last page back to the first.

The panel shows a still preview of the page and an estimate of the file size, with a note when it is large for chat and email. **Download GIF** makes the file, showing its progress; **Cancel** stops it and nothing downloads. A GIF can run up to 3 minutes in all; past that, shorten the hold or export one page. A page where nothing moves costs a single frame, so a GIF of mostly still pages stays small.

GIFs are made to move, so your **Reduce Motion** setting never stills one. A logo page's see-through paper comes out white, and a video shows its poster, as in a PNG.

## Importing

In Pages mode, **Import** takes **JSON** only: a livedesign tab, pages and all. A Markdown outline becomes a diagram; switch to Diagram mode to import one.

## Turning Pages Into Slides

Pages are often presented, so their deck is one press away: in the bottom-right corner, where the Layers button sits in the other modes, the **Slides** button opens the Slide Deck panel just above it.

In Pages mode **every page is already in the deck**, in page order, from the moment you make it: move a page and its slide moves with it, and drag a slide in the Slide Deck panel and its page moves to match. A page slide is the page itself:

- It shows whatever is on the page **now**, so it follows your edits, a new layout, a resize or a reorder without you touching the deck.
- It is framed to **exactly** the page.
- Its row reads the tab and the page ("Tab 1 · Page 2 · A4 · Portrait"), and its thumbnail is the page on its background.
- A page's own slide can be **hidden** but not deleted: hide it to keep it out of the talk. Delete the page and its slides leave the deck too; undo the delete and they come back as they were, notes and all.
- To show a page twice, pick it from the list and press **Add as slide**. That copy can be deleted like any slide. (Adding slides is for Editors: a Viewer or Participant can't.)

Page slides sit in the same deck as any other slides, so a deck can mix pages with slides built from other tabs. Name them, add notes, hide or reorder them as in [Building a Deck](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/building-a-deck/).

### The Deck Button

Each page's title bar holds its **deck button**, **Hide in Slides** (an open eye): press it to keep the page in the deck but skip it when presenting. The eye is then crossed, and **Show in Slides** brings it back. It is the same switch as the slide's visibility in the Slide Deck panel.

Beside it is **Add Notes**. Press it to write your speaker notes for that page's slide: they are the same notes the Slide Deck panel shows, and you see them in the presenter view, never on the page itself. Once a slide has notes the button reads **Notes**. The deck button and Add Notes are for Editors on a computer: Viewers and Participants see the pages without them, and they are not shown on a phone.

## Presenting

Press **Present** and each page fills the screen on its own: no labels, cogs or buttons around it, and none of its neighbouring pages beside it. A 16:9 page fills a 16:9 screen exactly; any other shape sits between black bars, as a projector shows it. Everything else about presenting is as for any deck; see [Presenting](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenting/).
