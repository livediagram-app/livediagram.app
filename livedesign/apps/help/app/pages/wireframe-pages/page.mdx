A **wireframe page** is a kind of page ([Working with Pages](/help/pages/working-with-pages/)): a screen to wireframe on, framed by the **device** it is for. The page is the device, drawn front on at its real size, and everything you put on the page lives on its screen.

## Adding a Wireframe Page

- Choose **Wireframe** when a new tab's first page asks what it is for. The tab then holds screens alone: the **+** after the last page adds another screen like it, and the button under it, **Add a Screen of Another Device**, adds one in another device.
- Then **Choose a Device**: an **iPhone**, an **iPhone Duo** (Apple's foldable, opened to one wide 890 x 626 screen), an **Android** phone, an **iPad** or **Android** tablet, a **Laptop**, a **Desktop** monitor, a **Browser** window, or a **Watch**. Each screen is the device's real size in pixels (an iPhone is 393 x 852), so a wireframe is drawn at the size it will be seen.
- A new wireframe page after another takes the same device and frame, so a flow of screens stays consistent.

## Changing the Device

Beside the page's cog sits the **Device** button, showing the page's device. It opens:

- **Device**: switch to any other device. What is on the screen comes with it, scaled down as one only if it no longer fits: the words in buttons, fields and other labelled shapes shrink with their boxes, so the screen reads the same, only smaller.
- **Orientation**: turn a phone or tablet to **Landscape**. The screen's content is fitted the same way.
- **Frame**: **Theme** (the default) draws the device in your tab theme's colours, browser toolbar included, so it changes when the theme does; or choose **Dark**, **Light**, or **Outline** for a low-fidelity line drawing.
- **System Bars**: the status bar (9:41, signal, Wi-Fi and battery) and the home indicator, on by default. They are drawn over your content, as a real screen draws them; turn them off for a bare screen.

The same choices are in the page's cog panel, on **Device**. A **Browser** page's address bar shows the page's name, so naming the page **Pricing** shows `example.com/pricing`.

## The Screen

- The page's **Background** paints the screen. A **Grid** pattern makes a handy layout grid.
- Anything that reaches past the screen's edge is cut off at it, rounded corners included, never drawn over the frame.
- Elements snap to the screen's edges and centre lines, and to its **safe area**: the screen less its system bars and a gutter (16 px on a phone, 24 on a tablet, 32 on a computer).

## Starting From a Screen

The **Layout** button above a wireframe page offers **forty screens** in nine categories, each built for the device you chose: a single column on a phone, sidebars and columns on a tablet or computer.

- **Start**: Onboarding, Sign In, Sign Up, Verify Code, Permissions and an Empty State.
- **Browse**: a Home Feed, Search Results, an Article and Places on a map.
- **Shop**: a Shop Grid, a Product Detail, a Cart, a Checkout and Order Tracking.
- **Social**: a Profile, a Post with comments, a Stories Feed, a New Post, a Chat and Notifications.
- **Work**: a Dashboard, a Kanban Board, a Task List, a Calendar, an Inbox and a Data Table.
- **Media**: a Music Player, a Video Player, a Podcast and a Photo Gallery.
- **Account**: Settings, Edit Profile and a Help Center.
- **Marketing**: a Landing Page, Pricing, a Team page, a Contact form and a Blog.
- **Travel**: a Flight Search, a Boarding Pass and a Ride.

A watch has nine of its own: a **Glance**, a **List**, a **Workout**, **Now Playing**, **Weather**, a **Timer**, **Heart Rate**, **Up Next** and a **Notification**. Every part of a layout is an ordinary element you can edit, move or delete.

## The Elements Palette

On a wireframe page the palette changes to suit it: **Components** and **Elements** come first on the strip, and Elements opens by itself. Move to another kind of page and the palette changes with you. It holds the parts a screen is made of: a **Button** and an **Outline Button**, a **Text Field** and a **Search Field**, a **Chip**, a **Badge** (with its presets: New, Beta, Version, Count, Draft and Warning), an **Image Placeholder**, an **Avatar**, a **Card**, a **Checklist** and a **Divider**. Text and sticky notes for comments on the design are in **Write**.

## Handing It to Developers

When the screens are agreed, the people building them can read every number straight off the wireframe. Press **Inspect** in the bottom-right corner (it is there on every wireframe tab, for every role) and click any element: the canvas measures its spacing to its neighbours and the screen's edges, and the **Inspect** panel lists its size, position, colours, border and type, ready to copy as CSS or export as a PNG or SVG. **Copy Inspect Link** shares a link that opens with Inspect already on. See [Inspect](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/inspect/).

## Exporting

Export a wireframe page as PNG, SVG or PDF from **Export**: you get the whole device with your screen in it. PNG and SVG are see-through around the device, ready to drop into a deck or a design.
