A snapshot keeps the whole canvas as it is right now, so you can try a big change (a reorganisation, an import, a clean-up with a teammate or an AI assistant) knowing you can put everything back in one step.

## Where Snapshots Are

Snapshots live in **History**: open **Tools** in the footer and press **History**. Signed in on your own or your team's canvas, History shows the canvas's timeline with each snapshot pinned on it at the time it was taken, marked **Snapshot**, and a **Take Snapshot** button at its foot. Signed out, or through an edit link, History shows the snapshots on their own.

## Taking a Snapshot

Press **Take Snapshot**. Give the snapshot a name if you like, such as "Before the workshop", and press **Take Snapshot** again. Without a name it is called by the time it was taken, such as "Snapshot of 15 Nov, 14:32".

Each snapshot lists its name, who took it, how long ago and its size. The latest **five** are kept: when five are there, the form says which one taking another will remove, and the oldest goes.

## Reverting to a Snapshot

Press **Revert** on a snapshot and confirm. The canvas goes back to exactly how it was: every tab, its folders, its comment threads and the slide deck. A tab made since is removed, and one removed since comes back. The canvas's name and who it is shared with stay as they are.

Reverting replaces everyone's work since that snapshot, so first it takes a snapshot of the canvas as it is now, marked **Before revert**. If you change your mind, revert to that one.

Everyone who has the canvas open sees it reload onto the reverted version straight away, so nobody keeps working on what was replaced.

## Deleting a Snapshot

Press the bin on a snapshot. It is deleted at once, with no question, and can't be brought back.

## Who Can Use Snapshots

Editors of the whole canvas take, revert and delete snapshots. Participants, Viewers and people with a link to one tab don't see them. Snapshots are there on canvases stored on the server: a canvas kept **Offline** has none, and neither does an embed or a canvas open in a workbench. AI assistants and scripts using an API token can't use them.
