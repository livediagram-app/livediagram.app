A project rarely lives in one file. The architecture is a canvas, the delivery plan is in liveplanner, the brief is a livedocuments document and the screens are in livedesign. A **connected file** is a tab in your canvas's tab bar that stands for one of those files. It shows the file's app logo and its name, and pressing it shows a preview of the file with a button to open it.

## Connecting a file

<Step n={1}>
  Add a tab with the **+** at the end of the tab bar (or open a canvas's empty tab). Its start
  screen opens.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **Connect File**, the last entry at the very bottom of the start screen's sidebar. Your most
  recent files from every liveworkspace app are listed, newest first, each with its app's logo.
  **Find a file** narrows the list by name.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Choose the **Role** anyone opening the tab gets on that file: **Editor** (chosen unless you change
  it), **Participant** or **Viewer**.
</Step>

<Step n={4}>
  Press the file. The connected tab takes the empty tab's place at the end of the tab bar, and
  opens.
</Step>

Connecting makes a share link on the file you connect, at the role you chose, so everyone who can open your canvas can open it from the tab, even if they could not open it before. It is listed in that file's **Share** dialog as **Connected from** your canvas, where its owner can see it and end it. You can only connect files you could share yourself: your own, or your team's.

### Both ways

Turn on **Add connection to both files** before you press a file, and that file gains a tab back to this canvas too. It needs you to be able to share this canvas as well (its owner, or a member of its team). If the other file cannot be reached just then, its tab back is not made and a message says so; your canvas keeps its connected tab.

## Opening a connected file

Press the connected tab to see its page: a preview of the file, its name, and a button under the preview. Press **Open in** and the app's name (for a file in another app), or **Open** (for another canvas), to go to the file. A file in another app opens in a new browser tab (the tab carries a small arrow for that), and another canvas opens in place. The tab's page stays behind with **Open Again**, in case your browser held the new tab back.

Nothing opens by itself. Pressing the tab, opening the canvas on a connected tab, or connecting one only shows its page; the file opens when you press the button under the preview.

The tab's name follows the file's: when the file is renamed, the tab takes the new name the next time an editor opens it. A connected tab holds no drawing of its own.

## Arranging and removing

A connected tab is a tab like any other: drag it to reorder, and put it in a [tab folder](/help/tabs/tab-folders/) from its menu (**⋯** on the open tab, or right-click it) with **Organise in Tab Folder**.

**Remove Tab** in its menu removes the tab and nothing else: the file stays exactly as it was. Its share link is deleted with it, so anyone it let in is let out again. Undo brings the tab back, without its link: it offers **Connect Again**.

## When a connected tab needs attention

A small amber dot on the tab means it opens nothing just now. Its page says why:

- **This file is no longer there.** The file was moved to the Trash or deleted. Remove the tab.
- **This connection's link was removed.** The file's owner ended its link from its Share dialog. **Connect Again** makes a new one.
- **Connect this copy.** The tab came with a copy of the canvas or an import, so it does not open the file yet. **Connect Again** connects it here.
- **Connected files open when you are online.** A canvas saved only in this browser, or no network.

## Who can do what

- **Editors** connect files, reorder, folder and remove connected tabs, and use Connect Again.
- **Participants** and **Viewers** see connected tabs and open them; the file opens at the role the tab was connected with.

Connected tabs are made from the editor only. Agents and the CLI can see a connected tab's app and name, never where it leads, and cannot make one.
