Sometimes a tab you built in one document belongs in another too: a glossary, a shared timeline, an architecture board two projects both rely on. **Add to Document** adds a copy of the active tab to another document you own, without leaving the editor.

## Adding a tab to another document

<Step n={1}>Open the tab's menu (the **⋯** button on the active tab, or right-click the tab).</Step>
<Step n={2}>Choose **Add to Document**.</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  In the **Add to Document** dialog, pick the destination. Each document shows as a tile with a
  preview of its canvas, and the filter box at the top narrows a long list. A message confirms when
  the tab has been added.
</Step>

The option is greyed out when there is nowhere to add the tab to: if this is your only document, until you create another, and in a [document in This Browser](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/). It isn't offered to Viewers or Participants, or on a link shared for [one tab](/help/collaboration/sharing/one-tab/).

<Figure caption="Add to Document opens a dialog of your other documents, each with a preview of its canvas, and a filter box to narrow the list.">

</Figure>

## A copy in each document

Add to Document makes a copy: the other document gets its own tab, with every element, comment and setting the tab had when you added it. From then on the two tabs are independent, so edits in one stay in that document.

Removing or editing the tab in one document never touches the other, and deleting a whole document leaves the copies it gave other documents where they are.
