Almost every diagram is shapes joined by arrows. livediagram makes both fast: shapes come from the **Palette**, and arrows snap between shapes with quick-connect. This article covers the basics; for the full catalogue see [Shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [Arrows](/help/palette/arrows/).

## Adding shapes

The **Palette**, the strip across the top of the canvas, holds everything you can add. It starts with the selection mode (Select, Hand, Eraser and the rest), then the palette's categories by name: in Diagram mode **Shapes**, **Write**, **Draw**, **Diagrams**, **Concepts**, **Layout**, **Icons**, **Tech** and **Media**. Rest the pointer on a category, or click it, and its elements open in a panel underneath. See [where each one lives](/help/palette/tools/) for the full map.

<Figure caption="Rest on Shapes and its panel opens under it: the basics, the flowchart shapes and more.">
  <ToolbarCategoryFlyout />
</Figure>

<Step n={1}>
  Rest on **Shapes** in the palette, or click it: its panel opens with the square, circle, diamond,
  cylinder, hexagon, stadium and many more, in groups. Click the one you want.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  **Click the canvas** to drop the shape there at its natural size, or **drag on the canvas** to
  draw it at exactly the size you want. You can also drag the tile straight onto the canvas.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  **Double-click** the shape to add a label, then type. Enter starts a new line, and a click
  elsewhere finishes the label. With a shape selected you can also just start typing, or press
  **Space**.
</Step>

Common shapes have their own keys, too: `R` square, `O` circle, `D` diamond, `C` cylinder and `G` parallelogram (see [Keyboard Shortcuts](/help/tips-and-tricks/keyboard-shortcuts/)).

The categories sit in groups along the strip, a thin line between each. In Diagram mode **Shapes**, **Write** (text, notes and comments) and **Draw** (the pencil, highlighter and arrows) come first; then **Diagrams** (mind maps, flowcharts, tables), **Concepts** (pyramids, funnels, cycles and the Sankey) and **Layout** (frames, lanes, cards); then **Icons**, **Tech** (the full-colour cloud and infrastructure marks, see [Technology icons](/help/palette/technology/)) and Media. Stickers, the **Data** charts and the workshop elements (**Ask** and **Session**) are in [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/)'s palette. The wireframing kit (**Components** and **Devices**: browser, phone and laptop frames) is on no palette strip: add it from the [Search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/).

## Connecting with arrows

The fastest way to draw an arrow is **quick-connect**:

<Figure caption="Click a selected shape's + to open its options, then drag Arrow onto a second shape to pin an arrow.">
  <QuickConnect />
</Figure>

<Step n={1}>Select a shape. Small **+** buttons appear on its sides.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Drag a **+** onto another shape, or click it to open its options (**Duplicate**, **Arrow**,
  **Pencil**, **Text**) and drag **Arrow** onto another shape. livediagram draws an arrow pinned to
  both, so the arrow follows whenever you move either shape. On a phone, tap **Arrow**, then tap the
  target shape.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  To draw a free connector instead, pick **Add Arrow** (in the **Draw** category), or press `A`, and
  drag between any two points.
</Step>

**Duplicate** in the same options drops a connected copy of the shape beside it, the quickest way to grow a flow one step at a time. Turn on **Quick-Add on Hover** in Settings and the options open as soon as you hover a **+**. See [Quick-Add on Hover](/help/palette/quick-add-on-hover/).

## Styling arrows

**Right-click** an arrow (long-press on touch) to open its formatting:

- **Line**: **Straight**, **Curved** or **Angled** (an L-shaped elbow); the pattern (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Dash-dot, Long dash or Dash-dot-dot); and the thickness, Thin, Medium, Thick or Extra thick.
- **Pointer**: the arrowhead shape, size, and which ends carry a head.
- **Label:** double-click an arrow to type a label. It sits on the middle of the line, with the line broken open behind it, and wraps onto more lines when it is long. Press **Shift+Enter** for a line break of your own.
- **Pass Behind Boxes:** on by default, so a long arrow crossing an unrelated shape ducks under it instead of drawing a line over its label. Turn it off per arrow from the **Line** section if you want the old always-on-top look.

Drag an arrow's line to bend it where you grabbed it, and use the handles on curved and angled arrows to shape the path exactly. See [Arrow Styles](/help/palette/arrows/arrow-styles/).

<Tip>
  Working in a flow? Drop a shape, label it, quick-connect to the next spot, and repeat. Because a
  new shape can inherit the selected one's size, chained nodes stay tidy without fiddling.
</Tip>
