## Where an arrow can attach

Every shape has sixteen points an arrow end can snap to: the four corners, the middle of each edge, and a quarter point between each corner and its middle. On round and slanted shapes (a circle, a diamond, a person) the points sit on the outline you see. Drag an arrow end near a shape and its points light up.

## What it does

As you move shapes, an arrow end stays exactly where you put it, with one exception: when the move leaves the arrow running **through** one of the shapes it connects. Then its ends move to the sides that face each other.

- An end keeps its kind of spot: a corner stays a corner, a quarter point a quarter point, a middle a middle. Only the side changes.
- A quarter point moves to the quarter on the new side nearer the other end, unless another arrow already sits there.
- Curved and angled arrows count by the line as drawn, not a straight line between their ends.
- It happens live while you drag, and there is no going back: once an end has moved, its new spot is where it stays.
- Ends you placed by hand follow the same rule, and so does the attached end of an arrow whose other end is free.

When two arrows leave the same side of a shape and cross each other, they swap their spots, so the lines untangle without changing sides.

## Many arrows, one point

When several arrows pin to the same spot on a shape, say a hub with every child pointing at its bottom edge, the arrowheads fan out side by side along that edge instead of piling onto one point. The fan is purely visual and automatic: the pins themselves don't change, the heads just stay readable however many connectors converge.

## When to turn it off

Switch it off when you want every arrow end to hold exactly where you put it, even when a move makes an arrow cross a shape. With it off, moving shapes never changes where an arrow is attached.
