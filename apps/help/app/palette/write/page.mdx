The **Write** elements (the palette's **Write** category in every mode with a palette strip) hold the elements whose content is _words_. Everywhere else in the palette you place something with a shape and then maybe label it; here the text is the point and the shape is just what carries it.

They differ mostly in **how much you have to say**. A Page holds paragraphs, a Sticky note holds a thought, a Text label holds a line, an Annotation holds a remark you have to hover to read, and a Pointer note holds a remark about one spot, joined to it by a line. The Page sits first because it is the largest writing surface on the canvas.

## What the category contains

- A **Page** is a paper-shaped surface for prose, and the largest writing surface on the canvas. Double-click to write. Reach for it when the diagram needs a paragraph rather than a caption: a brief beside the boxes, a summary at the top of the canvas. See [Pages](/help/palette/tools/pages/).
- A **Text** element is a free-standing label with no box around it: just words on the canvas. `T` drops one. Use it for titles, column headings, and anything that labels a _region_ rather than an element, since a shape's own label is the better way to name a shape. See [Text and Fonts](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/).
- A **Sticky note** is a coloured note card, the one that reads as somebody's contribution rather than the author's structure. `N` drops one. It is what retros, brainstorms and dot votes are made of, and it is what an [idea box](/help/palette/collaborate/idea-boxes/) scatters into. See [Sticky Notes](/help/palette/tools/sticky-notes/).
- An **Annotation** is a small marker you drop on the canvas that holds a note. Hover it to read, double-click to edit. It is the one that keeps its text _out_ of the way: the remark lives inside the marker instead of taking up canvas. See [Annotations](/help/canvas/annotations/).
- A **Pointer note** is a short note with a line out to a dot. Drag the dot onto a corner or edge of a shape and the note follows that shape wherever it moves: use it for "this is where it fails" remarks. See [Pointer Notes](/help/palette/pointer-notes/).

## In other editor modes

The five are Diagram mode's set. Each editor mode keeps its palette to what suits it:

- In [Workshop](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) mode, Write holds Text, Note and Pointer note, then Table and Checklist: no Page and no Annotation.
- [Plan](/help/canvas/plan-mode/) mode has no palette: its boards, sheets and dashboards carry their own tools.

Any of the five is still a search away in another mode, and works there once placed.

## Picking between them

Three of the five are surfaces you compose on; the annotation is not, and the pointer note sits between. That is the line worth knowing.

A Page, a Text label and a Sticky note all put their words **on the canvas**, where everyone reads them without doing anything. An Annotation puts a **marker** on the canvas and hides its words until somebody hovers it. So the question is whether the text is part of what the diagram says, or a side remark about it.

If the diagram is incomplete without the words, use one of the three surfaces. If the words are commentary (a caveat, a reminder, a "we should revisit this"), an annotation says exactly that by being small.

<Note>
  There is a fourth way to attach words, and it is not in this category: any element can carry a
  [note](/help/canvas/notes/) or a [comment](/help/collaboration/comments/) of its own. Those belong
  to the element they are on and move with it, which an annotation dropped nearby does not.
</Note>

## Cards, not pictures

Shapes show as a grid of pictures, because the picture is the explanation. These five are all rectangles with text in them, so at tile size they would look nearly alike. In their [palette panel](/help/palette/toolbar-layout/) each one is a card with a one-line description instead, the text surfaces (Page, Text) first, then the notes (Note, Annotation, Pointer note), because "a paper-shaped page for prose" against "a marker that holds a note" is what you are actually choosing between.

## Working with them

Each behaves like any other element once placed: select, restyle, lock and move it freely, and each can carry a [link](/help/canvas/links/) like anything else on the canvas. The one exception is [rotation](/help/canvas/rotation/), which the three surfaces allow and the annotation marker does not: it is a round marker of a fixed size, so turning it would change nothing worth seeing.

Text styling is shared with the rest of the canvas rather than special to this category: font, size, weight and alignment all come from the same controls that style a shape's label. See [Text and Fonts](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/).

<Tip>
  `T` and `N` drop a text label and a sticky note without opening the palette at all, which is the
  fast way through a brainstorm: type, `N`, type, `N`.
</Tip>
