A Selection Mode button is an element that hands out a [selection mode](/help/selection-modes/). Put one on the canvas and anybody can press it, without ever having opened the mode picker.

## Adding one

The **Selection Mode** category, one of the six [Collaborate](/help/palette/behaviour/) categories, holds eight buttons, one per mode: **Avatar**, **Select**, **Hand**, **Laser**, **Spotlight**, **Eraser**, **Format** and **Isometric**.

Pick the mode you want and it drops ready to press, reading "Switch to Avatar" and switching whoever presses it into that mode. To point an existing button at another mode, right-click it and open **Button**; the face relabels itself to match. You can also type your own label over the derived one ("Walk with me"), and clearing it brings the derived text back.

Selection Mode buttons are in the palette in Diagram and Draw mode.

## What pressing it does

Pressing it changes the mode for **the person who pressed it** and nobody else. Everyone else carries on in whatever tool they were already using.

Pressing it again while you are in that mode hands you back your previous one, so a button is never a one-way door: a button for the mode you are already in reads "Leave Avatar" rather than offering it again.

A click presses the button; a drag still moves it, so you can arrange a bar of them without setting one off.

An **Avatar** button drops your character just below the button, rather than in the middle of the screen, so it appears where you pressed.

## Why a bar of them works

A Selection Mode button keeps working while you are in a mode that makes the rest of the canvas read-only (Avatar, Spotlight or Isometric), so a row of them makes a usable little control bar. It also works for somebody on a view-only link, since switching your own tool changes nothing for anyone else (though an Eraser or Format button only changes the canvas for someone who can edit it). That is what makes it useful on a walkthrough: the viewer has the controls they need in front of them, on the canvas, rather than in a panel they were never told about.

In a read-only embed there is no tool to switch, so the button shows but does nothing.

<Tip>
  Pair an **Eraser** button with a **Select** button so a reviewer can tidy up what they see and put

</Tip>
