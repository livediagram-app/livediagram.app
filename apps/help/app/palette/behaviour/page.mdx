The **Behaviour** category (under **Dynamic** in the palette's category picker) holds the elements that _do_ something when somebody interacts with them. Everywhere else in the palette you are placing a picture; here you are placing a control.

Each is a row with its name and a one-line description of what it does, because a glyph cannot say what a Picker picks or where a Portal goes.

## What the category contains

A **Selection Mode** button reads "Switch to Avatar" and switches whoever presses it into that mode; right-click it and open **Tools › Button** to point it at any other mode — Select, Hand, Laser, Spotlight, Avatar, Eraser, Format, or Isometric — or type your own label over the derived one. Pressing it changes the mode for **the person who pressed it** and nobody else, pressing it again while you are in that mode hands you back your previous one, and it keeps working while you are in a mode that makes the rest of the canvas read-only, so a row of them makes a usable little control bar. A **Portal** is a ring of energy you link to another portal: click it — or walk an Avatar-mode character into it — and you come out the far side, even on another tab. See [Portals](/help/palette/tools/portals/).

A **Session** button starts a session tool for everyone in the room: a countdown **timer**, a **dot vote**, or a **poll** you write in advance. Once a timer is running, the same button **pauses** it, and pressing again **continues** it — it becomes the timer's control rather than restarting the countdown. Right-click it and open **Tools › Session** to pick which, and its one setting — the number of minutes, the dots per person, or the question and its answers. It is how a board carries its own running order: put "5 minutes" over the writing column and "Vote — 3 dots each" over the results, and anyone can run the session. Starting a tool needs edit access, so on a view-only link the button shows as unavailable, though you can still take part once somebody starts it.

A **Reaction pad** throws a burst of celebration over itself when somebody presses it — or walks an Avatar-mode character onto it, which is the same act. Right-click it and open **Tools › Reaction** to pick which of the five it throws: **Confetti** for a result worth celebrating, **Sparkles** for a good idea nicely done, **Hearts** for warmth towards a person rather than a result, **Applause** to thank somebody for the talk or the demo, and **Fireworks** for when it shipped. Everyone in the room sees the burst, and nothing is saved: a reaction is a moment, not a mark on the board, so there is nothing to undo and nothing for a latecomer to find. Drop one at the end of a retro column or under a demo and let the room use it.

A **Reveal** zone is a cover you put over part of the canvas — answers, estimates, a retro column nobody should read early. **Double-click** (or double-tap) uncovers it **on your screen only**, with a small Hide pill to put it back — two presses, so a stray click on a busy board doesn't give the game away; **Tools › Reveal › Reveal for all** takes it off for everyone at once. It hides content from a reader's eye, not from a determined reader: everything underneath is still in the diagram, so don't put anything genuinely private under one.

A **Picker** chooses at random when you press it — one of the people currently in the room, or one of the options you write in **Tools › Picker**. It spins the candidates past and slows to a stop on the answer — people appear with their own avatar as they go by — and everyone sees the same result: handy for "who demos next" without anyone having to volunteer.

## Working with them

Each behaves like any other element once placed: select, restyle, group, and move it freely. They keep working while you are in a selection mode that makes the rest of the canvas read-only, which is the point of a control bar made of them.

<Tip>
  Put a row of these along the top of a board and it carries its own running order: a Session button
  for the timer, a Reveal zone over the answers, and a Picker for who goes next.
</Tip>
