The **Devices** category holds device frames for wireframing and UI mockups. Each is a realistic outline of a screen you can build a design inside.

## Finding them

Devices are part of the [Illustrate mode](/help/canvas/illustrate/) palette: open the category dropdown and pick **Devices** under **Structure**. Tap a frame to add it, or drag it to where you want it. The Diagram mode palette doesn't offer the category, but the frames still work there: search for one by name in the editor's [Search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/) and add it from **Add to canvas**.

## What the category contains

- **Browser** for a desktop web page, with a tab strip and an address bar.
- **Monitor** for a full-screen desktop layout, on a stand.
- **Laptop** for a portable-screen view.
- **Phone** for a mobile app or page.
- **Tablet** for a larger touch layout.
- **Foldable** for a book-style phone, opened out to its inner screen with the fold down the middle.
- **Watch** for a compact smartwatch screen.

Frames pick up the tab theme's colours when you drop them, like any other shape.

## How to use a device frame

Drop a frame onto the canvas, then build your mockup inside or on top of it using shapes, text, images, and icons. The frame gives your design a true-to-life boundary so the proportions read correctly to anyone reviewing the work. Sketch a flow across several phone frames to show a journey, or pair a monitor with a phone to show a responsive layout side by side.

A device frame is an ordinary element, not a container: dragging it does not carry what you have drawn on it.

<Tip>
  Draw a [frame](/help/palette/build/frames/) around your finished mockup and its device, so the
  screen and its contents move together when you rearrange the canvas. Illustrate mode's palette
  leaves frames out, so add one from the Search panel there.
</Tip>
