A **Mind node** is a labelled node that knows its parent. It lives in the palette's **Build** category, and the whole point of it is the keyboard: you can build an entire branch while you are still talking.

## The two keys

<Step n={1}>Add a Mind node from the palette and type its label.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **Tab** to add a **child**, then keep typing. The new node is already connected, already
  selected, and already in edit mode.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Press **Enter** to add a **sibling** directly after the node you are on, and keep typing.
</Step>

<Step n={4}>
  Press **Escape** when you are done. What you typed is kept; a node you left empty is removed, so a
  Tab pressed one time too many costs nothing.
</Step>

You never have to stop typing to reach for the palette, and you never have to draw the connectors: each new node arrives joined to its parent. Type as fast as you like: keys pressed before the next node is on screen are held for it, so a burst of several nodes lands exactly as typed.

<Tip>
  The keys work from **inside** the label editor as well as from a selected node. Typing a name and
  pressing Tab commits what you typed and starts the next node in one keystroke, which is what makes
  a whole branch flow.
</Tip>

## Where nodes land

A map you build from the keyboard stays **tidy**: each parent sits centred on its children, and siblings stack neatly with no gaps or overlaps. As you add nodes, the map makes room for them, and the view scrolls to keep the node you are typing in on screen.

Once you drag a node yourself, the map is yours: new nodes are placed where they fit and nothing you arranged moves. To tidy it again, open the node's menu and choose **Mind Map → Tidy Map**.

## Flows

**Mind Map** in a node's menu picks the shape the whole map takes: **Tree** (branches to the right), **Balanced** (both ways off the centre), **Downward** (like an org chart) or **Bubble** (fanned around the centre). Picking one re-arranges the map straight away.

## Edit Outline

Select a map's root (the node it starts from) and press **Edit Outline** on its toolbar, or find it under **Mind Map** in the root's menu. The whole map opens as text: the root on the first line, every other node a `- ` line indented under its parent.

- **Rewrite it as a list.** Type new lines, delete lines, change their text, or move lines under a different parent. **Tab** indents the line you are on and **Shift+Tab** outdents it.
- **Paste one in.** A list from your notes works too: bullets, numbered items and `#` headings all become levels.
- **Save** (or **⌘/Ctrl+Enter**) applies the whole outline at once and lays the map out again in the outline's order. Nodes whose lines you kept stay as they were, with their colours, comments and links. New lines look like their neighbours. If any nodes would be removed, you are asked first.

Saving is one step, so **Undo** puts the map back exactly as it was.

## Sizes and looks

A new node looks like the others at its level: siblings match each other, and the first node of a new level takes after its parent. The connector matches the ones already in the map. So a map drawn with bold roots, tinted branches and dashed lines keeps growing that way.

A mind node can be made round: give a square node a full corner radius.

## Moving and deleting

Dragging a node takes its whole branch with it.

Deleting a node leaves its children in place, promoted to roots. It does not cascade, because deleting one node and silently losing nine is the kind of thing you only notice after the undo stack has moved on.

<Note>
  If you would rather use the mouse, select a mind node: **Add child** and **Add sibling** are on
  its toolbar, and on the **+** next to it, each with its shortcut. A root's toolbar also has **Edit
  Outline**.
</Note>

<Note>
  Every mind map template is made of mind nodes, so you can grow a template with Tab and Enter
  straight away.
</Note>
