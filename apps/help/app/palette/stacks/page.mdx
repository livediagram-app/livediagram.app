A **Stack** is a container that arranges what you put in it. Drop four cards into a stack and they line up with even gaps; drop in a fifth, take one out or give one a longer label, and the rest make room. A row of services never needs nudging into line again.

It sits in the palette's [Build](/help/palette/build/) category, after Frame, in Diagram and Illustrate mode. A new stack is an empty dashed outline: drag things into it.

## Take the tour

The first time you select a stack, a small **Tour** toolbar appears beside its toolbar. Press **Play** for a short tour on your own stack: how it lays out what it holds, dragging a child to a new slot (or dropping one into an empty stack), what it carries, and the Stack menu. Press the cross if you would rather not; either way it is not offered again. To be offered it again, open **Settings**, then **Tours**, open **Element Tours** and switch **Stack Tour** back on.

## What it holds

A stack holds the elements whose **centre** is inside it, the same rule as a [frame](/help/palette/build/frames/) and a [boundary](/help/palette/boundaries/). Dragging the stack carries them, and it paints behind them.

The order is position: left to right in a row, top to bottom in a column, and reading order in a grid. To reorder, drag a child past its neighbour and let go; it takes that slot.

The stack hugs its contents: it resizes to fit them plus its padding. An empty stack keeps its size, so it stays a target to drop into.

## Arranging it

Right-click and open the **Stack** section:

- **Row**, **Column** or **Grid**. A grid takes a number of **Columns**, from 2 to 6.
- **Gap**: the space between children, 0 to 64 px.
- **Padding**: the space inside the edge, 0 to 64 px.
- **Align**: where children sit across the flow, at the **Start**, **Centre** or **End**.

Type a title (double-click) and the stack keeps a band for it above its children.

Children glide to their new places when the stack reflows. Stacks nest: put a column stack inside a row stack for a grid of lists.
