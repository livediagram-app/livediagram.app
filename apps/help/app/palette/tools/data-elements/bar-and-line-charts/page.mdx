Where a [pie chart](/help/palette/tools/data-elements/pie-chart/) shows one total split into slices, a **bar chart** compares values side by side, one bar per category, and a **line chart** plots one or more series across shared categories, so it suits trends over time. Both are ordinary canvas elements: they drop in with sample data and take their colours from the active [theme](/help/canvas/themes/).

## Adding one

<Step n={1}>
  In [Illustrate](/help/canvas/illustrate/), open the **Data** category on the palette strip. In
  Diagram mode, use the [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/)
  instead: type "bar chart" or "line chart".
</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Pick **Column** or **Line**, then tap the canvas to drop it, or drag to draw it at a size. It
  lands with sample data so it looks finished straight away.
</Step>

## Chart types

Any chart can draw its data seven ways, named as Google Sheets and Excel name them. Right-click it, open **Tools**, then **Chart Type**, and pick one:

- **Column**: upright bars, one per category. The palette's **Column** tile makes one.
- **Bar**: the same bars lying down, growing to the right, which leaves room for long category names.
- **Line**: a line with a point per category, for each series.
- **Area**: the lines, with the space under each one filled in its colour, so a total's shape reads at a glance.
- **Scatter**: a point per category, no lines. When every category is a number, each point sits at its number along the bottom, so it shows how two measures relate.
- **Pie** and **Doughnut**: slices of a whole; a doughnut leaves the middle open.

Switching keeps the chart's size, colours, legend and animation. Moving between a column, bar or pie chart and a line, area or scatter chart moves the data too: the bars become one series named Series 1, or the first series becomes the bars.

## Editing a column or bar chart

A column or bar chart holds one value per bar, the same way a pie chart holds one per slice. Right-click it, open **Tools**, then **Data**: each row is a bar, with a swatch for its colour, a label and a value. Edit them in place, remove a bar with **×**, or add one with **+ Add Slice**. The chart updates as soon as you leave a field.

## Editing a line, area or scatter chart

A line chart's data is a grid (an area or scatter chart's too), too wide for the menu, so its **Tools** then **Data** section lists the series and offers **Edit Data**. That opens the **Chart data** dialog:

- **Edit the grid** directly: a row per category, a column per series. Add a series with the **+** at the end of the header row, add a category with **+ Add row**, and remove either with its **×** (a chart always keeps at least one of each).
- **Import CSV** loads a file: the header row's first cell labels the category column and the rest name the series, and each row below is a category and its values (up to 12 series and 200 rows), so you can save a sheet from a spreadsheet and load it straight in. Importing replaces what the grid held.

Changes apply as you make them (a typed cell when you leave it); **Done** closes the dialog.

## Display and colours

Both charts share the pie chart's display options: in **Tools**, **Chart** places the legend (**Off**, **Top**, **Left**, **Right** or **Below**, the default) and sets its **Text Size**, and the **Animation** section offers **Grow**, **Pop**, **Spin** and **Pulse**. Hovering a bar or a point shows its label and value. **Show Values**, under the legend, prints each bar's or point's number on the chart; a column chart also names each column under it, so you can turn the legend off.

Colours default to variants of the theme, so a multi-series chart stays legible and on-brand. To recolour the whole chart at once, open **Style** and pick a palette from its **Presets**: **Vivid**, **Ocean**, **Forest**, **Sunset**, **Berry**, **Earth**, **Grey** or **Contrast**. A colour you set on one bar by hand wins over the palette. Animations freeze on export, so a still image looks right.
