Sometimes the fastest way to capture an idea is to draw it. The palette's **Draw** category holds the drawing tools: **Freehand** for smooth strokes kept exactly as drawn, the **Shape Pen** for rough sketches that snap to real shapes, and the **Polygon** tool for clean point-by-point outlines (plus the **Highlighter**, **Arrow** and **Line**, each covered in its own guide). Everything they draw is a regular element you can move, resize, and style.

<Figure caption="The Draw category: the two pens, the Highlighter and Polygon, then Arrow and Line, with their keys on the right.">
  <PaletteRows category="Draw" active="Freehand" />
</Figure>

Looking for the marker? The **Highlighter** sits third in the Draw category, after the two pens: see [Highlighter](/help/palette/tools/highlighter/).

Draw is a Diagram-mode category. [Illustrate](/help/canvas/illustrate/) has no Draw category (the pens are still reachable from the [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/)), and [Draw mode](/help/canvas/draw-mode/) replaces the palette with a whiteboard's own pens, which stay in your hand stroke after stroke.

## Freehand and the Shape Pen

Both pens are gestural tools: pick one from the palette's **Draw** category (**Freehand** is `P` or `7`, **Shape Pen** is `6`) and the next drag on the canvas draws a stroke. A small mode banner tells you which pen is armed, with a **Cancel** action: Freehand's reads "Drag to draw", and the Shape Pen's says your rough shape will snap to the real one. The cursor swaps to a pen nib so you know you are in draw mode.

The only difference is what happens on release: Freehand keeps the stroke exactly as you drew it, and the Shape Pen matches it against real shapes.

<Step n={1}>
  Select Freehand or the Shape Pen from the palette's Draw category, or press its key.
</Step>
<Step n={2}>Drag across the canvas to draw. A live preview follows your pointer.</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Release to commit the stroke. It lands selected, and the pen puts itself down, so pick it again
  (or press its key) for the next stroke. A stray click with no drag does not create anything.
</Step>

To put a pen down before drawing, press `Escape`, choose **Cancel** on the banner, or pick another tile. One `⌘Z` takes back the last stroke.

On release, your raw points are smoothed into a clean, hand-drawn curve rather than a jagged polyline. With Freehand, if you release near where you started, the path closes into a custom shape and fills, which is the way to sketch a bespoke outline (on a desktop the banner reminds you: "release near the start to close").

## Styling a sketch

A committed sketch is a regular boxed element. Its stroke colour follows the tab's theme so it reads as part of the diagram rather than a separate annotation layer, and a closed path fills with the theme's fill colour. From there you can:

- Move it, resize it (the path scales proportionally), rotate it, and lock it.
- Recolour its stroke and fill from the element's Colours controls.
- Apply the format painter, themes, and comments, exactly as with any shape.

## The Polygon tool

The Polygon tool fills the gap between freehand sketching and the fixed shapes: clean, straight-edged outlines with exactly the corners you choose, for zones on an architecture diagram, floor plans, or any irregular region.

<Step n={1}>Select **Polygon** from the Draw category. It has no keyboard shortcut.</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Click to place each point. A preview line follows your pointer from the last point you placed.
</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Click the **start point** (a snap ring appears when you are close) to close the outline into a
  **filled polygon**, or **double-click** (or press `Enter`) to finish an **open line** at the last
  point.
</Step>

<Figure caption="Placing polygon points click by click, with the snap ring on the start point ready to close the outline.">
  <PolygonDraw />
</Figure>

While placing points, `Backspace` removes the last point so you can walk a mistake back without starting over, and `Escape` clears the in-progress outline (press it again to put the tool down). An open line needs at least two points and a closed polygon three; finishing with fewer simply cancels. On a touch screen, taps place points just like clicks. A closed polygon fills with the theme's fill colour like any closed sketch; an open line stays stroke-only.

## Shape recognition

Pick the **Shape Pen** instead of Freehand and your sketches are matched against real shapes on commit. A rough square becomes a square, a loop becomes a circle, and a straight stroke becomes a line. See [Shape recognition](/help/palette/tools/drawing/shape-recognition/) for the details and which kinds are detected.

<Figure caption="Drawn with the Shape Pen, a rough sketch snaps into a clean shape on commit.">
  <ShapeRecognition />
</Figure>

<Tip>
  Nothing is remembered between strokes: Freehand always keeps what you drew and the Shape Pen
  always tries to convert it, so a stroke never behaves differently from the last one for reasons
  you cannot see.
</Tip>
