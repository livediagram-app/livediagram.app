Sometimes the fastest way to capture an idea is to draw it. The palette's **Draw** category holds the drawing tools: **Freehand** for smooth strokes kept exactly as drawn, the **Shape Pen** for rough sketches that snap to real shapes, the **Path tool** for smooth editable curves, **Marker 1**, **Marker 2** and **Marker 3** (the same pens as a whiteboard's), and the **Polygon** tool for clean point-by-point outlines (plus **Arrow**, covered in its own guide). Everything they draw is a regular element you can move, resize, and style.

The **Highlighter** is here too, last in the category: a translucent marker that stays in your hand until you pick another tool. See [Highlighter](/help/selection-modes/highlighter/).

## Freehand and the Shape Pen

Both pens are gestural tools: pick one from the palette's **Draw** category (**Freehand** is `P`, **Shape Pen** is `6`) and the next drag on the canvas draws a stroke. A small mode banner tells you which pen is armed, with a Cancel action, and the cursor swaps to a pen icon so you know you are in draw mode.

The only difference is what the stroke becomes: Freehand keeps it exactly as you drew it, and the Shape Pen turns it into the real shape it looks like. Hold `Shift` as you finish a stroke to keep the pen for another.

<Step n={1}>Select Freehand or the Shape Pen from the palette's Draw category.</Step>
<Step n={2}>Drag across the canvas to draw. The stroke appears as you draw it.</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Release to commit the stroke. A stray click with no drag does not create anything.
</Step>

Both pens draw the same smooth ink as a whiteboard's markers: what you see while drawing is exactly what lands. With a stylus the line swells as you press harder. A stroke stays an open line even when it ends where it began; for a filled outline, use the Shape Pen or the [Polygon](#the-polygon-tool) tool.

## The Path tool and the markers

The whiteboard's own pens are in the **Draw** category too, so you can use them on any diagram.

The **Path tool** draws smooth vector paths: click to place a corner, press and drag to pull out a curve, and keep placing points until you click the first one to close the path or press Enter to finish. Every point and handle stays editable afterwards. See [Whiteboards](/help/canvas/whiteboards/) for the full set of path gestures.

**Marker 1**, **Marker 2** and **Marker 3** are the same three pens a whiteboard's dock holds, with the same colours and widths: set one up on a board and it draws the same on a diagram. Marker 1 draws in your theme's ink; Markers 2 and 3 start blue and red. With a marker in hand, the quick style panel sets its colour and width.

A marker draws one stroke, like Freehand: the stroke is selected when you finish and you are back to selecting, so pick the marker again for the next stroke, or hold `Shift` as you finish a stroke to keep the marker for another. (On a whiteboard the markers stay in your hand.) The Path tool does stay in your hand: each path you draw keeps it ready for the next, until you pick another tool or press Escape. On a diagram the markers never turn sketches into shapes; that is the Shape Pen's job.

## Styling a sketch

A committed sketch is a regular boxed element. Its stroke colour follows the tab's theme so it reads as part of the diagram rather than a separate annotation layer. From there you can:

- Move it, resize it (the path scales proportionally), and lock or group it.
- Recolour its stroke and fill from the element's Colours controls.
- Apply the format painter, themes, and comments, exactly as with any shape.

## The Polygon tool

The Polygon tool fills the gap between freehand sketching and the fixed shapes: clean, straight-edged outlines with exactly the corners you choose, for zones on an architecture diagram, floor plans, or any irregular region.

<Step n={1}>Select the Polygon tool from the Draw category.</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Click to place each point. A preview line follows your pointer from the last point you placed.
</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Click the **start point** (a snap ring appears when you are close) to close the outline into a
  **filled polygon**, or **double-click** (or press `Enter`) to finish an **open line** at the last
  point.
</Step>

<Figure caption="Placing polygon points click by click, with the snap ring on the start point ready to close the outline.">
  <PolygonDraw />
</Figure>

While placing points, `Backspace` removes the last point so you can walk a mistake back without starting over, and `Escape` clears the in-progress outline. A closed polygon fills with the theme's fill colour like any closed sketch; an open line stays stroke-only.

## Shape recognition

Pick the **Shape Pen** instead of Freehand and your sketches turn into real shapes. A rough square becomes a square, a loop becomes a circle, and a straight stroke becomes a line. Hold the pen still for half a second while drawing to see the shape it will become, then keep dragging to resize it (hold `Shift` to make it perfect). Press `Alt` mid-stroke to keep it as ink instead. See [Shape recognition](/help/palette/tools/drawing/shape-recognition/) for the details and which kinds are detected.

</Figure>

<Tip>
  Nothing is remembered between strokes: Freehand always keeps what you drew and the Shape Pen
  always tries to convert it, so a stroke never behaves differently from the last one for reasons
  you cannot see.
</Tip>
