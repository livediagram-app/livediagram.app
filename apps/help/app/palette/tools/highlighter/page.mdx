The Highlighter is a wide, semi-transparent marker for calling attention to things: circling a region during a review, underlining a label while you talk, marking the three boxes a workshop has to come back to. Strokes are translucent and blend like a real pen, so text and shapes underneath stay legible and overlapping passes darken.

It lives in the palette's **Draw** category, third after **Freehand** and the **Shape Pen**. It has no keyboard shortcut, but typing "highlighter" into the palette's search finds the tile.

## Marking things up

<Step n={1}>
  Pick **Highlighter** from the palette's **Draw** category. A banner reads **Drag to highlight**,
  with a Cancel action.
</Step>
<Step n={2}>Drag across the canvas. A live preview of the marker follows the pointer.</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Release to lay the stroke down. It lands selected, and the tile puts itself down, just like
  Freehand.
</Step>

Each stroke is **one pick of the tile**, so a stray tap afterwards never leaves a mark, and one `⌘Z` takes back just the last stroke. To change your mind before you drag, press `Escape`, choose **Cancel** on the banner, or pick another tile.

Every stroke starts out **marker yellow** at a medium width, wide enough to sit over a label. There is nothing to set up before you draw: if a highlight should be another colour, change it afterwards from the element's **Colours** menu like any other element.

## Strokes are ordinary elements

A committed highlight is a regular element, not a separate annotation layer. Once it is down you can:

- Move it, resize it (the marker keeps its width, so a scaled highlight still looks like the same pen), rotate it, and lock it.
- Recolour it from its **Colours** menu, or put it on a [layer](/help/canvas/layers/).
- Take it off again with the [Eraser](/help/selection-modes/eraser/): set the eraser to **Drawings only** to sweep away the mark-up while leaving everything else untouched.

Highlights are never converted into shapes: a stroke commits exactly as you drew it, and ending back where you started never closes or fills it. Turning a rough sketch into a real shape is the [Shape Pen's](/help/palette/tools/drawing/) job.

The Highlighter belongs to the palette, so it is not in [Draw mode](/help/canvas/draw-mode/): a whiteboard's own pens are its markers.

<Tip>
  Highlighting is quick and disposable by design. If a mark needs to say something, a [sticky
  note](/help/palette/tools/sticky-notes/) or a [comment](/help/collaboration/comments/) carries the
  words; the marker just says "look here".
</Tip>
