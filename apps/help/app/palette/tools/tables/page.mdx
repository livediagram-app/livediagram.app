A table is an editable grid for the parts of a diagram that are easier to read as rows and columns: a comparison, a schedule, a small data set, a legend. It lives on the canvas like any other element, so you can place, resize, recolour, group, and move it freely.

  <TableElement />

## Adding one

<Step n={1}>Open the floating palette and switch to the **Build** category.</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Click **Add table**. A small grid (3&nbsp;&times;&nbsp;3 to start) drops onto the canvas.
</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  **Double-click any cell** to type. Move between cells as you would expect and the table grows to
  fit your text.
</Step>

## Editing the grid

- **Select a cell** with a click, then use the keyboard like a spreadsheet: arrow keys move the selection, Tab walks cells (and adds a row off the last one), Enter edits, Backspace clears. **Shift-click** more cells to style or clear several at once.
- **Right-click a cell** for its context menu: text styling and size, colours, alignment, a link, and clear, applied to every selected cell.
- **Rows and columns**: with the table selected, a small **⋯** sits just outside the top of each column and the left of each row for insert, move, and delete. The quick **+** buttons on the table's edges add a **row** (bottom), a **column** (right), or start an **arrow**.
- **Sizing**: rest the pointer on a divider for a moment and drag to set a column's width or a row's height; double-click a divider to auto-fit.

## Picking a look

A table paints four surfaces (the cells, the grid lines, the header band and the header text) that only read well together, so the **Presets** section of its context menu offers eight complete looks rather than making you choose four colours. Four are built from your theme's accent, quiet to loud: **Theme**, **Banded**, **Bold Head** and **Minimal**. Four are neutrals that look the same whatever the theme: **Plain**, **Paper**, **Slate** and **Inked**. Hover a tile to preview it on the table, click to apply.

Zebra striping comes with the look, since it's part of the look. Which cells are **headers** doesn't: that's about your data, so a preset never re-reads your first row as a heading.

Pick a look and it stays your table's look: change the diagram's [theme](/help/canvas/themes/) and a Banded table becomes the new theme's Banded, the same way a shape's preset moves with the theme. Set any of those colours by hand, or toggle the striping yourself, and the table keeps what you chose from then on.

The table is a real canvas element underneath, so its colours follow the active [theme](/help/canvas/themes/), and you can restyle, group, and align it alongside everything else. The **Table** section of its context menu toggles a header row, header column, and zebra striping.

<Tip>
  Reach for a table when a cluster of shapes is really just rows and columns, a single grid stays
  tidier and is far quicker to edit.
</Tip>
