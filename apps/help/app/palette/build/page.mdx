The **Build** category (the first of the **Structure** band in the palette's category picker) holds the elements you lay a diagram _out_ with. Everywhere else in the palette you are adding something to the canvas; here you are deciding how the canvas is arranged.

What the five have in common is that **each one holds other work**. A frame gathers a region, a lane carries the steps in a row, a timeline sequences events along a track, a table organises cells, and a mind node hangs children off itself. You reach for them at the same moment: once you know how the diagram is arranged, before you have decided what goes in it.

## What the category contains

- A **Mind node** is a node that knows its parent. **Tab** adds a child to its right, **Enter** a sibling below, each one connected, selected, and ready to type into, so a whole branch is built from the keyboard. See [Mind Maps](/help/palette/mind-maps/).
- A **Lane** is a titled horizontal band for swimlane diagrams. Dragging the lane carries everything sitting in it, so a row of steps moves as one. See [Lanes](/help/palette/lanes/).
- A **Frame** is a labelled box that groups a section of the canvas. It draws behind its contents and carries them when you move it, and it is what exports treat as a section backdrop. `F` draws one. See [Frames](/help/palette/build/frames/).
- A **Timeline** is a track for sequencing events, with evenly spaced points you add to from the end. See [Timeline Rail](/help/palette/tools/data-elements/timeline-rail/).
- A **Table** is an editable grid of cells you can style, link, and fill row by row. See [Tables](/help/palette/tools/tables/).

## Why they are rows, not tiles

Most catalogue categories show a grid of pictures, because the picture is the explanation. These five are all containers, and at tile size all containers are a rectangle. So Build renders as rows with a one-line description each: "Tab adds a child, Enter a sibling" against "a titled band that carries its steps" is what you are actually choosing between, and no icon says that.

## Working with them

Each behaves like any other element once placed: select, restyle, group, lock, and move it freely, and each can carry a [link](/help/canvas/links/), a note, or a [comment](/help/collaboration/comments/) like anything else on the canvas.

Because they hold other work, two habits pay off. Put the container down **first** and fill it afterwards, so the things inside are captured by it as you go. And when you move one, check what came with it: a frame and a lane deliberately carry their contents, which is usually what you want and occasionally a surprise.

<Tip>
  Reach for Build at the start of a board and the rest goes faster: lay out the lanes or frames you
  know you need, then switch to Shapes and fill them in.
</Tip>
