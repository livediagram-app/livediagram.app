A **Lane** is a wide band with a titled gutter down one edge, for cross-functional flows: a role, a team or a system per lane, with its steps laid inside.

It sits in the palette's **Build** category, beside Frame.

## Dragging a lane takes its contents

Drag a lane and everything **fully inside** it comes along. That is the difference between a lane and a plain rectangle: a swimlane is a container, so moving the band moves the work.

Two rules make that predictable:

- Only elements the lane **completely** contains travel with it. Something half in and half out stays where it is.
- If two containers overlap, an element belongs to the one **furthest back**, so it travels with that one and not with whichever you happened to drag.

Resizing a lane is deliberately different: it resizes the band and leaves the contents where they are.

## The title

The title is an ordinary element label, so you edit, format and align it like any other. The tinted gutter is its backdrop and **follows its alignment** — align the title right and the gutter moves with it.

## Stacking lanes

Lanes are placed like anything else and are not auto-stacked; snapping and the alignment guides make butting one under another straightforward. Flush lanes look right: a lane's outline is a single hairline, so two touching lanes do not double into a heavier line.

<Tip>
  Build the flow first and drop the lanes underneath it afterwards. Because a lane picks up whatever
  it fully contains, sizing a band around a finished row is often quicker than placing the band and
  then working inside it.
</Tip>
