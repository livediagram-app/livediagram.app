A **Chair** is furniture. Walk your Avatar-mode character into one and it sits down.

It lives in the **Navigate** group of the palette's [Collaborate](/help/palette/behaviour/) category, beside the Portal, Bring Focus and the Link card. Chairs, like the rest of Collaborate, are in the Diagram and Draw palettes.

## Sitting down

1. Switch to [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/) (the canvas-tool picker, or press **W**).
2. Click the chair, or steer onto it with the arrow keys. Your character walks over and takes the seat, turning to face the way the chair faces.

While seated:

- Your character **ignores clicks elsewhere on the canvas**, so a stray click can't drag you out of your chair.
- To stand up, press any **arrow key**, double-click the canvas, or press the small **Stand** pill under your character (the easy way on a touch screen).
- You can still wave and play reactions. Sitting takes away walking, not personality.

## Everyone sees who is sitting where

An occupied chair shows a ring in the sitter's colour with their name above it, on everyone's screen.

Seating is **presence**, not part of the document. That means:

- A chair is never left stuck by somebody who closed their laptop: leaving Avatar mode, switching tab or disconnecting frees the seat immediately.
- Nothing about who sat where is saved, logged, or undoable.

Two people can end up in the same chair. That is allowed on purpose: a rule that stopped it would need a lock, and a locked chair is one that eventually gets stuck.

## Setting it up

- Right-click the chair and open **Chair** to point the seat one of four ways: **Down**, **Left**, **Up** or **Right**. A ring of chairs around a table can then face inward, and whoever sits down faces the same way.
- Give the chair a **label** to make it somebody's chair: a name, or a role like "Facilitator" or "Scribe". The label sits under the chair.
- Unlike most Collaborate elements, a chair has no **…** on its face; everything is in its right-click menu.
- Otherwise it behaves like any element: move, resize, rotate, theme, lock, copy and export.

## What it's for

Eight chairs around a drawn table is a seating plan, an attendance display and a turn-taking device at once, built from elements you already know how to place. "Everyone grab a seat" becomes something the canvas can actually do.
