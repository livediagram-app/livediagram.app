On an [event storming board](/help/canvas/event-storming-boards/), the palette's **Event Storming** category holds the sticky-note notation for exploring a business domain in a workshop. Every tile drops an ordinary [sticky note](/help/palette/tools/sticky-notes/) pre-filled with one of the notation's colours, because in event storming **the colour is the meaning**: orange says "something happened" to anyone who has ever stood at one of these boards.

They are normal stickies otherwise: type the fact on the note, move it, vote on it, comment on it. And because sticky notes keep their colour under every theme, the grammar survives restyling.

## Where it appears

The category belongs to event storming boards. On a board started from the Event storming [template](/help/canvas/templates/), the palette opens on it, and it is the only category there: the board hides the palette's header band (both the tool picker and the category dropdown), because adding a note, typing and dragging are all a workshop needs. Canvas tools stay on their keyboard shortcuts and in the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/).

On any other tab the category picker doesn't offer it. You can still add a note of any kind by name from the editor's [Search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/search-add-to-canvas/); it keeps its colour and shape there, and behaves like an ordinary sticky.

## The note grammar

- A **Domain event** (orange) is something that happened, written in the past tense: "Order placed", "Payment received". Events are the backbone of the board, laid left to right in time order.
- A **Command** (blue) is the intent that triggers an event: "Place order".
- An **Actor** (yellow) is who issues the command: a person or a role.
- A **Policy** (purple) is a reaction rule: whenever X happens, then do Y.
- A **Read model** (green) is the information an actor looks at to decide.
- An **External system** (pink) is a third party the flow touches.
- An **Aggregate** (pale yellow) is the thing commands act on, once the design gets that far.
- A **Hotspot** (red) marks a conflict, question, or risk, anywhere, at any stage.

Each kind has the shape of its real stationery: a square for events, commands, read models and hotspots, a wide note for policies, external systems and aggregates, and a small square for actors. Tap a row to place one where you click (a ghost of the note follows the pointer), or drag it onto the board; a note dragged in opens straight into typing.

## On the board

On an event storming board the notes behave like the physical kit rather than like diagram shapes:

- They are written in marker capitals and keep one size for life, with no resize handles; the text shrinks to fit a long phrase instead.
- Each lands with a slight hand-placed tilt, and snaps onto one of the board's [timeline lanes](/help/canvas/event-storming-boards/#timeline-lanes).
- Actors always sit in front of other notes, and hotspots in front of actors.
- Right-click a note for a short menu: Cut, Copy, Duplicate, Bring to Front, Send to Back, **Change Kind** and Remove. The styling controls are gone, because a note's colour and shape are the notation. **Change Kind** re-colours and re-shapes the note in place.

[Event storming boards](/help/canvas/event-storming-boards/) covers the rest of the board: inserting a note between two others, the next-note buttons and the lanes.

## Add from Photo

On an event storming board the category opens with **Add from Photo**, above
the eight note kinds: it reads the stickies out of a photograph of a real wall
and adds the ones your board does not already have. See
[Adding notes from a photo of the wall](/help/canvas/event-storming-boards/#adding-notes-from-a-photo-of-the-wall).
It is about the board rather than about adding one note, which is why it sits
above the notation with a rule under it. A self-hosted copy that hasn't been set
up for photo reading leaves the row out.

## One board, one wall

A board started from the Event storming template is a single [layer](/help/canvas/layers/): one wall of paper. Every note you drop lands on it, whatever its colour, so any two notes can be stacked in front of or behind each other (within the actor and hotspot rule above). The colours carry the meaning; nothing is filed away on a plane you cannot see.

On any other canvas the notes behave like ordinary stickies and land on the active layer.

<Tip>
  Start with orange only. The method's opening move is domain events, past tense, left to right in

</Tip>
