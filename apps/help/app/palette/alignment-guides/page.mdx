Alignment Guides is a switch, and it is on by default. You will find it in [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/), under **Editor**. It is your own preference: it applies to every document you open, follows you to other devices when you are signed in, and changes nothing for anyone else.

## What it does

As you drag or resize an element, it [snaps](/help/canvas/snapping/) into line when an edge or centre comes within a few pixels of a neighbour's, or settles at an equal gap between two others. With Alignment Guides on, a faint dashed line marks each match that is in effect, and a short coloured span marks a matched gap, so you can see why the element jumped. Under the switch the description reads: "Shows snap lines while you move or resize an element, so it lines up with its neighbours."

<Figure caption="Dragging B next to A snaps their top edges together; the dashed guide shows the alignment.">
  <SnappingGuides />
</Figure>

The same guides show while you drag an arrow's endpoint or bend, so a connector can line up with the shapes around it.

## Turning it off

Switch it off for a cleaner drag with no lines on screen. Switching it off **hides the lines only**: elements still snap to their neighbours exactly as before, you just no longer see the hint.

To place one element freely, with no snapping at all, hold **Cmd** (Mac) or **Ctrl** (Windows and Linux) while you drag or resize it. That turns both the snap and its guides off for that one gesture, whatever this setting says. See [Alignment & Snapping](/help/canvas/snapping/).
