A **workbench** is a developer tool that shows a livediagram document live beside a coding agent. You draw in the editor right there, everything reaches livediagram at once, and when you type a message to your agent, the tool attaches what you had selected so the agent reads exactly those elements.

The editor in a workbench is signed in as you, for one document at a time. It has the canvas, the tab bar, the palette and the panels; account settings, sharing and your document library stay in livediagram itself, one click away through **Open in livediagram**.

Building a tool of your own? [Building a Workbench](/help/developers/workbenches/building-a-workbench/) has the full integration contract.

## Pair a Workbench Once

A workbench opens your documents through your [CLI](/help/developers/cli/) sign-in, and only after you have allowed it. The first time, the tool asks you to approve it, or you can pair it yourself:

```bash
livediagram workbench pair --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --name "Acme Editor"
```

The command prints a link and, in a terminal, opens it in your browser: a page headed **Allow this workbench?**, asking **Allow Acme Editor at `https://127.0.0.1:5175` to open your documents with the token livediagram CLI?** Check the address: it is what livediagram enforces, while the name is only a label. Choose **Allow**, and the command finishes by itself. **Don't allow** pairs nothing.

Pairing belongs to one CLI sign-in and one address. If someone obtained your token, it would still open nothing in a tool you never allowed.

## Open a Document

The workbench does this for you when you open a diagram in it. By hand:

```bash
livediagram workbench open "Home screen" --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --json
```

It prints a link that works once, within a minute, and only inside the workbench at that address. Opened anywhere else, the page shows **This view opens only inside** the workbench's address and does nothing.

## Open Your Whole Workspace

A workbench can also keep your Explorer beside your code: Home, your folders, your teams' documents, the Inbox, Recent and the Trash, with every document opening in the same view and a **Back** to where you were. **New Document** makes the document right there. Your account stays out of it: no Settings, no API tokens, no team administration.

The whole workspace is a second approval, even for a tool you already paired:

```bash
livediagram workbench pair --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --name "Acme Editor" --workspace
livediagram folder ls
livediagram workbench open --workspace --folder Minigames --origin https://127.0.0.1:5175 --json
```

The page asks **Allow Acme Editor at `https://127.0.0.1:5175` to open your whole workspace, and to create, move, share and trash documents and folders in it, with the token livediagram CLI?** A token made read-only gives a workspace that browses and reads. `livediagram folder create <name> [--in <folder>]` makes a folder for a project and prints its id.

## Talk to Your Agent About a Selection

Select elements, then type your message. The workbench adds a short block to it, naming the document, the tab and each selected element, for example:

```text
[livediagram] "Home screen" › tab "Wireframe" (doc 3h9x2a, tab 0b34, rev 41)
selected: button 146b "Play", frame e4a8 "Game grid"
read: livediagram tab view 3h9x2a --tab 0b34 --view show --ref 146b
```

With nothing selected, it names the whole tab. The block is plain text in your message, nothing hidden. Your agent reads the elements with the CLI and its changes appear in the editor as you watch, with an **Undo** on each.

## Stop or Unpair

A workbench session lasts a working day and renews itself while the tool is open. To stop a tool for good, go to **Settings**, then **API Tokens** under **Account**: each token lists its **Paired workbenches**, each with what it may open (**One document at a time** or **Whole workspace**), and **Unpair** turns their open diagrams read-only straight away. Revoking the token does the same for every tool it paired.
