livediagram has an [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io) server, so you can connect it to an AI tool you already use, like Claude, and then find, view, create, and edit your documents from inside that tool. Point the AI at a codebase and ask for a control-flow diagram, or ask it to tweak an existing one, and the result is saved to your account.

## You need an account

Connecting an AI tool is a **signed-in-only** feature, the same as [API tokens](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/) and [teams](/help/collaboration/teams/). The canvas, sharing, and real-time collaboration stay account-free for guests, but driving your documents programmatically is an advanced, opt-in capability that needs an account. On a self-hosted instance that hasn't enabled accounts, the feature isn't available.

## Connecting

In your AI tool, add a connector / MCP server pointing at **`https://mcp.livediagram.app/mcp`**. The first time, your tool opens a livediagram page asking you to **approve** the connection (you'll sign in if you aren't already). Approving creates an [API token](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/) behind the scenes and hands it to your tool. From then on the connection just works.

The token it creates is an ordinary API token: it appears in the **API Tokens** category of **Settings** and you can **revoke** it there any time to disconnect the tool. Like all tokens, it lasts six months.

## What the AI can do

The connection exposes these tools:

- **Find documents** — search your documents by name, across your personal library and the shared libraries of every [team](/help/collaboration/teams/) you belong to.
- **Read a document** — get a tab's contents and an inline picture of it.
- **List templates** — browse the same hand-tuned [template library](/help/canvas/templates/) the editor's Quick Start offers.
- **Create a document** — turn a request into a new document, with one tab or several. A tab can start from a template — ask for "a kanban board" and the AI can lay down the curated scaffold, then fill in your content.
- **Add a tab** — add another tab (its own canvas) to an existing document, e.g. a detail view of one part. Tabs can start from a template too.
- **Update a document** — rework a whole tab or make a small adjustment.
- **Share a document** — turn a document into a [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/) to hand to someone.
- **Rename a document** and **Delete a document** — tidy your library from the AI tool.

For building the picture, the AI can just describe the **graph** — the nodes and what connects to what — and livediagram lays it out, so the tool doesn't have to work out coordinates. It only needs to say what exists and what links to what, or hand over the same thing as **Mermaid** text (a flowchart, state diagram, or ER diagram). It can group related boxes into labelled sections and choose a flow, a tree (an org chart), or a mind map. Box labels are kept to short headings: when the AI writes a long sentence, the box shows the heading and the full text goes into the box's note, so a chatty AI doesn't fill your diagram with paragraphs.

Beyond plain boxes and arrows, the AI can fill in **sticky notes** (in the sticky colours), **tables**, **code blocks**, **entities** (a titled record with field rows, for data models), **swimlanes**, and **bar, line, and pie charts**. Your AI tool may also surface a few ready-made **prompts** (like "Diagram this" or "Flowchart from steps") as quick actions, so you don't have to know the tool names.

When the AI changes a document you have open, you see it happen: the change arrives on your canvas straight away, outlined for a moment in your colour, and a note at the bottom says what changed. **Show** takes you to it and **Undo** puts it back (your own Undo shortcut only ever undoes your own edits). Anything you change at the same time is kept, and an element you have selected is left alone until you let go of it.

The AI does the thinking; livediagram validates, lays out, saves, and renders what it produces. New documents an AI creates land at the root of **My documents** (or your default folder, if you set one), each with a **Made by AI** badge; the **Made by AI** filter lists them all, wherever you file them.

## What a connection can and can't touch

By default the token behind a connection has full read and write access to your documents — including the documents in your teams' shared libraries — so only connect tools you trust, and revoke the token if you stop using one. If you'd rather a tool could look but not change anything, tick **read-only access** on the approval screen when you connect it: the connection can then find and read your documents but can't create, edit, share, rename, or delete anything (the block is enforced by our server, not just the tool). Its token shows a **Read-only** badge in the [API Tokens](/help/account-and-data/api-tokens/) category of Settings.

Either way, a connection can't manage your teams or your account: inviting members, changing roles, and deleting things other than documents stay in the app.
