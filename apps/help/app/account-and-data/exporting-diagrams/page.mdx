Your documents are not locked inside livediagram. When you want a copy to drop into a slide deck, a doc, or a chat, you can export a tab as an image or a PDF, or as a file another tool can open. And when you want the document to stay live wherever you put it, you can embed it instead.

## Exporting a tab

Export works on one tab at a time, so switch to the tab you want first. Then either:

- open the tab's **⋯** menu, expand **Content**, and choose **Export**; or
- open the Explorer panel's menu and choose **Export**, which exports the tab you are on.

To export only part of a tab, select two or more elements and choose **Export** on the toolbar that appears for a multi-selection.

  <ExportMenu />
</Figure>

Pick a format:

- **PNG**, **SVG** or **PDF** for a picture: PNG to paste into docs and chat, SVG to stay sharp at any size, PDF to print. An options screen lets you add the canvas background pattern, tilt the picture into the isometric view, and include hidden layers, then **Download**.
- **JSON** for a `.livediagram-tab.json` file that [imports](/help/tabs/import-tabs/) back into livediagram with everything intact. This is the one to keep as a backup.
- **Mermaid**, **Markdown** or **Excalidraw** to carry the structure into another tool. Each opens the text first, so you can copy it or download it.

A few things to know about image exports:

- Looping element animations and animated backgrounds **freeze to a static frame**, so a still image always looks clean.
- Frames are drawn behind their contents, just as they are on the canvas.

[Exporting a Tab](/help/tabs/export-tabs/) covers every format and option in detail, including how [Illustrate](/help/canvas/illustrate/) pages export one sheet at a time.

An image is the right choice when you need a fixed snapshot: a diagram for a report, an attachment, or a printout.

## Embedding a live view

When you want the document to keep updating wherever it lives, [embed it](/help/collaboration/sharing/embeds/) instead. An embed is an iframe of a shared document that you can drop into Notion, a wiki, or any page that accepts an iframe.

An embed follows the role of the pass it was made from: a **Viewer** pass embeds a read-only canvas, an **Editor** pass an editable one. It hides the editor's controls, leaving the canvas with pan and zoom, and it stays live: as the document is edited, the embed updates in place rather than showing a stale snapshot.

To grab one, open the **Share** dialog, find the pass you want, open its **Embed** menu and choose **Copy iframe** for a ready-made snippet you can paste straight into your page.

<Tip>
  Reach for an image when you need a fixed snapshot, and an embed when you want the document to keep
  updating in place wherever you have put it. For a picture that updates itself, see [Live
  Image](/help/collaboration/sharing/live-image/).
</Tip>
