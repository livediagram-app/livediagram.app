When a diagram gets messy, you usually want one of two things: either everything is nearly aligned and you just want the drift gone, or the layout never really happened and you want it computed for you. liveplanner has a tool for each, both one press away on the **Tidy Up** button in Diagram mode, and in the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/)'s **Actions**. They are complementary, not duplicates: one snaps the positions you already have, the other recomputes positions from how the diagram is wired together.

  <CleanupBeforeAfter />
</Figure>

## The two tools

- **Auto-align** is a grid snap. It rounds every element's size to a 10 px grid and lines shapes up by the edges and centres they nearly share, so near-aligned shapes become exactly aligned, small drift collapses, and nothing moves far. See [Auto-Align](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-align/).
- **Auto Layout** ("tidy up" in the command palette) reads the arrow graph and computes brand-new positions, laying out connected shapes into clean layers, or into a style you pick: flowchart (down or right), tree, or mindmap. It can legitimately move an element across the canvas. See [Auto Layout](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/).

## How to run them

In Diagram mode, press the **Tidy Up** button (the broom in the bottom-right corner, just left of Layers). It opens a panel with a picture of each Auto Layout style (**Smart**, **Flowchart Down**, **Flowchart Right**, **Tree** and **Mindmap**) and **Auto-align** below them. Rest the mouse on one and the tab is laid out that way behind the panel, so you can compare styles before you commit; move away and everything goes back. Click the one you want to keep it.

You can also open the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/) (⌘K / Ctrl+K), type **Auto Layout** or **Auto-align**, and pick the tidier you want under **Actions**. Both run on the whole active tab as a single undoable step, so if the result isn't what you hoped, one undo puts everything back exactly as it was.

<Figure caption="Typing layout in the command palette lists Auto Layout and its styles under Actions.">
  <SearchCommandPalette />
</Figure>

The tidiers are for editors: they aren't offered to a participant or in a view-only session, and they leave a [locked tab](/help/tabs/locking-tabs/) alone, so unlock the tab first. Inside an unlocked tab, a [locked element](/help/canvas/locking/), or anything on a [locked layer](/help/canvas/layers-visibility-and-locking/), stays exactly where it is.

Neither tool will trash your work. Each one is tried before it's offered, and one that would change nothing, cover a shape, or pull shapes out of their lanes is greyed out with the reason. Run one from the command palette and the same check applies: nothing moves, and a short notice says why. See [When a style isn't offered](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/#when-a-style-isn-t-offered).

<Note>
  Both tools work entirely offline and deterministically. They never call out to anything, so
  they're always available.
</Note>
