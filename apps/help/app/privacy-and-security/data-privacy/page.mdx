Your documents are the whole point, so it helps to know exactly where they live and who can reach them. livediagram stores documents on Cloudflare infrastructure behind an authenticated API, never in a third-party analytics product, and you keep control of them whether you sign in or stay a guest. This page explains how it works; the [Privacy Policy](/help/policies/privacy-policy/) is the formal statement for the hosted service.

## Where your documents live

Documents are saved through a single Cloudflare Worker API that holds the database binding. The browser never talks to the database directly.

- **Durable storage** lives in Cloudflare D1, the API's database. Each document, its tabs, folders, share links, and its Timeline events are rows there.
- **Real-time state** (who is in the room, live cursors, in-flight edits) lives in a Cloudflare Durable Object, one room per document, while people are actively collaborating.
- **Images** you add are stored in Cloudflare R2 object storage and served back through the same API. Location and camera details are stripped from a JPEG before it is stored.

**Or nowhere but your own machine.** A document created in [Offline Mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) lives only in this browser's IndexedDB. It is never sent to the API, so none of the storage above holds a copy of it, and it stays that way until you choose **Sync Document** to move it to the cloud.

The hosted version runs on Cloudflare, and a self-hoster runs the same stack on their own account. Your document content goes to another company only when you use a feature that needs one:

- **The AI assistant** sends your prompt and the elements on the current tab to the AI provider the deployment uses (OpenAI on livediagram.app) to get an answer. Reading sticky notes from a photo sends crops of the photo to Google's Gemini. Nothing is sent until you use them, and a deployment without an AI key has neither.
- **[Google Drive](/help/account-and-data/google-drive/)**, if you connect it, gets a copy of your documents in your own Drive, written straight from your browser.
- **Emails** about your work go through the email provider. They name the document, and a mention or an assigned action quotes a little of the comment or action (see [Email Notifications](/help/account-and-data/email-notifications/)).

## What is stored, and who can read it

A document is owned by an identity. That identity is either a signed-in account or, for guests, a per-browser id kept in your browser's local storage. Only the owner, people holding a valid [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/), and the members of the [team](/help/collaboration/teams/) whose library the document is in can read a document. A document you [share to the Community](/help/collaboration/sharing/community/) is public until you remove it. Requests for a document you cannot access return a plain "not found", so the API never even confirms a private document exists.

## Staying in control

- **Guest by default.** You can build and share documents without ever creating an account. See [working as a guest](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/).
- **Keep it in the browser.** [Offline Mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) keeps a document off the server entirely.
- **Share on your terms.** Add a [share password](/help/privacy-and-security/share-link-security/) to a document or an expiry to a share link, and revoke links at any time.
- **Delete what you own.** Deleting a document moves it to the [Trash](/help/account-and-data/trash/) for 30 days, then removes it with its tabs, comments and share links; you can delete it permanently from the Trash sooner. Signed-in users can [delete their whole account](/help/account-and-data/deleting-your-data/#deleting-your-account), which removes their documents, folders, images, tokens and preferences straight away.
- **Opt out of analytics.** The anonymous usage events have a switch in Settings. See [What We Collect](/help/privacy-and-security/what-we-collect/).
