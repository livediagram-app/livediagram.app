A clean, consistent look does not have to mean fiddling with colours one element at a time. Pair a [theme](/help/canvas/themes/) for the whole tab with [style presets](/help/palette/shapes/style-presets/) on the elements that matter, and a rough diagram becomes a polished one in seconds.

## Start with a theme

A theme sets the look of the whole tab at once. Open it from the **Theme & canvas** paintbrush in the bottom-right corner of the editor. Picking a theme writes its colours, background, and pattern onto the tab, and from then on new elements inherit the theme's fill, border, and text colours, so everything you add stays on-brand automatically.

The catalogue ships with twenty-six themes, grouped by temperament (**Formal**, **Cool**, **Warm**, **Dark** and **Multi-colour**), so you browse by mood rather than scanning a wall of swatches. Themes you save yourself sit in a **Custom** category beside them. See [Themes](/help/canvas/themes/) and [Custom Themes](/help/canvas/themes/custom-themes/).

Switching theme respects deliberate choices. Elements still on the old theme's colours take the new theme's; an element you coloured by hand keeps its colour, and a background pattern you hand-picked survives too. When you do want everything back in line, **Reset elements to theme** in the same dialog recolours every element on the tab.

## Add emphasis with presets

Once the overall look is set, **style presets** let you make individual elements stand out without dialling each field by hand. Right-click a shape (or a selection holding several) and open **Style**, where the presets lead.

For shapes you get one grid of looks derived from the active theme, in four tiers, each running quiet to loud: the **theme** accent at rising strength (plus a card per branch colour on a multi-colour theme), **neutral** greys, **border** treatments (hairline, outline, dotted and so on), and the semantic **status** colours (info, success, warning, danger, highlight). That is twenty looks on a single-colour theme. Each pairs a colour with a matching border weight and pattern, with the label colour auto-contrasted so text stays readable; your corner radius is left alone.

A preset follows the theme: switch theme later and a shape on the Bold preset becomes the new theme's Bold, rather than keeping the old colours.

Arrows get their own twelve presets: solid weights, dashed and dotted patterns, and animated flows. Every preset grid ends with **Reset to default**, which returns the element to its theme look in one click. On a computer, hovering a preset previews it before you commit. See [Style Presets](/help/palette/shapes/style-presets/).
