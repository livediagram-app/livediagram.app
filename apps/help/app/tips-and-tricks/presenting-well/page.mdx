A finished canvas shows everything at once, which is great for reference but harder to narrate. livediagram gives you a few ways to focus an audience as you walk through your work, whether you are on a call, a projector, or sharing your screen.

## Zen mode

When you want the full canvas with nothing else competing for attention, [Zen mode](/help/tools/zen-mode/) hides every part of the interface, the header, tab bar, palette, and panels, leaving only the canvas and zoom controls. It is purely a view setting: it changes nothing on the canvas and is not shared with other people, so each viewer focuses independently (view-only visitors can use it too). Turn it on with `Z` or **Zen** in the Palette's selection-mode dropdown, and leave with `Z`, `Escape`, or the **Exit zen mode** button beside the zoom controls.

## The laser pointer

For pointing things out live, switch to the **Laser** tool (`K`). Moving the pointer leaves a glowing trail in your participant colour that fades after about a second, and on a shared session other people see your laser in real time, so you can sweep across the canvas and direct the room's attention. Its panel sets the pen: width, colour, how long the trail lasts (Quick, Normal or Long) and its effect. See [Laser](/help/selection-modes/laser/).

## The spotlight

To focus on one element at a time, switch to the [Spotlight](/help/selection-modes/spotlight/) tool (desktop only). It dims the rest of the canvas and lights up whatever sits under your cursor, so the room's eye lands exactly where yours does. Unlike the laser, it is **only on your screen**: everyone else in the document still sees the whole canvas. It shines when you are sharing your screen or presenting in the room; move the cursor from shape to shape to walk through the canvas one piece at a time. Its panel sets the light's size, how far the rest dims, its edge and its shape.

## A slide deck

When the walkthrough has a running order, build one. The [Slide Deck](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/) tool (desktop only) lets you make slides out of the elements you select (a slide can draw from any tab, and the same element can appear on as many as you like), write presenter notes against each one, then present the lot full screen, one slide at a time. It broadcasts nothing: you show it by sharing your screen, the same as everything else in the meeting.

Two things worth knowing before a talk. **Slides you are not going to use can be hidden rather than deleted**, so last week's detail and the section you cut for time stay in the deck without turning up in the run. And the **Presentation** settings (the cog in the Slide Deck panel, and on screen while you present) carry the transition, an **Auto-advance** interval and **Loop the deck**: set those two and a deck runs itself on a screen in the room with nobody driving. While a deck runs, the editor's own shortcuts are off and the deck's take over; see [Keyboard Shortcuts](/help/tips-and-tricks/keyboard-shortcuts/#while-presenting).
