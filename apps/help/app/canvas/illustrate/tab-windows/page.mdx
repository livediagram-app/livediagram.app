A **Tab Window** shows another tab of the same document, live, inside a slide or page. Draw the diagram on its own tab, put a window onto it on a slide, and the slide follows every change you (or a collaborator) make to the diagram. There is no copy to paste again when the diagram changes.

## Take the tour

The first time you select a tab window, a small **Tour** toolbar appears beside its toolbar. Press **Play** for a short tour on your own window: the tab it shows live, Open (or Choose a Tab on an empty window), its menu, and resizing it. Press the cross if you would rather not; either way it is not offered again. To be offered it again, open **Settings**, then **Tours**, open **Element Tours** and switch **Tab Window Tour** back on.

## Adding a Tab Window

<Step n={1}>
  In Illustrate mode, open **Media** in the palette and pick **Tab Window**. Tap the page to drop
  one, or drag to draw it at the size you want.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **Choose a Tab** in the window and pick the tab to show. The list holds every tab of the
  document but the one you are on and the Plan tabs (Boards, Sheets and Dashboards).
</Step>

The window draws that tab's content **zoomed to fit**, centred on the tab's own background. Resize the window and the drawing follows.

To show a different tab later, open the window's menu, then **Tab Window**, and pick another under **Shows**.

## Opening the Tab

Hover over a window (or select it) and press **Open** in its top-right corner to go to the tab it shows. The menu's **Tab Window** section has the same **Open** row.

## What a Window Shows While It Waits

- **Loading Flow…** while the tab's content is on its way (a tab's content arrives when it is first shown).
- **Couldn't load Flow.** with **Retry** if it didn't arrive.
- **Nothing on Flow yet.** for a tab with nothing on it.
- **This tab isn't available.** when the tab has been deleted, or isn't shared with you.

A window inside the tab it shows is drawn as a frame with its tab's name, never the tab again, so windows never repeat inside each other.

## Exporting

A PDF, PNG or SVG export draws each window's tab inside it, just as on screen (see [Exporting and Presenting Pages](/help/canvas/illustrate/exporting-pages/)). Thumbnails, such as the Contents list and the Slide Deck, show the window as a frame named for its tab.

## Who Can Do What

An Editor places windows and chooses what they show. A Participant or Viewer sees them and can open the tab a window shows.
