A **layout** is a ready-made arrangement put onto one infographic page ([slide pages](/help/canvas/pages-mode/slide-pages/) and [logo pages](/help/canvas/pages-mode/logo-pages/) offer layouts of their own): a starting point you then make your own. It is made of ordinary elements (text, stat rows, charts, icons, image placeholders), so everything in it can be edited, restyled, moved or deleted like anything else on the canvas.

New to infographic pages? Open the page's cog and press **Show Me** at the top of its panel: it opens the Layout and Background popovers on your own page, then its size, so you can pick as you go. The [Pages Tour](/help/canvas/pages-mode/pages-tour/) offers it the first time you work on an infographic.

## Choosing a Layout

Every infographic page has a **Layout** button in its title bar, above its top-right corner, before **Background**, **Tidy**, the padlock and the cog. Press it, on an empty page or a full one, and the page's layouts open in a popover under it, the eight categories first, four across; on a phone they open in a sheet from the bottom of the screen, two across. Zoomed out, or on a phone, the button shows just its icon.

Press a category to see its layouts. They slide in from the right, and **All layouts** in the bar above them (the category named beside it) slides you back to the categories. Each tile is the real layout, drawn for **this** page at its size and orientation, in your tab's theme and font, with its sample words, icons and charts, so what you see is what you get.

**Hover a tile to preview it on the page.** The page shows that layout as it would land, with your own words and pictures already moved into it, in the page's own background; move away and your page comes back. A preview is only a picture: nothing is placed and nothing is added to your undo history until you press a tile.

**An empty page** also offers its layouts inside itself, on a card headed Start From a Layout in the middle of the page. Press a layout there to place it (the card has no hover preview, since it would cover the card's own tiles), or **Hide** the card until you next open the tab. It goes on its own once anything lands on the page, and the **Layout** button still has every layout.

## Placing It

**Press a tile and the layout is placed at once**, on an empty page or a full one, with nothing to confirm. It is a single step, so one **Undo** brings the page's old layout back.

**Your content comes with you.** Changing the layout of a page you have already written on keeps what you wrote: your title goes into the new layout's title, your subtitle and other large lines into its large lines, the rest of your words into its body text, figures like "73%" into its figures, your pictures into its picture places (the biggest first), and a stat row's, chart's or callout's contents into the same kind of element. Each piece takes its new place's position and style, and long words shrink to fit. Only what you wrote moves: sample text you never changed stays behind, so the new layout shows its own. Anything the new layout has no place for is removed with the old layout. The hover preview already shows your content in its new places.

After placing, nothing is selected and the popover closes, so the page reads clean.

## How Layouts Fit Your Page

- **Any size, any orientation.** A layout is measured against the page it lands on and keeps a margin all round. On a tall page it stacks; on a wide page (landscape, a slide) it sets things side by side, so Process and Timeline turn from a column of numbered discs into a strip across the page.
- **Type sized for a page.** Headlines are scaled to fill their space, body text uses the larger text sizes, so a page reads from across the room and exports exactly as it looks.
- **Your theme, your background.** A layout carries no colours of its own: the tab's theme paints it, and on a dark background its text is drawn light.
- **Change your mind later.** Turn the page or change its size and what is on it stays on it, scaled down as a whole where it no longer fits, never cut off. The layout is not rebuilt, so a stacked layout stays stacked: to get the side-by-side version, place the layout again.
