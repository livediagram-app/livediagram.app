A **finish** is how a tab's elements are drawn, apart from their colours. Every tab has one, and any shape, arrow, text, sticky note or table can carry its own. Switching a finish never changes an element's size, colour or text, so switching back puts everything exactly as it was.

## The three finishes

- **Flexible** is the everyday look: soft rounded corners, medium lines, a gentle shadow under boxes and a paper peel under sticky notes. New tabs start here.
- **Professional** is crisp and print-ready: square corners, thin lines (line icons included, unless you gave one a weight of its own), clean Inter lettering, no default shadows, and arrows you draw become right-angled connectors.
- **Sketch** looks drawn by hand on a whiteboard: wobbly double-stroked outlines, coloured fills shaded over with pencil lines, a handwriting font, arrowheads drawn as two loose strokes, and icons, technology icons and stickers drawn with a hand-drawn wobble.

Whatever you set on an element yourself wins in every finish. A corner, border width, shadow, arrow thickness or font you picked is drawn as you picked it; a finish only changes what you left unset.

## Setting a tab's finish

Open **Tab Style** (the **Theme & canvas** button in the bottom-right corner) and press **Finish** under **Fine-tune**. Click **Flexible**, **Professional** or **Sketch**, and the whole tab redraws in it straight away: any element that carried a finish of its own follows the tab again. The **Preview** under the options shows a small sample diagram in the chosen finish, on your tab's background.

## Setting one element's finish

Right-click an element, open **Style**, then **Finish**. The four tiles are **Follow Tab**, **Flexible**, **Professional** and **Sketch**. With a mouse, hovering a tile previews it; clicking commits it as one undo step. **Follow Tab** removes the element's own finish so it follows the tab again. Select several elements and the multi-selection menu's **Style** has the same category, which sets every selected element that can take a finish.

## Good to know

- **The sketch is the same everywhere.** Each element's wobble is worked out from the element itself, so it looks identical on every screen, after a reload and in exports, and only changes when you resize it.
- **Selecting and connecting ignore the wobble.** Arrows attach, guides snap and clicks land on an element's true outline.
- **Some things keep their look in Sketch.** Freehand strokes, images and the faces of charts, checklists and code blocks stay as they are. Sticky notes, frames, lanes and tables keep solid fills so writing on them stays easy to read, and circle and diamond arrowheads keep their shapes because they carry meaning.
- **Boxes with things on them stay solid in Sketch.** A card under captions, like a flowchart's key, keeps a plain fill so what sits on it stays readable; only shapes that stand alone get pencil shading. Shading always sits over the shape's own colour, so labels stay readable on a light or a dark canvas.
- **The palette follows the tab.** Shape, text, sticky note, arrow and table tiles show the tab's finish, so you can see how a new element will look before you add it.
- **Exports match the canvas.** PNG, SVG and PDF exports draw the same finish you see.
- **Only Editors change finishes.** Participants and Viewers see every element in its finish but can't change it.
