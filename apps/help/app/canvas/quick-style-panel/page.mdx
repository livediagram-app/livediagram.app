Select one or more shapes or arrows and a small **quick style** panel appears on the right edge of the canvas. It holds the handful of style choices people make most often, so restyling is one click rather than a trip through the right-click menu. The right-click menu is still the home of every setting; the panel only repeats the favourites.

## What's in it

From top to bottom:

- **Stroke**: seven colours for the outline or line. The first is the theme's own default.
- **Background**: seven colours for a shape's fill.
- **Stroke width**: thin, medium or thick.
- **Stroke style**: solid, dashed or dotted for shapes. For arrows it is solid, dashed or **flowing**, a dashed line that marches along its direction, so an animated connector is a single click.
- **Text alignment**: left, centre or right.
- **Icon alignment**, when a shape carries an icon: before, above or after its label.
- **Clear styles**, at the bottom.

A section only shows when something in the selection can use it, so an arrow on its own shows stroke, width and style, and a shape with an icon gains icon alignment.

## Colours that follow the theme

The seven colours come from the diagram's theme. A multi-colour theme offers its own six branch colours; any other theme offers red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet in its own tone, so a muted theme gets muted colours. Backgrounds always keep the label readable.

Switch the theme later and colours picked here move with it: a shape you made green stays green, in the new theme's green.

## Styling many at once

Select several shapes and arrows, even of different kinds, and a choice applies to every one that supports it. A highlighted option means every selected element shares it; when they differ, nothing is highlighted. Each choice is one undo step.

## The next shape remembers

When you change a shape's colours, width, style or alignment, from this panel or from the right-click menu, the next shape **of the same kind** you draw arrives the same way. Make a circle green and the next circle is green; the next square is not. Arrows remember their own style, separately from shapes.

This only applies to shapes and arrows you draw yourself, from the palette, a tool or a quick-connect plus. Pasted, duplicated and templated elements keep their own looks. The memory belongs to the diagram and stays in this browser.

## Clear styles

**Clear styles** returns the selection to the theme's defaults and forgets the remembered style for those kinds, so the next shape is plain again. Use it when a style was meant as a one-off.

  Building a flowchart? Style the first decision diamond, the first step box and the first arrow the
  way you want, then keep drawing: every new diamond, box and arrow follows on its own.

## Good to know

- The panel is there on desktop layouts only. On a phone the long-press menu has the same options.
- It steps aside for the [palette](/help/palette/) and other panels, and hides while a right-click menu is open.
- Every option has a name for screen readers and a tooltip; each row is a group you move through with the arrow keys.
