Select one or more shapes, arrows or text elements and a small **quick style** panel appears on the left edge of the canvas, halfway down, clear of the Palette on the right. It holds the handful of style choices people make most often, so restyling is one click rather than a trip through the right-click menu. The right-click menu is still the home of every setting; the panel only repeats the favourites.

## What's in it

From top to bottom:

- **Stroke**: seven colours for the outline or line. The first is the theme's own default.
- **Background**: seven colours for a shape's fill.
- **Text colour**, for text elements: seven colours for the words. The first is the theme's own text colour. (A shape's label colour is in the right-click menu's Colours section.)
- **Stroke width**: thin, medium or thick.
- **Stroke style**: solid, dashed or dotted for shapes. For arrows it is solid, dashed or **flowing**, a dashed line that marches along its direction, so an animated connector is a single click.
- **Text alignment**: left, centre or right.
- **Icon alignment**, when a shape carries an icon: before, above or after its label.
- **Clear styles**, at the bottom.

A section only shows when something in the selection can use it, so an arrow on its own shows stroke, width and style, a text element on its own shows text colour, and a shape with an icon gains icon alignment.

## Colours that follow the theme

The seven colours come from the tab's theme. A multi-colour theme offers its own six branch colours; any other theme offers red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet in its own tone, so a muted theme gets muted colours. Backgrounds always keep the label readable, and text colours are always dark enough (or, on a dark canvas, light enough) to read.

Switch the theme later and colours picked here move with it: a shape you made green stays green, in the new theme's green.

## Your own colours

Right-click any of the six theme colours in a row (or focus it and press **Shift+F10** or the context-menu key) to open a small colour picker. The colour you pick is saved into that swatch, replacing the theme's colour there, and a small dot in its corner marks it as yours. Screen readers and the tooltip name it too, for example "Custom orange, in place of Green".

Picking a colour only changes the swatch; click it afterwards to use it. **Clear override** in the same popover brings the theme's colour back. Your swatches belong to you and to the theme you made them in: they follow you to other documents and devices, and switching to another theme shows that theme's own colours (and any you changed there). The first swatch, the theme default, always stays the theme's. For colours a whole diagram should share, make a [custom theme](/help/canvas/themes/).

## Styling many at once

Select several shapes and arrows, even of different kinds, and a choice applies to every one that supports it. A highlighted option means every selected element shares it; when they differ, nothing is highlighted. Each choice is one undo step.

## The next shape remembers

When you change a shape's colours, width, style or alignment, from this panel or from the right-click menu, the next shape **of the same kind** you draw arrives the same way. Make a circle green and the next circle is green; the next square is not. Arrows remember their own style, separately from shapes, and text elements remember their colour.

You can also set the style **before** you draw: pick a tool (press `R` for a square, say) with nothing selected, and the panel shows **Next square**. Whatever you choose there is how the next square arrives, without changing anything already on the canvas.

This only applies to shapes and arrows you draw yourself, from the palette, a tool or a quick-connect plus. Pasted, duplicated and templated elements keep their own looks. The memory belongs to the document and stays in this browser.

## Clear styles

**Clear styles** returns the selection to the theme's defaults and forgets the remembered style for those kinds, so the next shape is plain again. Use it when a style was meant as a one-off.

## Good to know

- The panel is there on desktop layouts only. On a phone the long-press menu has the same options.
- It never covers the [palette](/help/palette/) or other panels: with a panel already on the left edge it moves below, above or beside it. It hides while a right-click menu is open.
- Every option has a name for screen readers and a tooltip; each row is a group you move through with the arrow keys.
