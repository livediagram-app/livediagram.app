Every element on the canvas starts from the palette. The palette organises its contents into categories so it stays compact however much it holds, and adding an element is usually a single tap.

## Picking a Category

The palette's category dropdown, at the right of the panel under its title, lists every category the current mode offers, grouped under headings. The palette opens on **Popular**, twelve tiles drawn from across the categories, and you can switch to any other category from the dropdown. In Diagram mode the categories are:

- **Shapes**: the common nodes, such as square, circle, diamond, cylinder, parallelogram, hexagon, document and stadium.
- **Write**: the wordy elements, such as text, sticky notes, pages and annotations.
- **Draw**: the gesture tools, such as the pencil, shape pen, highlighter, polygon and arrows.
- **Build**: the structural elements, such as mind nodes, lanes, frames, timelines and tables.
- **Icons**: a searchable grid of single-colour icons you can drop on the canvas or onto a shape.
- **Stickers**: colour emoji and word badges for reacting to work and marking status.
- **Tech**: full-colour cloud and infrastructure marks for system-architecture diagrams.
- **Media**: an uploaded image, or a circular avatar.
- **Collaborate**: elements that come alive with the room, such as estimates, temperature checks, quizzes, comments and actions, timers, votes, polls and agendas.

Other modes arrange their own set. [Illustrate mode](/help/canvas/illustrate/) adds **Components** (themed composites such as Banner, Hero and Header), **Devices** (browser, laptop, phone and other frames for wireframing) and **Data** (charts), and [Plan mode](/help/canvas/plan-mode/) leads with its cards and boards. If you have imported a [shape library](/help/explorer/shape-libraries/), its shapes appear under **My shapes**.

## Dropping or Drawing an Element

Almost every tile arms a combined gesture when you click it.

<Step n={1}>
  Click a tile in the palette. It stays pressed to show it is armed, and a banner along the top of
  the canvas says what comes next (for a shape, "Tap to drop or drag to draw" and the shape's name),
  with a Cancel button.
</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Tap an empty spot on the canvas to drop the element at its natural size on that point.
</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Or, instead of a single tap, drag out a box and release to create the element at that size. Both
  corners snap to nearby shapes as you drag.
</Step>

While a tile is armed you can even start the drag on top of another element: the new one is drawn over it rather than selecting it. Press Escape (or Cancel on the banner) to disarm a tile without adding anything. Text lands ready for typing; everything else lands selected, ready to move or restyle. Choosing **Image** opens the image picker once you have placed it.

If a boxed element is already selected when you pick a tile, the new one inherits its width and height, so you can chain together similarly sized nodes quickly. Circles and diamonds are an exception: they are inherently square, so they snap back to a square using the larger inherited dimension.

You can also **drag a tile straight from the palette onto the canvas** instead of tapping. As you drag, a translucent preview of the element follows your cursor at its real size, so you can see exactly where, and how big, it will land before you let go.

## Adding from the Keyboard

Single keys arm the most common elements without opening the palette: `R` for a rectangle, `O` for an oval, `D` for a diamond, `C` for a cylinder, `G` for a parallelogram, `T` for text, `N` for a sticky note, `A` for an arrow, `F` for a frame and `9` for an image. See [Keyboard Shortcuts](/help/tips-and-tricks/keyboard-shortcuts/) for the full list. You can also **double-click empty canvas** to drop a text element and start typing straight away.

## Annotations Land in the Middle

An [annotation](/help/canvas/annotations/) is the one element without the draw gesture. It is a fixed-size marker, so there is no size to choose: it lands at the centre of the visible canvas, already selected, so you can drag it where you want.

<Tip>
  Icons can be added to a shape rather than as standalone elements: drag an icon tile onto a shape,
  or click an icon while a shape is selected, and it attaches beside that shape's label.
</Tip>
