Changing a diagram's look is a matter of picking a new theme and watching the canvas update. livediagram brings the theme picker front and centre in a dedicated dialog, so there is plenty of room to compare looks.

## Opening the Dialog

Click the **Theme & canvas** button in the bottom-right corner of the editor, beside the zoom controls (it is there on every screen size, phones included). It opens the **Tab Style** dialog on its home, which has two parts: **Looks** at the top, and **Fine-tune** below. You can also run **Open Theme** from the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/) to open the dialog straight on its **Colours** panel, or **Open Canvas Options** to land on its **Canvas** panel.

The button only appears for people who can edit the tab, so it is missing for a Participant or a Viewer on a shared link, in an embed, in zen mode, and in [Draw mode](/help/canvas/draw-mode/).

The dialog is an editor, not a wizard: there is no Apply or Cancel. Every change applies to the active tab as you click, and closing it (with the X, the Escape key, or a click outside it) simply dismisses it without rolling anything back. Undo takes a theme change back like any other edit. **Show Me** in the dialog's title row takes you on a short tour of it.

## Hover to Preview

With a mouse, hover any option in the dialog (a look, a theme, a finish, a typeface, a text size or a pattern) and the canvas behind it shows the tab in that option, easing the colours across. Move away and the tab goes back to how it was; nothing is saved until you click. On a touch screen there is no hover, so a tap applies the option straight away (and Undo takes it back).

## Picking a Look

A look restyles the whole tab in one click. Each one sets four things together: the theme, the [finish](/help/canvas/finish/), the tab font and the canvas pattern. There are twelve, six to a page of the carousel: **Everyday**, **Whiteboard**, **Graph Paper**, **Boardroom**, **Notebook**, **Midnight**, **Seaside**, **Editorial**, **Engineer**, **Workshop**, **Garden** and **Studio**. Each card shows a small sample diagram drawn in that look, with a line on what it is made of.

Picking a look counts as one change, so a single undo takes it back. Elements that carry their own finish or font keep them. A look's card stays selected while the tab still matches it. Change any of the four settings yourself and the tab has its own mix: a **Current** card then leads the carousel, showing the tab as it is.

## Fine-tuning

Below the looks, **Fine-tune** lists four rows, each showing its current setting: **Colours** (the theme), **Finish**, **Text** (the tab's font and text size) and **Canvas** (the backdrop pattern). Press a row to open that panel in place; the bar across its top (**Tab Style**, with the panel's name beside it) takes you back to the home. The **Canvas** panel holds the [backdrop pattern and colours](/help/canvas/the-canvas/changing-the-background/), the **Text** panel the [tab's font and text size](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/choosing-fonts/), and the **Finish** panel the tab's [finish](/help/canvas/finish/). The rest of this article covers the **Colours** panel, where you pick a theme on its own.

## Picking a Theme

The Colours panel lists every theme in one carousel, nine to a page: **Default** first, then the rest of the twenty-four built-in themes, then your own [custom themes](/help/canvas/themes/custom-themes/) and a **New theme** card. It opens on the page holding the tab's current theme; the arrows or the page dots move between pages.

  Hover a theme to preview it on the canvas.

<Step n={2}>

Every theme works in light and dark mode: each person sees the tab in their own [appearance's](/help/tools/dark-mode/) version of the theme, so you can pick any theme without thinking about who works in which mode.

To build on a built-in theme, hover its card and press **Copy**: the theme builder opens with its colours filled in, ready to save as a [custom theme](/help/canvas/themes/custom-themes/).

## Recolouring Elements

Applying a theme recolours the tab's backdrop and the elements on it, but keeps colours you have hand-set, so deliberate customisations survive the switch. That goes for the backdrop too: a canvas colour, pattern or pattern opacity you chose yourself stays put. Arrows are the one exception: every arrow's stroke always snaps to the new theme's colour, because connectors left on an old hue read as broken. Sticky notes keep their own note colours. A shape carrying a [style preset](/help/palette/shapes/style-presets/) moves to the new theme's version of that preset, and **Reset to Default** in a shape's presets brings a hand-coloured shape back to the theme's colours.
