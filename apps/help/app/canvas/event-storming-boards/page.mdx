Event storming is a workshop method for exploring a business domain together. A group puts sticky notes on a wall — things that happened, the commands that caused them, the people and systems involved — until the story of the domain is visible end to end. Alberto Brandolini's notation gives each colour a fixed meaning, so a wall of paper reads the same to everyone who knows the method.

Starting a diagram from the **Event storming** [template](/help/canvas/templates/) gives you a board built for that, rather than a blank canvas you have to shape yourself. It starts with a single orange domain event, **Board Created**, the first thing that happened, and nothing else on the wall.

## What makes it a board, not just a diagram

The board knows what it is, and the editor adapts around it:

- **The notation is the palette.** The [Event Storming category](/help/palette/event-storming/) opens first and fills the panel, so the eight note kinds are one click away instead of buried behind a dropdown.
- **Notes behave like paper.** Each kind has a fixed size and a slight tilt, and the text scales to fit rather than the note growing to fit the text. You cannot resize a note, the same way you cannot resize a sticky you have already peeled off the pad.
- **Notes are written in marker, in capitals.** Type however you like — the board paints it in a marker face, in caps, the way a pen on paper does. It stays readable from across the room and the wall reads as one hand rather than eight. Your typed wording is what is saved, so a note you export or search for keeps the capitals you actually used.
- **Dropping a note starts typing.** Drag one out and the cursor is already in it — the drag said where and what, so all that is left is the words.
- **Right-click offers verbs, not styling.** Cut, copy, duplicate, bring to front, send to back, remove. A note's colour, shape and text treatment _are_ the notation, so controls that could only break the grammar are not offered.

## Adding a step in the middle

The left-to-right order of the wall is the story: this happened, then that. So when a step turns out to be missing, you should not have to shuffle everything after it by hand.

Start dragging a note, hold it over the **gap between two notes**, and **press Alt** (Option on a Mac). The board makes room: everything from that gap onwards slides right to open a slot, and the note you are dragging sits in it. Drop, and the note lands exactly there.

It works two ways round, which is the point:

- **A new note from the palette** — it lands in the slot, ready to type into.
- **A note already on the wall** — drag it to where it should have been all along. This is the one you will reach for most.

<Tip>
  Press Alt **after** you have started dragging, not before. Some desktops treat Alt-and-click as
  "move the window", so a drag that begins with Alt already down never reaches the page.
</Tip>

- **Nothing happens unless you press Alt.** An ordinary drag is an ordinary drag — drop a note next to a row and nothing else on the board stirs. Let go of Alt mid-drag and the board closes up again, there and then.
- **Nothing moves until you drop.** The opening slot is a preview. Nobody else sees it and nothing is saved until you let go.
- **One undo puts it all back.** The note and everything that moved aside are a single step in the history — including where a moved note started out.
- **The whole board makes room, not just the row.** A command sitting above its event, or a policy below it, moves with the event it belongs to, so the columns stay lined up.
- **A moved note leaves its gap behind.** The wall opens up where the note is going; where it came from is left as it is, for you to tidy or fill as you like.
- **A note you have locked stays put** and the board opens around it. Arrows between notes stay attached: one that spans the new note simply stretches.

This only happens on an event storming board, where left-to-right means time, and only for notes — a shape or an icon is dropped where you drop it. On an ordinary diagram, a note dropped in a gap is just a note dropped in a gap.

## Timeline lanes

An event-storming board has a rhythm built in: notes you drag snap into evenly
spaced rows and line themselves up with the notes already on the board. There is
nothing to switch on.

- **Nothing on the board ever moves to make room.** The rows arrange themselves
  around the work that is already there.
- **You see them only while you are dragging** — the lane a note is about to
  land in lights up, with its neighbours fainter either side, and everything
  disappears again when you let go.
- **In the same row, the board offers you the rhythm.** The next place along is
  one gap from your neighbour, and the one after that leaves a sticky's worth of
  wall empty in between — room for the event nobody has written yet. Nothing
  touching, nothing half-way.
- **In the row above or below, edges line up.** Squarely above or below a note,
  or one place along from it either way — the diagonal. Same size of sticky also
  gets the brick position, centred under the gap between two squares; different
  sizes line up on the left edge or the right edge. That is all of it.
- **Push a note right up against another** and it takes the next place in the
  row rather than landing half on top of it: a row stays regular however
  impatiently you build it.
- **The offer is outlined on the lane before you let go**, so you always drop
  into a place you have already seen.
- **One gap, always the same.** Stickies sit a finger's width apart, and the
  notes already there never move to make room. Drop a note somewhere with
  nothing near it and it stays exactly where you put it.
- **Drag several notes together** and the note you grabbed takes the place,
  just as it would alone; the others follow and keep the spacing they already
  had.
- Hold **Cmd / Ctrl** while dragging to ignore the lanes for that one note.

Lanes are never exported: they are there to help you place a note, not to be
part of the picture.

## Adding the next note

The notation reads left to right, and some notes have an obvious neighbour:

- a **command** comes before the **domain event** it triggers,
- a **policy** comes after the **domain event** it reacts to,
- a **command** comes after the **policy** that issues it.

Select a note and a small tab in the next note's colour peeks from each side
that has one. Point at a tab to see a dashed outline of the note it will add,
exactly where it will land. Click it and the note is added right beside, one
gap away, open for typing. If something is already there, the board makes room. The new note is an
ordinary note: move it, delete it or change its kind like any other.

## Adding notes from a photo of the wall

Most sessions happen on a real wall first. **Add from photo** at the top of the
Event Storming palette reads that wall in:

1. Take or drop a photo of the wall (you can also paste one onto the board).
2. The photo fills the screen and a box appears on every sticky found in it,
   with the words read from its handwriting. The box's colour is the kind, taken
   from the paper.
3. Check it right there: **untick** a box that is not a note, **click the
   words** to fix a misreading, and **drag round** a sticky it missed. A box
   that is slightly off can be fixed rather than redrawn: **click it** to select
   it, then drag it to move it, drag a corner to resize it, pick a colour to
   change its kind, or press **Delete** to remove it. A big
   wall of small notes is far easier up close: **scroll** to zoom (or pinch, on
   a trackpad or a phone, or use the **+** button), and drag with the **middle
   mouse button** to move around (or hold **Space** and drag). **Fit** shows
   the whole photo again.
4. **Add** puts the notes **on the board, as a draft**: outlined, laid out where they
   were on the wall, with the rest of the board faded behind them — the second look, at
   the notes in place and at the size they will really be.
5. Fix anything from right there: double-click to correct a word it misread,
   drag one that landed awkwardly, delete what you do not want, or right-click
   a note and **Change kind** if the light made a colour ambiguous.
6. **Add** keeps them, in one step that a single Undo takes back. **Discard**
   leaves the board exactly as it was.

A note your board already has is marked **"already here"** and left alone —
never moved, never re-worded, even if the photo reads it differently. That is
what makes the next photo of the next piece of wall safe to import: only what
is new is added.

<Tip>
  The photo never leaves your machine. Your browser finds the stickies in it and sends only small
  crops of individual notes to be read, so the room, the whiteboard behind, and whoever is standing
  in front of it stay with you. The first import also downloads a small note-finding model (under
  half a megabyte), which runs in your browser too.
</Tip>

<Note>
  It will not get a whole wall in one go. Handwriting in pen, a note half-covered by another, a
  colour your team uses differently, the far side of a corner — expect to fix some and add a few
  yourself. It is a head start on the typing, not a replacement for reading your own wall.
</Note>

## One wall of paper

The board is a single [layer](/help/canvas/layers/). Every note lands on it, whichever colour it is, so any two notes can be stacked in front of or behind each other and nothing is filed away on a plane you cannot see.

One thing is always true of the stack: **actors sit in front of every other note, and hotspots in front of actors**, the way a person or a red flag is stuck on top of the note it belongs to. Bring to front and send to back still reorder notes of the same kind.

<Tip>
  Add your own layers if you want them — an ES board is an ordinary diagram underneath, and the
  Layers panel works exactly as it does anywhere else.
</Tip>

## How a session usually runs

The method works in passes, and the note kinds are named for them:

1. **Big picture** — orange domain events only, past tense, left to right in time order. Arguments about wording are the point; that is where the domain knowledge comes out.
2. **Process modelling** — add the commands that cause events, the actors issuing them, the policies reacting, the read models people decide on, and the external systems the flow touches.
3. **Software design** — add aggregates, the things commands act on, once the story is stable enough to draw boundaries around.

Red **hotspots** belong at any stage: a conflict, a question, or a risk nobody can resolve in the room.

Nothing in the editor enforces the passes. They are a way of running the session, not a mode you have to switch — the board is one surface throughout, and you can work whichever way the room does.

## Everything else still works

An event storming board is a normal diagram with a specialised presentation, so the rest of the product applies unchanged: [live presence](/help/collaboration/live-presence/), [comments](/help/collaboration/comments/), [exporting the tab](/help/tabs/export-tabs/), [themes](/help/canvas/themes/), and [offline mode](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/). Export a board and import it elsewhere and it arrives still knowing it is a board.
