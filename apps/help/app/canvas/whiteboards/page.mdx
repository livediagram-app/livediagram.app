A **whiteboard** is a kind of tab made for sketching by hand. It sits beside your ordinary diagram tabs in the same document, so a workshop can keep a free sketch next to a tidy diagram. Everything else works as on any tab: sharing, live collaboration, comments, layers, undo and export.

## Making one

- **A new document:** in the New Document wizard, pick **Whiteboard** beside Blank. There is no theme to choose, so the wizard goes straight on to where the document lives.
- **A new tab:** press **+** in the tab bar, then pick **Whiteboard** in Quick Start.

A tab stays the kind it was made as: a whiteboard does not turn into a diagram tab, or back.

## The dock

Instead of the palette, a whiteboard has one toolbar floating at the bottom of the canvas. From left to right:

1. **Select** (key **V**), to pick up, move and resize what you have drawn.
2. **Three pens**: the **main pen**, the **second pen** and the **third pen** (keys **1**, **2** and **3**). Press a pen to pick it up; press the pen already in your hand to change its **width** (Fine, Medium or Bold). The main pen always draws in the board's own ink; the second and third pens start blue and red and can also change **colour**, so they can be any two colours you like. Right-click a pen to reset it to how it started. With a pen in your hand, the style panel on the left shows its colour and width too. Your pens are yours: they are remembered in this browser, not saved with the document.
3. **Eraser** (key **E**, see below).
4. **Sticky note** (**N**) and **Text** (**T**): press, then click or drag on the board.
5. **Shapes**: a rectangle (**R**), ellipse (**O**), diamond (**D**), cylinder (**C**), line (**L**) or arrow (**A**). Point at the button to open it. Shapes are drawn in the board's ink, whichever pen you last used: the pens colour only their own strokes. To change a shape's colour or width, select it and use the style panel on the left; a selected pen stroke gets its colour and width there as well.
6. **Undo** and **Redo** (in [power user mode](/help/user-interface/power-user-mode/) on a computer these live only in the bottom-right corner).
7. **More** (**…**): the board's **Background** and **Drawing** (Basic or Shape recognition).

Pick a shape, line, arrow or text tool and the style panel shows what it will draw next ("Next rectangle"): choose a colour or width there and every rectangle after it follows. The board remembers its own styles, apart from your diagram tabs.

To select a pen stroke, click its line; clicking elsewhere in its box selects what is underneath, or nothing. Hold **Shift** with Select to drag a selection box from anywhere, even over a shape, adding what it encloses to the selection; **Shift-click** a mark to add it or take it out. With a pen in your hand the cursor stays a pen everywhere, because the pen always draws.

What you draw shows in the pen's colour and width as you draw it, and nothing animates in: a mark appears exactly as you made it. A pen stays in your hand after each stroke, so you can keep writing. Press **Select** or **Escape** to put it down. Opening a whiteboard puts your last pen in your hand straight away.

The dock works from the keyboard too. Every tool has a key (shown on its button) that picks it from anywhere on the board, and **Escape** puts the tool down again (unless you are typing into a note or text box); the other single-letter shortcuts of a diagram tab do nothing here. To reach the dock itself, Tab to it, move along it with the **arrow keys**, and press **Enter** or **Space**. Escape closes a pen's colour and width options and takes you back to the dock.

## Pen, finger and mouse

- A **pen** (stylus) always draws.
- A **mouse** draws with a left drag. Middle-drag or hold **Space** to pan, as on any tab.
- A **finger** draws until you use a pen. After that, on the same device, one finger **pans** instead, so a palm resting on the screen never leaves a mark. Reload the page to go back.
- **Two fingers** always pan and pinch to zoom. A stroke the second finger interrupts is thrown away rather than left half-drawn.

## The two erasers

Press the eraser again to switch its mode:

- **Stroke** removes a whole stroke you touch. Only the ink counts: erasing beside a long stroke leaves it alone.
- **Partial** rubs out only the part of a stroke under the eraser, leaving the pieces either side. Stickies, text and shapes are never cut.

Each sweep of the eraser is one undo. Locked elements, and elements on a hidden or locked [layer](/help/canvas/layers/), are never erased.

## Shape recognition

Under **More** (**…**), **Drawing** switches between **Basic** and **Shape recognition**. With it on, a roughly drawn circle, rectangle, triangle, diamond or line becomes the clean shape when you lift the pen, in the pen's colour and weight. Hold the pen still for half a second before lifting it to see the shape first: move on and the preview goes, lift and it lands. With it off, which is the default, strokes stay exactly as drawn. The setting is remembered in this browser, and Undo removes a recognised shape.

## Backgrounds

Under **More**, choose a **Plain**, **Dots** or **Grid** board. The background is saved with the tab, so everyone sees the same board.

## Light and dark

A whiteboard has no theme. In light mode it is an off-white board with black marker ink; in dark mode it takes on the editor's own dark canvas with light ink. The **main pen**, and anything you draw without choosing a colour, follows whichever you are looking at, so two people in different modes each see their own board. The second and third pens keep their colour in both.
