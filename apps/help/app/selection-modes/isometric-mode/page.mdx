Isometric Mode shows the current tab as a tilted, three-dimensional scene: the canvas is laid onto an isometric plane and each box is raised into a solid block, giving a flat diagram a sense of depth. It is a way to look at a diagram (for an architecture walkthrough or a screenshot), not a way to edit one. Choose **Isometric** from the tool picker at the top of the floating palette, or press **`I`**.

## How it works

The whole tab tilts at once. Shapes, sticky notes, tables, images and cards become raised blocks with sides in their own colour; arrows, text, icons and frames stay flat on the floor.

<Figure caption="The palette's tool picker set to Isometric, tilting the tab's shapes into a three-dimensional view.">
  <IsometricMode />
</Figure>

It is purely a view, and it is yours alone. Nothing about the document changes, nobody else's view tilts, and the angle is not saved: other people stay on the flat canvas unless they pick Isometric themselves.

## Moving around

- **Drag** to pan, as with the [Hand](/help/selection-modes/hand/) tool. Pinch or use the zoom controls to zoom.
- **Shift-drag, or drag with the right mouse button**, to orbit the camera: sideways spins the scene round, up and down tilts it between top-down and edge-on.
- **The orbit button** appears in the zoom controls at the bottom right while the view is on. Drag it to orbit without holding a key, or click it to snap back to the default angle.

<Figure caption="The orbit button in the zoom controls: drag it to orbit the scene, click it to reset the angle.">
  <IsometricOrbit />
</Figure>

Nothing can be selected, moved or edited while the view is on. Press `Esc`, or pick another tool, to go back to the flat canvas (`Esc` lands on Select, or on Hand on a phone).

## Exporting in isometric

To keep the picture, turn on **Isometric view** in the Export dialog's image options. PNG, SVG and PDF exports then come out tilted and raised the same way. See [Exporting a Tab](/help/tabs/export-tabs/).

## Availability

Isometric Mode is greyed out on an empty canvas; add an element and it becomes available. It works on desktop and touch, and view-only visitors can use it too (press `I`), since looking changes nothing.
