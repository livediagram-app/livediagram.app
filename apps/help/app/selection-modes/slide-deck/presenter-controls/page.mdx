Top-right of a running deck sits a small strip of controls carrying, in order:

- the position (`7 / 23`) and the slide's name,
- the elapsed clock and this slide's budget, when you have turned them on,
- **previous** and **next** buttons,
- **jump to a slide**,
- a **notes** button, on slides that have notes,
- a **settings** cog,
- and a close button.

<Figure caption="The presenter's strip: position, pacing, the step buttons, jump, notes, settings and close.">
  <PresenterHud />
</Figure>

It **fades away when the pointer sits still and comes back the moment you move it**, so a still screen is clean for the room while every control stays one twitch of the mouse away. It stays put while a popover is open, so nothing is ever left stranded over the slide.

## Jumping to a slide

Arrow keys walk the deck, which is everything you need until somebody asks about the slide from nine slides ago and you arrow back through nine of them in front of a room.

The **jump** button (or `G`) opens the deck as a list, in running order, with the current slide marked. Press any row to go straight there. Hidden slides are not in it, for the same reason they are not in the count: the list is the run, not the deck.

## Presenter notes

The notes button opens your notes for this slide in a popover under it, sized so the slide stays readable behind. Press it again, press `Esc`, or advance the slide to close it.

**The button is only there on slides that have notes.** Its presence is the signal: no button means nothing to read, which you can tell at a glance without opening anything.

<Note>
  The notes popover is on the screen you are sharing, so the room sees it while it is open. That is
  the trade for notes being one press away rather than living on a second display.
</Note>

## Pacing a talk

Two optional readouts, both off by default, because a timer nobody asked for is a timer reminding the room you are behind.

- **Elapsed** counts up from the moment you pressed Present. It is the "am I ten minutes in or twenty" question, answered without a wristwatch.
- **Budget** shows time-on-this-slide against the minutes you gave that slide when you wrote the deck (see [Building a Deck](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/building-a-deck/)). It turns amber when you go over, and that is all it does: a deck that advanced itself because your four minutes were up would cut off the answer you were giving.

Slides with no budget simply show nothing, so turning it on doesn't clutter the deck with numbers for slides you never timed.

## Presenter settings

The cog opens eleven settings in three groups. They are meant to be decisions you can make in one glance and undo in one more, because this popover opens on a projector in front of a room.

**Transition**

- **Slide** (the default, the whole screen travelling), **Fade** (one slide dissolving into the next), or **None** (a straight cut).
- A **speed**, **Quick**, **Normal** or **Slow**, which disappears when the transition is **None** rather than sitting there greyed out.

**Playback**

- **Auto-advance**: **Off**, or every 5, 10, 30 or 60 seconds. It pauses while a popover is open, so something you are reading is never swept away by a timer you had forgotten about.
- **Click to advance**: turn it off if you gesture at the screen with the mouse and would rather drive from the keys and buttons alone.
- **Loop the deck**: after the last slide, start again instead of ending. With auto-advance, that is the whole "leave it running on the wall" setup.

**Display**

- **Fill screen** (a small slide is blown up) or **Actual size** (never past 100%), for authors whose slides are already the size they meant.
- **Show position**: turn the counter and slide name off for a clean screen.
- **Keep controls visible**: stop the strip fading when the pointer rests.
- **Hide the pointer**: a still cursor left on a projector is a distraction. It goes and comes back with the controls, so a hidden pointer is never a trap.
- **Show elapsed time** and **Show slide budget**: the two pacing readouts above.

<Note>
  These settings are **yours and this device's**, like the eraser's brush or the laser's pen. They
  are how you drive a deck on this machine, not part of the diagram, so a colleague presenting the
  same deck gets their own.
</Note>
