The Eraser mode is the fastest way to remove elements from the canvas. Choose it from the mode picker at the top of the floating palette, or press **`E`**.

## Erasing elements

- **Click** an element to delete it.
- **Drag** across several elements to remove them all in one sweep.

This is quicker than selecting elements and then deleting them, especially when you want to clear out a cluster of shapes or arrows at once.

## Setting up the brush

While the mode is active an **Eraser** panel sits under the palette, over a preview of the brush:

- **Mode** — **Sweep** (drag across things to erase them) or **Tap** (one press, one thing), which is the safer choice on a crowded canvas.
- **Size** — **Point** for the exact pixel, or Small, Medium, and Large brushes. Above Point, a ring follows the cursor at the brush's real size, so you can see what a sweep will take before you press.
- **Erases** — **Anything**, **Drawings only** (pencil and highlighter strokes), or **Arrows only**. Drawings only is what makes sketching over a diagram safe: sweep the whole thing at Large and the diagram underneath is untouched. Arrows only lets you rewire without disturbing the boxes.
- **Groups** — whether erasing one member of a group takes **just the piece** or the **whole group**.

The ring turns amber whenever a filter is on, so a restricted eraser never looks like a broken one. Your settings are remembered on this device.

Locked elements, and anything on a locked or hidden layer, are never erased whatever the panel says — and a whole sweep is still a single undo.

## When it is unavailable

The Eraser is disabled when the canvas is empty, since there is nothing to erase. Add at least one element and the mode becomes available again.

<Note>
  Erasing removes elements straight away. If you take off something you meant to keep, undo the
  change to bring it back.
</Note>
