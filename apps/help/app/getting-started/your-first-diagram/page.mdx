The quickest way to learn livediagram is to build something. This walkthrough takes you from a blank canvas to a small connected diagram. You do not need to sign in, the [canvas always works as a guest](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/).

## Create a new diagram

<Step n={1}>
  Open [a new diagram](/new). This is livediagram's entry point: the **New Document** wizard, in two
  steps, **Template** and **Location**.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  On the **Template** step, pick what to start from. **Popular** opens first, led by **Blank
  Diagram**; search the [templates](/help/canvas/templates/), or narrow them with the mode filter
  (**Everything**, Diagram, Draw, Illustrate, Plan). Clicking a card picks it and moves you on.
  Choose **Blank Diagram** to follow along from scratch.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  On the **Location** step, give the document a name (it starts as an "Untitled" name for the
  template you chose), pick where it is saved (**livediagram**, your account, or **Local Browser**,
  this device only) and which folder it goes in. Press **Create** and the editor opens on your new
  document.
</Step>

<Figure caption="The New Document wizard: pick a template on the first step, then name it and choose where it lives.">
  <NewDocumentWelcome />
</Figure>

On your very first document the editor offers **Show me around**, the [welcome
tour](/help/getting-started/welcome-tour/) of the essentials. Take it or dismiss it; you can rerun it
any time from **Settings**, under **Accessibility**.

<Tip>
  In a hurry? **Skip** on the Template step creates a Blank Diagram straight away, with no Location
  step. **Start Blank** in the livediagram site header (on a computer) does the same from any page
  of the site, or open [/new?blank=1](/new?blank=1) directly. The wizard's **X** (or Escape) backs
  out without creating anything, and **Open Existing Document** jumps to your library instead.
</Tip>

You can rename the document later: click its title in the header at the top of the editor and
type. Renaming is instant and saved for you. Names cap at **60 characters**: typing simply stops
there, which is plenty for a title and short enough to stay readable in a tab bar or a list row.

## Add your first shapes

<Step n={1}>
  Find the **Palette**, the strip across the top of the canvas. It opens on **Popular**, the tiles
  most people reach for, with Square, Circle and Diamond among them. Everything else is one press
  away in its category dropdown.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  **Click a shape tile**, then **click the canvas** where you want it: it drops there at its default
  size. Or, after picking the tile, **drag on the canvas** to draw it at the size you want, or drag
  the tile itself straight onto the canvas. Try a Square to start.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  **Double-click the shape** to add a label, then type. Press Enter or click away to commit the
  text. A selected empty shape also shows an **Add text** button in its toolbar, and simply typing
  while a shape is selected starts a label too.
</Step>

<Step n={4}>
  Drop a second shape nearby the same way. If a shape is selected when you click to drop the next
  one, the new shape inherits its size, handy for chaining similarly-sized nodes.
</Step>

For the full set of shapes, devices, and icons you can drop, see the [Palette](/help/palette/) guides.

<Figure caption="Drag a tile from the Palette onto the canvas to drop a shape, which lands selected.">
  <AddingElements />
</Figure>

## Connect them with arrows

<Step n={1}>
  Select a shape and look for the quick-connect **+** buttons around it. Click one to open its
  options, then drag **Arrow** onto another shape to draw an arrow that stays pinned to both. (On a
  phone, tap **Arrow**, then tap the target.) **Duplicate** in the same options makes a connected
  copy of the shape beside it in one go.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Prefer to draw freely? Pick **Add arrow** from the Palette (it is in Popular, and in the **Draw**
  category), or press `A`, and drag between two points.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Right-click an arrow (long-press on touch) to change its style: **Straight**, **Curved** or
  **Angled** in the **Line** section, with its thickness and pattern, and the arrowheads under
  **Pointer**. Double-click an arrow to give it a label.
</Step>

## Keep going

You now have a connected diagram. From here you can:

<Figure caption="A few shapes joined by arrows, with the Palette strip across the top: the editor at a glance.">
  <CanvasOverview />
</Figure>

- **Move and multi-select.** Drag to reposition; drag on empty canvas to marquee-select several elements at once, or Shift-click to add one at a time.
- **Recolour everything** with a [theme](/help/canvas/themes/).
- **Share it live.** Hand a colleague a link and they join the same canvas. See [Sharing your document](/help/getting-started/sharing-your-document/).

<Tip>
  Made a mistake? [Undo](/help/canvas/undo/) and [Redo](/help/canvas/redo/) sit in the controls in
  the bottom-right corner (or press `Cmd/Ctrl+Z`), and the common keyboard shortcuts are in
  [Keyboard Essentials](/help/getting-started/keyboard-essentials/).
</Tip>
