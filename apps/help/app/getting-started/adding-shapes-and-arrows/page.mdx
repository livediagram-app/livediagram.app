Almost every diagram is shapes joined by arrows. livediagram makes both fast: shapes come from the **Palette**, and arrows snap between shapes with quick-connect. This article covers the basics; for the full catalogue see [Shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [Arrows](/help/palette/arrows/).

## Adding shapes

The **Palette**, the strip across the top of the canvas, holds everything you can add. It starts with two dropdowns: the selection mode (Select, Hand, Eraser and the rest) and the category. It opens on **Popular**, the twelve tiles most people reach for: Square, Circle, Diamond, text, an arrow, a frame, a sticky note, an image, the shape pen, a table, a code block and an entity. See [where each one lives](/help/palette/tools/) for the full map.

<Figure caption="The Palette strip: the selection-mode and category dropdowns, Popular's tiles, then More and Search.">
  <ShapePalette />
</Figure>

<Step n={1}>
  Pick a tile from **Popular**, or open the category dropdown and choose **Shapes**: the strip shows
  its first tiles, and **More** (⋯) the full set: square, circle, diamond, cylinder, hexagon,
  stadium, and many more.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  **Click the canvas** to drop the shape there at its natural size, or **drag on the canvas** to
  draw it at exactly the size you want. You can also drag the tile straight onto the canvas.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  **Double-click** the shape to add a label, then type. Enter or a click elsewhere commits it. With
  a shape selected you can also just start typing, or press **Space**.
</Step>

Common shapes have their own keys, too: `R` square, `O` circle, `D` diamond, `C` cylinder and `G` parallelogram (see [Keyboard Shortcuts](/help/tips-and-tricks/keyboard-shortcuts/)).

The category dropdown groups the rest into bands. In Diagram mode, **Common** holds Shapes, Write and Draw; **Structure** holds Build (frames, lanes, mind maps); **Decorate** holds Icons, Stickers, **Tech** (the full-colour cloud and infrastructure marks, see [Technology icons](/help/palette/technology/)) and Media; and **Dynamic** holds Collaborate. The wireframing kit (**Components** and **Devices**: browser, phone and laptop frames) and the **Data** charts live in [Illustrate mode](/help/canvas/illustrate/)'s palette.

## Connecting with arrows

The fastest way to draw an arrow is **quick-connect**:

<Figure caption="Click a selected shape's + to fan out its options, then drag Arrow onto a second shape to pin an arrow.">
  <QuickConnect />
</Figure>

<Step n={1}>Select a shape. Small **+** buttons appear on its sides.</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Click a **+** to fan out its options (**Duplicate**, **Arrow**, **Pencil**, **Text**), then drag
  **Arrow** onto another shape. livediagram draws an arrow pinned to both, so the arrow follows
  whenever you move either shape. On a phone, tap **Arrow**, then tap the target shape.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  To draw a free connector instead, pick **Add arrow** (in Popular, or the **Draw** category), or
  press `A`, and drag between any two points.
</Step>

**Duplicate** in the same options drops a connected copy of the shape beside it, the quickest way to grow a flow one step at a time. Turn on **Quick-Add on Hover** in Settings and the options open as soon as you hover a **+**. See [Quick-Add on Hover](/help/palette/quick-add-on-hover/).

## Styling arrows

**Right-click** an arrow (long-press on touch) to open its formatting:

- **Line**: **Straight**, **Curved** or **Angled** (an L-shaped elbow); the pattern (solid, dashed, dotted and a few dash-dot variants); and the thickness, thin, medium or thick.
- **Pointer**: the arrowhead shape, size, and which ends carry a head.
- **Label:** double-click an arrow to type a label. It sits on the middle of the line, with the line broken open behind it, and wraps onto more lines when it is long. Press **Shift+Enter** for a line break of your own.
- **Pass behind boxes:** on by default, so a long arrow crossing an unrelated shape ducks under it instead of drawing a line over its label. Turn it off per arrow from the **Line** section if you want the old always-on-top look.

Drag an arrow's line to bend it where you grabbed it, and use the handles on curved and angled arrows to shape the path exactly. See [Arrow Styles](/help/palette/arrows/arrow-styles/).

<Tip>
  Working in a flow? Drop a shape, label it, quick-connect to the next spot, and repeat. Because a
  new shape can inherit the selected one's size, chained nodes stay tidy without fiddling.
</Tip>
