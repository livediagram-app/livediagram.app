**Import** brings content into the active tab from pasted text or a file. It is the counterpart to [exporting](/help/tabs/export-tabs/): export a tab from one diagram, import it into a tab somewhere else.

## Import formats

From the tab ellipsis menu, expand the **Content** section and choose **Import**, then pick a format:

- **JSON** reads a livediagram tab export (a `.livediagram-tab.json` file) and restores its elements exactly, the highest-fidelity round trip.
- **Mermaid** reads a [Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/) flowchart (`graph` / `flowchart`), state diagram (`stateDiagram`), or ER diagram (`erDiagram`) and lays it out on the canvas, keeping every connection, its style (dashed, thick, two-headed, headless), `click` links, and any `subgraph` or composite-state blocks, which come in as frames drawn around their nodes. Other Mermaid diagram types (sequence, gantt, pie, …) aren't supported.
- **Markdown** turns a Markdown outline into a themed tree diagram, the same conversion as [Markdown Import](/help/tools/markdown-import/), so you can draft structure in text and bring it onto the canvas.
- **Excalidraw** reads a `.excalidraw` scene (what [excalidraw.com](https://excalidraw.com/)'s Save to disk produces) and keeps its shapes, labels, arrow connections, groups, freehand drawings, and images. It also reads a PNG or SVG exported from Excalidraw with **Embed scene** switched on (a `.excalidraw.png` or `.excalidraw.svg` file), which carries the whole scene inside the picture. The hand-drawn look flattens to clean strokes.
- **draw.io** reads a diagram from [draw.io](https://www.drawio.com/) (diagrams.net, the desktop app, or its Confluence and VS Code plugins): a `.drawio` or `.xml` file, compressed or not, or a PNG or SVG exported with **Include a copy of my diagram** ticked. See [Importing from draw.io](#importing-from-draw-io) below.

Each format opens the same two-step panel: **paste or write** the content into the editor and press **Import**, or press **Import a file instead** to pick one from disk. Either way the tab updates once it parses.

## Images in an imported board

When a board carries images, they come across with it. Each one is resized in your browser (to at most 2048 pixels on its longest side) and saved to your [image gallery](/help/explorer/image-gallery/), so it counts towards the gallery's space like any image you add yourself. An image you already have is recognised and reused rather than stored twice.

While the images save, the panel counts them ("Importing images 3 of 12…"). When it finishes, the dialog tells you how many were imported, how many were already in your gallery, and how many were left as placeholders and why: the file didn't include the image, its format couldn't be read, it was too large, or your gallery is full. The rest of the board always imports. Double-click a placeholder to add its image later.

In an [offline diagram](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/), images are kept inside the diagram in this browser instead of your gallery, up to a limit for each import.

## Importing from draw.io

A draw.io file can hold several pages. **The first page replaces the active tab, and every further page becomes a new tab** right after it, named after its page, so a three-page file arrives as three tabs. One undo takes the whole import back, new tabs included, and links between pages become links between the tabs.

What carries over:

- **Shapes** keep their kind, size, position, colours, borders, rounded corners, rotation, opacity and shadow. Flowchart shapes (process, decision, terminator, document, database, data) map one to one; less common ones come in as the nearest livediagram shape.
- **Text** keeps its formatting: bold, italic, underline, colours, links, headings and lists in rich labels, plus font size and alignment.
- **Connections** stay attached to the shapes they join, with their route (straight, curved or right-angled, through their bend points), arrowheads, line style and labels.
- **Swimlanes** become lanes, **UML classes and ER entities** become records with one row per field, **tables** become tables, and draw.io **layers** become the tab's layers, hidden and locked ones included.
- **Cloud and network icons** (AWS, Azure, Kubernetes, servers, users and more) become the matching livediagram icon where there is a clear match. A stencil with no match comes in as a labelled box, so nothing disappears.
- **Colours left at draw.io's default** take on your tab's theme, like anything drawn in livediagram. Colours you picked in draw.io stay as they were.

Pictures embedded in the diagram come across like any imported board's [images](#images-in-an-imported-board): resized and saved to your gallery. Pictures linked from the web, or from draw.io's own icon library, are never fetched; they come in as placeholders.

When anything could not come across exactly, the dialog shows one summary before closing, the same one every import uses: how the images came across, then one line per kind of change with its count: shapes that came in as labelled boxes (with the stencil names), lane titles that now read across, arrowheads livediagram draws differently, hidden items left out, and so on.

## Import replaces the tab

Importing **replaces the active tab's contents** with what comes in, it does not merge (a multi-page draw.io file also adds a tab for each further page). Because that is destructive, the dialog leads with a warning naming the tab about to be overwritten, so you can back out before committing. If you want to keep the current board, add a new tab first and import into that.

Import is unavailable while a tab is locked, since a locked tab is protected from edits.
