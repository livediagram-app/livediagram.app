## Open Automations

Press **Tools** in the footer, then **Automations…** under **Automate**, where Webhooks used to be. It opens its own window over the <Noun />. A badge at the row's end counts the rules that are on, and turns amber when a rule has paused itself (below); the **Tools** button then shows an amber dot too, so you see it with the menu shut.

The screen has four tabs:

- **Rules**: every rule in the <Noun />, each with its switch, its name, what it does in a sentence ("When Every Day at 0:00 then Revert to Snapshot"), when it **Last Run** and how many runs it made **This Week**. A rule on a time also says when it runs next. Find one by name, or narrow the list by trigger or by **On and Off**. A row's **⋯** menu holds **Run Rule** (for a rule you start by hand), **Edit**, **Duplicate**, **Audit Trail** and **Delete**.
- **Audit Trail**: what each rule did, newest first (below).
- **Templates**: ready-made rules (below).
- **Connections**: the Slack and Microsoft Teams channels and your own services that rules post to (below).

At the right of the tabs, **Runs today** shows how many of the <Noun />'s 1,000 runs a day have been used.

## Describe a Rule

Press **New Rule**, then **Describe a Rule**, and say in your own words when the rule should run and what it should do: "Every Friday at 5, take a snapshot and email me". The examples above the box, under **Try an Example**, fill it in for you. Press **Draft Rule**, and a few seconds later the rule opens in the builder, drafted from your words. It is not saved and it is off: check each step, try it with **Test Rule**, then press **Save Rule**. If no rule could be made from your words, the dialog says so and keeps them to change.

Only your words, the names of the <Noun />'s <TabWord plural /> and the kinds of trigger and step are sent, never what the <Noun /> holds, its people or its connections. Drafting runs on [your own AI key](/help/account-and-data/ai-keys/) when you have one (or the team's), else on built-in AI, where it counts toward the <Noun />'s 100 AI asks a day; the line under the box says which. AI steps in a rule always run on built-in AI.

## Make a Rule

Press **New Rule**, then **Blank Rule**. The rule reads from top to bottom: **When** (the trigger), **If all of these** (the conditions, every one of which must hold) and **Then** (the steps, in order). **Change** on the trigger opens a gallery of every trigger in groups, each with a picture of what it hears, and **+ Add Action** opens the same for steps. Click any step to change it in the panel on the right. Name the rule at the top, then press **Save Rule**.

### Triggers

Every app has these:

- **Scheduled**: every day, every weekday, weekly on the days you pick, or monthly on a day, at a time on the quarter hour, in your time zone when you saved the rule.
- **Run by Hand**: you start it with **Run Rule** (below).
- **Incoming Webhook**: another tool starts it by calling the rule's own address ([below](#start-a-rule-from-another-tool)).
- **<TabWord capital /> Added** and **<TabWord capital /> Removed**.
- **Edited, Then Quiet**: the <Noun /> changed and then nothing changed for a while you choose (5 minutes to 24 hours), on any <TabWord /> or one. It runs once when a burst of edits settles, never on every keystroke, so it is the trigger for "tell me when this changed".
- **Not Edited For**: nothing in the <Noun /> has changed for 1 to 365 days, checked once a day at a time you pick. It runs once each time the <Noun /> goes that long untouched.
- **Snapshot Taken**: someone takes a snapshot by hand.
- **Someone Joined**: a person opens the <Noun /> through a share link for the first time.
- **Access Requested**: someone asks for access to the <Noun />.

### Conditions

**+ Add Condition** checks, before any step runs:

- **<TabWord capital />**: the run's <TabWord /> is, or is not, one of the <TabWord plural /> you pick.
- **Who Did It**: whoever started the rule is, or is not, a person you pick, an Editor, a Participant or an agent. A rule on a time or an Incoming Webhook has nobody behind it, so this isn't offered there.
- **Day of Week**: today, in the rule's time zone, is one of the days you pick.
- **Text**: a value (a comment, a webhook field) contains, or does not contain, some text, in any case.

### Steps

- **Take Snapshot**: a snapshot of the whole <Noun />, named by the rule.
- **Revert to Snapshot**: puts the whole <Noun /> back to a snapshot you pick, for everyone ([below](#revert-to-snapshot)).
- **Send Email**: to the <Noun />'s owner, whoever started the rule, a person you pick, or addresses you type in ([below](#email-from-a-rule)).
- **Post to Slack**, **Post to Microsoft Teams** and **Send to Webhook**: a message through one of the <Noun />'s [connections](#connections).
- **Summarise with AI**: reads the <Noun />'s text (or one <TabWord />'s) and keeps a summary for the steps after it, as the **Summary** value.
- **Wait**: holds the rest of the rule for 1 hour to 30 days. The run shows as **Waiting** in the Audit Trail until then.
- **If / Else**: runs one list of steps when its conditions hold, another when they don't.

  - **Email the Page Owner** emails the owner of the run's page. A page with no owner fails the step, and the Audit Trail says so.
  - **Add to Always in Context** and **Remove from Always in Context** put a page in, or take it out of, the pages every agent is handed, within the 8,000 token budget.

## Test Rule and Run Rule

**Test Rule** runs the rule as it stands, saved or not, and changes nothing: you see each condition and each step as it would go ("Would post to Launch Channel", "Would revert to “Clean board”"). When the trigger is about a <TabWord /> it asks which one first.

A rule whose trigger is **Run by Hand** runs for real with **Run Rule**, on its row or in its **⋯** menu. A message says it ran, or why it couldn't.

## The Audit Trail

The **Audit Trail** tab lists what the <Noun />'s rules did, newest first: each run with its rule, when, and how it went: **Done**, **No Match** (its conditions didn't hold), **Skipped**, **Failed** or **Waiting**. Open a run to see each condition and what it found, and each step. It keeps 30 days, up to 2,000 runs and, apart from them, the last 500 No Matches. A rule's own **⋯** menu opens the Audit Trail on that rule alone.

## Connections

A **connection** is an address a rule's message goes to. It sends only what a rule's step tells it to, and it belongs to this <Noun /> alone. Connections replace webhooks: a connection hears nothing on its own, and a rule says when to post and what.

Open the **Connections** tab and press **Add Connection**:

1. **Where Should It Post?** **Slack** (a message in a channel), **Microsoft Teams** (a card in a channel) or **Your Own Service** (signed JSON to an address you run).
2. **Connect It.** Paste the address and give the connection a name, like the channel it posts to. For Slack, the form shows how to get a **Slack Webhook URL** (an app with Incoming Webhooks turned on); for Microsoft Teams, a **Teams Workflow URL** (the channel's Workflows, "Post to a channel when a webhook request is received"). Your own service needs an `https://` address.

A message step's panel picks a connection by name, or offers **Add a Connection…**, which opens this tab and keeps your rule as you left it, with **Back to Rule**.

Each connection in the list shows where it posts and who added it, with **Send Test**, **Pause Connection** (and **Resume Connection**), **Delete Connection** and its **Recent Deliveries**: what it sent lately and whether each one arrived. A failed delivery is tried again for about a day. A connection pauses itself after 20 deliveries in a row fail, when the address says it is gone, or when whoever added it can no longer edit the <Noun />; a rule that a paused connection stopped offers **Fix Connection**. A <Noun /> holds up to **10** connections, and a rule up to 5 message steps.

**Signed messages.** For Your Own Service, the connection's signing secret is shown once, when you add it: copy it then. Each delivery is a JSON `POST` with the rule's message, the <TabWord /> it is about (if any) and a link back, signed with that secret. The headers carry the signature as `X-Liveworkspace-Signature: v1=<hex>`, an HMAC-SHA256 over `<timestamp>.<body>` keyed with the secret, the Unix time in `X-Liveworkspace-Timestamp`, `automation` or `ping` (a Send Test) in `X-Liveworkspace-Event`, and the delivery's id in `X-Liveworkspace-Delivery`, the same on every retry. Your service recomputes the HMAC, compares it in constant time and refuses a timestamp more than five minutes old.

A team <Noun />'s connections show in the team's [Audit Log](/help/collaboration/teams/audit-log/) when one is added or removed.

## Start a Rule from Another Tool

A rule whose trigger is **Incoming Webhook** is started by another tool: your build server when a release ships, a form service, a script. Save the rule, then in its **When** panel:

1. Copy the rule's address.
2. Press **Make Secret**. The secret is shown once, with "You won’t see it again": keep it where the other tool keeps its passwords. Afterwards the panel shows only its last 4 characters, when it was made and when it was last used.
3. Have the tool send a `POST` with JSON, up to 16 KB, to the address. **How to Send** in the panel shows both ways, with the address filled in.

With the secret in a header:

```bash
curl -X POST '<address>' \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SECRET" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"tab": "Launch", "version": "2.4"}'
```

With a signature, so the secret itself never travels. Put the Unix time in seconds in `X-Liveworkspace-Timestamp`, and in `X-Liveworkspace-Signature` put `sha256=` and the HMAC-SHA256, keyed with the secret, of exactly this string: the timestamp, a full stop, then the body as sent (`<timestamp>.<body>`):

```bash
BODY='{"tab": "Launch", "version": "2.4"}'
TS=$(date +%s)
SIG=$(printf '%s.%s' "$TS" "$BODY" | openssl dgst -sha256 -hmac "$SECRET" | sed 's/^.* //')
curl -X POST '<address>' \
  -H "X-Liveworkspace-Timestamp: $TS" \
  -H "X-Liveworkspace-Signature: sha256=$SIG" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d "$BODY"
```

A signed call is refused if its timestamp is more than five minutes away from now, either way, or if the same signed call already reached the rule in the last ten minutes, so a call someone copies can't be sent again. Make a fresh timestamp and signature for each call. A call answered `429` or `400` wasn't taken, so it can be sent again as it is.

Name a <TabWord /> in `tab`, by its name or its id, to run the rule on that <TabWord />; with none, it runs once on the <Noun /> alone. Anything else in the body is yours to read with **Webhook Field…** values (`{{webhook.version}}`). The tool hears back `202` with how many runs were queued. A wrong or old secret, a missing or stale timestamp, a call sent twice, or a rule that is off or deleted, answers `401`; a call not sent as JSON `415`; a body over 16 KB `413`; more than 60 calls a minute, or a <Noun /> past its runs for the day, `429`. **Regenerate** makes a new secret and stops the old one at once.

## Revert to Snapshot

**Revert to Snapshot** puts the whole <Noun /> back to a snapshot you pick, for everyone, the way **Revert** in History does: a safety snapshot is taken first, then the content is replaced and every open editor reloads. It is what "put the workshop back every night" needs: take a snapshot of the board as it should start, then make a rule **Every Day at 0:00**, **Revert to Snapshot**, that snapshot.

- **A snapshot a rule names is kept.** It doesn't count toward the latest five [snapshots](/help/collaboration/snapshots/) and is never removed to make room while any rule, on or off, names it. History marks it **Kept by a Rule** and offers no Delete; delete the rule, or pick another snapshot, and it goes back among the five. Up to 5 snapshots are kept this way in a <Noun />.
- **It is the last step.** Nothing can follow a revert in the same rule, as the content it would act on is gone, and a rule that reverts starts no other rule.
- **Only a person saves it.** An AI agent or a script can't save a rule that reverts.
- A snapshot deleted before the rule runs fails the run, and the rule's row says "The snapshot is gone".

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  It is what a meeting room needs: **Everyone Left**, then **Revert to Snapshot**, puts the room
  back as it was, chairs, whiteboard and all, once it has emptied.
</AppNote>

A snapshot a rule took, and the safety snapshot its revert takes first, show in History by the rule's name, never as you.

## Email From a Rule

**Send Email** to a person of the <Noun /> (its owner, a person you pick, or whoever started the rule) goes to their verified address, and only while they haven't turned off email about work on a <Noun />. You can also type in up to 10 **Email Addresses** for people outside the <Noun />. Each of those emails says which rule and which <Noun /> sent it, and carries a link to unsubscribe: it opens a page with one **Unsubscribe** button, so a mail scanner that follows the link unsubscribes nobody. An address that unsubscribes gets no more email from this <Noun />'s rules. A <Noun /> sends at most 50 emails a day to typed-in addresses.

## Steps in Other Apps

**Add a Step** has an **In Other Apps** group: steps that act on another of your items, in another app.

- **Create a Card in a Plan**: a card on a board and column you pick, with a title, a description, an assignee and a due date in so many days. It links back to what started the rule, and can skip itself while an open card already links there.
- **Write a Page in a Knowledgebase**: a page under a parent you pick, from a title and a body. The page says **Written by the rule** and the rule's name, and reads **Never verified** until someone presses **Mark Verified**.
- **Post in a Space**: a note on a Space's tab you pick.
- **Add a Tab**: a tab from a template you pick, in a livedocuments or livediagram item.
- **Notify**: a line in the Inbox of the people you pick, in every app.

Each text box takes values with **Insert a Field**, and the **Preview** under it shows what will land as you type. A rule holds up to 5 of these steps, at its top level.

**Who it acts as.** A step in another app acts as the person who last saved the rule, and only on items they can edit: the picker shows the rest as **View Only**, with a lock. The rule's side panel says **Runs As**, what it **Reaches** and its **Limits**. If that person loses access, the run stops with **Stopped: no access**, and the rule pauses. Anyone who can edit the <Noun />'s rules can press **Take Over Rule**, on its row or in the Audit Trail: the rule is saved as theirs and turned back on.

**Limits.** A rule makes up to 100 of these steps a day, and a person's rules up to 500 a day across every app. A rule in another app that a step starts is part of the same chain: the chain stops after 3 hops, and the same rule never fires twice in one chain (**Skipped: would loop back to this rule**). AI steps keep their own daily allowance: at it, only the AI steps wait.

## Rules and Other Rules

A rule's changes don't start other rules, so two rules can never keep answering each other. To let them, open **Rule Details** in the rule and tick **Let this rule’s changes trigger other rules**. Even then a chain stops after 5 rules, and a rule never hears its own change.

## Limits and Pausing

- A <Noun /> holds up to 50 rules, each with up to 10 conditions and 20 steps, a name of up to 60 characters, unique in the <Noun />.
- A <Noun /> makes up to 1,000 runs a day (UTC); a No Match doesn't count. Up to 500 triggers can wait to run at once; a burst past that is let go, and the Audit Trail says so once.
- A <Noun /> makes up to 100 AI asks a day, rules and Describe a Rule together.
- A rule holds up to 3 Waits, and up to 500 runs can be waiting in a <Noun />.
- A rule that fails 10 times in a row turns itself off and is marked **Paused** with the reason. Fix what it names, then press **Resume**.
- A time the <Noun /> missed runs late, once, when it is less than a day late; older is skipped, and the Audit Trail says so.
- A <Noun /> that is archived or in the Trash runs no rules. Its rules and their Audit Trail are kept, and it takes up again when it comes back.

## Who Can

Only an **Editor of the whole <Noun />**, signed in, makes, changes, turns on or off, tests and runs rules and manages connections: its owner, its team, or someone with an Editor link to every <TabWord />. Everyone's changes can start a rule, a Participant's or a Viewer's comment included, and what a rule does it does as **Automation**, with an Editor's rights.

- **Participants and Viewers** don't see the Automations row, and neither do Editors through a link to one <TabWord />. Where a rule changed something, they see it by **Automation** with the rule's name beside it ("Automation · Reset Every Night"), as everyone does.
- **Signed out**, even with an Editor link, the row carries a **Sign In** badge: a rule sends email and messages in a person's name, so it asks you to sign in first.
- **Offline.** Rules run on our servers, so a <Noun /> kept [Offline](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) has none: its row carries a **Sync <Noun capital />** badge, and pressing it offers to sync the <Noun /> first.

<AppNote app="liveknowledge">
  A **Participant** writes pages but doesn't see Automations, as with the Review Period and
  Style. An agent lists rules and the Audit Trail with any scope, and changes them only with the
  **write** scope an Editor grants it in AI Knowledge.
</AppNote>

## Copies and Files

A copy of the <Noun /> (**Duplicate** in the Explorer), and the <Noun />'s exported file when it is imported, bring its rules along **turned off**. What was the original's own (a snapshot, a connection) is left for you to pick again. A copy made through a share link or a Community post takes no rules. Reverting to a snapshot puts back content only: the rules stay as they are.

## Agents and the Command Line

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedocuments,livedesign">
  An AI tool connected through the [MCP server](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/), or a script with the [command line](/help/developers/cli/), works your rules as you would: `list_rules` reads them and their recent runs, and `change_rules` adds, changes, turns on and off, duplicates, deletes, tests and runs them; on the command line, the `rule` command does the same. It never makes or reads an Incoming Webhook secret, picks a connection, or saves a rule that reverts to a snapshot.

</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  livehuddle has no MCP server or command line, so rules are made in the editor.
</AppNote>
