## The Tools

- **Auto-align** is a grid snap. It rounds every element's size to a 10 px grid and lines shapes up by the edges and centres they nearly share, so near-aligned shapes become exactly aligned, small drift collapses, and nothing moves far. See [Auto-Align](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-align/).

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  - **Auto Layout** ("tidy up" in the command palette) reads the arrow graph and computes brand-new
  positions, laying out connected shapes into clean layers, or into a style you pick. It can
  legitimately move an element a long way. See [Auto
  Layout](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">The styles are flowchart (down or right), tree, or mindmap.</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign,livedocuments">
  The styles are layered (down or right), tree, or radial.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Auto-align snaps sticky notes and images onto a 10 px grid, lining up what nearly lines up.
</AppNote>

## How to Run Them

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  In Diagram mode, press the **Tidy** button (the broom in the bottom-right corner, just left of
  Layers). It opens a panel with a picture of each Auto Layout style (**Smart**, **Flowchart Down**,
  **Flowchart Right**, **Tree** and **Mindmap**) and **Auto-align** below them. Rest the mouse on
  one and the tab is laid out that way behind the panel, so you can compare styles before you
  commit; move away and everything goes back. Click the one you want to keep it.
</AppNote>

You can also open the [command palette](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/) (⌘K / Ctrl+K), type **Auto Layout** or **Auto-align**, and pick the tidier you want under **Actions**. Both run on the whole active tab as a single undoable step, so if the result isn't what you hoped, one undo puts everything back exactly as it was.

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  In livehuddle there is one tidier: type **Auto-align** and pick **Auto-align to Grid** under
  **Actions**.
</AppNote>

Tidying is for editors: it isn't offered to a participant or in a view-only session.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  The tidiers also leave a [locked tab](/help/tabs/locking-tabs/) alone, so unlock the tab first.
  Inside an unlocked tab, a [locked element](/help/canvas/locking/) stays exactly where it is.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Anything on a [locked layer](/help/canvas/layers-visibility-and-locking/) stays where it is too.
  Neither tool will trash your work. Each one is tried before it's offered, and one that would
  change nothing, cover a shape, or pull shapes out of their lanes is greyed out with the reason.
  Run one from the command palette and the same check applies: nothing moves, and a short notice
  says why. See [When a style isn't
  offered](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/#when-a-style-isn-t-offered).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Neither tool will trash your work. Each one is tried before it runs, and one that would change
  nothing or cover a shape leaves everything where it is, with a short notice saying why. See [When
  a style isn't offered](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/#when-a-style-isn-t-offered).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  Neither tool will trash your work. Each one is tried before it runs, and one that would change
  nothing, cover a shape, or pull shapes out of their lanes or frames leaves everything where it is,
  with a short notice saying why. See [When a style isn't
  offered](/help/tools/layout-cleanup/auto-layout/#when-a-style-isn-t-offered).
</AppNote>

<Note>
  Both tools work entirely offline and deterministically. They never call out to anything, so they
  are always available.
