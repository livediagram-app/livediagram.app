A snapshot keeps the whole <Noun /> as it is right now, so you can try a big change (a reorganisation, an import, a clean-up with a teammate or an AI assistant) knowing you can put everything back in one step.

## Taking a Snapshot

Snapshots live in **History**: open **Tools** in the footer and press **History**. Press **Take Snapshot** at its foot. Give the snapshot a name if you like, such as "Before the workshop", and press **Take Snapshot** again. Without a name it is called by the time it was taken.

Each snapshot sits on History's timeline at the time it was taken, marked **Snapshot**, with its name, who took it, how long ago and its size. The latest **five** are kept: when five are there, the form says which one taking another will remove, and the oldest goes.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Signed in on your own or your team's canvas, History shows the canvas's timeline with each snapshot pinned on it, and a **Take Snapshot** button at its foot. Signed out, or through an edit link, History shows the snapshots on their own. Without a name, a snapshot is called like "Snapshot of 15 Nov, 14:32".
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">

If you opened the design through an Editor link, History shows the design's snapshots on their own, without the timeline.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">

Each snapshot is marked with a camera among the document's other changes. Someone who can take snapshots but has no timeline to read, such as an Editor on a share link, sees the snapshots on their own.
</AppNote>

If you came in through an Editor share link, History has no timeline for you, so it shows the snapshots on their own.
</AppNote>

## Reverting to a Snapshot

Press **Revert** on a snapshot and confirm. The <Noun /> goes back to exactly how it was: every tab and its folders. A tab made since is removed, and one removed since comes back. The <Noun />'s name and who it is shared with stay as they are.

Reverting replaces everyone's work since that snapshot, so first it takes a snapshot of the <Noun /> as it is now, marked **Before revert**. If you change your mind, revert to that one.

Everyone who has the <Noun /> open sees it reload onto the reverted version straight away, so nobody keeps working on what was replaced.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  It also restores the canvas's comment threads and the slide deck.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  It also restores the component library, and the comment threads on the design's elements come back as they were.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  It also restores the slide deck, and the comment threads on the document's elements come back as they were.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Calls, attendance and the characters walking around are not part of a snapshot.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  It also restores card types, every card (each with its number) and every Sheet. Comment threads and Automations rules stay as they are. A card that was deleted since comes back with its old number; a card made since goes.
</AppNote>

## Deleting a Snapshot

Press the bin on a snapshot. It is deleted at once, with no question, and can't be brought back.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  The bin is labelled **Delete snapshot**.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  The bin is labelled **Delete snapshot**.
</AppNote>

## Who Can Use Snapshots

Editors of the whole <Noun /> take, revert and delete snapshots. Participants, Viewers and people with a link to one tab don't see them. Snapshots are there on <Noun plural /> stored on the server: a <Noun /> kept in **This Browser** has none, and AI assistants and scripts using an API token can't use them.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  An embed has none either, and neither does a canvas open in a workbench.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Signed in, an Editor sees them on History's timeline; an Editor who came in through a share link, with no History to read, sees the snapshots on their own.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  **Snapshots and Version History.** [Version History](/help/tabs/version-history/) keeps earlier versions of **one tab** for you as you work. A snapshot is one you take yourself, of the **whole document** at once: every tab together. Reverting to a snapshot keeps each tab's Version History, so a tab's earlier versions are still there afterwards. **Open Version History** in History takes you to it.
</AppNote>
