A share password is an optional password on a <Noun />. When it is set, anyone opening any share link for that <Noun /> must enter the password before they can see it. It is there to stop people guessing or stumbling onto a share URL, not to provide strong encryption.

## How It Works

The password belongs to the **<Noun />**, not to a single link. One password covers every share link to it, [embeds](/help/collaboration/sharing/embeds/) included. Once a visitor enters it correctly, it stays in effect for their whole visit.

As the owner you always bypass the password. So do members of the [team](/help/collaboration/teams/) whose library holds the <Noun />, since they don't come in through a link. The prompt only ever appears for people coming in through a share link.

## Setting a Password

<Step n={1}>
  Open the **Share** dialog on your <Noun />.
</Step>
<Step n={2}>
  Switch on **Password Protection** at the foot of the dialog, beneath the list of passes.
</Step>
<Step n={3}>
  Type a password of 6 to 256 characters in the field that appears (spaces at either end don't
  count) and choose **Save**.
</Step>

Once saved, the row shows **Password Set** with **Replace** and **Remove**. Only a scrambled form of the password is kept, so nobody can read it back afterwards, you included: note it down before you save, and use **Replace** to choose a new one if you lose it.

## Changing or Removing It

- **Replace** brings back the field. Type the new password and choose **Save**.
- **Remove**, or turning the switch off, takes the password away. Every link then opens without one again.

People already in the <Noun /> through a link are asked for the new password after a change.

<AppNote app="liveplanner">They are asked within a few minutes of the change.</AppNote>

</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram,livehuddle">

## What Visitors See

When someone opens a protected share link, they get a card headed **This <Noun /> is password-protected**, with a single password box and an **Open <Noun capital />** button, instead of the <Noun />. A wrong password shows "That password didn't match. Try again." and lets them try again.

The browser remembers a password that worked, for that link, so a visitor isn't asked again next time unless you change the password or they clear their browser's site data.

Embedded <Noun plural /> behave the same way: the password prompt appears inside the embed frame.

## What a Password Turns Off

- **Live images.** An image tag can't supply a password, so a [live image](/help/collaboration/sharing/live-image/) of a <Noun /> with a password shows nothing.
- **The Community.** A public <Noun /> can't ask its visitors for a password, so a <Noun /> can't be in the [Community](/help/collaboration/sharing/community/) and have a password at the same time. While it is listed, **Password Protection** is switched off and locked, with a note saying why; a <Noun /> with a password can't be shared there until you remove it.

The **Live image** menu disappears from every pass while a password is set.

In an embed, because browsers keep each site's storage separate, a visitor enters the password once per site where the canvas is embedded.
