A shared timer time-boxes an activity for the whole room, ticking in sync for every participant. It comes in two flavours: a **countdown** for "you have five minutes", and a **stopwatch** for "let us see how long this takes".

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Set it going and the **Timer** button in the bottom bar shows the clock for every participant.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  Set it going and the **Timer** button in the bottom-right cluster shows the clock for every participant.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  The **Timer** and the **Stopwatch** are pieces you place in a room, like a Screen or a World Clock. A Timer counts down ("you have ten minutes"); a Stopwatch counts up ("let's see how long this takes"). Put one on the wall of each meeting room, and every room can keep its own time.

  **Placing One.** Both are in the palette's **Meet** category, beside the Screen, Room Sign, Notice Board, World Clock, Calendar Wall, Suggestion Box and Team Photo Wall, and search finds them too. Pick one and click where it goes, as with any other piece. It drops at its own size and has no resize handles; move or delete it like any piece. A new Timer is set to 5 minutes, and a new Stopwatch reads 0:00.

  **Running It.** Under each piece sit its own buttons. Pressing them never selects or moves the piece, and in Avatar mode they work without walking over. While you walk, clicking the piece itself presses its main button (Start, Pause or Restart).

  - **Start** sets it going (or carries on from where it was paused), and while it runs the same button reads **Pause**. **Reset**, which shows once it has run, puts it back to the start.
  - On a Timer, **Set** opens its length: tap **5**, **10**, **15** or **25 min**, or type the minutes (1 to 120) and use **−** and **+**, then press **Set Timer**. A new length starts a new round.
  - When a Timer reaches **0:00** its display turns red and flashes three times (no flash if you've asked your device for less motion), its word reads **Time's Up**, and **Restart** runs it again.

  The word under the time says where it is: **Ready**, **Running**, **Paused** or **Time's Up**. A Stopwatch runs until you pause it.

  **The Chime.** A Timer plays a short, gentle chime when it reaches zero. While you're walking in [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/), you hear the chime of a Timer in the room you're standing in; otherwise you hear every Timer on the tab. It plays once, on time, and never late: open the tab after a Timer ran out and you see 0:00 in silence. Your browser only lets a page play sound once you've pressed something on it.

  **Who Can Run It.** Anyone with an **Editor** or **Participant** pass can start, pause, reset and set a Timer or Stopwatch, even while someone is the [facilitator](/help/collaboration/facilitator/). A **Viewer** sees the time tick but gets no buttons, and clicking the piece does nothing. Placing, moving and deleting the pieces is an Editor's.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  **Setting It Up.** Press the **Timer** button in the bottom bar (in [Workshop](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) mode and on an Event Storming board) to open the set-up above it. You need edit access to run it, and while someone is the [facilitator](/help/collaboration/facilitator/) it is theirs to run.

  You can also put the timer **on the canvas**: a [session button](/help/palette/behaviour/session-buttons/) set to Timer counts down where it sits, carrying its own pause, resume and reset. It is the same timer as the **Timer** button's, not a second one, which is what lets a template ship its own running order.

  <Figure caption="Drag round the dial to set a countdown's length, or tap a preset. One lap of the dial is an hour.">
    <TimerControl />
  </Figure>

  <Step n={1}>
    Choose **Countdown** or **Stopwatch**. For a countdown, set the length by dragging the handle
    round the dial (the coloured wedge is the time; one lap is an hour), tapping a preset (1, 3, 5,
    10, 15, or 30 minutes), or using the **−** and **+** buttons for a minute at a time, from one
    minute up to two hours (past an hour, the dial shows an extra ring). The dial also takes the arrow
    keys, with **Home** for one minute and **End** for an hour.
  </Step>

  <Step n={2}>
    Press the start button, which names what it will do (**Start 5 min countdown**, or **Start
    stopwatch**). While it runs the dial becomes the clock, the wedge shrinking as time goes, with a
    **Running**, **Paused** or **Time's up** label above it. Underneath you can **Pause** and
    **Resume**, **Reset** back to the start, or **End** the timer altogether. A countdown also offers
    **+30s**, **+1 min**, and **+5 min** to give the room a little longer without starting over, and
    **Again** once it reaches zero.
  </Step>

  While it runs, the wedge turns amber in the last minute and red at zero, so the dial reads at a glance. A tab runs one timer at a time: while one exists the panel shows it rather than the set-up, and you end it to start a different kind. The timer is scoped to the tab, so each tab can have its own. Because it syncs like everything else, anyone who joins late or reloads sees the current time straight away rather than a fresh clock.

  **What Everyone Sees.** The live clock shows on the **Timer** button in the bottom bar, right after Undo and Redo, ticking smoothly while it runs. A countdown also **drains**: the button starts fully tinted and empties from left to right as the time goes, so someone across the room can read how long is left without parsing the digits. It turns red and **flashes when it reaches 0:00** so the room knows time is up. A stopwatch has no end to work towards, so it gets no fill. There is no sound.

  Press the button to open the timer above it. People with edit access get the dial with **+30s**, **+1 min** and **+5 min**, plus **Reset**, **Pause** or **Resume**, and **End**; Participant and Viewer visitors get the same dial to read, without the controls. While no timer runs, the button shows just its icon, and pressing it opens the set-up, so you can start one from there too.
</AppNote>

## How It Stays in Sync

Rather than sending a tick across the network every second, each participant's clock works out the time locally from a shared anchor point: the moment a countdown will end, or the instant a stopwatch started. Everyone computes the same value from that anchor, so the clock stays in step without constant network chatter. Pausing freezes the value, and resuming re-anchors it.

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Moving the piece never stops it, and people who join late or reload see the time straight away. Starting or pausing is not something [Undo](/help/canvas/undo/) takes back. A downloaded picture of the tab shows the time as it was when you saved it.
</AppNote>

<Note>
  The clock is shared but not second-perfect across machines: tiny differences in device clocks are
  fine for keeping a session together. It is there to keep the room roughly together, not to referee
  a stopwatch race.
</Note>
