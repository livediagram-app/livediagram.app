Dot-voting is a quick way for a group to prioritise. Each participant gets a budget of dots to spend on the things they care about most, the dots tally up live, and you reveal the winners at the end.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  You spend dots on elements. It is ideal for narrowing a wall of retro stickies or brainstorm ideas
  down to the few worth acting on.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  You spend dots on cards. It is ideal for narrowing a column of retro notes or ideas down to the
  few worth acting on.
</AppNote>

## Running a Vote

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Press the **Vote** button in the bottom bar (in [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) and
  on Event Storming boards) to open the vote set-up above it.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  Press the **Vote** button in the bottom-right cluster to open the vote set-up above it. It shows
  on a tab whose board takes a card type with a **Votes** field, such as a retro board (see [Session
  Tools](/help/collaboration/session-tools/#where-to-find-them)).
</AppNote>

<Step n={1}>
  Set the **Dots per person** by tapping the row of dots (tap the fifth for five each; anywhere from
  1 to 10, starting at 3). With more than one dot, choose whether people can stack dots under **Dots
  per item** (**Any number**, or **One each** so every dot goes on a different item). Decide the two
  [privacy switches](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/vote-privacy/), then choose **Start
  vote**. The popover becomes the [Vote
  panel](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/vote-panel/), and the **Vote** button of everyone
  who can cast (Editors and Participants) counts the dots they have left.
</Step>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Switch on **Only items with text** to leave blank sticky notes and shapes out of the vote. On a
  tab with [layers](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/vote-layers/), you also choose which
  layer takes dots.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  When the tab's card types have a [Votes field](/help/plan/card-types/) of their own beside
  **Votes**, say a **Priority Vote**, **Count In** picks which one the dots are added to (**Votes**
  first); a card whose type lacks it keeps its dots in **Votes**.
</AppNote>

<Step n={2}>
  While the vote is open, participants [put dots on what they want to
  back](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/casting-dots/). The Vote panel shows your own dots
  left at the top and how far through the room you are underneath. On a desktop it stays open while
  the vote runs, until you press the **Vote** button again.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Choose **End vote** to close casting (the tallies stay put), then **Show results** to start the
  [guided results walkthrough](/help/collaboration/session-tools/voting/vote-results/), or **Clear**
  to skip it. During the walkthrough, **Done** on the last pick, or **Clear vote** at any point,
  puts the tab back. All of these are on the Vote panel, so you never have to leave it mid-session.
</Step>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">When the vote ends, each card keeps its dots.</AppNote>

## Who Drives a Vote

**A vote belongs to whoever started it.** Only they can end it. The same goes for the rest: reveal the results, clear it, or move the walkthrough; everyone else's Vote panel says so in place of the buttons. You can always run another vote, but you can't get the dots back, so an accidental **End vote** from a participant would cost the room the whole round.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  While someone holds the [facilitator](/help/collaboration/facilitator/) role, only the facilitator
  can end it, and the same goes for the rest. Starting a vote also needs nobody else to be holding
  the facilitator role.
</AppNote>

Starting a vote needs edit access. Visitors on a Participant link cast dots like editors; on a Viewer share link they can watch the counts, the reveal, and the walkthrough but cannot cast a dot, so observers see the outcome without skewing it. A tab runs one vote at a time, and each tab can run its own.

<Tip>
  A common rule of thumb is roughly one dot per three or four options. Set the dots before you start
  the vote, then leave it: keeping budgets small forces real choices.
</Tip>
