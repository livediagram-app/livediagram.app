A mode button is an element that hands whoever presses it a mode. The part below for your app says which modes and where it lives.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livehuddle">
Pressing it changes the mode for **the person who pressed it** and nobody else. Everyone else carries on in whatever tool they were already using.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
A Selection Mode button is an element that hands out a [selection mode](/help/selection-modes/). Put one on the canvas and anybody can press it, without ever having opened the mode picker.

Avatar mode and its Avatar button now live in livehuddle. A board saved with an Avatar button keeps it, shown as a card that says **Moved to livehuddle**; you can still move or delete it.

**Adding one**

The **Selection Mode** group of the palette's **Session** category, in Workshop mode's [Collaborate](/help/palette/behaviour/) kit, holds seven buttons, one per mode: **Select**, **Hand**, **Laser**, **Spotlight**, **Eraser**, **Format** and **Isometric**.

Pick the mode you want and it drops ready to press, reading "Switch to Spotlight" (or whichever mode you picked) and switching whoever presses it into that mode. To point an existing button at another mode, right-click it and open **Button**; the face relabels itself to match. You can also type your own label over the derived one ("Light it up"), and clearing it brings the derived text back.

Selection Mode buttons are on [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/)'s palette; in any other mode, search adds one.

<Figure caption="A row of Selection Mode buttons: two offering a mode, one offering the way back out.">
  <DiagramModeButtonBar />
</Figure>

**What pressing it does**

Pressing it again while you are in that mode hands you back your previous one, so a button is never a one-way door: a button for the mode you are already in reads "Leave Spotlight" rather than offering it again, even if you gave it a label of your own.

A click on the round icon presses the button; a click anywhere else on the card selects it, and a drag moves it, so you can arrange a bar of them without setting one off.

**Why a bar of them works**

A Selection Mode button keeps working while you are in a mode that makes the rest of the canvas read-only (Spotlight or Isometric), so a row of them makes a usable little control bar. It also works for somebody on a view-only link, since switching your own tool changes nothing for anyone else. The exceptions are **Eraser** and **Format** buttons: they change the canvas, so for anyone without edit access they show but do nothing. That is what makes it useful on a walkthrough: the viewer has the controls they need in front of them, on the canvas, rather than in a panel they were never told about.

In a read-only embed there is no tool to switch, so the button shows but does nothing.

<Tip>
  Pair an **Eraser** button with a **Select** button so a reviewer can tidy up what they see and put
  the eraser down again without leaving the canvas.
</Tip>
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
A Selection Mode button is an element that hands out [Avatar mode](/help/selection-modes/avatar-mode/). Put one on the canvas and an editor can press it to start walking; Participants and view-only visitors are always walking, so for them it is just a sign. Avatar is the one mode a button hands out in livehuddle, so it is called the **Avatar Button** in the palette.

It is the board's own way into Avatar mode, beside **Walk** at the start of the palette strip, and a space with one opens walking, just below the first, once each visit. The templates no longer carry one: every Space, and every blank tab you start, has an [Avatar Pad](/help/palette/avatar-pads/) where people arrive instead. Spaces made before keep their **Walk In** button, and it still works; add an Avatar button yourself when you want a visible door on the board.

**Adding One**

The palette's **Travel** category, one of the [meeting elements](/help/palette/behaviour/), holds the **Avatar Button**, last in the list after the Portal, Bring Focus, the Avatar Pad and the other ways of getting about. Search adds one too: type "avatar".

It drops ready to press, reading "Switch to Avatar". You can type your own label over the derived one ("Walk with me"), and clearing it brings the derived text back.

<Figure caption="An Avatar button before and after it is pressed: it offers Avatar mode, then the way back out.">
  <HuddleModeButtonBar />
</Figure>

**What Pressing It Does**

Pressing it again while you are walking hands you back your previous tool, so the button is never a one-way door: while you are in Avatar mode it reads "Leave Avatar" rather than offering it again.

A click on the round icon presses the button; a click anywhere else on the card selects it, and a drag moves it, so you can move it without setting it off.

Your character appears just below the button, rather than in the middle of the screen, so it shows up where you pressed. An Avatar Pad on the tab wins: everyone starts on the pad.

**Where It Works**

The button is for Editors: it keeps working while you walk, even though walking makes the rest of the canvas read-only, which makes it a way back out. Participants and Viewers are always walking, so for them the button does nothing.

In a read-only embed there is no walking, so the button shows but does nothing.

<Tip>
  Older boards may still have Select, Hand, Eraser or Format buttons on them. They open as Avatar
  buttons now, keeping their place, label and colours.
</Tip>
</AppNote>
