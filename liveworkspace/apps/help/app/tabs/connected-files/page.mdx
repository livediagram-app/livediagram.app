<ArticleLayout path="tabs/connected-files" title="Connected Files" description="Add a tab that opens a file from any liveworkspace app, so every part of a project is one tap away." categoryTitle="Tabs" categorySlug="tabs">

A project rarely lives in one file. The architecture may be a canvas in livediagram, the delivery plan in liveplanner, the brief in livedocuments and the screens in livedesign. A **connected file** is a tab in your <Noun />'s tab bar that stands for one of those files. It shows the file's app logo and its name, and pressing it shows a preview of the file with a button to open it.

## Connecting a file

<Step n={1}>
  Add a tab with the **+** at the end of the tab bar (or open a <Noun />
  's empty tab). Its start screen opens.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Press **Connect File**, the last entry at the very bottom of the start screen's sidebar. Your most
  recent files from every liveworkspace app are listed, newest first, each with its app's logo.
  **Find a file** narrows the list by name.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Choose the **Role** anyone opening the tab gets on that file: **Editor** (chosen unless you change
  it), **Participant** or **Viewer**.
</Step>

<Step n={4}>
  Press the file. The connected tab takes the empty tab's place at the end of the tab bar, and
  opens.
</Step>

<AppNote app="livediagram,liveplanner,livedocuments,livedesign">
  On a desktop there is a quicker way: the **liveworkspace logo** just right of the **+** at the end
  of the tab bar is **Connect a File**. Press it and the same list opens in a window over your
  file, with the same **Role**, **Add connection to both files** and **Find a file**. Press a file
  and it is connected as a new tab at the end of the tab bar, which opens; close the window to
  connect nothing. The button shows only to editors who can change the file's tabs, and not on a
  phone or a narrow window, where **Connect File** stays on the new tab's start screen.
</AppNote>

A file that is already connected shows **Connected**, and pressing it takes you to its tab. A file you can't connect is greyed out with the reason: **Only its owner can share it**, or, for a role its app doesn't have (livehuddle has no Viewer), that the app has no such role. A <Noun /> holds up to 50 connected tabs.

Connecting makes a share link on the file you connect, at the role you chose, so everyone who can open your <Noun /> can open it from the tab, even if they could not open it before. It is listed in that file's **Share** dialog as **Connected from** your <Noun />, where its owner can see it and end it. You can only connect files you could share yourself: your own, or your team's.

### Both ways

Turn on **Add connection to both files** before you press a file, and that file gains a tab back to this <Noun /> too. It needs you to be able to share this <Noun /> as well (its owner, or a member of its team). If the other file cannot be reached just then, its tab back is not made and a message says so; your <Noun /> keeps its connected tab.

## Opening a connected file

Press the connected tab to see its page: a preview of the file, its name, and a button under the preview. Press **Open in** and the app's name (for a file in another app), or **Open** (for another <Noun />), to go to the file. A file in another app opens in a new browser tab (the tab carries a small arrow for that), and another <Noun /> opens in place. The tab's page stays behind with **Open Again**, in case your browser held the new tab back.

Nothing opens by itself. Pressing the tab, opening the <Noun /> on a connected tab, or connecting one only shows its page; the file opens when you press the button under the preview.

The tab's name follows the file's: when the file is renamed, the tab takes the new name the next time an editor opens it. A connected tab holds no drawing of its own.

<AppNote app="livediagram,liveplanner,livedocuments,livedesign">
  You can give a connected tab a name of its own, as any tab: choose **Rename** in its menu, or
  double-click the open tab, type the name and press Enter. The name is this file's label for the
  tab only; the file keeps its own name. Point at a renamed connected tab to see the file's name
  (**Connected to** and the file's name). To go back to the file's name, rename the tab to nothing
  (or to the file's name): it then follows the file again. A rename reaches everyone with the file
  open, and stays after a reload or Offline.
</AppNote>

## Arranging and removing

A connected tab sits on the tab bar like any other: drag it to reorder, and put it in a [tab folder](/help/tabs/tab-folders/) from its menu (**⋯** on the open tab, or right-click it) with **Organise in Tab Folder**. Its menu is short: **Organise in Tab Folder**, **Remove Tab**, and **Connect Again** when it opens nothing. There is no Duplicate or Add to Another <Noun capital /> for a connected tab, and its name comes from its file.

**Remove Tab** in its menu removes the tab and nothing else: the file stays exactly as it was. Its share link is deleted with it, so anyone it let in is let out again. Undo brings the tab back, without its link: it offers **Connect Again**.

## When a connected tab needs attention

A small amber dot on the tab means it opens nothing just now. Its page says why:

- **This file is no longer there.** The file was moved to the Trash or deleted. Remove the tab.
- **This connection's link was removed.** The file's owner ended its link from its Share dialog. **Connect Again** makes a new one.
- **Connect this copy.** The tab came with a copy of the <Noun /> or an import, so it does not open the file yet. **Connect Again** connects it here.
- **Connected files open when you are online.** A <Noun /> saved only Offline, or no network.

## Who can do what

- **Editors** connect files, reorder, folder and remove connected tabs, and use Connect Again.
- **Participants** and **Viewers** see connected tabs and open them; the file opens at the role the tab was connected with.

Connected tabs are made from the editor only. Agents and the CLI can see a connected tab's app and name, never where it leads, and cannot make one.

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  A meeting rarely needs only its space: the agenda in livedocuments, the plan in liveplanner, the
  architecture in livediagram, the screens in livedesign. Another livehuddle space has no Viewer
  role, so with Viewer chosen a space in the list cannot be picked: connect it as an Editor or a
  Participant. livehuddle's share roles are **Editor** and **Participant**; **Participants** see
  connected tabs and open them, and so does anyone on an older Viewer link. A connected tab holds no
  room of its own: nothing is placed on it, and no one walks into it. API scripts (not agents and
  the CLI) can see a connected tab's app and name, never where it leads, and cannot make one.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The empty tab's start screen is **Start Making Pages**. When the empty tab is the document's only
  tab, it stays, and the connected tab joins it. **Connect File** is offered only when you are
  signed in and can change the document's tabs: not to Viewers or Participants, on a link for one
  tab, in an embed, or in a document kept Offline. A connected tab holds no pages of its own.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  A connected tab holds no pages of its own. Connected files in livedesign read as "a diagram, a
  plan, a document or another design".
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  The start screen is **Start Planning**, and **Connect File** is at the very bottom of its sidebar (just below **Import**). A connected tab holds no boards, sheets or cards of its own.

**Start a Connected Project.** To start a whole project across the apps at once, open the [Explorer](/help/explorer/explorer-page/)'s **Home** with **All Apps** picked and press **Start a Project** beside **What you can make**. Pick a kit: **Software Project** (a plan, an architecture diagram, a design doc and a team Space), **Product Launch** (a launch plan, a keynote, a roadmap and a launch room), **Team Workshop** (a kick-off deck, an affinity map, a retro board and a workshop room) or **Website Build** (a build board, wireframes and a site brief). Give it a **Project Name**, pick the role the connections give (**Editor**, **Participant** or **Viewer**: anyone who can open one of the files can then edit, take part in or view the others), and press **Create Project**. One press makes a new folder in My Work holding every file, each started from its app's template (the plan with its card types and example cards), and connects each one to all the others as connected tabs. When it's done, **Open Folder** takes you there and **Start Another** begins again. If part of it couldn't be made, **Retry** finishes it, or **Remove What Was Made** takes it all away. Starting a project needs an account, so signed out you see **Sign In to Create**.

</AppNote>
