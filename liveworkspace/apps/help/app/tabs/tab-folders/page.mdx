When a <Noun />'s tab bar fills up, a flat list of tabs gets hard to scan. A **tab folder** gathers related tabs under one named chip that takes the room of a single tab. Put three tabs under "Organisation" and two under "Plans", and the bar stays calm however many tabs each folder holds.

## How folders work

A few things are worth knowing about how tab folders behave:

- **One level.** A folder holds tabs. Folders never contain other folders.
- **A folder is just a name.** There is no separate folder object to create or delete. A folder exists as long as at least one tab carries its name, and it disappears when the last tab leaves it.
- **Per-<Noun />.** If a tab is shared into more than one <Noun />, it can sit in a folder in one <Noun /> and be loose in another. The folder belongs to the <Noun />, not to the tab's content.
- **Folders are contiguous.** A folder's tabs always sit together in the tab order, behind a single chip in the folder's colour with its name and a folder icon that stacks to show how many tabs it holds.
- **Shared with collaborators.** Folder names and membership are part of the <Noun />, so everyone sees the same folders.

## Opening a folder

A folder takes up one chip on the bar, however many tabs it holds:

- **Click the chip** to open the folder in place: its tabs slide out on the bar right after the chip, in order, and the tabs after the folder slide along to make room. Click the chip again to tuck them away, unless you are on one of them (below). Nothing pops up over the <Noun />.
- **The folder you are in stays open.** Open a tab that lives in a folder, from the Tabs list, search or a link, and the folder opens round it so you see every tab it holds. While you are on one of its tabs the folder can't be shut: clicking the chip just brings your tab back into view. Move to a tab outside the folder and it goes back to how you left it, shut again if it was shut.
- **Each folder has its own colour**, picked from its name, so "Plans" looks the same every time you come back. The chip shows a folder icon and the name, open or shut. The icon stacks to show how full the folder is: one folder for one tab, two for two, three for three or more. Open the folder to see every tab it holds.
- **A folder opens for this visit.** On a computer, a small <Noun /> opens with its folders open: up to 3 folders, none holding more than 3 tabs, and 9 tabs in all. A small <Noun /> shows every tab at once; a busy one starts tidy with its folders shut, and so does a phone. On a phone, opening a folder scrolls the bar so its tabs are in view.
- **Presence comes along.** If collaborators are on tabs tucked inside a shut folder, their avatars show beside the chip.
- **From the keyboard**, the arrow keys move between the tabs and chips on the bar, and Enter or Space opens or shuts the folder you are on.

## Putting tabs in folders

There are two ways to change which folder a tab belongs to.

<Step n={1}>
  Drag and drop. Drag a tab onto a folder's tabs (or onto its chip, which files it at the front of
  the folder) to join that folder. Hold it over a shut folder's chip for a moment and the folder
  opens, so you can drop it exactly where you want. Drop it among the loose tabs to make it loose
  again, or among a different folder's tabs to move it there.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  The tab menu. Open the tab's **⋯** or right-click menu and choose **Organise in Tab Folder**. In
  the dialog, pick an existing folder's tile to file the tab there, the **No Folder** tile to make
  it loose on the bar, or the **New Folder** tile to type a fresh name in place. Typing an existing
  folder's name simply files the tab into that folder, since same name means same folder.
</Step>

## Renaming a folder

**Double-click the chip** to rename the folder. Renaming rewrites the name on every tab in it, so the folder keeps all its tabs. Renaming it to another folder's name merges the two.

To get rid of a folder, move its tabs out (drag them to the loose tabs, or choose **No Folder**): the folder disappears with its last tab. Its tabs are never deleted.

On a share link for a Viewer or a Participant, or a link scoped to one tab, you can open folders and switch tabs, but you can't drag tabs between folders or rename them.

## From an AI tool or the command line

An AI tool connected through [Connect an AI Tool](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/), and the [command line](/help/developers/cli/), see and use folders too. Reading a <Noun /> lists each tab with its folder. Adding a tab can put it straight into a folder: an existing name adds it as that folder's last tab, a new name starts a folder. The AI can also move a tab into or out of a folder, rename a folder, or ungroup one so all its tabs are loose, and no other tab changes place. Everyone with the <Noun /> open sees the change at once. As in the editor, only an Editor can change folders.

<Tip>
  Give folders short names like "Plans" or "Back office". The name shows on the chip beside its
  folder icon, so a tight name keeps the bar readable.
</Tip>
