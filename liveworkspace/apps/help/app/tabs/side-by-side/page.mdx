Tabs keep related work together: the draft and the reference, the overview and the detail. Side by side puts two of them on screen at once, so you can compare them, copy an idea across, or keep one in view while you work on the other.

## Opening a tab beside another

Drag a tab from the tab bar toward the **right edge** of the screen. A glowing rail wakes along the edge as you head for it, and once you're close, a preview of the pane slides in showing that tab's content. Let go there, and the tab opens on the right.

- Drag a **different** tab and it opens on the right, while you keep working where you are on the left.
- Drag the tab you're **on** and it moves to the right, taking your toolbars with it; the tab you were on before fills the left.
- With two tabs already side by side, drop another tab on the right to show it there instead.

You can also open the tab's menu (the **⋯** button on the active tab, or right-click the tab) and press **Side by Side** in the row of quick actions along the top.

## Working across both

Each tab keeps its side. The site header runs across the top of both, and the tab bar across the bottom, just as with one tab. Your toolbars and panels (the palette, the Explorer, the map and zoom) sit in the pane you're working in.

To work on the other tab, **click** in its pane, or simply **rest the pointer** there for a moment. The toolbars glide across to it. Neither content moves: each tab stays exactly where it was on screen, at the same zoom.

The pane you're not working in is **live**: anything you or a collaborator changes on that tab shows there straight away. Scroll to pan around it, and hold **Ctrl** (or **⌘** on a Mac) while you scroll to zoom.

Collaborators see you on **both** tabs in the tab bar, since both are on your screen. The tab in your other pane has a dashed outline so you can tell which it is.

## Resizing and closing

The seam between the two panes is a handle: drag it to give one side more room, or double-click it to go back to halves. You can also focus it with **Tab** and use the arrow keys.

Two buttons sit on the seam, just above the handle:

- **Fit Both Sides** fits each tab's content to its pane.
- **Close Side by Side** goes back to one tab, the one you were working on, across the whole window. The same button appears in the tab's menu while two tabs are side by side.

Which two tabs you had side by side is remembered on this device for each <Noun />, so they come back the next time you open it. Where you left the divider is one setting for this device, used by every <Noun />.

## Screens it works on

Side by side needs room for two working tabs, so it's offered on screens at least **1,400 pixels wide** and **700 pixels tall** (inside the browser window): most desktop monitors and larger laptops. On smaller laptops, tablets and phones it isn't offered, and you switch between tabs from the tab bar as usual.

It also steps aside in zen mode, and comes back when you leave it.
