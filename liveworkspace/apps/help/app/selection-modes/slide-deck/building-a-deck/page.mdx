<AppNote app="livediagram">
  A deck is built from the **Slide Deck**, opened from **Tools** in the bottom bar, one slide per point you mean to make. Each row shows the slide's position, its name, which tab it draws from, and how many elements are on it, plus a marker when it has notes or is hidden.

  Building a deck needs edit access: on a view-only or participant link the Slide Deck shows the slides and **Present**, but no **New slide** button and no slide menus, and its rows cannot be dragged.

  **A presented template comes with its deck.** Start from the Roadmap, the Business Model Canvas, the Incident Postmortem, the Gantt chart or another template people present, and the deck already holds an overview and one slide per part, in the order you would talk through it, each with a line of presenter notes. On a canvas that already has slides, they go on after yours. They are ordinary slides from then on: rename, reorder, hide or delete them as you like.

  **A row does one thing: press it to open that slide.** The editor switches to that slide's tab and selects its elements, which is how you check a slide without presenting it. Everything you can _do_ to a slide lives in its `…` menu instead, so a narrow list never has to carry five controls per row.

  **The slide menu.** Press the `…` at the right of a row and you get the same shape of menu the Explorer's rows and the tab context menu use: a row of quick actions across the top, then two labelled categories you can open.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  A deck is built in **Contents**, the list of your pages down the left of the canvas (or behind the **Contents** button in the bottom-right corner when you have tucked it away). Every [slide page](/help/pages/slide-pages/) is in the deck already. Everything else you do to a slide is on its page's **⋯** menu in Contents: rest the pointer on a row and press **⋯**, or right-click the row.

  Building a deck needs edit access: on a view-only or participant link the rows have no menu. Presenting doesn't: the **Present** button at the top right of the canvas is there for everyone on the document.

  **Adding a page.** An infographic or article page is not in the deck until you add it. Open its **⋯** menu and press **Add to Slides**: the page joins the show in its place in the page order, and from then on it is a slide like any other.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  The quick actions are **Rename**, notes (**Add notes**, or **Edit notes** once there are some, lit when the slide has a script waiting), **Duplicate** (a copy with the same elements, straight after it, for a "same picture, different point" pair) and **Delete**, pinned to the right on its own.

  **Deleting asks first.** A confirmation opens beside the menu button, because the elements survive a deletion but the arrangement does not, and the arrangement is what you spent the time on. Either way the elements stay exactly where they are on the canvas.

  **Selection: what is on the slide.** A slide's membership is editable for the life of the deck, not just at the moment you made it. Open the menu's **Selection** category and it tells you what the two buttons will act on and how many elements the slide holds now. **Add** puts the elements you have selected onto the slide (with several selected it counts them, as in **Add 3**). **Remove** takes them off again. Only the slide changes; nothing leaves the canvas.

  Both are offered only while you are on **that slide's own tab** with something selected, and the category tells you which is missing when they aren't. A slide holds one tab's elements, so adding from a different tab isn't something to refuse politely; it is something that cannot be expressed.
</AppNote>

## Hiding a slide from the run

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  The slide menu's **Visibility** category carries a single switch: **Hide** the slide, or **Show** it again.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  On a page that is in the deck, the menu offers **Hide in Slides**, and on a hidden one **Show in Slides**.
</AppNote>

Hiding is a different idea from deleting. It is for the slide you might want next week, the backup detail for a question you may not get, the section you cut for time: the slide stays in the deck, it just doesn't run.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  A hidden slide shows in the panel with its name struck through and an eye marker beside it, so you can always see it is there.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The page stays, and so does its slide. Contents sets a hidden slide's picture aside under a **Hidden** veil, so you can always see it is there.
</AppNote>

Hidden slides are skipped everywhere at once: the slide count, the `7 / 23` in the presenter's display, the jump list, and what advancing lands on all leave them out, so nothing can disagree about how long the deck is.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  The count in question is the one on the **Present** button.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The count in question is the one on the **Present** button's hover card.

  **Presenting from a slide.** **Present From Here**, on a slide's menu, starts the show at that slide, so you can rehearse the second half without clicking through the first.
</AppNote>

## Ordering the deck

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  **Drag a row** up or down the list to move it. The list does not reshuffle while you drag: a caret shows where the row will land, and the move happens when you let go, because a list that reorders live moves the very row you were aiming at. It works on touch as well as with a mouse.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  **The deck runs in Contents' order.** Drag a row up or down the list to move its page, and its slide moves with it. The list does not reshuffle while you drag: a caret shows where the row will land, and the move happens when you let go. Each tab presents on its own: **Present** runs the tab you are on, so a talk lives on one tab and another tab can hold another talk.
</AppNote>

## Presenter notes

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Open a slide's `…` menu and press **Add notes**, and a notes box opens under the list: what you mean to _say_ over this slide. Press **Done** to put it away again. It is behind the menu rather than always on screen so the panel never grows a text area you did not ask for.

  Notes belong to the slide, not to anything on it, which matters because the same box on two slides usually wants two different sentences said about it. They are plain text, since it is a script you read off rather than something anybody needs to bold, and during the presentation they stay hidden until you ask for them.

  **A time budget per slide.** Under the notes box sits **Budget**, a number of minutes for this slide, shown while you present once you turn the budget on in the presenter settings. It is blank for most slides and that is the normal case; fill it in for the ones you know you tend to overrun. It sits with the notes deliberately: both are decided while you are _writing_ the talk, not while you are giving it. While you present, a slide with a budget can show time-on-this-slide against it in the presenter's display, and going over is marked rather than enforced. See [the pacing display](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenter-controls/).

  <Tip>
    Build the deck in the order you will speak, then walk it once by pressing each row in turn. You
    are checking that every slide's elements make sense on their own, which is the one thing the panel
    cannot tell you.
  </Tip>
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  Each slide page's title bar has **Notes**. Press it to write what you mean to _say_ over this slide, as note cards, one point a card. Press **Add First Card** and type; `⌘↵` (`Ctrl+Enter`) starts the next card, and `Esc` or **Done** closes the one you are writing. Drag a card by its grip, or press **Move Up** or **Move Down** (`Alt+↑` / `Alt+↓`), to change the order; the bin removes one, with **Undo** for a few seconds. A slide holds up to 20 cards.

  Notes belong to the slide, not to anything on it. Each card is plain text with its speaking time in the corner, since it is a script you read off rather than something anybody needs to bold, and during the presentation they stay hidden until you ask for them.

  <Tip>
    Build the deck in the order you will speak, then walk it once with Present From Here on each slide
    you are unsure of. You are checking that every slide makes sense on its own, which is the one
    thing the list cannot tell you.
  </Tip>
</AppNote>
