A wireframe is easier to agree on when people can walk through it. On a tab of [wireframe pages](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/), any element can **link** to another screen of the same tab. **Prototype** then plays the screens one at a time in their device frame: click a linked element and you go to its screen. The **Flow View** draws every link as an arrow on the canvas, so you can see all the paths at once while you work on them.

Every ready-made flow on **Start Making Pages** (the [Wireframe templates](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/)) arrives already linked: its next steps (Continue, Sign In, a product, Checkout, the tab bar) go to their screens and its back arrows go back, so you can press **Prototype** on a fresh template and click straight through it.

## Linking a Screen

There are two ways to link an element to another screen. Both work on any element on a wireframe page: a button, a card, a picture, an icon or a line of text.

<Step n={1}>
  **Drag the link handle.** Select one element and a small round **link handle** appears just
  outside its bottom-right corner (on a tab with two screens or more). Drag it onto another screen:
  the screen under the pointer is outlined with its name ("Link to Cart"). Let go to link the
  element to it. Letting go on the element's own screen, off every screen, or pressing `Escape`
  changes nothing.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  **Pick from a menu.** Click the link handle without dragging (or press `Enter` on it), or
  right-click the element and choose **Link Element**. On a wireframe tab it opens on **Screen**:
  every screen of the tab by its name (or "Screen 1", "Screen 2"), the one the element is on marked
  **This screen**, and **Back** at the foot. Pick one and the menu closes.
</Step>

<Figure caption="Dragging the link handle from the Add to Cart button onto the Cart screen, which is outlined with its name.">
  <LinkHandleScene />
</Figure>

- **Back** goes to the screen shown before, like a phone's back button. On the first screen shown it does nothing.
- **Transition**: above the list in the Screen menu, choose how the screen changes: **Instant**, **Slide** (the new screen slides in from the right, and a Back link's from the left) or **Dissolve** (the new screen fades in). Pick the transition first, then the screen.
- A linked element shows a small **link marker** while you edit: a screen with an arrow. Hover it to read where it goes ("Goes to Checkout"), and click it to bring that screen into view.
- To change a link, link the element again. To remove it, choose **Remove Link** in Link Element.

### When a Screen Is Deleted

Deleting a screen leaves the links to it in place, so undoing the delete brings them back. Until then, a link to a deleted screen is **broken**: its marker turns red with a broken arrow and reads "Broken link: its screen was deleted", it does nothing in Prototype mode, and the Flow view marks it. Click the red marker to pick another screen, or remove the link. A broken link never stops the design opening, saving or exporting.

## Playing the Prototype

Press **Prototype** (the play button) in the card at the top right on any wireframe tab. The editor gives way to the prototype: one screen at a time, in its device frame, starting on the screen you were looking at.

- **Click a linked element** to follow its link, with its transition. The pointer turns into a hand over anything clickable.
- **Click anywhere else** and every clickable part of the screen lights up for a moment, so you can see where to go.
- The bar at the top shows the screen's name and its place ("Cart · 2 of 3"). **Previous** and **Next**, or the `←` and `→` keys, step through the screens in the tab's order.
- `Tab` moves between the clickable parts and the bar, and `Enter` or `Space` follows a link.
- **Close** (or `Escape`) returns to the editor, showing the screen you were last on.

<Figure caption="Prototype mode on the Cart screen: the bar with Previous, Next, Copy Prototype Link and Close, and the clickable parts lit after a click elsewhere.">
  <PrototypeModeScene />
</Figure>

Nothing you do in Prototype mode changes the design. It draws the screens live, so a collaborator's edit shows as it lands. Transitions follow **Reduce Motion** in Settings and your system's setting: with either on, every change is instant.

## Sharing a Prototype

**Copy Prototype Link**, in the prototype's bar, copies a link that opens the design straight into Prototype mode.

- If you came in on a share link, it copies that link with the prototype added.
- If you own the design (or can share it), it copies the design's **Viewer** link with the prototype added. With no Viewer link yet, the Share dialog opens with the note "Make a Viewer link, then copy the Prototype link."
- A design kept only in This Browser has no link to share, so the button is not there.

A prototype link opens into Prototype mode only on a wireframe tab; on any other tab it opens as usual. Closing the prototype leaves the visitor in the editor with whatever the link lets them do.

### On a Phone

Open a prototype link on a phone and the screen fills the whole display: the device frame steps aside, so it feels like the real app. The bar becomes a small floating pill at the top with **Previous**, the screen's place, **Next** and **Close**. Tap a linked element to follow it. The pill and the screen stay clear of the notch, the home indicator and the browser's bars, upright or held sideways.

## The Flow View

Press **Flow** in the bottom-right corner, after **Inspect**, to see the prototype's map on the canvas. You can also search for **Show Flow View** in the [search panel](/help/search-panel/the-search-panel/command-palette/) (`⌘K` or `Ctrl+K`). Press **Flow** again to hide it.

- Every linked element is outlined.
- A link to the **screen beside it** curves across the gap into that screen's edge.
- A link to a **screen further along** arcs over the row of screens, drops down the gap beside that screen and enters its side just below the top, so it never looks as if it goes to a screen it passes and never crosses the screen's name or buttons above it.
- A **Back** link is tagged **Back**: where it goes depends on how you got there.
- A **broken** link is outlined in red, with a short dashed line and a **Broken** tag.
- A **Back** or **Broken** tag moves to the element's left when its right would cover an arrow.

<Figure caption="The Flow view: Home's card curving to Product, its Shop button arcing over to Cart, a broken link on Product, and Cart's back button tagged Back.">
  <FlowViewScene />
</Figure>

The arrows follow every edit as it happens. They are only on your screen: they never get in the way of clicking or dragging, they are never part of an export, and nobody else sees them. The Flow view stays on while you work in that browser tab, hides on tabs that are not wireframe tabs and in [Zen mode](/help/tools/zen-mode/), and is off again next time you open the design.

## Who Can Do What

| Role        | Link screens                 | Play the prototype and copy its link | Flow view |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------- |
| Editor      | Any element                  | Yes                                  | Yes       |
| Participant | Only the elements they added | Yes                                  | Yes       |
| Viewer      | No                           | Yes                                  | Yes       |

Someone who cannot link an element still sees its link marker, but no link handle and no way into the menu. See [Sharing](/help/collaboration/sharing/) for what each role can do elsewhere.

## For Agents

AI tools and the command line can read and set links too. A link is an element's `link` field: `{"kind": "page", "pageId": "page-1a2b3c4d", "transition": "slide"}` for a screen, or `{"kind": "back"}`. The page ids are the ones the pages view lists, and a link to a deleted page reads as broken. See [Connect an AI Tool (MCP)](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/) and the [command line](/help/developers/cli/).

  Broken tags. Then send the prototype link so everyone can click through on their own phone.
</Tip>
