## Exporting

Open **Export** as usual (see [Exporting a Tab](/help/tabs/export-tabs/)). A tab offers the formats that suit pages: **PDF**, **PNG** and **SVG**, plus **JSON** to keep a copy of the tab.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  A tab with a logo page also offers the **Logo Kit** (see [Logo Pages](/help/pages/logo-pages/)), and a tab with a photo page the **Photo** card, for **PNG**, **JPEG**, **WebP** or a layered **PSD** (see [Photo Pages](/help/pages/photo-pages/)).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  A tab also offers **Print** and **GIF**, **PowerPoint** for a tab of slides or **Word** for an article. An article also offers **Markdown**, its writing as text you can copy or save as a `.md` file; a page of slides or an infographic is not an outline, so those tabs offer none.
</AppNote>

Pick a format and choose **All pages** or **One page** above the preview:

- **PDF** starts on **All pages**: one PDF with every page in order, each at its own size and orientation. An A4 page comes out at print size, a true A4 page. With **One page** you get a one-page PDF of the page you pick.
- **PNG** and **SVG** start on **One page**, the first by default, and you get that page's image. With **All pages** you get a **.zip** holding one image per page, numbered in page order.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  The files are numbered like `01 Page 1 - 1024 x 1024 - Logo.png`.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The files are numbered like `01 Page 1 - A4 - Portrait - Infographic.png`. The PDF is drawn in real text and shapes, not a flat picture, so you can select, copy and search its words, and it stays sharp however far you zoom in. In a PDF of an article, the entries of its table of contents jump to their pages and the links in its text open their addresses.
</AppNote>

A line under the choice says what the download will be ("3 pages, one PDF.", "A .zip of 3 PNG files, one per page.").

The file is named after the <Noun /> and tab, plus the page's label when you export one page. With All pages, the page list picks which page the preview shows.

Each page exports **exactly its sheet**: its size, its background and pattern, and what is on it, cut off at its edges just as the canvas shows it. The space around the pages is never included. The plain paper exports white. The preview shows the page you picked.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  A [logo page](/help/pages/logo-pages/) is the exception: its PNG and SVG leave the paper transparent.
</AppNote>

The **Background pattern** switch is not offered for pages: a page is its own background.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  **Markdown.** An article's **Markdown** card opens its writing as text: headings, lists, quotes, code, tables, links, bold and italic, and footnotes as `[^1]` with the notes at the end. Press **Copy**, or **Download .md** to save a file; the text is yours to tweak first, and nothing you change there touches the article. Import it into another article with **Import** and it comes back as the same writing.

  Markdown holds text only. Under the text, the panel says how many pictures and other items (charts, shapes, drawings) sit beside the writing and are not in the file, so nothing goes missing without a word. Headers, footers and the article's look stay in the document: **JSON** keeps them. An article of several parts is written in page order, a `\pagebreak` line between them.

  **Print.** **Print** sends pages to your printer without saving a file first, from any kind of page. Choose **Current Page** (with a page list when the tab has more than one) or **Whole Document**, every tab's pages in tab order; a tab still on the start screen is skipped. A line says what will print ("12 pages from 3 tabs."), then **Print** opens your browser's print dialog.

  Each page prints on its own sheet at its own size: an A4 page as A4 portrait, a slide landscape, a card at the card size, with its background and nothing else (no headers, footers or page numbers). It prints exactly as the PDF draws it, kept sharp. Margins, copies and which printer are set in the print dialog. Anyone who can export can print, a viewer included.

  **Animated GIF.** **GIF** makes one animated image that plays anywhere a picture does: a chat message, an email, a README or a social post. Everything that moves on a page moves in the GIF, exactly as it does on the canvas: an animated background mid-motion, and every element animation. A page's animations start from the beginning each time it comes round, as when a slide opens while presenting.

  - **Pages**: **All pages** plays them in order and loops back to the first; **One page** loops that page alone.
  - **Each page holds**: how long every page shows, from 1 to 20 seconds. It starts at the deck's **Auto-Advance** time when that is on, otherwise 3 seconds.
  - **Size**: **Small**, **Medium** or **Large**, the page's longer side at 480, 960 or 1440 pixels.
  - Between pages, the GIF uses the transition and speed set in the **Presenting** settings, so it moves from page to page as your presentation does, including from the last page back to the first.

  The panel shows a still preview of the page and an estimate of the file size, with a note when it is large for chat and email. **Download GIF** makes the file, showing its progress; **Cancel** stops it and nothing downloads. A GIF can run up to 3 minutes in all; past that, shorten the hold or export one page. A page where nothing moves costs a single frame, so a GIF of mostly still pages stays small.

  GIFs are made to move, so your **Reduce Motion** setting never stills one. A video shows its poster, as in a PNG.

  **PowerPoint and Word.** A tab of [slide pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/) offers **PowerPoint**, and an [article](/help/pages/articles/) offers **Word**. Each makes a file you can keep editing: in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides for a deck, in Word or Google Docs for an article. The panel says what the file will hold and what stays editable, with a preview of the first page.

  - **PowerPoint** makes one slide per page, in order, at PowerPoint's widescreen (16:9) or standard (4:3) size. Text stays text boxes with their font, size, colour and alignment, and rectangles, circles, diamonds and the other basic shapes stay shapes with their label (one with an icon, a shadow or the Sketch finish comes as a picture, so it looks the same). Charts, tables, icons, pictures, arrows and anything else come as pictures, exactly as the page shows them, each one on its own so you can move it. Each page's **speaker notes** go into the slide's notes, and a hidden slide stays hidden.
  - **Word** writes the article: titles, headings, quotes and code as Word's own styles, so restyling a heading in Word restyles them all; bulleted, numbered and to-do lists; links, bold, italic and the rest of the formatting. Tables come as Word tables, a [table of contents](/help/pages/articles/contents-headers-footers/) as Word's own table of contents, and the header and footer as Word's, with the page number and page count as Word fields. Pictures, charts and drawings in the article come as images, in line or with the text wrapping beside them as on the page, and page numbers go in the footer when the article has them. The document uses the article's paper size and margins.

  Press **Download PowerPoint** or **Download Word**. A progress bar shows the file being made; **Cancel** stops it and nothing downloads. The file is made on your device, and named after the document and tab (`Report - Tab 1.docx`).

  A font travels by name: an app that has it shows it, and one that doesn't uses a similar font.
</AppNote>

## Importing

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  **Import** takes **JSON** and **SVG** (see [Importing a Tab](/help/tabs/import-tabs/)):

  - **JSON** is a livedesign tab, pages and all. It replaces everything on the tab.
  - **SVG** (a logo, icon or mark) adds to the tab instead: its drawing arrives as shapes you can recolour and reshape, fitted to the page you are on.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  **Import** takes **JSON** (a livedocuments tab, pages and all) on every tab, **Markdown** or **Word**, which become an article, on an article tab or an empty one, and **PowerPoint**, which becomes slide pages, on a slide deck tab or an empty one (see [Importing a Tab](/help/tabs/import-tabs/) and [Markdown Import](/help/tools/markdown-import/)).

  **Turning pages into slides.** Pages are often presented, so the deck is built where the pages are listed: in **Contents**.

  Every slide page is already in the deck, in page order ([Slide Pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/#slides-in-the-deck)). Any other page joins **a page at a time**: open its **⋯** menu in Contents (or right-click its row) and press **Add to Slides**. (Adding slides is for Editors: a Viewer or Participant can't.) A page slide is the page itself:

  - It shows whatever is on the page **now**, so it follows your edits, a new layout, a resize or a reorder without you touching the deck.
  - It is framed to **exactly** the page.
  - Delete the page and the slide stays, empty, so you can see it and fix it.

  Hide page slides, present from one or add notes as in [Building a Deck](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/building-a-deck/).

  **Presenting.** Press **Present** and each page fills the screen on its own: no labels, cogs or buttons around it, and none of its neighbouring pages beside it. Everything else about presenting is as for any deck; see [Presenting](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenting/).
</AppNote>
