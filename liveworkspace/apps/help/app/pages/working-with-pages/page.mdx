Every tab in <AppName /> is a row of **pages**, made for building finished work. Instead of an endless canvas you work on pages, sheets laid out in a row, each one a picture you build up and then export.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Pages build finished work such as a logo, a set of app screens, or an edited photo.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  Pages build finished work such as a poster, a one-pager, a set of social posts, an article or a short deck. Each one is a picture you build up and then export or present.
</AppNote>

## Kinds of Page

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  - **Logo**: a square artboard (1024 x 1024) to design a logo on, with construction guides, mirror drawing, wordmark type, combining shapes and a logo kit export. See [Logo Pages](/help/pages/logo-pages/).
  - **Wireframe**: a screen framed as a device (a phone, a tablet, a laptop, a browser or a watch), to sketch an app or a site on. See [Wireframe Pages](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/).
  - **Photo**: a picture made of layers, edited with brushes, selections, adjustments and filters, its page the photo's own size. See [Photo Pages](/help/pages/photo-pages/).

  A tab's first page asks **What Is This Page For?** while it is the only page and still empty, and your answer is the kind of the whole tab: **every page of a tab is the same kind**. A logo tab holds only logo pages, a wireframe tab only screens (of any devices), a photo tab only photos. For another kind, add a new tab. Until you choose, the page is a **blank page**: a plain A4 sheet, and no page is added after it.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  - **Infographic**: a sheet to lay out freely, starting from a layout if you like. Everything on this page is about infographic pages unless it says otherwise.
  - **Article**: a page to write on, like a document, its writing flowing onto new pages as it grows. See [Writing Articles](/help/pages/articles/).
  - **Slide**: one slide of a deck, always landscape in a slide size (16:9, or the classic 4:3). It starts from its own **slide layouts** (Title Slide, Section Header, Title and Bullets, Two Columns, Image and Text, Statement, Thank You and more), has no portrait to turn to, and is in the slide deck from the moment you make it. See [Slide Pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/).
  - **Flashcards**: a stack of small text cards to speak from, flipped one at a time. See [Flashcards](/help/pages/flashcards/).

  A tab's first page asks **What Is This Page For?** while it is the only page and still empty, and your answer is the kind of the whole tab: **every page of a tab is the same kind**. A slide deck holds only slides, an infographic only infographic pages, an article tab only articles, and a stack only flashcards. For another kind, add a new tab.
</AppNote>

No page is drawn behind it until you choose, and it has no **×**: something has to be picked. **Blank Pages** holds **Blank Slide Deck**, **Blank Article**, **Blank Infographic** and **Blank Flashcards**, each one empty page of that kind with nothing to choose, and the **Project Brief** template, one article page written as a short project brief, is under **Plans** ([Templates](/help/canvas/templates/)). ## Content That Does Not Fit a Page

Open a tab whose content does not fit on its first page (an imported tab, say), and a page is made **around it, where it is**: nothing moves, shrinks or is scaled. The page is A4 when the content fits on it, and otherwise **Fit to Content**, a page exactly the size of the content plus a margin all round. A message says so, and **Undo** takes the page away. Content you later leave off the pages (in the space around them) stays where you put it.

**Split Into Pages** breaks a Fit to Content page into separate pages, from its page panel. Things joined by arrows, or sitting close together, stay together; each group gets a page of its own, A4 when it fits or Fit to Content when it does not, turned portrait or landscape to suit its shape, in reading order (top to bottom, then left to right). Each group moves onto its page at its own size, never scaled. At most 20 pages are made in one go; anything left over shares the last of them.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  - **Add a page** with the **+** after the last page. It adds another page like the last one, of the tab's kind (its tooltip says which, **Add Logo Page**, say). On a wireframe tab, the smaller button under it, **Add a Screen of Another Device**, adds a screen in the device you choose. A new logo or wireframe page **carries over the background** of the page before it (its colour, gradient or picture, and its pattern; a logo page takes the fill without the pattern), so a series keeps its look. It is the new page's own from then on. With no background to carry over, a new page starts on plain paper. A photo page is never painted: it is its picture. A tab holds up to 100 pages.
  - **Each page's label** sits above its top-left corner: its name when it has one (in darker type, so a row of pages reads by name), then its size ("1024 x 1024", the device such as "iPhone", or a photo's pixel size), then its kind ("Logo", "Wireframe", "Photo"). A blank page reads "A4 · Portrait". **Press the label** to fit that page to the screen.
  - **Reorder the pages** by dragging a row in [Contents](#contents), or with **Move page left** and **Move page right** in a page's cog panel. The page moves with everything on it. The label itself does not drag.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  - **Add a page** with the **+** after the last page. It adds another page like the last one, of the tab's kind (a slide on a deck, a card on flashcards). A new infographic page takes the last one's size and orientation, and every new page **carries over the background** of the page before it (its colour, gradient or picture, and its pattern), so a series keeps its look. It is the new page's own from then on. With no background to carry over, a new infographic page starts on a deep gradient in your theme's colours (the Theme Dusk gradient), and the slide of a new slide deck on the light grey Steel gradient; a new article page starts on plain paper. A tab holds up to 100 pages.
  - **Each page's label** sits above its top-left corner: its name when it has one (in darker type, so a row of pages reads by name), then its size ("A4 · Portrait", "Square", "Story (9:16)"), then "Article" or "Slide" on those kinds of page. An infographic page, the plain page, leaves its kind off, so its title bar keeps the room for its buttons. **Press the label** to fit that page to the screen.
  - **Reorder the pages** by dragging a row in [Contents](#contents), or with **Move Left** and **Move Right** in a page's cog panel. The page moves with everything on it. The label itself does not drag.
</AppNote>

- **Content belongs to its page.** Anything whose centre is on a page moves with it when the pages around it change, and whatever hangs off a page's edge is cut off at the edge, on screen and in exports, as if the pages were the only paper.
- **Arrows stay on one page.** An arrow's end pins to an element only when that element is on the same page as the arrow's other end; elements on another page show no connection points, so the end stays free where you drop it.
- **The page navigator** in the bottom-right corner, beside Undo and Redo (once there are two or more pages, while [Contents](#contents) is minimised to its button: the sidebar already shows every page), shows "2 of 5" between **Previous page** and **Next page**, each fitting that page to the screen.
- **The arrow keys** do the same with nothing selected: **Right** or **Down** for the next page, **Left** or **Up** for the previous one.
- **Back where you left off.** Come back to a tab of pages, from another tab or after a reload, and it opens on the page you were last on rather than the first. Each person's place is their own, kept in this browser.
- **Snapping:** move or resize an element and it snaps to its page's edges, centre lines and margins, with the same guides as lining up with other elements.
- **The title bar** above each page holds its buttons, ending in the padlock and the cog: see [Beside the Cog](#beside-the-cog).

- **The view always lands on a page.** Pan and zoom freely, and when you let go the view glides onto the page you are on, never stuck between pages or lost off the end of the row. A short swipe or flick turns to the next or previous page. Zoom out past a whole page and it springs back to fit; zoom in as far as you like and it stays zoomed in, just kept on the page.
- In [Zen mode](/help/tools/zen-mode/), the pages show on their own, without labels, cogs or buttons.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  A short swipe or flick on the view works like a slide deck. In Zen mode, and while presenting, the pages show on their own, without labels, cogs or buttons.
</AppNote>

## Contents

**Contents** lists every page in the tab, each with a picture of the page as it is, its number and its name. The page you are looking at is highlighted.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Photos and pictures show in those thumbnails too.
</AppNote>

On a computer, once a tab has more than one page, Contents sits as a sidebar against the left edge of the canvas and keeps highlighting the page you are on as you move around. Press the cog at its foot, beside **Add Page**, then **Collapse Contents** to tuck it into a **Contents** button in the bottom-right corner, beside the theme button; press that button for the same list, and **Dock as Sidebar** at its top to bring the sidebar back. <AppName /> remembers which you chose, on every <Noun /> and every device you sign in on. You can also choose in **Settings › Editor**, under **Pages**. On a phone, Contents is always the button.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The sidebar works the way a slide editor shows its slides.
</AppNote>

- **Go to a page**: press its row, or move with the arrow keys and press Enter. The canvas glides there. On a computer the list stays open so you can hop around, and the page lands beside the sidebar rather than under it; on a phone it closes so you can see the page.
- **Fit**: **Fit** in the bottom-right corner fits the page you are nearest, beside the sidebar.
- **Page actions**: in the sidebar, rest the pointer on a page and its tools appear down its left, under its number: the grip to drag it, **Delete**, and **⋯** for its menu. In the popover the **⋯** sits under the number and the grip at the row's end, the two appearing together when you rest the pointer on the row, and **Delete** is in the menu. On a touch screen the tools are always there. The menu (a right-click on the row, or a long press on a phone, opens it too) holds **Page Settings**, **Duplicate**, **Move Up**, **Move Down**, **Lock** or **Unlock**, and **Delete** last.
- **Add a page**: at the foot, the button named for what it adds adds another page like the last one, of the tab's kind. The new page lands right after the page you are on (the one the list marks), and the list marks it instead.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  The button reads **Add Logo Page**, **Add Screen** or **Add Photo Page**, and its tooltip names the device on a wireframe tab (**Add iPhone Screen**, say).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The menu also holds the page's place in the slide deck (**Add to Slides**, **Hide in Slides** or **Show in Slides**), after **Page Settings**, and a page hidden in Slides shows a crossed eye beside its name. The add button reads **Add Slide**, **Add Card**, **Add Article**, or **Add Page** on infographic pages.
</AppNote>

Anyone can open Contents to find their way round. Only Editors see the page actions and the add buttons.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  **Stacked or side by side.** Flashcards stand as a **stack**: the page you are on in front, the pages after it fanned out behind it, so the canvas shows one page at a time. Infographic pages and slides stand side by side in a row, and you can stack them too. Going to another page brings it to the front with a quick flip, and the page in front has its own **Previous** and **Next** in its bottom-right corner. On a stack, scrolling sideways moves nothing; scroll up and down to see the rest of a tall page.

  To change it for a tab, press the cog at the foot of **Contents** and switch **Stack Pages** on or off. It works for every kind of page except an article, whose writing runs on from page to page. Undo puts it back, and everyone on the document sees the tab the same way.
</AppNote>

## Beside the Cog

A page's title bar, above its top-right corner, holds the buttons you reach for most, so you rarely need its panel.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  On a wireframe page, The buttons sit together on one small card, in groups with a thin line between them, so the bar reads at a glance: how the page looks first (**Frame**, **Layout** and **Background**; a logo or photo page's own tools), then the page itself, its **padlock** and its **cog**, last at the corner. The label beside the card shows the page's name, when it has one, in darker type than its size and kind.

  - **Frame** opens the page's device, its orientation, the frame's look and its system bars in a popover (hover it to see the device, **Frame: iPhone**, say). See [Wireframe Pages](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/).
  - **Layout** opens the page's layouts in a popover: press a category, hover a layout to preview it on the page, press it to place it. It is there on every wireframe page, empty or not, and placing a layout replaces what is on the page at once (one **Undo** brings it back). See [Wireframe Pages](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/).
  - **Background** opens the page's backgrounds and patterns in a popover, with the same hover previews as the panel (see [Backgrounds and Patterns](#backgrounds-and-patterns)). It stays open for another pick.
  - The **padlock** locks the page ([Locking a Page](#locking-a-page)), and the **cog** opens the page panel.

  While the page is wide enough on screen, each button shows its name beside its icon; zoom out and they drop to their icons (hover one for its name). On a phone the bar is simpler: a wireframe page's **Appearance** holds Frame, Layout and Background (pick one and it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen). A phone shows no Tidy and no padlock (lock or unlock a page from the cog's sheet), and no page label (the page's name is in Contents and in the cog's sheet). A locked page shows no Layout or Background button. Only Editors see these buttons: Participants and Viewers see the pages without them.

  A logo page has its own buttons here instead (**Tidy** for a drawing, **Mirror** and **Guides**, and **Start From a Layout** while it is empty): see [Logo Pages](/help/pages/logo-pages/). A photo page has its **Image**, **Select** and **Filter** menus: see [Photo Pages](/help/pages/photo-pages/). A blank page has the padlock and the cog alone.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  On an infographic or slide page, The buttons sit together on one small card, in groups with a thin line between them, so the bar reads at a glance: how the page looks first (**Style**, **Layout** and **Background**), then tools (**Notes** on a slide, **Tidy**), then the page itself, its **padlock** and its **cog**, last at the corner. The label beside the card shows the page's name, when it has one, in darker type than its size and kind.

  - **Style** opens the tab's **Tab Style** dialog, the look of the whole tab: its theme, finish, fonts and canvas ([Changing the Theme](/help/canvas/themes/changing-theme/)).
  - **Layout** opens the page's layouts in a popover: press a category, hover a layout to preview it on the page, press it to place it. It is there on every page, empty or not, and placing a layout replaces what is on the page at once (one **Undo** brings it back). A slide page offers the [slide layouts](/help/pages/slide-pages/#slide-layouts). See [Infographic Layouts](/help/pages/infographic-layouts/).
  - **Tidy** (the broom, among the tools, on infographic and slide pages only while a press would visibly move something) straightens the page in one press: it nudges anything poking past the margins back inside, lines up edges that nearly line up, and evens out gaps that are nearly even. It keeps your layout and your sizes, never moves anything far, never makes things overlap, and leaves locked and turned elements where they are. Hover it to see the tidied page first; a page that is already tidy says so and nothing changes.
  - **Background** opens the page's backgrounds and patterns in a popover, with the same hover previews as the panel (see [Backgrounds and Patterns](#backgrounds-and-patterns)). It stays open for another pick.
  - The **padlock** locks the page ([Locking a Page](#locking-a-page)), and the **cog** opens the page panel.

  While the page is wide enough on screen, each button shows its name beside its icon; zoom out and they drop to their icons (hover one for its name). On a phone the bar is simpler: **Appearance** holds Style, Layout, Background and an article's Text (pick one and it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen), then Notes on a slide, then the cog. A phone shows no Tidy and no padlock (lock or unlock a page from the cog's sheet), and no page label (the page's name is in Contents and in the cog's sheet). A locked page shows no Layout, Tidy or Background button. Only Editors see these buttons: Participants and Viewers see the pages without them.

  An article page and a card have **Style** and **Text** in place of Layout, and the same **Background** button beside the cog. An article's **Style** leads with **Tab Style**, so a page never shows two Style buttons.
</AppNote>

## The Page Panel

Press the cog above a page's top-right corner to open its panel, which opens beside the page so the page stays in view (on a phone it slides up from the bottom of the screen). At the top is the page's **name** (leave it empty to go back to "Page 2").

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Below the name, what the page offers:

  - A **logo** page: the **Background** and **Layouts** tabs, with no size to choose (see [Logo Pages](/help/pages/logo-pages/)).
  - A **wireframe** page: its **Device**, the frame's look, its orientation and its system bars. Its layouts and background are on the **Layout** and **Background** buttons beside the cog (see [Wireframe Pages](/help/pages/wireframe-pages/)).
  - A **photo** page: its name and the page actions alone (its size is in the photo's **Image** menu, see [Photo Pages](/help/pages/photo-pages/)).
  - A **blank** page: its orientation, until you choose what it is for.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  An article page or a card with no name of its own goes by its title, so writing a card's title names it. Below it, the page's size and orientation, where it has a choice of them. Everything else is a button beside the cog: **Style** and **Layout** on an infographic or slide page, **Style** and **Text** on an article page or a card (see [Writing Articles](/help/pages/articles/)), and **Background** on every page.
</AppNote>

### Size and Orientation

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Each kind of page has its own size: a logo page is always its 1024 x 1024 artboard, a wireframe page is its device's screen, and a photo page is its picture's size (change it from the photo's **Image** menu). A blank page is A4, and a page made around content that did not fit is **Fit to Content** (see [Content That Does Not Fit a Page](#content-that-does-not-fit-a-page)).

  **Portrait** and **Landscape** turn a blank page or a Fit to Content page's sheet; a wireframe page turns from its **Frame** button (or the cog panel's **Device**). Turning a page keeps everything on it: content that still fits stays as it is, and content that no longer fits is scaled down as one to fit inside the margins, its text with it (the words inside shapes too, so a button's label still fits its button). Nothing is cut off.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  The page's settings start with the **Size** tiles, each drawn to scale:

  | Size   | For                                                         |
  | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
  | A4     | Print, the default                                          |
  | Letter | US Letter, for print in North America                       |
  | A3     | Posters                                                     |
  | Square | Square social posts                                         |
  | Post   | Portrait posts for Instagram and LinkedIn (4:5)             |
  | Story  | Stories (9:16); turned landscape it reads Widescreen (16:9) |
  | Fit    | Fit to Content: sized around the content it was made for    |

  **Fit** shows only on a page already in it, a page made around content that did not fit (see [Content That Does Not Fit a Page](#content-that-does-not-fit-a-page)); choose another size and it is gone for good. Slide sizes belong to slide pages: a deck is made of [slide pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/), which offer only **Slide** (16:9) and **Classic** (4:3), and no orientation. (An infographic page made before this, still on the Slide size, keeps that tile until you move it off.)

  Below them, **Portrait** and **Landscape** turn the page (a square page, a slide size and Fit to Content have no orientation). Changing a page's size or orientation keeps everything on it: content that still fits stays as it is, and content that no longer fits is scaled down as one to fit inside the margins, its text with it (the words in a shape shrink with the shape, so a button's label still fits its button). Nothing is cut off.
</AppNote>

### Backgrounds and Patterns

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Logo and wireframe pages have the same backgrounds, sorted into **Solid**, **Gradient** and **Image** at the top of the backgrounds (a logo page has no Image); they open on the kind the page has now. A wireframe page keeps them on the **Background** button beside its cog; a logo page has the **Background** tab. A photo page has no background: it is its picture.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  Every kind of page has the same backgrounds, sorted into **Solid**, **Gradient** and **Image** (and **Animated** on a slide or a card) at the top of the backgrounds; they open on the kind the page has now. Every page keeps them on the **Background** button beside its cog.
</AppNote>

- **Solid**: **Paper** (the plain page) leading your tab theme's colours, then a row of light colours and a row of dark ones (the same colours every colour picker in <AppName /> offers), the colours already used in the design, and **Add a custom colour** (four coloured dots) for a **custom colour** of your own.
- **Gradient**: a **Theme** group of fourteen gradients drawn from your tab's theme colours, light ones first, then bold and deep ones (change the tab's theme and these follow it), then a **Gradients** group of thirteen presets, from Sunrise and Peach to Night, Forest and Ember, plus the rainbow swatch for a **custom gradient**: pick its **From** and **To** colours from the same colours, turn its **Angle**, or **Swap** the two.
- **Image**: a photo covering the whole page. Choose one from your images, or **Upload an Image** of your own. Over the picture, an **Overlay** keeps your content readable: **None** leaves it bare, **Light** or **Dark** washes it, **Tint** washes it in your theme's colour, and **Fade** fades it to white towards the bottom; **Strength** sets how much, and a new picture starts with a 60% wash. **Blur** softens the picture behind your content, from **Off** to 100%, so words over a busy photo stay easy to read; it is kept in every export. **Position** chooses which part of a tall picture stays in view (**Top**, **Centre** or **Bottom**). To change it, choose or upload again under **Replace Image**; **Remove** puts the page back on plain paper.
- **Pattern**, over any of them: **None**, **Dots**, **Grid** or **Lines**, drawn faintly so they never fight the content.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  - **Animated**, on a slide or a flashcard only: moving backgrounds in your theme's colours (see [Slide Pages](/help/pages/slide-pages/)).
  - **Apply to All**, at the foot, gives every other page of the tab this background and pattern in one step (**Apply to All Slides**, **Apply to All Cards** and so on). A locked page keeps its own. It shows only while there is another page to reach.
</AppNote>

**Hover a swatch to preview it** on the page; press it to keep it. A dark background keeps the page readable: elements without colours of their own are drawn in light ink on it, and text, arrows and icons you coloured yourself are lightened if they would no longer stand out (or darkened again on a light page). On a bright or mid-tone colour, such as teal or yellow, icons, progress bars and outlines are lightened or darkened the same way, keeping their hue, and charts adjust their colours as they are drawn.

### Page Actions

Along the bottom of the panel: **Split Into Pages** (on a Fit to Content page only, see above), **Duplicate page** (a copy right after it, with everything on it), **Move page left**, **Move page right** and **Delete page** (the page and everything on it; the pages after it close the gap, and the view glides to the page before it so you are never left looking at empty canvas). Each one is a single step that **Undo** reverses.

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  On an article page the same four act on the whole article.
</AppNote>

## Locking a Page

Press the padlock beside a page's cog to **lock** it; it reads **Locked** while it is. A locked page, of any kind, stays as it is:

- What is on it can't be selected, moved, changed or deleted, and nothing new can be drawn, dropped or pasted onto it (a short notice says why).
- Its name, size, orientation, background and layout can't change, and it can't be deleted. You can still move it along the row or duplicate it (the copy is unlocked).

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  - Locking a page of an article makes the article's writing read-only.
</AppNote>

Press the padlock again to unlock it. The lock is part of the <Noun />, so everyone you share it with sees the page locked too.
