Select something and `⌘C` copies it, `⌘X` cuts it, `⌘V` pastes it. (On Windows and Linux, Ctrl.) It is the clipboard you already know, and it behaves the way you would expect it to.

## It is the real clipboard

The elements go on your **operating system's clipboard**, not a private one inside the page. That is what makes the useful cases work:

- paste into **another tab** of the same <Noun />,
- paste into a **different <Noun />** entirely,
- paste into a **second browser window** you have the editor open in,
- copy, reload the page, and paste what you copied before the reload.

  A very large copy (over 2,000 elements) is too big for the system clipboard: it pastes only in the window you copied it in, and a message says so.
</AppNote>

## What paste does

The pasted elements land **offset from the original's position** (a little down and to the right), not exactly on top of it, so you can see what arrived and drag it where it belongs. That holds in another tab or <Noun /> too, so if the copy isn't in view, it is where the original sat. They come in selected, ready to be moved as one.

Without a keyboard, **Paste** is also the first row under the icon toolbar when you right-click (or long-press) empty canvas, and an icon in a tab's menu. See [Context Menus](/help/user-interface/context-menus/). The menu's Paste uses what you copied in this window; something copied in another window or before a reload pastes with `⌘V`.

**Cut is copy plus delete**: the originals go as soon as you cut, and an undo puts them back where they were.

<AppNote app="livedesign,livediagram,livedocuments">
  Locked elements are copied but stay where they are.
</AppNote>

Copy, cut and paste are for editors. On a view-only or Participant [share link](/help/collaboration/sharing/) they do nothing, and a paste onto a tab you cannot edit does nothing either.

## Pasting a screenshot

<AppNote app="livedesign,livedocuments">
  If what you have on the clipboard is an **image** (a screenshot, or an image copied from a web page) rather than elements, `⌘V` drops it on the page as an [image element](/help/palette/tools/images/) and uploads it to your gallery. In a [<Noun /> in This Browser](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) it is embedded locally instead, so no copy leaves your machine.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  If what you have on the clipboard is an **image** (a screenshot, or an image copied from a web page) rather than elements, `⌘V` drops it on the canvas as an [image element](/help/palette/tools/images/) and uploads it to your gallery. In a [canvas in This Browser](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/) it is embedded locally instead, so no copy leaves your machine.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  If what you have on the clipboard is a **picture** (a screenshot, or an image copied from a web page) rather than elements, `⌘V` uploads it to your image gallery and adds it as an image in the middle of the screen. Dropping an image file on the canvas does the same where you let go. Any other file is turned away with "The canvas takes pictures, not other files.", except a <AppName /> export dropped on the canvas, which points you at **Import** in the tab menu. See [Write Elements](/help/palette/write/).
</AppNote>

Anything else on the clipboard is left alone. Pasting ordinary text into a label being edited types the text, as it should; ordinary text pasted onto the canvas itself does nothing.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Copied from [Excalidraw](/help/tabs/import-tabs/)? An Excalidraw copy (or a `.excalidraw` file) pastes as a scene of <AppName /> elements, in one undo step.
</AppNote>

## When you just want another one

For a copy of what you already have, on the same tab, two things are quicker than the clipboard:

- **`⌘D`** duplicates the selection, offset from the original.
- **Shift and drag** a selection drops a copy where you release it and leaves the originals put. See [Duplicating Elements](/help/tips-and-tricks/duplicating-elements/).
