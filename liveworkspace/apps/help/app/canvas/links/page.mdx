Links connect your <Noun /> to the rest of your work. Shapes, text, sticky notes, images and tables can all become clickable jump points, so a <Noun /> can point at the things it describes instead of just naming them.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Drawings and annotations can become jump points too, and a dedicated link card turns a URL into a
  tidy bookmark with a title, favicon, and preview image.
</AppNote>

## Linking an Element

Give a shape, text, sticky note, image or table a link (arrows and lines take none), and it gains a small "Follow link" badge whose hover card names the target. A link can point at:

- **A tab** in the same <Noun />, or **a specific element** on another tab.
- **Another <Noun />** entirely, or **a file from another product**.
- **An external URL**, which opens in a new browser tab.

To add one, right-click the element, open **Resources** at the foot of its menu and press **Add Link** (it reads **Edit Link** once there is one). The **Link** screen lists where it can go:

- **External URL**: type a **Web address** and press **Save Link** (or Enter). Leave off the `https://` and it is added for you.
- **Tab**: pick one of the <Noun />'s tabs. The one you are on is marked **This tab**.
- **Files**: pick one of your recent files, from this app's own list first (your other <Noun plural />), then one list for each other product. Each list has a search box.

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  On a wireframe tab, a link can also go to a screen, for Prototype mode. Select the element and
  drag the small round link handle at its bottom-right corner onto another screen, or press the
  handle to open the **Link element** dialog on **Screen**, listed first among its choices. There,
  pick the screen that pressing the element goes to, or **Back** for the screen shown before, and
  choose **Instant**, **Slide** or **Dissolve** above the list for the transition. The screen the
  element sits on is marked **This screen** and can't be picked. The handle shows once the tab has
  two or more screens.
</AppNote>

To change a link, open the same screen again and pick a new target; **Remove Link**, last on the screen, takes the link off. The link follows the element if you move it, and like any edit it can be undone. For more on tab and element targets, see [Linking Across Tabs](/help/tabs/linking-tabs/).

<Note>
  For safety, only `http`, `https`, and `mailto` addresses are allowed as external links. Other URL
  schemes are blocked.
</Note>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  **Link cards.**

A **link card** is a rectangular element that turns a URL into a rich preview: the page's favicon, its title, the address under it, and, when available, its preview image. It is the right choice when the link is the content, such as a reference doc, a ticket, or a related page you want visible on the canvas.

<Step n={1}>
  In [Workshop mode](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/), open the palette's **Session** category and pick
  **Link Card**, under **Navigate**; in Diagram mode, press `S` and search for it. Click the canvas
  to drop it at its default size, or drag to draw it the size you want.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  The empty card prompts you to add a link. Double-click it to open the link dialog and paste in a
  URL.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  The card fills in with the page's title, favicon, and preview image automatically. Clicking the
  row along the bottom of the card, with its title, follows the link.
</Step>

A link card recolours, moves, locks, layers, and resizes like any other element. To learn how the preview is fetched and what gets shown, see [Link Cards](/help/canvas/links/link-cards/).

**Choosing between them.**

Both rely on the same kind of URL, so the choice is about emphasis:

- Use an **element link** when an existing shape or node should also be clickable.
- Use a **link card** when the bookmark itself is what you want people to see on the canvas.

  <Tip>
    Drop a link card next to the part of the diagram it supports, so a reader can jump straight from
    the picture to the source without hunting for it.
  </Tip>
</AppNote>
