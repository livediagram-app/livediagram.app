Most themes paint every element one colour. A multicolour theme is different: it carries a palette of several colours and spreads them across the tab, so colour helps you tell one part from another.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  Each branch of your hierarchy gets a different colour, the way a rainbow mind map tints each main branch a distinct hue. They suit mind maps, org charts and any picture where colour helps you tell one limb from another.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Each element on your board gets the next colour in turn, so a board of scattered notes and boxes gets a spread of hues rather than collapsing to one. They suit brainstorms and any board where colour helps you tell one thing from another.
</AppNote>

## What They Are

A multicolour theme holds an ordered palette of colours plus a neutral trunk colour. The backdrop (the canvas background and pattern) works exactly as it does for any other theme; only the element colours change. When there are more branches or elements than colours, the palette simply repeats rather than running out.

<AppName /> ships three multicolour themes in the [Tab Style dialog](/help/canvas/themes/changing-theme/): a saturated **Rainbow** spectrum, a soft **Pastel** version and a bright, summery **Tropical** palette.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livehuddle">
  A tab saved on the retired Autumn or Jewel themes opens on Tropical or Rainbow, with any colours you picked yourself kept.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  A tab saved on the retired Autumn or Jewel themes opens on Default, with its stored colours drawn as they were.
</AppNote>

## How the Colours Are Decided

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  The branches come from your arrows. An arrow whose ends are both pinned to elements defines a parent-to-child link. The roots of the hierarchy (the centre of a mind map, or the top of an org chart) take the trunk colour, and each of a root's direct children starts a fresh branch hue that flows down the whole subtree beneath it, so a top-level limb and all of its sub-topics share one colour.

  Elements that no arrow touches each pick up the next colour in turn, so even a flat <Noun /> of scattered shapes gets a spread of hues. Shapes caught in a loop with no root (A to B to C and back to A) take the trunk colour, since a loop has no natural limb. Arrows take the colour of the branch they feed into, so each connector matches its limb.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  When you pick a multicolour theme, each element on the tab takes the next colour of the palette in turn. Sticky notes and images keep their own colours, as they do under every theme, and a colour you set by hand is kept.
</AppNote>

## Adding to a Multicolour Tab

A brand-new element you drop on a multicolour tab takes the neutral trunk colour. To give it a hue, paint a neighbour's look onto it with the [Format Painter](/help/tips-and-tricks/format-painter/). Re-picking the theme does not do it: by then the trunk colour counts as one you chose, so it is kept. This is deliberate: <AppName /> never silently re-colours the whole tab every time you add something, which would fight any hand-colouring you have done.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  A new element is not part of the hierarchy until an arrow connects it in; paint it once it is.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  A mind-map node you add from the keyboard is the exception: it copies its sibling's look, so it arrives in its branch colour.
</AppNote>

<Tip>

</Tip>
