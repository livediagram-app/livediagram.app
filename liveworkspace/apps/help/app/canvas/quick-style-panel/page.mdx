## Colours That Follow the Theme

The seven colours come from the tab's theme. A multi-colour theme offers its own six branch colours; any other theme offers red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet in its own tone, so a muted theme gets muted colours. Backgrounds always keep the label readable, and text colours are always dark enough (or, on a dark canvas, light enough) to read.

Switch the theme later and colours picked here move with it: a shape you made green stays green, in the new theme's green.

**Ink** is different: it is the same drawing colour on every theme, dark on a light canvas and light on a dark one.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  In Draw mode the Stroke and Text colour rows offer Draw mode's stock colours instead of the
  theme's.
</AppNote>

## Your Own Colours

Right-click any of the six theme colours in a row (or focus it and press **Shift+F10** or the context-menu key) to open the custom colour picker. Press **Use** and the colour is saved into that swatch, replacing the theme's colour there, and a small dot in its corner marks it as yours. Screen readers and the tooltip name it too, for example "Custom orange, in place of Green".

Picking a colour only changes the swatch; click it afterwards to use it. **Clear override** in the same popover brings the theme's colour back, and anything you already painted keeps its colour. Your swatches belong to you and to the theme you made them in: they follow you to other <Noun plural /> and devices, and switching to another theme shows that theme's own colours (and any you changed there). The first swatch, the theme default, always stays the theme's, and so does Ink. A custom colour does not follow later theme changes. For colours a whole <Noun /> should share, pick a [theme](/help/canvas/themes/).

## Styling Many at Once

Select several shapes and arrows, even of different kinds, and a choice applies to every one that supports it. A highlighted option means every selected element shares it; when they differ, nothing is highlighted. Locked elements are left alone. Each choice is one undo step.

## The Next Shape Remembers

When you change a shape's colours, width, style or alignment, from Quick Style or from the right-click menu, the next shape **of the same kind** you draw arrives the same way. Make a circle green and the next circle is green; the next square is not. Arrows remember their own style, separately from shapes, and text elements remember their colour. Picking the theme default again forgets that choice.

This only applies to shapes and arrows you draw yourself, from the palette, a tool or a quick-connect plus. Pasted, duplicated and templated elements keep their own looks. The memory belongs to the <Noun /> and stays in this browser.

## Turning It Off

Open **Settings**, then **Panels**, **Quick Style**, and switch off its enable switch. Every style is still in the element's right-click menu, and the next shape still remembers its style. See [Settings](/help/user-interface/settings/).

<AppNote app="livediagram">The switch reads **Enable Quick Style Panel**.</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign,livedocuments">The switch reads **Enable Quick Style Bar**.</AppNote>

## Good to Know

- It hides while a right-click menu is open.
- Every option has a name for screen readers; each row of choices is a group you move through with the arrow keys.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  It never covers the [palette](/help/palette/) or other panels: with a panel already on the left
  edge it moves below, above or beside it. It also stands aside in zen mode, while presenting, for
  Viewers and Participants, and on a locked tab. It is a compact strip, without a panel header. The
  options drawn as a glyph (not a colour) show their name in a tooltip too.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  It also stands aside in zen mode, in split view, for Viewers and Participants, and on a locked
  tab. Each icon-only option has a tooltip.
</AppNote>
