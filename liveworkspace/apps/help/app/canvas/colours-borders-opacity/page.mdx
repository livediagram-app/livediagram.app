Every element starts in your tab's [theme](/help/canvas/themes/) colours, so a new <Noun /> already looks coherent. When one element needs to stand apart (a warning in red, a dashed outline for something planned, a faded background note), its colours, border and opacity are all in its right-click menu. Open the menu by right-clicking the element (a long press on a touch screen). Colours and Border live under **Appearance**, which opens beside the menu; Opacity lives in the menu's first section.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">

<AppNote app="livehuddle">

## Colours

Open **Appearance**, then **Colours**. Each row names the surface it paints, shows the colour in force on its right, and opens the colour picker beside the menu:

- **Text** colours the element's label (all of it; to colour only some words, see [Formatting Label Text](/help/canvas/text-and-fonts/formatting-label-text/)).
- **Background** fills the element.
- **Border** colours its outline.
- **Heading** appears on a table, for its header row.
- **Icon** replaces Border on a line-art icon, because its stroke is the glyph itself.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  **Heading** also appears on a lane, for the lane's title strip.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  A [Technology](/help/palette/technology/) icon keeps its brand colours, so it has no Icon row. An arrow's Colours category has **Line** and **Pointer** instead, so the arrowheads can differ from the line ([Arrows](/help/palette/arrows/)).
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  Only a line-art icon a Space already holds has an Icon row; a full-colour technology icon keeps its brand colours, so it has none.
</AppNote>

Under the rows, **Reset to Theme** puts the element's colours back to the tab theme's, as one step you can undo. It only touches colours: borders, shadows and fonts stay as they are.

### The Colour Picker

Every colour row opens the same picker, the one used everywhere in the editor. From the top:

- **No colour first.** The first swatch, a white square with a slash ("No border colour", "No background colour"), clears the colour: a shape shows the canvas through it, or a border simply disappears.
- **Theme Palette**: the active tab's theme colours, spun into light, base and dark steps plus a neutral ramp, each named by a colour word ("Blue", "Light blue", "Deep blue"). Switch themes and this group follows.
- **Standard Colours**: ten colours, the same everywhere. Text, Border and Icon rows get the strong tone (Ink, Grey, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Teal, Blue, Violet, Pink); Background and Heading rows get the soft tone, washes of the same hues with White in Ink's place.
- **Custom Colours**: colours anyone has picked with **Add a custom colour** in this <Noun />, newest first, shared with everyone on it. **Add a custom colour** (four coloured dots) opens an editor with a saturation square, a hue slider, a hex field and, in browsers that support it, an eyedropper to pick a colour from anywhere on screen. Press **Use** to apply it.

With a mouse, hovering a swatch previews it on the element; clicking commits it and closes the picker. The keyboard works too: arrow keys move between swatches, **Enter** picks and **Escape** closes. On a text row, the custom colour editor warns you when a colour would be hard to read on a light or dark canvas, and offers a readable version beside the warning.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  Line and Pointer rows (on arrows) get the strong tone too, and the same warning shows on a line row.

  **Reading where it sits.** Every text colour picker (an element's **Text** row, a table cell's text and the other text rows) checks the colour against what the text sits on: the canvas or page, the shape's fill, or its cell. While you hover a swatch it judges that one, otherwise the colour in force. A colour that reads well shows its contrast quietly. One that falls short says **Hard to read** and by how much, such as "1.9:1, aim for 4.5:1" (3:1 for large text), with **Use** beside it: press it for the nearest shade of the same hue that does read. It is only a hint, never a block: you can keep the colour you picked.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  The same warning shows on a border row.
</AppNote>

### Ink and Colours That Adapt

**Ink** is the first strong standard colour: near-black on a light canvas, near-white on a dark one. A strong standard colour you pick for a shape's text or border, a text element or a sticky note's text is stored by its name, so it is drawn in its version for the canvas you see it on and stays readable when a tab or a reader switches between light and dark. Text with no colour of its own is drawn in Ink. Any other colour is kept exactly as picked.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  The same goes for a strong standard colour you pick for an arrow or a pen stroke.
</AppNote>


## Borders

Open **Appearance**, then **Border**. Grids of small preview tiles set the outline, and hovering a tile previews it before you click:

- **Strength**: no border (a faint dashed outline), then thin, medium, thick and extra thick. New shapes start at medium.
- **Pattern**: solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, long dash and dash-dot-dot.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  Tables start at thin. A third grid, **Radius**, sets the corners: from none through small (4 px), medium (12 px) and large (24 px) to full. Full rounds the shorter sides completely, giving a pill on a long box and a circle on a square.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  **Accent Edge.** Under the Radius grid, **Accent Edge** puts a coloured strip down the left side, or across the top or bottom, of a rectangle, a stadium or a [card](/help/palette/cards-and-callouts/): a way to colour-code nodes by team or status without filling them. **Edge Colour** picks its colour, and **Match Border** sets it back to the border's colour. It is drawn inside the shape's corners, and the format painter copies it with the border. A corner is never rounded more than a quarter of the box's shorter side, so a small rounded square stays a rounded square instead of turning into a circle (Full is the exception, by design). Unset, a shape's corners are slightly rounded and the Radius grid highlights small.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Accent Edge takes a mind node too, and a mind node's corners start at medium.
</AppNote>

### Which Elements Have a Border

- **Strength and Pattern** are offered on shapes.
- **No Border category** on text elements, sticky notes (a note's **Border** colour row still draws an edge), images, stickers, icons, and the elements that draw their own faces.

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  Strength and Pattern are also offered on pen strokes, paths and tables. **Radius** is offered only where a corner can be rounded: the rectangle, the browser frame, cards (callouts included), boundaries and mind nodes. Circles, diamonds, hexagons and the other outlined shapes have their corners built in. The actor has no Border category either, and the elements that draw their own faces are: charts, progress bars and rings, ratings, timeline rails, code blocks, checklists, legends, portals, reveals, chairs, quizzes, and the banner, header bar, stat row and process components.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedesign">
  Strength and Pattern are also offered on pen strokes, paths and tables. **Radius** is offered only where a corner can be rounded: the rectangle, the browser frame, and cards (callouts included). Circles, diamonds, hexagons and the other outlined shapes have their corners built in. The elements that draw their own faces are: charts, progress bars and rings, ratings, timeline rails, code blocks, checklists, legends, and the banner, header bar, stat row and process components.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livedocuments">
  Strength and Pattern are also offered on freehand strokes, paths and tables. **Radius** is offered only where a corner can be rounded: the rectangle, the browser frame, and cards (callouts included). Circles, diamonds, hexagons and the other outlined shapes have their corners built in. The elements that draw their own faces are: charts, progress bars and rings, ratings, timeline rails, code blocks, checklists, legends, and the banner, header bar, stat row and process components.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  The elements that draw their own faces are portals, chairs and the other Space pieces. **Elements livehuddle no longer has** (anything a space saved before they were dropped) show as a placeholder that keeps its look as it was saved, with no style controls; an Editor can delete it.
</AppNote>

A few elements skip Colours too: images and stickers keep their own colours.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  A code block picks a colour scheme under **Presets** instead, and charts are coloured from their own data.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livediagram">
  A note on an [event storming board](/help/canvas/event-storming-boards/) has a short menu of its own with no Appearance at all, since its colour is what it means.
</AppNote>


## Opacity

Open the first section of the menu and drag the **Opacity** slider from 100% (solid) down to 0% (invisible). It fades the whole element, its fill, border, text and shadow together, and the canvas shows it live as you drag. A whole drag is one undo step. Opacity sits beside **Bring to Front** and **Send to Back**, since fading and stacking are both about how an element sits among its neighbours. Exports and snapshots show the same opacity you see.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  See [Layer Order](/help/canvas/layer-order/) for stacking.
</AppNote>

<AppNote app="livehuddle">
  **Bring to Front** moves the element to the end of the tab's order so it paints above everything else, and **Send to Back** moves it to the start so everything else paints over it (**Cmd+Shift+]** and **Cmd+Shift+[** do the same from the keyboard; Ctrl in place of Cmd on Windows and Linux). Newly added elements land on top.
</AppNote>


## Styling Several Elements at Once

Select several elements and open the menu: its **Appearance** section has **Colours** and **Border** too, and its first section has **Opacity**.

- Each row and grid applies to **every selected element that supports it**: a background colour skips text elements, and a border strength skips sticky notes.
- The values shown are read from the first element that has them.
- **Colours** shows **Text**, **Background** and **Border** as the selection allows.
- **Opacity** sets every selected element to the same value.
- **Reset to Theme** resets every selected element's colours in one step.

<AppNote app="livediagram,livedesign,livedocuments">
  A selection of only arrows shows **Line** instead of Border in **Colours**, and on a mixed selection the Border colour recolours the arrows too. **Radius** appears when at least one selected element can round its corners, and sets those that can.
</AppNote>


<Tip>
  Use colour for meaning, not decoration. Keep most elements in the theme's colours and give one
  kind of thing (risks, decisions, external systems) one colour, and a reader learns the code at a
  glance. **Reset to Theme** is the quick way back when a <Noun /> gets too colourful.
</Tip>
