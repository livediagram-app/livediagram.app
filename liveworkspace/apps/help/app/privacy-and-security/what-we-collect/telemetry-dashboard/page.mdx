The [telemetry dashboard](/telemetry) is a public page, headed **Telemetry, in the open**, that shows everything <AppName /> measures. It lives at liveworkspace.app, one dashboard for every liveworkspace app: the link opens it with the app's own address picked, and **All apps** shows every app's events together. There is no sign-in and nothing hidden behind it: the page reads the same anonymous counts the project itself uses to decide what to build next. This article walks through what it shows and how to read it. For what an event is and how to switch it off, see [What We Collect](/help/privacy-and-security/what-we-collect/).

## What the Numbers Are Made Of

Every number on the dashboard is a count of small events, each made of just three fields: a category, an action and an optional preset type, such as `Element`, `Added`, `Square`. No event carries a user id, an IP address, a <Noun /> or anything you typed, so the dashboard can only ever show totals, never a person or a path through the product.

<AppNote app="liveplanner">
  In liveplanner the example type reads `Plan`, `Created`, `DashboardTab`.
</AppNote>

Events are deleted automatically after **60 days**. The dashboard's longest window is 30 days, so what you see is recent trend data rather than a permanent archive.

Most events come from browsers that have telemetry switched on. A few are counted by the server itself, because only the server can see them once: a sign-up or sign-in, a share-link visitor's first visit to a <Noun />, a <Noun /> used by several people at once, an email sent, and a server error. They are the same three anonymous fields, and they appear in the same charts.

The counts are refreshed a few minutes at a time, so a brand-new event can take a short while to show.

## Choosing a Window

At the top, three cards set the time window for the whole page: **Today**, **Last 7 days** and **Last 30 days**, each with its total number of events. The page opens on **Last 7 days**. Pick a card and every view below counts over that window.

The windows are whole calendar days in UTC: **Today** is since midnight UTC, **Last 7 days** is today and the six days before, and **Last 30 days** is today and the 29 days before. Under the cards, **Daily Volume, Last 30 Days (UTC)** draws one line across the whole month, with the span of the chosen window highlighted, so a card's total always matches its highlighted stretch of the line.

## The Views

The dashboard is one page for every app, so its views are one component: pick an app with the chips under the heading (**All apps**, or one app) and the same row of tabs appears over that app's own numbers. **Dashboard** and **Search** stand on their own at the left; the other views are a closer look at one area, grouped in a bar titled **Detail** that scrolls sideways when it does not fit. Picking another app keeps the window you chose.

- **Dashboard**: the overview, and where the page opens. It leads with **At a glance**, then groups a fixed list of charts by the question they answer: Visitors, Content, Features, Collaboration, Connections, and Health & support. Each card shows the window's count with a line for the last 30 days, and a stack opens into the cards it combines. A stack's **See also** link jumps to the Detail section that breaks it down further.
- **Features**: what people build with, in sections: **Editing** (the tools that organise work, then formatting controls, export formats and dialogs), **Palette** (what people add, most to least used), **Modes** (the modes people switch into and the options they set inside them), **Look & Feel** (which templates and themes get picked), **Settings** (how often each setting is changed, and which Settings categories people open), the app's people tools (see below) and **Connections** (API tokens and the changes AI tools make).
- **Pages & Help**: how people find their way. **Pages** (the most-viewed pages across every app, and each app's own top ten; ids never leave the browser, so every <Noun /> counts as one page), **Help** (help centre reads and article feedback, and the articles opened from inside the editor) and **Tours & Prompts** (how far people get through each guided tour, and how they answer the one-off prompts).
- **Community**: only for an app that has a Community page, where it shows what people do with what they share there and why posts get reported.
- **Health**: whether it all works and is fast. **Exceptions** (what failed and where, counted only as a short code such as a status and the request or area, never an error message or stack; an empty section here is the goal) and **Timings** (see below).
- **Search**: find any one metric, by typing or by clicking through a word cloud of categories, actions and types, and see just that metric charted over 30 days.

**All apps** is different: the apps keep their own vocabularies, so it shows only what they share. **Apps Compared** sets each app against the others on events, visitors and sign-ups, then come the Project Kits liveworkspace.app starts and the **Busiest Events**, added up across apps.

## Timings and Core Web Vitals

Browsers never send an exact time, only the range a timing fell in (such as "under one second"). So every number on the **Timings** view is an estimate within its range.

The view opens with **Editor Timings**, one card each for **Load** (opening a <Noun />), **Warm Paint** (reopening a <Noun /> this browser has opened before, until its last seen copy is on screen), **Tab Switch**, **Save**, **Live Connect** and **Live Reconnect**. Each card leads with the **p75** (three timings in four were at least this quick), shows the **Median** and **p95** beside it, draws how many timings fell in each range, and plots each day's p75 across the last 30 days. A card with fewer than 20 timings in the window says it has too few to read a percentile, rather than showing a misleading number.

The Timings section sits under the **Health** tab for every app, and the load card is named for what the app opens.

| Measure | What it tracks                                    |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **LCP** | How long the main content took to show            |
| **INP** | How quickly the page answered a click, tap or key |
| **CLS** | How much the page jumped around while it loaded   |
