There are plenty of places to write down what a team knows. liveknowledge is built around four ideas that keep it useful after the first month: it is **free**, it is **open source**, it never puts a **sign-in wall** in front of your first page, and every page says **how far to trust it**. Here is what each of those means for you.

## Free to use

liveknowledge is **free for everyone**. There is no trial that expires, no card to enter, and every feature works on the free plan. A Knowledgebase holds up to 500 pages. Ask and Describe to Set Up run on built-in AI, included with daily limits, and your team's own Claude or OpenAI key is optional.

## Open source

The whole codebase is **MIT-licensed** and publicly viewable. That has real consequences:

- **You can check the claims.** How a Knowledgebase is stored and what telemetry records are in the open for anyone to audit.
- **No lock-in.** Because the code is open and your pages [export to Markdown](/help/knowledgebase/exporting-a-knowledgebase/), you are never trapped.

## No sign-in wall

A first-time visitor can open [a new Knowledgebase](/new) and start writing, with no modal in the way. As a guest, your Knowledgebase is kept [Offline](/help/privacy-and-security/offline-mode/), in this browser, and writing and Search work there. An [account](/help/getting-started/guest-vs-account/) adds the rest: saving to every device, Verify, Ask, Insights and sharing. It is never a gate in front of the first page.

## Pages you can trust

A wiki goes stale quietly. In liveknowledge a page has an **owner**, an Editor presses **Verify** to say it is right, and the page comes **Review Due** when the Knowledgebase's Review Period has passed. Readers, and the AI agents that read through the MCP server, see each page's state beside its words, so they know which pages to rely on and which to check first. Where two pages disagree, Insights says so.
