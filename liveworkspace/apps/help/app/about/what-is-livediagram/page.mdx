livediagram is a web app where people build **diagrams, whiteboards, mind maps and workshops together in real time**. It is a multiplayer canvas in the browser: anyone with access to a canvas can join, see other collaborators' cursors and edits live, and contribute at the same time. The result is a shared visual artifact, a flowchart, an architecture diagram, a mind map, or a brainstorm board, that a team builds together rather than one person authoring and the rest reviewing.

## A multiplayer canvas

The heart of livediagram is the [canvas](/help/canvas/the-canvas/). You drop down shapes, connect them with arrows, drag things around, and the diagram takes shape as you work. When more than one person is in the same canvas, everyone sees live cursors, who has what selected, and every change as it happens.

- **Shapes and arrows** for flowcharts, system diagrams, and process maps. See [Shapes](/help/palette/shapes/) and [Arrows](/help/palette/arrows/).
- **Mind maps and brainstorms** built from quick, keyboard-driven nodes: Tab adds a child, Enter a sibling. See [Mind Maps](/help/palette/mind-maps/).
- **Real-time presence** so collaborators rarely overwrite each other: everything someone else has selected, one element or many, is locked for everyone else, and stays locked for a moment after they let go, until their change is saved.
- **Tabs** to keep related views of one canvas in a single place. See [Tabs](/help/tabs/using-tabs/).
- **Modes** for different kinds of work on a tab: Diagram for shapes and arrows, [Draw](/help/canvas/draw-mode/) for freehand whiteboarding, and [Workshop](/help/canvas/workshop-mode/) for running a session such as a retro or workshop, with timers, votes and polls to hand. A mode is how a tab is worked on, so everyone on the tab shares it. See [Editor Modes](/help/canvas/editor-modes/).
- **Templates** for retrospectives, flowcharts, mind maps and more, so you rarely start from a blank page. See [Templates](/help/canvas/templates/).
- **Presenting and walkthroughs** to step through a canvas as a slide deck, or record a narrated tour for anyone who missed the meeting. See [Presenting](/help/selection-modes/slide-deck/presenting/) and [Walkthroughs](/help/collaboration/walkthroughs/).
- **AI tools** such as Claude connect over MCP to find, draw and edit your canvases for you. See [Connect an AI Tool (MCP)](/help/account-and-data/connect-ai-mcp/).

## Built for teams who think visually

livediagram is aimed at people who need to think together, visually: engineering teams sketching architecture and sequence flows, product and design teams mapping journeys and information architecture, and cross-functional groups in workshops, planning sessions, and retrospectives. The unit of value is the team, not the single author.

To make that frictionless, the canvas **always works without signing in**. You can land on a blank diagram, build something real, and hand the link to a colleague who edits it straight away, no account required. See [Sharing your canvas](/help/getting-started/sharing-your-document/).

## Free and open source

livediagram is **MIT-licensed** and **free for everyone**. Every feature works on the free plan, with no trial and no card. It runs at livediagram.app, and the whole codebase is open for anyone to read. Paid plans raise a few limits. See [Why use livediagram?](/help/about/why-livediagram/).
