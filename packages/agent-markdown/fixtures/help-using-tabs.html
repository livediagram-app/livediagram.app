Tabs

One piece of work, many tabs. Tabs let you split a project across several parts, each with its own content, theme, and background.

Applies To

livediagram, liveplanner, livedocuments, livedesign and livehuddle

A canvas is a collection of tabs. A new canvas usually starts with one tab, and you add more whenever a piece of work deserves its own space. The tab bar sits along the bottom of the editor, and the active tab fills the area above it.

In livediagram and livehuddle

A canvas is not a single page: each tab has its own elements, layout, theme, and background.

What a tab is

Each tab is a self-contained canvas inside the canvas. A tab carries its own elements, its own theme and background pattern, and an optional lock, all independent of the other tabs around it. Common ways people split work across tabs:

  • An Overview tab with the high-level picture, and detail tabs behind it.
  • One tab per system, team, or phase of a project.
  • A scratch tab for ideas you are not ready to file yet.

In livediagram

Each tab also has its own mode (Diagram, Draw or Workshop), the same for everyone on it, chosen on its start while it is empty. The small icon at the start of a tab's name shows it.

Adding a tab

Press the + button at the end of the tab bar, or run Create New Tab from the command palette (Cmd / Ctrl + K). The new tab:

  • is named Tab 2, Tab 3 and so on, after the number of tabs in the canvas;
  • takes the theme, background and font of the tab you were on, so it matches the work around it;
  • starts on its own start, so you can begin from a template or close it to keep the tab blank.

In livediagram

It opens in the same mode as the tab you were on: made from a Workshop tab it is a Workshop tab, and from a Draw tab a Draw tab. It starts on that mode's own start, such as Start Drawing or Start a Workshop.

Duplicate in the tab menu makes a copy instead: it is named after the original with "copy" on the end, sits right after it, and is a separate tab with its own content from then on.

Naming and reordering tabs

Tabs start with default names like Tab 1 and Tab 2, and you can rename any of them:

  • Double-click the active tab's name to edit it inline. Enter or clicking away saves, Escape cancels, and an empty name keeps the old one.
  • Rename in the tab menu (and Rename Tab in the command palette) opens the same editor.

In livediagram, livedocuments, livedesign and livehuddle

Auto-rename on first edit: while a tab still has its default name, editing the label of its first element renames the tab to match. This only happens once, so later edits leave a renamed tab alone.

Tab names are capped at 60 characters. Runs of spaces and line breaks collapse to one, and anything longer is cut at the last whole word with an ellipsis, so a paragraph typed into a sticky can't turn into an unreadable tab.

Drag a tab left or right to reorder it. A blue bar shows where it will land: drop on the left half of a tab to land before it, the right half to land after it. Reordering only moves the tab in the list, it never touches the content on any tab.

Drag a tab up off the bar to the right edge of the screen instead, and it opens beside the tab you're on: see Side by Side Tabs.

The tab menu

Click the ⋯ button on the active tab, or right-click any tab (which switches to it first), to open its menu. On a phone it opens as a sheet from the bottom of the screen.

In livediagram, livedocuments, livedesign and livehuddle

Right-clicking an empty spot on the canvas opens the same menu, with Paste moved to a row of its own at the top.

Along the top sits a row of quick actions: Rename, Duplicate, Paste (greyed out until you have copied something), Side by Side (on a large enough window; Close Side by Side while two tabs are open), Lock Tab and Delete. Below them, everything else is a full-width row, in groups split by a line:

  • Organise in Tab Folder and Add to Another Canvas.
  • Import, Export, and Clear, which removes every element from the tab after a confirmation (undo brings them back; it is greyed out on an empty tab).

In livediagram, livedocuments, livedesign and livehuddle

To tidy a tab's layout, search for Auto Layout or Auto-align in the command palette. See Cleaning Up a Tab.

In livediagram

In Diagram mode you can also press Tidy in the bottom-right corner. The session tools (timer, vote and poll) are not in this menu: they have their own buttons in the bottom bar.

In livediagram

Deleting a tab

Choose Delete in the tab menu. The tab goes straight away, and Undo brings it back. Once you reload or leave the canvas it is gone, unless another canvas also holds it, so export or duplicate anything you might want later. A few things to know:

  • A canvas always keeps at least one tab, so Delete is greyed out on the last one.
  • Links on other tabs that pointed at the deleted tab are removed with it.
  • If the tab is also in another canvas, it is only removed from this one.

In livediagram, liveplanner, livedocuments and livedesign

A locked tab can't be deleted until you unlock it.

Share links

On a share link for a Viewer or a Participant, and on any link scoped to one tab, you can still click between tabs, but the + button, the tab menu, renaming and drag-to-reorder are all hidden. Only an Editor with a link to the whole canvas changes its tabs.

More with tabs

Each tab also carries its own menu of actions, covered in their own guides:

  • Tab Folders group related tabs under one named chip so a long tab bar stays readable.
  • Side by Side Tabs puts two tabs on screen at once, each live, with the toolbars following you between them.
  • Connected Files adds a tab that opens a file from any liveworkspace app: a plan, a document, a design or another canvas.
  • Importing and Exporting move a tab's contents in and out as a file.

In livediagram, livedocuments, livedesign and livehuddle

Cleaning Up a Tab tidies a tab's layout in one click.

In livediagram and livedesign

Linking Across Tabs turns any element into a jump point to another tab.

In livediagram, livedocuments and livehuddle

Add a Tab to Another Canvas shares a tab into a different canvas: it is the same tab in both places, not a copy, so edits appear everywhere it lives.

In livediagram, liveplanner, livedocuments and livedesign

Locking a Tab makes a whole tab read-only.

A shared reference tab such as a glossary can live in more than one canvas at once with Add to Another Canvas. Editing it in one place updates it everywhere.

How this works in 4 other apps

In liveplanner

Each tab holds exactly one thing: a Board of cards in columns, a Sheet of cells and formulas, a Dashboard of metrics and charts, or a Form whose answers become cards.

In liveplanner

Common ways to split a plan across tabs: a Board for the day-to-day work and a Dashboard beside it that sums it up; a Sheet for the budget or the numbers behind the plan; a Form for requests, whose answers land as cards on a board. The cards on a plan's boards belong to the plan rather than to one tab, so two boards can show the same cards, and a Dashboard can chart them. See Planning Basics. The small icon at the start of a tab's name shows what it holds.

In liveplanner

It takes the look of the tab you were on, and opens on Start Planning, where you pick what it holds: a board, sheet, form, visualisation or dashboard, from a template or blank. Picking one usually names the tab too, such as "Kanban" for a Kanban board.

In liveplanner

There is no row for adding the tab to another plan: a tab belongs to one plan (see below). Import… opens the Import dialog: a CSV into a Board or Sheet tab, a tab's JSON, or a board from Trello, Asana, Jira or Linear. Clear empties the tab after a confirmation (Undo brings it back).

In liveplanner

Delete asks first only when it does something Undo can't take back: a form's tab deletes the form's link with it, and a board or sheet that forms send their responses to stops them taking responses. Links on other tabs are not mentioned in liveplanner.

In liveplanner

Tabs in more than one plan. A tab belongs to one plan. Tabs used to be addable to another plan from the tab menu, but a plan's tabs show its cards, and a card is kept in the plan it came from, so a tab carried into another plan couldn't take its cards with it. Tabs that were already shared between plans stay shared: an edit in either plan shows in both, and deleting the tab in one plan only removes it from that one. To reuse a tab's layout somewhere new, export it and import it into the other plan.

In liveplanner

Lots of tabs? Press ⌘K and type part of a tab's name to jump straight to it, or group them into folders. In liveplanner a tab belongs to one plan, so a shared reference tab as above does not apply.
